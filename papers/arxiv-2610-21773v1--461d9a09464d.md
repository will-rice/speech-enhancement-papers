# CF-AVSS: A Coarse-to-Fine Audio-Visual Speech Separation Framework with Target-Speaker Refinement and Post-Fusion for Real-World AVSE Challenge

###### Abstract

Audio-visual speech enhancement (AVSE) leverages visual cues to recover
target speech from mixtures corrupted by reverberation, noise, and
overlapping speakers. We propose a coarse-to-fine two-stage AVSE
framework. In the first stage, a progressive SPAV-TFGridNet is extended
to two synchronized visual streams to produce visually ordered coarse
estimates by progressively suppressing interfering speech while
preserving target reverberation and shared background noise. In the
second stage, a TFGridNet-based target speaker refinement network takes
the original mixture and both coarse estimates as input to further
recover target speech and improve signal quality. The coarse and refined
estimates are finally combined using development-set-calibrated fusion.
On the ISCSLP 2026 Real-World AVSE Challenge, our system tied for first
place on Track 1 and ranked second on Track 2 in OVRL, while achieving
the lowest CER on both tracks at 12.31% and 14.79%, respectively.

## 1 Introduction

Audio-visual speech enhancement (AVSE) combines acoustic observations
with visual speech cues to recover desired speech from adverse
recordings \[1, 2, 3, 4\]. Lip motion and facial articulation provide
target-dependent information that does not suffer from acoustic
noise \[5\], so AVSE can improve communication, automatic speech
recognition, and human-machine interaction when reverberation, noise, or
concurrent speakers make audio-only processing unreliable \[6\].

Most AVSE systems encode a noisy mixture and one target face track,
align the two feature streams, and estimate one enhanced waveform \[7\].
Time-domain separators and time-frequency masking networks commonly
follow this paradigm: an acoustic encoder represents the mixture, a
visual encoder represents mouth motion, a fusion module injects the
visual cue into the acoustic stream, and a decoder reconstructs the
target signal. This direct mapping works well for additive simulations
and clean frontal videos, but it leaves several problems in real
recordings. Natural overlap does not follow ideal additive assumptions;
rooms introduce long reverberant tails and device-dependent responses;
and imperfect faces, side views, occlusion, motion blur, low resolution,
frozen frames, or audio-visual desynchronization can weaken visual
conditioning. Aggressive one-step enhancement may also suppress weak
target phonemes while removing interference, which creates a conflict
between perceptual quality, intelligibility, and identity preservation.

The ISCSLP 2026 Real-World AVSE Challenge was designed to bridge the gap
between clean-video benchmarks and practical deployment conditions \[8,
9\]. Track 1 evaluates naturally captured multi-speaker recordings with
spontaneous speech overlap, room reverberation, background noise, and
device-dependent acoustic responses. Track 2 further degrades the visual
modality through occlusion, low resolution, frozen or dropped frames,
and audio-visual desynchronization, while also introducing far-field
recordings captured at a distance of 3 m. Each track contains real _mix_
scenes without isolated acoustic references and reference-available
_remix_ scenes constructed from separately recorded sources. These
conditions require a system to maintain reliable target-speaker
association when either the acoustic or visual evidence becomes
unreliable. The evaluation consequently combines intrusive fidelity
metrics, non-intrusive quality estimators, recognition accuracy, and
speaker similarity, emphasizing balanced performance rather than
optimization of a single enhancement criterion.

In this paper, we address these conditions with coarse separation
followed by fine extraction. We first extend SPAV-TFGridNet \[10\] from
a single visually conditioned output to two jointly estimated, visually
ordered outputs. Progressive supervision removes reverberant non-target
speakers in stages while preserving the target room response and common
noise. We then use a TFGridNet-based\[11\] refinement model to extract
cleaner target speech from the original mixture, guided by both coarse
estimates. An asymmetric over-suppression loss discourages the removal
of weak target components, while PESQ-oriented and adversarial losses
complement SI-SDR and compressed-spectral reconstruction. Finally, we
select one linear-fusion coefficient from the development set and keep
it fixed for test inference. The resulting system obtained an official
OVRL of 2.86 on both tracks, corresponding to joint first place on
Track 1 and second place on Track 2. Its CER of 12.31% and 14.79% was
the best submitted result on Track 1 and Track 2, respectively.

## 2 Preliminary

### 2.1 Problem formulation

We consider a single-channel noisy and reverberant mixture containing
$`M\geq 2`$ speakers,

```math
y=\sum_{m=1}^{M}u_{m}+n,\hskip 18.49988ptu_{m}=h_{m}*s_{m}, \tag{1}
```

where $`s_{m}`$ is the clean speech of speaker $`m`$, $`h_{m}`$ is its
room impulse response, $`u_{m}`$ is the corresponding reverberant
speech, and $`n`$ denotes background noise. Given the mixture $`y`$ and
two visual streams $`(v_{1},v_{2})`$, our system estimates two enhanced
signals $`(\tilde{s}_{f1},\tilde{s}_{f2})`$, each aligned with its
corresponding visible speaker, while suppressing the remaining
interfering speakers.

## 3 Method

In this section, we first describe the audio-visual representations used
to associate the mixture with two target speakers. We then introduce the
progressive coarse AVSS stage, which suppresses competing speakers while
preserving target reverberation and shared background noise. Next, we
present the contextual TSR network for recovering cleaner target signals
from the original mixture under the guidance of the coarse estimates.
Finally, we describe the development-set-calibrated post-fusion strategy
that combines the complementary information retained by the coarse and
refined outputs.

### 3.1 System overview

As shown in Fig. 1, given an observed mixture $`y`$ and two synchronized
video streams, the visual encoder extracts target-specific features
$`e_{v_{i}}`$, where $`i\in\{1,2\}`$. Conditioned on these features, the
coarse audio-visual speech separation (AVSS) stage suppresses competing
speech and generates visually ordered estimates $`\tilde{s}_{c_{i}}`$,
keeping each estimate aligned with its corresponding visual stream. The
TFGridNet-based target speaker refinement (TSR) stage then jointly
processes $`y`$ and both coarse estimates to produce refined estimates
$`\tilde{s}_{r_{i}}`$, which are combined through
development-set-calibrated post-fusion to obtain the final outputs
$`\tilde{s}_{f_{i}}`$.

### 3.2 Visual representation

For each video stream, we apply the official AV-HuBERT preprocessing
pipeline, which detects 68 facial landmarks, aligns the face to a
canonical mean shape, and crops a mouth-centered region of interest
(ROI) \[12\]. The ROI sequence is then processed by AV-HuBERT Base to
directly extract visual speech features $`e_{v_{i}}`$ at 25 frames per
second, where $`i\in\{1,2\}`$. The two visual features $`e_{v_{1}}`$ and
$`e_{v_{2}}`$, together with the mixture $`y`$, are fed to the coarse
separation stage while retaining their correspondence to the input video
streams.

### 3.3 Progressive coarse separation

As shown in Fig. 2, the coarse AVSS stage suppresses competing speech
while preserving target reverberation and shared background noise. We
adapt SIR-Progressive Audio-Visual TF-GridNet \[10\] to jointly estimate
two visually ordered targets. After Conv2D encoding, the mixture $`y`$
is processed by $`K`$ cascaded AVTFGrid blocks, each comprising a TFGrid
block and two feature-wise audio-visual attention (FAVA) modules
conditioned on $`e_{v1}`$ and $`e_{v2}`$ \[13\]; their internal
structures follow \[10\]. The $`k`$-th decoder predicts

```math
\left(\tilde{s}_{c1}^{(k)},\tilde{s}_{c2}^{(k)}\right)=F_{\mathrm{sep}}^{(k)}(y,e_{v1},e_{v2}),\hskip 18.49988ptk=1,\ldots,K, \tag{2}
```

where $`\tilde{s}_{ci}=\tilde{s}_{ci}^{(K)}`$ is the final coarse
estimate for visual stream $`i`$. Tying each output index to its visual
stream avoids permutation ambiguity during both training and inference.

For target $`i`$, let $`q_{i}=\sum_{m\neq i}u_{m}`$ denote the competing
reverberant speech. The final coarse target is

```math
c_{i}=u_{i}+n, \tag{3}
```

which retains the complete reverberant target and shared background
noise while excluding competing speakers. Since $`n`$ is shared by both
targets, we impose no mixture-consistency constraint requiring
$`\tilde{s}_{c1}+\tilde{s}_{c2}=y`$.

Following the progressive training strategy in \[10\], the target at
level $`k`$ is

```math
c_{i}^{(k)}=c_{i}+\gamma_{k}q_{i},\hskip 18.49988pt1\geq\gamma_{1}>\cdots>\gamma_{K}=0, \tag{4}
```

where $`\gamma_{k}`$ follows the stage-wise SIR schedule. Thus, earlier
levels retain more interfering speech, whereas the final level reaches
the background-preserving coarse target.

For a complex STFT coefficient $`Z`$, we define power compression as
$`C_{p}(Z)=|Z|^{p}\exp(j\angle Z)`$. All progressive outputs are
supervised by

```math
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sep}}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}w_{k}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\Big[ \displaystyle-\lambda_{s}\mathrm{SI\mbox{-}SDR}\left(\tilde{s}_{ci}^{(k)},c_{i}^{(k)}\right)
```

```math
\displaystyle+\lambda_{c}\left\|C_{p}\left(\tilde{S}_{ci}^{(k)}\right)-C_{p}\left(C_{i}^{(k)}\right)\right\|_{2}^{2}\Big], \tag{5}
```

where $`\tilde{S}_{ci}^{(k)}`$ and $`C_{i}^{(k)}`$ are the STFTs of the
prediction and target, respectively, and $`w_{k}`$ is the level weight.
The compressed spectral term preserves absolute scale and emphasizes
low-energy components \[14\].

### 3.4 Contextual target speaker refinement

Although the coarse estimates preserve target identity, they may contain
residual interference or missing target components. We therefore employ
a TFGridNet-based TSR network that revisits the original mixture $`y`$.
For each target, a contextual encoder jointly encodes $`y`$ and the
corresponding coarse estimate $`\tilde{s}_{ci}`$; we adopt the
contextual encoder from \[15\]. The two contextual representations are
concatenated and processed by stacked TFGrid blocks followed by a
Deconv2D decoder:

```math
\left(\tilde{s}_{r1},\tilde{s}_{r2}\right)=F_{\mathrm{TSR}}\left(y,\tilde{s}_{c1},\tilde{s}_{c2}\right), \tag{6}
```

where $`\tilde{s}_{ri}`$ is the refined estimate for target $`i`$. The
mixture provides target components suppressed during coarse separation,
while the two coarse estimates preserve target identity and provide
complementary separation cues.

We supervise each refined estimate using its clean target $`s_{i}`$. The
reconstruction loss combines SI-SDR with compressed complex-spectral
reconstruction:

```math
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left[-\mathrm{SI\mbox{-}SDR}\left(\tilde{s}_{ri},s_{i}\right)+\lambda_{r}\left\|C_{p}\left(\tilde{S}_{ri}\right)-C_{p}\left(S_{i}\right)\right\|_{2}^{2}\right], \tag{7}
```

where $`\tilde{S}_{ri}`$ and $`S_{i}`$ are the complex STFTs of the
refined estimate and clean target, respectively.

To reduce over-suppression, let
$`A_{i,tf}=\left|[C_{p}(S_{i})]_{tf}\right|`$,
$`B_{i,tf}=\left|[C_{p}(\tilde{S}_{ri})]_{tf}\right|`$, and let
$`W_{i,tf}`$ indicate target-active time-frequency bins. We define the
asymmetric loss

```math
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{over}}=\frac{\sum_{i,t,f}W_{i,tf}\left[\max\left(A_{i,tf}-B_{i,tf},0\right)\right]^{2}}{\sum_{i,t,f}W_{i,tf}+\epsilon}, \tag{8}
```

which penalizes missing target magnitude in active bins without directly
encouraging excess energy.

We further employ a differentiable PESQ-oriented loss, implemented using
torch-pesq⁰⁰ 0
[https://github.com/audiolabs/torch-pesq](https://github.com/audiolabs/torch-pesq),
together with the adversarial training scheme of PASE \[16\]. Let
$`\mathcal{D}=\{D_{t},D_{f}\}`$ contain the time- and frequency-domain
discriminators. The generator adversarial loss is

```math
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{adv}}=\sum_{D\in\mathcal{D}}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(D(\tilde{s}_{ri})-1\right)^{2}\right], \tag{9}
```

while the discriminators use the corresponding least-squares real/fake
objective. The overall refinement objective is

```math
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TSR}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}}+\mu_{o}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{over}}+\mu_{p}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pesq}}+\mu_{a}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{adv}}+\mu_{f}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fm}}, \tag{10}
```

where $`\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fm}}`$ denotes discriminator feature
matching and $`\mu_{o}`$, $`\mu_{p}`$, $`\mu_{a}`$, and $`\mu_{f}`$ are
the corresponding loss weights.

### 3.5 Post-fusion

The coarse and refined estimates are complementary: the former preserves
the reverberation and background ambience, whereas the latter favors
cleaner target extraction. We linearly combine them as

```math
\tilde{s}_{fi}=(1-\alpha)\tilde{s}_{ci}+\alpha\tilde{s}_{ri},\hskip 18.49988pt0\leq\alpha\leq 1, \tag{11}
```

where $`\tilde{s}_{fi}`$ is the final estimate for target $`i`$ and
$`\alpha`$ is a global fusion weight selected on the development set.
The selected value is fixed for all test samples without reference-based
or utterance-dependent tuning.

## 4 Experimental Setup

### 4.1 Datasets

During training, we use VoxCeleb2 \[17\] and the MISP 2023 Challenge
dataset \[18\]. VoxCeleb2 provides diverse synchronized audio-visual
utterances, while MISP introduces multi-party and far-field recording
conditions. To construct each training mixture, we sample
$`M\in\{2,3,4\}`$ speakers with probabilities 0.75, 0.20, and 0.05,
respectively. We designate two speakers as visible targets and treat the
remaining speakers as off-screen interferers. Each source utterance is
independently convolved with a 16-kHz room impulse response sampled from
OpenSLR SLR26 or SLR28 \[19\]. We then sum the reverberant sources, add
environmental noise, and crop the audio and corresponding video streams
into synchronized 4.0-s segments.

We use official development sets for validation and parameter selection.
The Track 1 development set contains 1,242 real-recorded _mix_ clips and
900 reference-available _remix_ clips, while the Track 2 development set
contains 1,527 _mix_ clips and 1,098 _remix_ clips. We use these data
for model selection and hyperparameter tuning, including the post-fusion
coefficient.

We perform the final evaluation on the official test sets, which contain
4,257 clips for Track 1 and 4,941 clips for Track 2. The reference
signals remain hidden and all test scores are computed by the
organizers. Track 2 further introduces degraded visual conditions and
additional far-field recordings, resulting in more severe visual
corruption and acoustic mismatch than Track 1.

### 4.2 Evaluation metrics

We follow the official evaluation protocol of the ISCSLP 2026 Real-World
AVSE Challenge \[8, 9\]. SI-SDR \[20\], PESQ \[21\], and STOI \[22\] are
evaluated on the _remix_ subset against clean references, whereas the
non-intrusive UTMOSv2 \[23, 24\] and DNSMOS metrics are computed on both
_mix_ and _remix_. DNSMOS includes P.808 overall quality and P.835 SIG,
BAK, and OVRL scores \[25, 26\]. CER is computed from FunASR \[27\]
hypotheses after text normalization, and SPK-SIM is the cosine
similarity between WeSpeaker \[28\] embeddings of enhanced speech and
enrollment voiceprints. Higher is better for all metrics except CER and
the official OVRL rank score.

### 4.3 Implementation details

We train both the AVSS and TSR networks for 50 epochs using AdamW with
an initial learning rate of $`10^{-3}`$, a weight decay of $`10^{-4}`$,
and a batch size of 8. For progressive separation, we construct the
intermediate targets using SIR levels of $`\{0,5,10,15\}`$ dB, followed
by the interference-free coarse target. In the AVSS objective, we set
the SI-SDR and compressed complex-spectral loss weights to
$`\lambda_{s}=\lambda_{c}=1.0`$. For TSR, we assign unit weights to the
SI-SDR and compressed complex-spectral reconstruction terms and set the
over-suppression, PESQ-oriented, adversarial, and feature-matching loss
weights to $`\mu_{o}=0.1`$, $`\mu_{p}=0.05`$, $`\mu_{a}=0.01`$, and
$`\mu_{f}=0.1`$, respectively. For Track 2, we apply random visual
degradation, including spatial masking, Gaussian blur, and frame
dropout, and drop the visual modality with a probability of 0.1. We
evaluate the post-fusion coefficient on the development set and select
$`\alpha=0.34`$ according to the trade-off among intrusive quality,
non-intrusive quality, recognition accuracy, and speaker similarity. The
selected coefficient is fixed for both tracks during test inference.

## 5 Results and Discussion

### 5.1 Official test-set results

Table 1 compares our system with five anonymized teams on the official
test set. Our system shares first place on Track 1 and ranks second on
Track 2. It also achieves the lowest CER on both tracks, reaching 12.31%
on Track 1 and 14.79% on Track 2. On Track 1, our method maintains
competitive intrusive metrics together with strong speaker similarity.
When evaluated under the more challenging visual conditions of Track 2,
it exhibits only moderate degradation in SI-SDR, STOI, and SPK-SIM,
suggesting that the coarse visual identity cues and mixture-conditioned
TSR remain effective when the visual evidence becomes less reliable.

The results further demonstrate the multi-objective nature of real-world
AVSE. Team 1 achieves better intrusive metrics, whereas several other
systems obtain higher scores on individual non-intrusive quality
estimators. In contrast, our method delivers consistently strong
performance across speech quality, intelligibility, speaker
preservation, and recognition accuracy. In particular, its leading CER
results indicate that the proposed system effectively suppresses
interference without severely damaging target-speech content.

### 5.2 Development-set ablation study

We conduct a compact ablation study on the Track 2 development set to
evaluate the contributions of the TSR network and post-fusion. All
variants are evaluated under the same protocol and differ only in the
removed component.

Table 2: Ablation results on the Track 2 development set.

Table 2 demonstrate that coarse separation and TSR provide complementary
estimates. TSR raises DNS-OVRL from 1.56 to 2.20, but decreases SI-SDR,
PESQ, and SPK-SIM and increases CER, indicating that stronger perceptual
enhancement may discard target-speech components. Post-fusion balances
these behaviors, achieving the best SI-SDR, PESQ, CER, and SPK-SIM while
preserving a clear DNS-OVRL improvement over coarse separation alone.
The coarse and refined estimates act as correlated observations that
retain different target information: the former preserves speaker
identity and acoustic context, whereas the latter emphasizes perceptual
quality. Combining them preserves their shared target content while
recovering complementary components attenuated by either estimate alone.

## 6 Conclusion

We presented a two-stage method for real-world AVSE. Progressive,
visually conditioned coarse separation establishes the two target
identities while retaining their room responses and shared background
noise. A mixture-conditioned TFGridNet-based TSR then produces cleaner
target signals while mitigating over-suppression. We linearly fuse the
coarse and refined outputs using a coefficient selected on the
development set. The system ranked joint first on Track 1 and second on
Track 2 of the ISCSLP 2026 Real-World AVSE Challenge and achieved the
best CER on both tracks.
