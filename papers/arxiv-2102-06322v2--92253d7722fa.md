# JOINT DEREVERBERATION AND SEPARATION with ITERATIVE SOURCE STEERING

Taishi Nakashima ^(†)^(†)thanks: This work was done while Taishi
Nakashima was an intern at LINE Corporation.    Robin Scheibler   
Masahito Togami    Nobutaka Ono

###### Abstract

We propose a new algorithm for joint dereverberation and blind source
separation (DR-BSS). Our work builds upon the IRLMA-T framework that
applies a unified filter combining dereverberation and separation. One
drawback of this framework is that it requires several matrix
inversions, an operation inherently costly and with potential stability
issues. We leverage the recently introduced iterative source steering
(ISS) updates to propose two algorithms mitigating this issue. Albeit
derived from first principles, the first algorithm turns out to be a
natural combination of weighted prediction error (WPE) dereverberation
and ISS-based BSS, applied alternatingly. In this case, we manage to
reduce the number of matrix inversion to only one per iteration and
source. The second algorithm updates the ILRMA-T matrix using only
sequential ISS updates requiring no matrix inversion at all. Its
implementation is straightforward and memory efficient. Numerical
experiments demonstrate that both methods achieve the same final
performance as ILRMA-T in terms of several relevant objective metrics.
In the important case of two sources, the number of iterations required
is also similar.

###### Index Terms: 

Blind source separation, dereverberation, joint optimization,
independent low-rank matrix analysis, iterative source steering.

## 1 Introduction

Speech signals recorded by a microphone are routinely contaminated by
reverberation and interference. Blind source separation
(BSS) \[[1](#bib.bib1), [2](#bib.bib2), [3](#bib.bib3)\], e.g.,
independent component analysis (ICA) \[[4](#bib.bib4)\], independent
vector analysis (IVA) \[[5](#bib.bib5), [6](#bib.bib6), [7](#bib.bib7),
[8](#bib.bib8)\], and dereverberation (DR) \[[9](#bib.bib9)\]
techniques, e.g., weighted prediction error (WPE) \[[10](#bib.bib10)\],
are all countermeasures that have been proposed to recover the speech
quality required for communication, speech diarization, and automatic
speech recognition (ASR) systems. Historically, DR and BSS have evolved
separately, and their joint optimization has not yet matured. Joint
optimization is highly desirable to realize DR and BSS in the same
framework (DR-BSS) as it typically leads to higher speech quality.

DR-BSS algorithms have been actively studied since
WPE \[[10](#bib.bib10)\] was introduced \[[11](#bib.bib11),
[12](#bib.bib12), [13](#bib.bib13), [14](#bib.bib14), [15](#bib.bib15),
[16](#bib.bib16)\]. A popular approach is to combine
WPE \[[10](#bib.bib10)\] with a BSS algorithm such as Independent
Low-Rank Matrix Analysis (ILRMA) \[[17](#bib.bib17)\]. Early
studies \[[12](#bib.bib12), [13](#bib.bib13)\] use separate DR and BSS
filters. However, computational cost of these approaches is very high
due to the necessity of computing the inverse of a large matrix whose
dimension is the product of the square of the numbers of microphones
with the number of taps of the DR filter. The recently proposed
ILRMA-T \[[14](#bib.bib14), [15](#bib.bib15)\] overcomes this difficulty
by introducing a unified filter combining the DR and BSS filter.
Nevertheless, ILRMA-T still requires to invert two matrices per source
and iteration. Because DR-BSS algorithms are typically needed in edge
and embedded devices, where computational power is at a premium, inverse
matrix computations are best avoided.

ILRMA-T derives the update equations for its DR-BSS matrix from the
iterative projection (IP) rules of BSS \[[8](#bib.bib8)\]. In the BSS
context, some of the authors have proposed iterative source steering
(ISS), an alternative to IP that is more computationally efficient and
does not require matrix inversion \[[18](#bib.bib18)\]. Thus, the ISS
based approach is more stable than the IP one. To the best of our
knowledge, ISS based DR-BSS has not been studied yet.

In this paper, we propose a joint optimization framework for DR-BSS with
ISS \[[18](#bib.bib18)\]. The proposed method optimizes the same cost
function as ILRMA-T, but using the ISS updates. Thus, we call it
ILRMA-T-ISS. Two variants of ILRMA-T-ISS are proposed. The first one is
obtained by updating all the weights in the DR-BSS matrix corresponding
to dereverberation in a single step, and apply ISS for the rest. The
resulting algorithm turns out to be a natural combination of WPE and
ISS, with their respective updates applied alternatingly. We call this
algorithm ILRMA-T-ISS-JOINT. ILRMA-T-ISS-JOINT reduces the number of
matrix inversions to only one per iteration and source. The second
variant, ILRMA-T-ISS-SEQ, applies sequential ISS updates to the whole
matrix. This has the happy consequence that not a single matrix
inversion is required. One practical consequence is that its
implementation is straightforward and no external linear algebra library
is needed. These properties are all highly desirable in edge and
embedded systems. We conduct numerical experiments to confirm the
efficacy of the proposed method in noisy reverberant environment with
multiple speech sources. We confirm that separation and dereverberation
performance are on par with ILRMA-T-IP, even without the matrix
inversions.

## 2 Background

### 2.1 Signal model and notation

Let $`N`$ and $`M`$ be the numbers of sources and microphones,
respectively. Henceforth, we consider the determined case, $`N=M`$. We
use the short-term Fourier transform (STFT) representation of microphone
input signals. The microphone input signal is modeled as the following
convolutive mixture:

|     |                                                       |                                                                                                        |     |     |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\bm{x}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f,t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{d=0}^{D-1}\bm{A}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f,d}\bm{s}_{f,t-d}\in\mathbb{C}^{N},`$ |     | (1) |

where $`f\in\{1,\dots,F\}`$ and $`t\in\{1,\dots,T\}`$ are the frequency
bin and the time frame indices, respectively,
$`\bm{A}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f,d}`$ is the mixing matrix with
$`(\bm{A}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f,d})_{n,m}=a_{n,m,f,d}`$,
$`\bm{s}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f,t}`$ is the source signal, and
$`n=\{1,\dots,N\}`$ is the source channel index.

In the rest of the manuscript, $`{}^{\top},^{\mathsf{H}}`$, and $`\det`$
denote the transpose, Hermitian transpose, and determinant of a
vector/matrix, respectively. We denote the $`n`$th canonical basis
vector by $`\bm{e}^{\phantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{n}`$, an all zero vector
$`\bm{0}`$, and the identity matrix by $`\bm{I}`$.

### 2.2 Dereverberation based on Weighted prediction error (WPE)

WPE \[[10](#bib.bib10)\] is a popular approach for DR. In
WPE, ([1](#S2.E1 "Equation 1 ‣ 2.1 Signal model and notation ‣ 2 Background ‣ JOINT DEREVERBERATION AND SEPARATION with ITERATIVE SOURCE STEERING"))
is converted to the following auto-regressive (AR) model:

|     |                                                       |                                                                                                       |     |     |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\bm{x}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f,t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{\tau=0}^{L-1}\bm{Z}_{f,\tau}\bm{x}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f,t-\Delta-\tau},`$ |     | (2) |

where $`\bm{Z}`$ is a matrix which contains the AR coefficients, and
$`L`$ is the tap-length of the AR model. The WPE assumes that there is
only one speech source, and $`\bm{Z}`$ is optimized with the
time-varying variance of the speech source $`r_{f,t}`$ as follows:

|     |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |     |     |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\bar{\bm{Z}}_{f}=\left(\sum_{t}\frac{\overline{\bm{x}}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f,t}\overline{\bm{x}}^{\mathsf{H}}_{f,t}}{r_{f,t}}\right)^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}\sum_{t}\frac{\overline{\bm{x}}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f,t}x_{1,f,t}^{\ast}}{r_{f,t}}&\dots&\sum_{t}\frac{\overline{\bm{x}}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f,t}x_{N,t}^{\ast}}{r_{f,t}}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}`$ |     | (3) |

where
$`\overline{\bm{x}}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f,t}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{x}^{\top}_{f,t-\Delta}&\cdots&\bm{x}^{\top}_{f,t-\Delta-L+1}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\in\mathbb{C}^{NL}`$,
$`\Delta`$ is the delay, and
$`\overline{\bm{Z}}_{f}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{Z}_{f,0}&\cdots&\bm{Z}_{f,L-1}\end{bmatrix}`$.
The dereverberated signal $`\bm{z}_{f,t}`$ is obtained as
$`\bm{z}_{f,t}=\bm{x}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f,t}-\overline{\bm{Z}}_{f}\overline{\bm{x}}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f,t}`$.
Then, we update $`r_{f,t}=\frac{\lVert\bm{z}_{f,t}\rVert^{2}}{M}`$.
Thus, $`\overline{\bm{Z}}_{f}`$ and $`r_{f,t}`$ are updated in an
iterative manner.

### 2.3 Joint dereverberation and separation

Cascade connection of the WPE and the BSS is not optimum because the WPE
assumes that there is only one source. In \[[12](#bib.bib12),
[13](#bib.bib13)\], joint optimization of the WPE and the BSS is
performed by using a WPE filter followed by a BSS filter. The output
signal is obtained as
$`\bm{y}_{f,t}=\bm{W}_{f}\left(\bm{x}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f,t}-\overline{\bm{Z}}_{f}\overline{\bm{x}}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f,t}\right)`$.
A determined approach is proposed in \[[13](#bib.bib13)\] for
optimization of $`\bm{W}_{f}`$ and $`\overline{\bm{Z}}_{f}`$
sequentially, such that the separated signal $`\bm{y}_{f,t}`$ is the
maximum likelihood estimator of $`\bm{s}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f,t}`$
under the assumptions

1.  1.  the sources are statistically independent,
2.  2.  a source signal at each time-frequency bin belongs to a complex
        Gaussian distribution:  
        $`p(y_{n,f,t})=\frac{1}{\pi r_{n,f,t}}\exp\left(-\frac{\lvert y_{n,f,t}\rvert^{2}}{r_{n,f,t}}\right)`$,
        where $`y_{n,f,t}`$ is the $`n`$th element of
        $`\bm{y}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f,t}`$ and $`r_{n,f,t}`$ is the
        time-varying variance of the $`n`$th source,
3.  3.  $`r_{n,f,t}`$ is modeled as
        $`r_{n,f,t}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}c_{n,k,f}b_{n,t,k}`$, where $`K`$ is the
        number of basis vectors, $`c_{n,k,f}\geq 0`$ is the basis
        coefficient of the $`n`$th component, and $`b_{n,t,k}\geq 0`$ is the
        time-varying activity of the $`n`$th component.

Parameters are updated to maximize the following negative log-likelihood
function $`\mathcal{J}`$:

|     |                              |                                                                                                                  |            |                                                   |
| --- | ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- | ------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{J}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum*{f,t}\Biggl[-2\log\lvert\det\bm{W}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}*{f}\rvert+\sum\_{n}\left(\frac{{ | y\_{n,f,t} | }^{2}}{r*{n,f,t}}+\log r*{n,f,t}\right)\Biggr].`$ |     | (4) |

The IP based parameter optimization \[[8](#bib.bib8)\] can be
straightforwardly applied for optimization of $`\bm{W}_{f}`$.
Non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) is used to update $`c_{n,k,f}`$
and $`b_{n,t,k}`$ \[[17](#bib.bib17)\]. The optimal
$`\overline{\bm{Z}}_{f}`$ is also obtained straightforwardly by
minimizing $`\mathcal{J}`$. However, when $`\overline{\bm{Z}}_{f}`$ is
updated, it is necessary to calculate the inverse matrix of a
large-scale matrix whose dimension is proportional to $`M^{2}L`$. Thus,
computational cost is quite high.

As an alternative, ILRMA-T \[[14](#bib.bib14), [15](#bib.bib15)\] has
been proposed. ILRMA-T combines WPE and ILRMA \[[17](#bib.bib17)\] for
joint dereverbertion and separation. In ILRMA-T, the output signal is
obtained by a unified filter $`\bm{P}_{f}`$ as
$`\bm{y}_{f,t}=\bm{P}_{f}\tilde{\bm{x}}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f,t}`$,
where
$`\tilde{\bm{x}}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f,t}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{x}^{\top}_{f,t}&\overline{\bm{x}}^{\top}_{f,t}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\in\mathbb{C}^{N(L+1)}`$
and
$`\bm{P}_{f}=\bm{W}_{f}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}&-\overline{\bm{Z}}_{f}\end{bmatrix}`$.

The cost function of ILRMA-T is equivalent
to ([4](#S2.E4 "Equation 4 ‣ 2.3 Joint dereverberation and separation ‣ 2 Background ‣ JOINT DEREVERBERATION AND SEPARATION with ITERATIVE SOURCE STEERING")),
that is,

|     |                              |                                                                                                                                                                                                            |     |     |
| --- | ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{J}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{f}\left[-2\log\lvert\det\bm{W}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f}\rvert+\sum_{n}\bm{p}^{\mathsf{H}}_{n,f}\bm{V}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{n,f}\bm{p}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{n,f}\right],`$ |     | (5) |

where
$`\displaystyle\bm{V}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{n,f}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t}\frac{\tilde{\bm{x}}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f,t}\tilde{\bm{x}}^{\mathsf{H}}_{f,t}}{r_{n,f,t}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N(L+1)\times N(L+1)}`$
is the weighted covariance matrix of
$`\tilde{\bm{x}}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f,t}`$.

Instead of optimizing $`\bm{W}_{f}`$ and $`\bm{Z}_{f}`$ sequentially,
ILRMA-T optimizes each row vector of $`\bm{P}_{f}`$ sequentially based
on IP \[[8](#bib.bib8)\]. The filter to separate and dereverberate the
$`n`$th source is defined as $`\bm{p}^{\mathsf{H}}_{n,f}`$, i.e., the
$`n`$th row vector of $`\bm{P}_{f}`$. It is updated as follows:

|     |                                                                                                                                                                     |     |     |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\bm{p}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{n,f}\leftarrow\frac{\bm{V}_{n,f}^{-1}\bm{a}_{n,f}}{\sqrt{\bm{a}_{n,f}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{V}_{n,f}^{-1}\bm{a}_{n,f}}},`$ |     | (6) |

where $`\bm{a}_{n,f}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{W}_{f}^{-1}\bm{e}_{n}\\
\bm{0}\end{pmatrix}`$. Thus, calculation of two types of inverse
matrices are needed in the $`\bm{p}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{n,f}`$
update. The updates of $`c_{n,k,f}`$ and $`b_{n,t,k}`$ are those of NMF.
We call this algorithm ILRMA-T-IP.

## 3 Proposed method: ILRMA-T-ISS

We propose a new DR-BSS method to reduce the number of inverse matrix
computations. The cost function is the same as that of the ILRMA-T,
which is defined as

|     |                              |                                                                                                                                                                                                            |     |     |
| --- | ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{J}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{f}\left[-2\log\lvert\det\bm{W}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f}\rvert+\sum_{n}\bm{g}^{\mathsf{H}}_{n,f}\bm{V}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{n,f}\bm{g}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{n,f}\right],`$ |     | (7) |

where

- •
  $`\bm{G}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{P}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f}\\
  \bm{0}_{NL\times N}\quad\bm{E}^{\phantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{NL}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{C}^{N(L+1)\times N(L+1)}`$
- •
  $`\bm{g}^{\mathsf{H}}_{n,f}`$: $`n`$th row vector of
  $`\bm{G}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f}`$.

Optimization of the parameters is done via ISS \[[18](#bib.bib18)\].
When $`n\leq N`$, ISS updates $`\bm{G}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}`$ (the
index of frequency bins omitted) like this,

|     |                                                                                                                 |     |     |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\bm{G}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}\leftarrow\bm{G}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}-\begin{bmatrix}v\_{1,n}\\ |

\vdots\\
v*{N,n}\\
\bm{0}*{NL\times 1}\end{bmatrix}\bm{g}^{\mathsf{H}}\_{n}.`$ | | (8) |

This update rule is the same as that for BSS. The minimization
of ([7](#S3.E7 "Equation 7 ‣ 3 Proposed method: ILRMA-T-ISS ‣ JOINT DEREVERBERATION AND SEPARATION with ITERATIVE SOURCE STEERING"))
with respect to $`v_{m,n}`$ gives,

|     |                           |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |     |     |
| --- | ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle v_{m,n}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\bm{g}^{\mathsf{H}}_{m}\bm{V}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{m}\bm{g}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{n}}{\bm{g}^{\mathsf{H}}_{n}\bm{V}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{m}\bm{g}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{n}}&(m\neq n),\\ |

&\\
1-(\bm{g}^{\mathsf{H}}_{n}\bm{V}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{n}\bm{g}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}\_{n})^{-\frac{1}{2}}&(m=n).\end{cases}`$ |  | (9) |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\qquad\forall 1\leq m\leq N`$ | | (10) |

For $`n>N`$, we propose two update rules, i.e., IRLMA-T-ISS-JOINT and
ILRMA-T-ISS-SEQ. These update rules correspond to the dereverberation
part of the algorithm.

### 3.1 ILRMA-T-ISS-JOINT

We call the first update rule ILRMA-T-ISS-JOINT as it jointly updates
$`v_{m,n>N}`$ in the following way,

|     |                                                                                                                        |     |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\bm{G}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}\leftarrow\bm{G}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}-\begin{bmatrix}\bm{v}\_{1,n>N}\\ |

\vdots\\
\bm{v}_{N,n>N}\\
\bm{0}_{NL\times NL}\end{bmatrix}\bm{G}\_{n>N}^{\mathsf{H}}`$ | |

where
$`\bm{v}_{m,n>N}=\begin{bmatrix}v_{m,N+1}&\cdots&v_{m,N(L+1)}\end{bmatrix}`$
and
$`\bm{G}_{n>N}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{g}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{N+1}&\cdots&\bm{g}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{N(L+1)}\end{bmatrix}`$.
Minimization
of ([7](#S3.E7 "Equation 7 ‣ 3 Proposed method: ILRMA-T-ISS ‣ JOINT DEREVERBERATION AND SEPARATION with ITERATIVE SOURCE STEERING"))
with respect to $`\bm{v}_{m,n>N}`$ for $`1\leq m\leq N`$, yields,

|     |                                 |                                                                                                                                                                                                |     |      |
| --- | ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\bm{v}_{m,n>N}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\left(\bm{g}^{\mathsf{H}}_{m}\bm{V}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{m}\bm{G}_{n>N}\right)\left(\bm{G}_{n>N}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{V}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{m}\bm{G}_{n>N}\right)^{-1},`$ |     | (11) |

which can be further expanded as follows,

|     |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |     |      |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\bm{v}_{m,n>N}=\left(\sum_{t}\frac{y_{m,f,t}\overline{\bm{x}}^{\mathsf{H}}_{f,t}}{r_{m,f,t}}\right)\left(\sum_{t}\frac{\overline{\bm{x}}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f,t}\overline{\bm{x}}^{\mathsf{H}}_{f,t}}{r_{m,f,t}}\right)^{-1}.`$ |     | (12) |

This equation is very similar to the update of the WPE filter by (). The
latter is updated from the cross correlation between the current
microphone input signal and the past microphone input signal. On the
other hand,
in ([12](#S3.E12 "Equation 12 ‣ 3.1 ILRMA-T-ISS-JOINT ‣ 3 Proposed method: ILRMA-T-ISS ‣ JOINT DEREVERBERATION AND SEPARATION with ITERATIVE SOURCE STEERING")),
cross-correlation between the estimated output signal of the $`m`$th
speech source and the past microphone input signal is calculated. Thus,
WPE based DR and ISS based BSS are naturally combined in this framework.
Moreover, it only requires inversion of one $`NL\times NL`$ matrix,
in ([12](#S3.E12 "Equation 12 ‣ 3.1 ILRMA-T-ISS-JOINT ‣ 3 Proposed method: ILRMA-T-ISS ‣ JOINT DEREVERBERATION AND SEPARATION with ITERATIVE SOURCE STEERING")),
per iteration and source.

### 3.2 ILRMA-T-ISS-SEQ

We call the second update rule ILRMA-T-ISS-SEQ. Instead of the joint
update of $`\bm{v}_{m,n>N}`$, $`v_{m,n}`$ is updated for each $`n>N`$
sequentially as follows,

|     |                                                                                                                 |     |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\bm{G}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}\leftarrow\bm{G}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}-\begin{bmatrix}v\_{1,n}\\ |

\vdots\\
v*{N,n}\\
\bm{0}*{NL\times 1}\end{bmatrix}\bm{g}^{\mathsf{H}}\_{n}.`$ | |

Minimization
of ([7](#S3.E7 "Equation 7 ‣ 3 Proposed method: ILRMA-T-ISS ‣ JOINT DEREVERBERATION AND SEPARATION with ITERATIVE SOURCE STEERING"))
with respect to $`v_{m,N}`$ gives,

|     |                           |                                                                                                                                                                                               |     |
| --- | ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle v_{m,n}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{\bm{g}^{\mathsf{H}}_{m}\bm{V}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{m}\bm{g}_{n}}{\bm{g}_{n}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{V}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{m}\bm{g}_{n}},\quad\forall 1\leq m\leq N.`$ |     |

This can be further expanded as,

|     |                                                                                                                                                              |     |      |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle v_{m,n}=\frac{\sum_{t}\frac{y_{m,f,t}\tilde{x}_{n,f,t}^{*}}{r_{m,f,t}}}{\sum_{t}\frac{\tilde{x}_{n,f,t}\tilde{x}_{n,f,t}^{*}}{r_{m,f,t}}},`$ |     | (13) |

where $`\tilde{x}_{n,f,t}`$ is the $`n`$th element of
$`\tilde{\bm{x}}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f,t}`$. It is shown that
inverse calculation is completely unnecessary in ILRMA-T-ISS-SEQ.

### 4.1 Setup

We use speech sources from the WSJ corpus \[[19](#bib.bib19)\] for
evaluation. To make the reverberant mixtures, we perform room
simulations with the pyroomacoustics Python package \[[20](#bib.bib20)\]
in random rectangular rooms with walls between $`5\text{\,}\mathrm{m}`$
and $`10\text{\,}\mathrm{m}`$ length, ceiling between
$`3\text{\,}\mathrm{m}`$ and $`4\text{\,}\mathrm{m}`$ high. Simulated
reverberation times range from $`200\text{\,}\mathrm{ms}`$ to
$`600\text{\,}\mathrm{ms}`$. The microphone array is circular, with a
radius between $`0.075\text{\,}\mathrm{m}`$ and
$`0.125\text{\,}\mathrm{m}`$, such that the spacing is at least
$`0.05\text{\,}\mathrm{m}`$. The horizontal location of the microphone
array and the speech sources is randomly chosen at least
$`0.2\text{\,}\mathrm{m}`$ away from the center of the rooms and at
least $`1.5\text{\,}\mathrm{m}`$ away from the center of the microphone
array, respectively. The vertical location of the microphone array and
the sources ranges from $`1.0\text{\,}\mathrm{m}2.0\text{\,}\mathrm{m}`$
and from $`1.5\text{\,}\mathrm{m}`$ and $`2.0\text{\,}\mathrm{m}`$ high,
respectively. The distance between the sources is randomly set to be at
least $`1.0\text{\,}\mathrm{m}`$. We add background noise selected from
the CHiME3 dataset \[[21](#bib.bib21)\] to each simulated signal. The
source signals are normalized to have unit power at the first
microphone. Then we define signal-to-noise ratio
$`\mathsf{SNR}=N/{\sigma^{2}}`$, where $`\sigma^{2}`$ is the variance of
uncorrelated white noise at the microphones. The $`\mathsf{SNR}`$ ranges
from $`10\text{\,}\mathrm{dB}30\text{\,}\mathrm{dB}`$.

We performed separation and dereverberation for $`234`$ sources for 333
simulated mixtures. The sampling frequency was
$`16\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}`$, and the STFT frame size $`1024`$
($`64\text{\,}\mathrm{ms}`$) is with three-quarter overlap. We used a
Hann window for analysis and the optimally matching window for
synthesis. The proposed methods were compared with
ILRMA-T-IP \[[15](#bib.bib15)\], ILRMA-IP \[[17](#bib.bib17)\], and
ILRMA-ISS. We also evaluated ILRMA-IP and ILRMA-ISS initialized by
WPE \[[10](#bib.bib10)\], that we call WPE+ILRMA-IP and WPE+ILRMA-ISS,
respectively. For all ILRMA-T-based methods; ILRMA-T-ISS-JOINT,
ILRMA-T-ISS-SEQ, ILRMA-T-IP, we set the tap length $`L`$ to $`5`$, the
delay parameter $`\Delta`$ to $`2`$, the initial DR and BSS filter
$`\{\bm{P}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f}\}_{f}`$ to
$`\begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_{N}&\bm{0}_{NL}\end{bmatrix}`$, respectively.
For all ILRMA-based methods; ILRMA-ISS and ILRMA-IP, we set the initial
BSS filter $`\{\bm{P}^{\vphantom{\mathsf{H}}}_{f}\}_{f}`$ to the
identity matrix. For all methods, we set the number of iterations to
$`100`$, the number of NMF bases $`K`$ to $`2`$, initial value of
$`\{c_{n,f,k}\}_{n,f,k}`$ to $`1`$, and initial value of
$`\{b_{n,k,t}\}_{n,k,t}`$ to a random number uniformly distributed over
$`\mathopen{[}0.1,1\mathclose{)}`$, respectively. After separation and
dereverberation, the scale of the output was restored by projection back
onto the first microphone \[[22](#bib.bib22)\].

### 4.2 Results

We measured the scale-invariant signal-to-distortion ratio (SI-SDR) and
the scale-invariant signal-to-interference ratio
(SI-SIR) \[[23](#bib.bib23)\], the cepstrum distance (CD), and the
speech-to-reverberation modulation energy ration (SRMR). We define
$`\Delta`$SI-SDR and $`\Delta`$SI-SIR as the difference of SI-SDR and
SI-SIR, respectively, between before and after the processing.

[Figure 1](#S4.F1 "In 4 Experiment ‣ JOINT DEREVERBERATION AND SEPARATION with ITERATIVE SOURCE STEERING")
shows the separation performance after 100 iterations of each algorithm.
As a whole, the proposed ILRMA-T-based methods significantly
outperformed the conventional ILRMA-based methods. Also, they can
slightly improve performance compared with WPE+ILRMA-IP and
WEP+ILRMA-ISS. $`\Delta`$SI-SDR and $`\Delta`$SI-SIR of ILRMA-T-ISS are
slightly less than that of ILRMA-T-IP but achieve comparable performance
in less time, as described below. We can find that dereverberation
improves the separation performance. The proposed ILRMA-T-ISS-JOINT and
ILRMA-T-ISS-SEQ can achieve comparable performance to ILRMA-T-IP. This
result is consistent with the reported difference between IP and
ISS-based methods for BSS \[[18](#bib.bib18)\].

[Figure 2](#S4.F2 "In 4 Experiment ‣ JOINT DEREVERBERATION AND SEPARATION with ITERATIVE SOURCE STEERING")
shows the comparison of convergence speed. The total runtime of the
proposed ILRMA-T-ISS-SEQ is about the same as that of ILRMA-T-IP, where
$`N=2`$. On the other hand, it is much less where $`N=3,4`$. The
convergence speed of the proposed ILRMA-T-ISS is slightly slower than
ILRMA-T-IP but the final performance is the same. WPE+ILRMA-ISS and
WPE+ILRMA-IP seem to converge the fastest, but the WPE initialization
time was not included in the figure.

## 5 Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed a joint optimization technique for source
separation and dereverberation based on ILRMA-T with ISS. We use this
technique to derive two new algorithms. ILRMA-T-ISS-JOINT performs a
sequence of ISS updates corresponding to the separation part of the
algorithm, followed by a joint update corresponding to the parameters of
the dereverberation. Interestingly, this can be seen as a combination of
the ISS and WPE updates applied alternatingly. This form of the
algorithm reduces the number of matrix inversion to just one per
iteration and source. ILRMA-T-ISS-SEQ gets rid of inversion altogether
by updating all parameters via ISS-style rules. This algorithm is very
simple and does not need fancy linear algebra libraries. It is a very
good candidate for processing in practical edge or embedded systems.
Experimental results showed that while conceptually simpler, the
proposed method performs just as well on a challenging dataset of noisy
reverberant speech mixtures. In future work, we intend to push the
method towards real-time applicability, and explore advantages provided
by extra microphones, the so-called overdetermined
case \[[24](#bib.bib24)\].
