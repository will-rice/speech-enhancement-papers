# Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement

Yen-Ju Lu    Zhong-Qiu Wang    Shinji Watanabe    Alexander Richard   
Cheng Yu    Yu Tsao

###### Abstract

Speech enhancement is a critical component of many user-oriented audio
applications, yet current systems still suffer from distorted and
unnatural outputs. While generative models have shown strong potential
in speech synthesis, they are still lagging behind in speech
enhancement. This work leverages recent advances in diffusion
probabilistic models, and proposes a novel speech enhancement algorithm
that incorporates characteristics of the observed noisy speech signal
into the diffusion and reverse processes. More specifically, we propose
a generalized formulation of the diffusion probabilistic model named
conditional diffusion probabilistic model that, in its reverse process,
can adapt to non-Gaussian real noises in the estimated speech signal. In
our experiments, we demonstrate strong performance of the proposed
approach compared to representative generative models, and investigate
the generalization capability of our models to other datasets with noise
characteristics unseen during training.

###### Index Terms: 

speech enhancement, diffusion probabilistic model, generative model,
deep learning

## 1 Introduction

Speech enhancement, a key element for immersive audio experiences in
telecommunication as well as a crucial front-end processor for robust
speech recognition \[[1](#bib.bib1), [2](#bib.bib2)\], assistive hearing
 \[[3](#bib.bib3)\], and robust speaker recognition \[[4](#bib.bib4),
[5](#bib.bib5)\], is a challenging and still unsolved problem in audio
processing. Riding on the advance of deep learning, considerable
progress has been made in the past decade \[[6](#bib.bib6),
[7](#bib.bib7)\]. Deep learning based approaches can be roughly divided
into two categories, based on the criteria used to estimate the
transformation function from noisy-reverberant speech to clean speech.
The first category trains discriminative models to minimize the
difference between enhanced and clean speech, where the difference can
be a point-wise $`L_{p}`$-norm distance \[[8](#bib.bib8)\], or can be
computed based on a perceptual metric \[[9](#bib.bib9),
[10](#bib.bib10)\]. The second category considers the distribution of
the clean speech signals to form the objective function. Well-known
examples along this direction include generative adversarial networks
(GANs) \[[11](#bib.bib11), [12](#bib.bib12)\], Bayesian
wavenet \[[13](#bib.bib13)\], variational
autoencoders \[[14](#bib.bib14)\], and flow-based
models \[[15](#bib.bib15)\]. While the best performing approaches
typically fall into the first category \[[16](#bib.bib16),
[6](#bib.bib6)\], they usually introduce unpleasant speech distortion
and phonetic inaccuracies to the enhanced speech \[[17](#bib.bib17),
[18](#bib.bib18), [19](#bib.bib19)\]. Generative approaches that aim to
match the distribution of speech signals rather than regressive
approaches optimizing a point-wise loss hold the promise to produce more
natural sounding speech, although they are currently lagging behind
regressive approaches and require more research to unfold their
potential.

This work investigates diffusion probabilistic
models \[[20](#bib.bib20)\], a class of generative models that have
shown outstanding performance in image generation \[[21](#bib.bib21),
[22](#bib.bib22)\] and audio synthesis \[[23](#bib.bib23),
[24](#bib.bib24), [25](#bib.bib25)\], for speech enhancement. Diffusion
probabilistic models convert clean input data to an isotropic Gaussian
distribution in a step-by-step diffusion process and, in a reverse
process, gradually restore the clean input by predicting and removing
the noise introduced in each step of the diffusion process. These
models, in their vanilla formulation, assume isotropic Gaussian noise in
each step of the diffusion process as well as the reverse process.
However, in realistic conditions, the noise characteristics are usually
non-Gaussian, which violates the model assumption when directly
combining the noisy speech signal in the sampling process. We address
this problem by formulating a generalized conditional diffusion
probabilistic model that incorporates the observed noisy data into the
model. We derive the corresponding conditional diffusion and reverse
processes as well as the evidence lower bound (ELBO) optimization
criterion \[[21](#bib.bib21)\], and show that the resulting model is a
generalization of the original diffusion probabilistic model. In our
experiment, we will demonstrate that our formulation can not only
improve over the vanilla diffusion probabilistic model, but also
outperform other generative models.

## 2 Diffusion Probabilistic Model

A $`T`$-step diffusion model \[[21](#bib.bib21)\] consists of two
processes: the diffusion process with steps $`t\in\{0,1,\cdots,T\}`$ and
the reverse process $`t\in\{T,T-1,\cdots,0\}`$. We start with a brief
summary of the vanilla diffusion probabilistic model, _i.e_., we revisit
the original diffusion- and reverse process.

### 2.1 Diffusion Process

Given the clean speech data $`x_{0}`$, the diffusion process
$`q_{\text{data}}(x_{0})`$ of the first diffusion step ($`t=0`$) is
defined as the data distribution $`x_{0}`$ on $`\mathbb{R}^{L}`$, where
$`L`$ is the signal length in samples. For the the $`t`$-th diffusion
step, we have a step-dependent variable $`x_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{L}`$ with
the same signal length $`L`$. The diffusion process from data $`x_{0}`$
to the variable $`x_{T}`$ can be formulated based on a fixed Markov
chain:

|     |                                      |           |                                        |              |
| --- | ------------------------------------ | --------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------ | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle q(x*{1},\cdots,x*{T} | x\_{0})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\prod*{t=1}^{T}q(x*{t} | x\_{t-1}),`$ |     | (1) |

with a Gaussian model $`q(x_{t}|x_{t-1})=\mathcal{N}(x_{t};`$
$`\sqrt{1-\beta_{t}}x_{t-1},\beta_{t}I)`$, where $`\beta_{t}`$ is a
small positive constant. In other words, in each step a Gaussian noise
is added to the previous sample $`x_{t-1}`$. According to the
pre-defined schedule $`\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{T}`$, the overall process
gradually converts clean $`x_{0}`$ to a latent variable with an
isotropic Gaussian distribution of
$`p_{\text{latent}}(x_{T})=\mathcal{N}(0,I)`$.

By substituting the Gaussian model of $`q(x_{t}|x_{t-1})`$ into
Eq. ([1](#S2.E1 "In 2.1 Diffusion Process ‣ 2 Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement"))
and by marginalizing $`x_{1},\dots,x_{t-1}`$, the sampling distribution
of $`x_{t}`$ can be derived as the following distribution conditioned on
$`x_{0}`$:

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
q(x_{t}|x_{0})=\mathcal{N}(x_{t};\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t}}x_{0},(1-\bar{\alpha}_{t})I),
``` |  | (2) |

where $`\alpha_{t}=1-\beta_{t}`$ and
$`\bar{\alpha}_{t}=\prod_{s=1}^{t}\alpha_{s}`$.

### 2.2 Reverse Process

The reverse process converts the latent variable $`x_{T}\sim N(0,I)`$ to
$`x_{0}`$, also based on a Markov chain similar to
Eq. ([1](#S2.E1 "In 2.1 Diffusion Process ‣ 2 Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement")):

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
p_{\theta}(x_{0},\cdots,x_{T-1}|x_{T})=\prod_{t=1}^{T}p_{\theta}(x_{t-1}|x_{t}),
``` |  | (3) |

where $`p_{\theta}(\cdot)`$ is the distribution of the reverse process
with learnable parameters $`\theta`$. Unlike the diffusion process, the
following marginal likelihood is intractable:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle p_{\theta}(x_{0})=\int p_{\theta}(x_{0},\cdots,x_{T-1}|x_{T})\cdot p_{\text{latent}}(x_{T})dx_{1:T}.`$ |  | (4) |

Therefore, we use the ELBO to form an approximated objective function
for model training. In  \[[21](#bib.bib21)\], it is reported that
minimizing the following equation leads to higher generation quality:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
c+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\kappa_{t}\mathbb{E}_{x_{0},\epsilon}\parallel\epsilon-\epsilon_{\theta}(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t}}x_{0}+\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t}}\epsilon,t)\parallel^{2}_{2},
``` |  | (5) |

with constants $`c`$ and $`\kappa_{t}`$. Here $`\epsilon_{\theta}`$ is
the model trained to estimate the Gaussian noise $`\epsilon`$ in
$`x_{t}`$. After optimizing
Eq. ([5](#S2.E5 "In 2.2 Reverse Process ‣ 2 Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement")),
the corresponding reverse process equation becomes:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
p_{\theta}(x_{t-1}|x_{t})=\mathcal{N}(x_{t-1};\mu_{\theta}(x_{t},t),\tilde{\beta}_{t}I),
``` |  | (6) |

where the mean $`\mu_{\theta}(x_{t},t)`$ is:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mu_{\theta}(x_{t},t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}(x_{t}-\frac{\beta_{t}}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t}}}\epsilon_{\theta}(x_{t},t)).
``` |  | (7) |

The $`\mu_{\theta}(x_{t},t)`$ predicts the mean of $`x_{t-1}`$
distribution by removing the estimated Gaussian noise
$`\epsilon_{\theta}(x_{t},t)`$ in the $`x_{t}`$, and the variance is
fixed to a constant
$`\tilde{\beta}_{t}=\frac{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t}}\beta_{t}`$.

## 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model

The original diffusion process in Section
[2.1](#S2.SS1 "2.1 Diffusion Process ‣ 2 Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement")
starts from the clean data $`q_{\text{data}}(x_{0})`$ and adds Gaussian
noise into the speech signal. In the proposed conditional diffusion
probabilistic model, we incorporate the noisy data $`y`$ into the
diffusion process, as shown in
Fig. [1](#S3.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement").

![Refer to caption](2202.05256v1/CDiff.png)

Figure 1: Diffusion process (solid arrows) and reverse process (dashed
arrows) of the proposed conditional diffusion probabilistic model.

### 3.1 Conditional Diffusion Processes

In the conditional diffusion process, we use an interpolation parameter
$`m_{t}`$ to combine the clean data $`x_{0}`$ and the noisy data $`y`$,
the summation of $`x_{0}`$ and the real noise $`n`$, on the solid arrows
in
Fig. [1](#S3.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement").
Instead of starting from the Markov chain Gaussian model
$`q(x_{t}|x_{t-1})`$ in the original diffusion process, we first define
the following conditional diffusion process $`q(x_{t}|x_{0},y)`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q_{\text{cdiff}}(x_{t}|x_{0},y)=\mathcal{N}(x_{t};(1-m_{t})\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t}}x_{0}+m_{t}\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t}}y,\delta_{t}I),
``` |  | (8) |

where $`\delta_{t}`$ is the variance. Unlike the original diffusion
process $`q(x_{t}|x_{0})`$ in
Eq. ([2](#S2.E2 "In 2.1 Diffusion Process ‣ 2 Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement")),
we assume that the Gaussian mean in
Eq. ([8](#S3.E8 "In 3.1 Conditional Diffusion Processes ‣ 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement"))
is represented as a linear interpolation between the clean data
$`x_{0}`$ and the noisy data $`y`$ with the interpolation ratio
$`m_{t}`$. $`m_{t}`$ starts from $`m_{0}=0`$ and is gradually increased
to $`m_{T}\approx 1`$, turning the mean of $`x_{t}`$ from the clean
speech $`x_{0}`$ to noisy speech $`y`$ as in
Fig. [1](#S3.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement").

Given the interpolation formulation in
Eq. ([8](#S3.E8 "In 3.1 Conditional Diffusion Processes ‣ 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement")),
we can derive
$`q_{\text{cdiff}}(x_{t}|x_{0})=\int q_{\text{cdiff}}(x_{t}|x_{0},y)p_{y}(y|x_{0})dy`$
by marginalizing $`y`$ in the multiplication of
$`q_{\text{cdiff}}(x_{t}|x_{0},y)`$ and $`p_{y}(y|x_{0})`$ with the
special case where $`n\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)`$. Then,
$`q_{\text{cdiff}}(x_{t}|x_{0})`$ becomes equivalent to the original
diffusion process $`q(x_{t}|x_{0})`$ in Eq.
 ([2](#S2.E2 "In 2.1 Diffusion Process ‣ 2 Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement"))
when

|     |                                                            |     |     |
|-----|------------------------------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \delta_{t}=(1-\bar{\alpha}_{t})-m_{t}^{2}\bar{\alpha}_{t}.
       ```                                                         |     | (9) |

This analytical result indicates that our model is a generalization of
the original diffusion probabilistic model. In our previous study
\[[25](#bib.bib25)\], we investigated directly utilizing noisy signal in
the reverse process; the idea is found to work well empirically, but
there lacks a theoretical justification. In
Sec. [3.2](#S3.SS2 "3.2 Conditional Reverse Processes ‣ 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement"),
we will propose a conditional reverse process that is theoretically
sound. To further research the effect of incorporating noisy signal in
the diffusion model, in
Sec. [3.3](#S3.SS3 "3.3 Coefficient Estimation by Optimizing ELBO ‣ 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement"),
we will set $`\delta_{t}`$ according to
Eq. ([9](#S3.E9 "In 3.1 Conditional Diffusion Processes ‣ 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement"))
so that the conditional diffusion process becomes a generalized version
of the original diffusion process¹¹ 1 It is difficult to derive
$`q_{\text{cdiff}}(x_{t}|x_{t-1})`$ for satisfying the original
diffusion process if we first define
$`q_{\text{cdiff}}(x_{t}|x_{t-1},y)`$, because the distribution of $`y`$
depends on $`x_{0}`$ as $`y=x_{0}+n`$. .

### 3.2 Conditional Reverse Processes

In the conditional reverse process, we start from $`x_{T}`$, noisy
speech signal $`y`$ with variance $`\delta_{T}`$, according to Eq.
([8](#S3.E8 "In 3.1 Conditional Diffusion Processes ‣ 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement"))
with $`m_{T}=1`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
p_{\text{cdiff}}(x_{T}|y)=\mathcal{N}(x_{T},\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{T}}y,\delta_{T}I).
``` |  | (10) |

Based on the Markov chain, similar to Eq.
([6](#S2.E6 "In 2.2 Reverse Process ‣ 2 Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement")),
the conditional reverse process on the dashed arrows in Figure
[1](#S3.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement")
aims to predict $`x_{t-1}`$ based on $`x_{t}`$ and $`y`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
p_{\text{cdiff}}(x_{t-1}|x_{t},y)=\mathcal{N}(x_{t-1};\mu_{\theta}(x_{t},y,t),\tilde{\delta}_{t}I),
``` |  | (11) |

where the $`\mu_{\theta}(x_{t},y,t)`$ is the estimated mean of the
conditional reverse process. The concrete form of the variance
$`\tilde{\delta}_{t}`$ is introduced later. In contrast to the vanilla
diffusion model, we further condition the diffusion model on $`y`$.
Therefore, similar to Eq.
([7](#S2.E7 "In 2.2 Reverse Process ‣ 2 Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement")),
the mean $`\mu_{\theta}(x_{t},y,t)`$ in each reverse step is a linear
combination of $`x_{t}`$, $`y`$, and estimated noise $`\epsilon`$ with
weights $`c_{xt},c_{yt}`$ and $`c_{\epsilon t}`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mu_{\theta}(x_{t},y,t)=c_{xt}x_{t}+c_{yt}y-c_{\epsilon t}\epsilon_{\theta}(x_{t},y,t),
``` |  | (12) |

where the $`\epsilon_{\theta}(x_{t},y,t)`$ is the model to estimate the
Gaussian and non-Gaussian noise combination. The coefficients
$`c_{xt},c_{yt},`$ and $`c_{\epsilon t}`$ can be derived from the ELBO
optimization criterion, see
Section [3.3](#S3.SS3 "3.3 Coefficient Estimation by Optimizing ELBO ‣ 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement").

### 3.3 Coefficient Estimation by Optimizing ELBO

By modifying the derivations in \[[21](#bib.bib21)\], we obtain the ELBO
condition for the conditional diffusion process to optimize the
likelihood:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
ELBO=-\mathbb{E}_{q}\Big(D_{\text{KL}}(q_{\text{cdiff}}(x_{T}|x_{0},y)||p_{\text{latent}}(x_{T}|y))\\
+\sum_{t=2}^{T}D_{\text{KL}}(q_{\text{cdiff}}(x_{t-1}|x_{t},x_{0},y)||p_{\theta}(x_{t-1}|x_{t},y))\\
-\log p_{\theta}(x_{0}|x_{1},y)\Big).
``` |  | (13) |

To optimize
Eq. ([13](#S3.E13 "In 3.3 Coefficient Estimation by Optimizing ELBO ‣ 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement")),
we first need the distribution $`q_{\text{cdiff}}(x_{t}|x_{t-1},y)`$.
Generally, the diffusion process define
$`q_{\text{cdiff}}(x_{t}|x_{t-1},y)`$ first and derive
$`q_{\text{cdiff}}(x_{t}|x_{0},y)`$ by marginalizing
$`x_{0},\cdots,x_{t-1}`$ . Instead, we first design the interpolation
form in Eq.
([8](#S3.E8 "In 3.1 Conditional Diffusion Processes ‣ 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement"))
as mentioned in
Sec. [3.1](#S3.SS1 "3.1 Conditional Diffusion Processes ‣ 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement").
Therefore, we compare the coefficients of the marginalized result and
Eq.
([8](#S3.E8 "In 3.1 Conditional Diffusion Processes ‣ 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement"))
to compute the coefficients of $`q_{\text{cdiff}}(x_{t}|x_{t-1},y)`$ as:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle q_{\text{cdiff}}(x_{t}|x_{t-1},y)=\mathcal{N}\Bigl(x_{t};\frac{1-m_{t}}{1-m_{t-1}}\sqrt{\alpha}_{t}x_{t-1}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad+\Bigl(m_{t}-\frac{1-m_{t}}{1-m_{t-1}}m_{t-1}\Bigr)\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}}_{t}y,\delta_{t|t-1}I\Bigr),`$ |  | (14) |

where the $`\delta_{t|t-1}`$ is also calculated by $`\delta_{t}`$ to
satisfy Eq.
([9](#S3.E9 "In 3.1 Conditional Diffusion Processes ‣ 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement"))
as:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\delta_{t|t-1}=\delta_{t}-\left(\frac{1-m_{t}}{1-m_{t-1}}\right)^{2}\alpha_{t}\delta_{t-1}.
``` |  | (15) |

Then, by combining Eqs.
([8](#S3.E8 "In 3.1 Conditional Diffusion Processes ‣ 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement"))
and
([14](#S3.E14 "In 3.3 Coefficient Estimation by Optimizing ELBO ‣ 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement")),
$`q_{\text{cdiff}}(x_{t-1}|x_{t},x_{0},y)`$ can be derived through
Bayes’ theorem and the Markov chain property:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
q_{\text{cdiff}}(x_{t-1}|x_{t},x_{0},y)=\mathcal{N}\Bigl(x_{t-1};\frac{1-m_{t}}{1-m_{t-1}}\frac{\delta_{t-1}}{\delta_{t}}\sqrt{\alpha}_{t}x_{t}+(1-m_{t-1})\frac{\delta_{t|t-1}}{\delta_{t}}\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}}_{t-1}x_{0}+\Bigl(m_{t-1}\delta_{t}-\frac{m_{t}(1-m_{t})}{1-m_{t-1}}\alpha_{t}\delta_{t-1}\Bigr)\frac{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}}{\delta_{t}}y,\tilde{\delta}_{t}I\Bigr),
``` |  | (16) |

where $`\tilde{\delta}_{t}`$, the variance term of
$`q_{\text{cdiff}}(x_{t-1}|x_{t},x_{0},y)`$, is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\tilde{\delta}_{t}=\frac{\delta_{t|t-1}*\delta_{t}}{\delta_{t-1}}.
``` |  | (17) |

To optimize the KL divergence term in
Eq. ([13](#S3.E13 "In 3.3 Coefficient Estimation by Optimizing ELBO ‣ 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement")),
$`\tilde{\delta}_{t}`$ is also used as the variance of
$`p_{\text{cdiff}}(x_{t-1}|x_{t},y)`$ in
Eq. ([11](#S3.E11 "In 3.2 Conditional Reverse Processes ‣ 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement"))
to match $`q_{\text{cdiff}}(x_{t-1}|x_{t},x_{0},y)`$, and the
coefficients $`c_{xt}`$, $`c_{yt}`$, $`c_{\epsilon t}`$ in
Eq. ([12](#S3.E12 "In 3.2 Conditional Reverse Processes ‣ 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement"))
are then be derived as:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle c_{xt}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{1-m_{t}}{1-m_{t-1}}\frac{\delta_{t-1}}{\delta_{t}}\sqrt{\alpha}_{t}+(1-m_{t-1})\frac{\delta_{t|t-1}}{\delta_{t}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}_{t}},`$ |  | (18) |
|  | $`\displaystyle c_{yt}`$ | $`\displaystyle=(m_{t-1}\delta_{t}-\frac{m_{t}(1-m_{t})}{1-m_{t-1}}\alpha_{t}\delta_{t-1})\frac{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}}{\delta_{t}},`$ |  | (19) |
|  | $`\displaystyle c_{\epsilon t}`$ | $`\displaystyle=(1-m_{t-1})\frac{\delta_{t|t-1}}{\delta_{t}}\frac{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t}}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}}.`$ |  | (20) |

Now, given the explicit form of all distributions in
Eq. ([13](#S3.E13 "In 3.3 Coefficient Estimation by Optimizing ELBO ‣ 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement")),
the ELBO to be optimized simplifies to

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
c^{\prime}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\kappa^{\prime}_{t}\mathbb{E}_{x_{0},\epsilon,y}\parallel(\frac{m_{t}\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t}}}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t}}}{(y-x_{0})}+\frac{\sqrt{\delta_{t}}}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t}}}\epsilon)-\epsilon_{\theta}(x_{t},y,t)\parallel^{2}_{2}
``` |  | (21) |

with constants $`c^{\prime}`$ and $`\kappa^{\prime}_{t}`$, and the
$`\epsilon`$ is the Gaussian noise in $`x_{t}`$. Because we have the
interpolation form of $`x_{t}`$ with the coefficient $`m_{t}`$ in Eq.
([8](#S3.E8 "In 3.1 Conditional Diffusion Processes ‣ 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement")),
the optimization target in Eq.
([21](#S3.E21 "In 3.3 Coefficient Estimation by Optimizing ELBO ‣ 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement"))
keeps the simple form in training. Comparing to Eq.
([5](#S2.E5 "In 2.2 Reverse Process ‣ 2 Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement")),
the $`\epsilon_{\theta}(x_{t},y,t)`$ in the conditional diffusion model
estimates both Gaussian noise $`\epsilon`$ and non-Gaussian noise
$`y-x_{0}`$ in $`x_{t}`$. Therefore, the proportion of $`y-x_{0}`$ and
$`\epsilon`$ coefficients is the same as y and the standard deviation in
Eq.

## 4 Experiments

In this section, we evaluate the performance of our approach against
other generative speech enhancement models and we show generalization
capabilities under conditions where state of the art approaches such as
Demucs \[[16](#bib.bib16)\] collapse. The samples of the
CDiffuSE-enhanced signals can be found online²² 2
https://github.com/neillu23/CDiffuSE.

### 4.1 Experimental Setup

Dataset: we evaluate the CDiffuSE model on the VoiceBank-DEMAND dataset
\[[26](#bib.bib26)\]. The dataset consists of 30 speakers from the
VoiceBank corpus \[[27](#bib.bib27)\], which is further divided into a
training set and a testing set with 28 and 2 speakers, respectively. The
training utterances are artificially contaminated with eight
real-recorded noise samples from the DEMAND database
\[[28](#bib.bib28)\] and two artificially generated noise samples
(babble and speech shaped) at 0, 5, 10, and 15 dB SNR levels, amounting
to 11,572 utterances. The testing utterances are mixed with different
noise samples at 2.5, 7.5, 12.5, and 17.5 dB SNR levels, amounting to
824 utterances in total. We consider perceptual evaluation of speech
quality (PESQ) \[[29](#bib.bib29)\], prediction of the signal distortion
(CSIG), prediction of the background intrusiveness (CBAK), and
prediction of the overall speech quality (COVL) \[[30](#bib.bib30)\] as
the evaluation metrics. Higher scores mean better performance for all
the metrics.

Model Architecture and Training: we implement CDiffuSE based on the same
model architecture and the same pre-training strategy with clean
Mel-filterbank conditioner as that of DiffuSE reported in
\[[25](#bib.bib25)\]. We investigate two systems, namely Base and Large
CDiffuSE, which respectively take $`50`$ and $`200`$ diffusion steps.
The linearly spaced training noise schedule is reduced to
$`\beta_{t}\in[1\times 10^{-4},0.035]`$ for Base CDiffuSE, and to
$`\beta_{t}\in[1\times 10^{-4},0.0095]`$ for Large CDiffuSE. The
interpolation parameter $`m_{t}`$ in
Section [3.1](#S3.SS1 "3.1 Conditional Diffusion Processes ‣ 3 Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement")
is set to $`m_{t}=\sqrt{(1-\bar{\alpha}_{t})/\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t}}}`$
which satisfies the $`m_{0}=0`$ and $`m_{t}\approx 1`$ requirement. We
train both Base and Large CDiffuSE models for 300,000 iterations, based
on an early stopping scheme. The batch size is set to 16 for Base
CDiffuSE and to 15 for Large CDiffuSE. The fast sampling scheme
\[[23](#bib.bib23)\] is used in the reverse processes with the inference
schedule $`\gamma_{t}=`$ $`[0.0001,0.001,0.01,0.05,0.2,0.35]`$ for both
Base CDiffuSE and Large CDiffuSE. The proposed CDiffuSE model performs
enhancement in the time domain. After the reverse process is completed,
the enhanced waveform further combine the original noisy signal with the
ratio $`0.2`$ to recover the high frequency speech in the final enhanced
waveform, as suggested in \[[16](#bib.bib16), [31](#bib.bib31)\].

### 4.2 Evaluation results

#### 4.2.1 Results on VoiceBank-DEMAND

In Table
[1](#S4.T1 "Table 1 ‣ 4.1 Experimental Setup ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement"),
we report the results of CDiffuSE and DiffuSE using the supportive
reverse process from \[[25](#bib.bib25)\]. As expected, the large models
for DiffuSE and CDiffuSE both outperform the smaller base models.
Moreover, CDiffuSE shows improved performance over the diffusion
probabilistic model baseline DiffuSE. Note that the key to success here
is that CDiffuSE has had direct access to the noisy data while learning
the reverse diffusion process, allowing it to actively compensate for
the noise characteristics in the input signals. Being able to leverage
noise from the input signal, our approach improves on all the metrics,
confirming that the theoretically sound CDiffuSE leads to improved
results in practice. We additionally compare CDiffuSE to other
time-domain generative models, namely SEGAN \[[11](#bib.bib11)\],
SE-Flow \[[15](#bib.bib15)\], and improved deep SEGAN
(DSEGAN) \[[32](#bib.bib32)\]. CDiffuSE outperforms its competitors on
all metrics - with the exception of CBAK - and achieves a particularly
significant improvement in PESQ, see
Table [2](#S4.T2 "Table 2 ‣ 4.1 Experimental Setup ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement").

#### 4.2.2 Results on CHiME-4

Generative models typically aim to fit the distribution of the training
samples instead of optimizing a point-wise $`L_{p}`$-loss. This property
has made them state of the art in applications like text-to-speech and
vocoding \[[35](#bib.bib35), [36](#bib.bib36)\] and also makes them more
robust against domain shifts in the input data.

In this section, we investigate this property of our proposed CDiffuSE.
We compare the generalization abilities of our approach to other,
$`L_{p}`$-loss based approaches and demonstrate that our approach is
particularly resistant towards shifts in noise characteristics of the
speech data. The models in this section are trained on VoiceBank-DEMAND
and evaluated on the simulated test data of
CHiME-4 \[[37](#bib.bib37)\]. The CHiME-4 simulated test data is created
based on real-recorded noises from four real-world environments
(including street, pedestrian areas, cafeteria and bus) based on four
speakers. We use the signals from the fifth microphone for evaluation.

As mentioned previously and as
Table [2(a)](#S4.F2.sf1 "In Table 3 ‣ 4.1 Experimental Setup ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement")
shows, generative speech enhancement models are still lagging behind the
performance of their regressive counterparts. A model from the latter
category trained on VoiceBank and evaluated on the VoiceBank test set
performs far better than most generative methods. Particularly,
Demucs \[[16](#bib.bib16)\] and Conv-TasNet \[[34](#bib.bib34)\]
outperform our CDiffuSE, which was the strongest generative model in
Table [2](#S4.T2 "Table 2 ‣ 4.1 Experimental Setup ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement").

Given a domain shift in test data, however, regression based approaches
such as Demucs, Conv-TasNet, and WaveCRN suffer from a significant drop
in performance, see
Table [2(b)](#S4.F2.sf2 "In Table 3 ‣ 4.1 Experimental Setup ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Conditional Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Speech Enhancement").
Different signal characteristics between the VoiceBank training data and
the CHiME-4 test set suffice to let the evaluation scores fall
drastically, in some cases even below the scores of unprocessed data.
Our proposed CDiffuSE, on the contrary, proves to be much more resilient
against such shifts in signal characteristics. While the scores on the
CHiME-4 test set are lower than the VoiceBank scores, CDiffuSE degrades
to a much smaller degree than its regressive competitors, leaving it
with the best scores on the CHiME-4 test data and demonstrating its high
robustness to variation in noise characteristics.

## 5 Conclusion

We proposed CDiffuSE, a conditional diffusion probabilistic model that
can explore noise characteristics from the noisy input signal explicitly
and thereby adapts better to non-Gaussian noise statistics in real-world
speech enhancement problems. We showed that our model is a strict
generalization of the original diffusion probabilistic model and
achieves state of the art results compared to other generative speech
enhancement approaches. In contrast to non-generative approaches, our
method exposes great generalization capabilities to speech data with
noise characteristics not observed in the training data. We were able to
show that CDiffuSE maintains strong performance when regression-based
approaches such as Demucs and Conv-TasNet collapse.
````
