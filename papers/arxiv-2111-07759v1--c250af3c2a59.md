# Joint Far- and Near-End Speech Intelligibility Enhancement based on the Approximated Speech Intelligibility Index

Andreas Jonas Fuglsig^(⋆†), Jan Østergaard^(†), Jesper Jensen^(†), Lars
Søndergaard Bertelsen^(⋆), Peter Mariager^(⋆), Zheng-Hua Tan^(†)
^(†)^(†)thanks: This work is partly supported by Innovation Fund Denmark
Case no. 9065-00204B.

###### Abstract

This paper considers speech enhancement of signals picked up in one
noisy environment which must be presented to a listener in another noisy
environment. Recently, it has been shown that an optimal solution to
this problem requires the consideration of the noise sources in both
environments jointly. However, the existing optimal mutual information
based method requires a complicated system model that includes natural
speech variations, and relies on approximations and assumptions of the
underlying signal distributions. In this paper, we propose to use a
simpler signal model and optimize speech intelligibility based on the
Approximated Speech Intelligibility Index (ASII). We derive a
closed-form solution to the joint far- and near-end speech enhancement
problem that is independent of the marginal distribution of signal
coefficients, and that achieves similar performance to existing work. In
addition, we do not need to model or optimize for natural speech
variations.

###### Index Terms: 

Multi-microphone, speech intelligibility enhancement, ASII, beamformer

## 1 Introduction

Speech communication systems, such as mobile telephony, hearing aids and
intercom systems, are required to work in numerous environments. As a
consequence, the user environment is often noisy which can lead to
intelligibility problems.

For speech communication systems we may consider two different
environments, cf.
Fig. [1](#S1.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ Joint Far- and Near-End Speech Intelligibility Enhancement based on the Approximated Speech Intelligibility Index");
the far-end environment (at the target talker) and the near-end
environment (at the listener). Both the far- and near-end environment
are often noisy, which leads to degradations in both speech quality and
Speech Intelligibility (SI) for the listener. To remedy these effects,
speech enhancement techniques may be applied at both the far- and
near-end.

![Refer to caption]()

Figure 1: Classic speech communication system with Far-end Speech
Enhancement (FSE) and Near-end Listening Enhancement (NLE).

Depending on the availability, Far-end Speech Enhancement (FSE)
algorithms may utilize either a single or multiple microphones
\[[1](#bib.bib1), [2](#bib.bib2), [3](#bib.bib3), [4](#bib.bib4)\]. In
contrast to the far-end scenario, in Near-end Listening Enhancement
(NLE)\[[5](#bib.bib5), [6](#bib.bib6)\] the interfering noise is mixed
with the target speech after processing. Therefore, the noise cannot be
reduced by the usual post-processing techniques of the former scenario.
Instead, before playback in the noisy environment, NLE increases SI by
adaptively pre-processing the FSE signal received from the far-end,
while exploiting knowledge about the near-end noise.

Most work on NLE assumes the signal received from the far-end is
noise-free \[[6](#bib.bib6)\]. However, in many communication scenarios
both the target talker and listener may be in noisy environments. Even
so, until recently the processing to mitigate the effects of
disturbances in the far- and near-end environments have been considered
separately. However, recently, in \[[7](#bib.bib7), [8](#bib.bib8),
[9](#bib.bib9)\] it was shown that optimization of SI under joint
consideration of the far- and near-end noise is superior to disjoint
processing. The work of \[[7](#bib.bib7)\] considers jointly controlling
a single-channel noise reduction filter along with a post-filter gain
for NLE designed to increase SI. In \[[9](#bib.bib9)\] a new training
strategy is proposed for deep learning based single-channel enhancement
given that speech has already been processed at the far-end. For joint
multi-microphone FSE and NLE \[[8](#bib.bib8), [10](#bib.bib10)\]
proposes to optimize the Mutual Information (MI) \[[11](#bib.bib11)\]
between the clean speech and the signal received by the listener. The
results of \[[8](#bib.bib8)\] are the first to show both theoretically
and experimentally that joint processing, using knowledge of processing
and conditions at both ends, is superior to the classic disjoint
processing.

MI as a SI optimization objective provides a target that unifies
heuristic views on SI and mathematically founded SI
measures \[[8](#bib.bib8), [12](#bib.bib12)\]. However, solving it in
closed form requires simplifying assumptions. The resulting optimization
objective is an SNR-type of measure that is approximately equal to the
Approximated Speech Intelligibility Index (ASII) \[[13](#bib.bib13),
[8](#bib.bib8)\]. Furthermore, the method of \[[8](#bib.bib8)\] depends
on the choice of the correlation of the so-called production and
interpretation noise with the clean speech. With the choice made in
\[[8](#bib.bib8)\], the objective function of \[[8](#bib.bib8)\] reduces
fully to the ASII, whereas for more recent choices in
\[[14](#bib.bib14)\] it does not.

In this paper, we illustrate how, by using a simpler well established
signal model and optimizing for the ASII directly, we can derive a
closed-form solution to the joint far- and near-end speech enhancement
problem that is independent of the marginal distribution of signal
coefficients, and without the need for introducing additional parameters
in terms of a production and interpretation noise model. The proposed
approach can also be seen as an extension of the ASII optimization
problem \[[13](#bib.bib13)\] to joint far- and near-end optimization.
Furthermore, we analyze model choices and assumptions of
\[[8](#bib.bib8)\], and how these relate the approximated MI of
\[[8](#bib.bib8)\] to the ASII, thus motivating our model choices.
Finally, we experimentally compare the performance of \[[8](#bib.bib8)\]
using the production noise model that was later derived by some of the
same authors in \[[14](#bib.bib14), [15](#bib.bib15), [16](#bib.bib16)\]
to our proposed ASII based optimization. We see, that our proposed
method achieves similar or slightly better intelligibility in terms of
ESTOI \[[17](#bib.bib17)\] when the near-end SNR is low and the far-end
SNR is intermediate or high.

In summary, the contributions of this paper are: (i) A closed-form
solution to ASII based joint far- and near-end speech enhancement, (ii)
which is optimal independently of underlying marginal signal
distributions, (iii) which does not introduce additional free
parameters, e.g., in terms of production and interpretation noise, and
(iv) which performs as good or slightly better than existing schemes.

## 2 Existing Work Based on Mutual Information

MI-based methods for joint far- and near-end SI enhancement
\[[8](#bib.bib8), [10](#bib.bib10)\] improve SI by maximizing the MI,
$`I(S;Z)`$, between the clean speech, $`S`$, and the signal received by
the listener, $`Z`$.

### 2.1 Existing model assumptions

In \[[12](#bib.bib12)\], production and interpretation noise terms are
introduced to model natural variations in speakers and listeners,
respectively, and adopted into the signal model of \[[8](#bib.bib8)\].
The production noise, $`Q`$, is due to the convolution of the
time-domain clean speech and the vocal tract, hence, theoretically, it
should be a _multiplicative_ noise in the frequency domain. However, in
\[[8](#bib.bib8)\] in order to simplify mathematical expressions,

1.  1.  Multiplicative production noise is modelled as _additive_.

Thus, the single-microphone signal model of \[[8](#bib.bib8)\] in the
absence of processing is, in the complex short-time DFT (STFT) domain,

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

       ``` math
       Z_{k,i}=d_{k,i}S_{k,i}+d_{k,i}Q_{k,i}+U_{k,i}+N_{k,i}+W_{k,i},
       ```                                                             |     | (1) |

where $`k`$ is the frequency-bin index and $`i`$ the time-frame, $`W`$
is the interpretation noise, $`U`$ is the far-end environmental noise,
$`N`$ is the near-end environmental noise, and $`d_{k,i}`$ are the
time-frequency coefficients of the room transfer function from target
talker to the microphone. The work on MI \[[8](#bib.bib8)\] relies on
several common signal model assumptions in the speech processing
literature, e.g., that speech and noise STFT coefficients are
statistically independent. However, to derive a speech enhancement
procedure based on MI, \[[8](#bib.bib8)\] introduces additional
assumptions and approximations;

1.  2.  The production noise, $`Q`$, and interpretation noise, $`W`$, are
        independent of the clean speech level and may be represented by a
        fixed gain (correlation), $`\rho_{0,k}`$, at each frequency band.
2.  3.  Critical band powers are assumed to be zero-mean independent
        Gaussian random variables.

In \[[8](#bib.bib8)\], the third assumption is needed since critical
band powers are in-fact Chi-squared distributed and the MI between
Chi-squared random variables is not expressible in closed form. However,
we note that critical band powers are positive by definition, hence a
zero-mean model is not necessarily appropriate.

### 2.2 Approximated Mutual Information vs ASII

The resulting approximated MI expression in \[[8](#bib.bib8)\] is

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
I(S;Z)\approx-\sum_{j}\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1-\rho_{0,j}^{2}\frac{\xi_{j}}{\xi_{j}+1}\right),
``` |  | (2) |

where $`\xi_{j}`$ is the SNR in critical band $`j`$. By
\[[8](#bib.bib8), sec. VIII.A\] we may take a first order Taylor
approximation of the MI in $`\rho_{0,j}^{2}`$ around zero, such that we
can approximate the cost function as,

|     |                                                      |     |     |
|-----|------------------------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       I(S;Z)\approx\sum_{j}I_{j}\frac{\xi_{j}}{\xi_{j}+1},
       ```                                                   |     | (3) |

for $`\rho_{0,j}^{2}\to 0^{+}`$, where
$`I_{j}\triangleq-\frac{1}{2}\log(1-\rho_{0,j}^{2})`$. In the absence of
a production noise model at the time, the values for $`\rho_{0,j}`$
where derived in \[[8](#bib.bib8)\] based on the band importance
functions, $`\gamma_{j}`$, of the SII \[[18](#bib.bib18)\] such that
$`\rho_{0,j}^{2}={1-2^{-2\gamma_{j}}}`$. Inserting this, the cost
function is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
I(S;Z)\approx\sum_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\log(1-(1-2^{-2\gamma_{j}}))\frac{\xi_{j}}{\xi_{j}+1}.
``` |  | (4) |

We recognize,
([4](#S2.E4 "In 2.2 Approximated Mutual Information vs ASII ‣ 2 Existing Work Based on Mutual Information ‣ Joint Far- and Near-End Speech Intelligibility Enhancement based on the Approximated Speech Intelligibility Index"))
resembles the ASII introduced in \[[13](#bib.bib13)\] as a cost function
for SI enhancement. The ASII is defined as,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
ASII\triangleq\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}f(\xi_{j}),~\quad f(\xi_{j})\triangleq\frac{\xi_{j}}{\xi_{j}+1},
``` |  | (5) |

where the weights $`\gamma_{j}`$ are the critical-band importance
functions as defined in \[[18](#bib.bib18)\], and $`f(\xi_{j})`$ is the
audibility function per critical band. We notice from
\[[18](#bib.bib18), Table 1\] that band importance functions,
$`\gamma_{j}`$, are in the interval of $`[0.01,0.06]`$, resulting in
$`\rho_{0,j}^{2}\in[0.0138,0.0798]`$. As shown in \[[10](#bib.bib10),
Fig. 1\] the approximation
([3](#S2.E3 "In 2.2 Approximated Mutual Information vs ASII ‣ 2 Existing Work Based on Mutual Information ‣ Joint Far- and Near-End Speech Intelligibility Enhancement based on the Approximated Speech Intelligibility Index"))
holds for $`{\rho_{0,j}^{2}\leq 0.4}`$. Hence, we can conclude the
choice of $`\rho_{0,j}^{2}=1-2^{-2\gamma_{j}}`$ to be sufficiently close
to zero for equality to hold in
([4](#S2.E4 "In 2.2 Approximated Mutual Information vs ASII ‣ 2 Existing Work Based on Mutual Information ‣ Joint Far- and Near-End Speech Intelligibility Enhancement based on the Approximated Speech Intelligibility Index")).
Thus, the MI problem in \[[8](#bib.bib8)\] is equal to the ASII problem,
when the parameter modelling production- and interpretation noise,
$`\rho_{0,j}`$, is chosen according to the band importance functions of
the SII.

## 3 Signal Model

In this section we introduce the proposed signal model, cf.
Fig. [2](#S3.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 3 Signal Model ‣ Joint Far- and Near-End Speech Intelligibility Enhancement based on the Approximated Speech Intelligibility Index").
The single-microphone signal model follows,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle X_{k,i}=d_{k,i}S_{k,i}+U_{k,i},~Y_{k,i}=vX_{k,i},~Z_{k,i}=Y_{k,i}+N_{k,i},`$ |  | (6) |

where $`X_{k,i}`$ is the recorded signal in STFT domain, i.e., the clean
speech, $`S_{k,i}`$, recorded by the microphone contaminated by the
far-end noise, $`U_{k,i}`$. To increase SI of the received message, the
noisy microphone signal, $`X_{k,i}`$, is linearly processed prior to
playout, producing the modified signal $`Y_{k,i}`$. The signal received
by the listener, $`Z_{k,i}`$, is finally contaminated by the noise in
the near-end environment, $`N_{k,i}`$. The speech and noise processes,
$`S`$, $`U`$, and $`N`$, are assumed to be stationary sequences of
complex random vectors consisting of the STFT coefficients. Both the
far-end noise, $`U`$, and the near-end noise, $`N`$ are assumed to be
independent of each other and of the target speech, $`S`$. These
assumptions are similar to \[[8](#bib.bib8)\]. However, compared to
\[[8](#bib.bib8)\] we do not need to model a multiplicative production
noise as additive, or to introduce an additional interpretation noise.
Further, we do not need assumptions on the particular marginal
distributions of the signals.

![Refer to caption]()

Figure 2: Our signal model of optimal joint SI enhancement.

### 3.1 Multi-Microphone Signal Model

Let us denote the acoustic transfer function from source to microphone
$`m`$ by $`d_{k,i,m}`$ with vector notation,
$`\bm{d}_{k,i}=\left[d_{k,i,1},\ldots,d_{k,i,m}\right]^{T}`$, and
letting vector $`\bm{U}_{k,i}`$ denote far-end noise recorded by the
microphones. Then the noisy microphone signals are given by

|     |                                                |     |     |
|-----|------------------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \bm{X}_{k,i}=\bm{d}_{k,i}S_{k,i}+\bm{U}_{k,i}.
       ```                                             |     | (7) |

Denoting the linear multi-microphone processor by $`\bm{v}_{k,i}`$, the
processed microphone signal is,

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle Y_{k,i}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\bm{v}_{k,i}^{H}\bm{d}_{k,i}S_{k,i}+\bm{v}_{k,i}^{H}\bm{U}_{k,i}`$ |  | (8) |

where super-script $`H`$ denotes conjugate transpose.

## 4 Optimal ASII Linear Processor

In this section we derive the optimal linear processor based on the ASII
defined
in ([5](#S2.E5 "In 2.2 Approximated Mutual Information vs ASII ‣ 2 Existing Work Based on Mutual Information ‣ Joint Far- and Near-End Speech Intelligibility Enhancement based on the Approximated Speech Intelligibility Index")).
The derivation steps are similar to \[[8](#bib.bib8)\] and
\[[13](#bib.bib13)\]. However, contrary to \[[8](#bib.bib8)\] we
consider the ASII instead of MI. Further, we expand on
\[[13](#bib.bib13)\] by including joint (multi-microphone) processing
with far-end noise.

The energy of the clean speech signal within one critical band, $`j`$,
and time-frame, $`i`$, is defined as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{S}_{j,i}^{2}\triangleq\sum_{k}\lvert S_{k,i}\rvert^{2}\lvert H_{j}(k)\rvert^{2},
``` |  | (9) |

where $`H_{j}(k)`$ is the STFT coefficients of the $`j`$’th critical
band filter. Similarly, we define the critical band energy of the
near-end noise, $`\mathcal{N}_{j,i}^{2}`$, and the processed far-end
speech, $`\mathcal{\widetilde{S}}_{j,i}^{2}`$, and noise,
$`\mathcal{\widetilde{U}}_{j,i}^{2}`$. Since we assume stationarity of
the speech and noise, we can disregard the time-index, $`i`$, and let
the average energy per DFT bin and critical band be based on a long-term
average over several short-time frames,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sigma_{S_{k}}^{2}\triangleq\frac{1}{I}\sum_{i}\lvert S_{k,i}\rvert^{2},\quad\sigma_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}^{2}\triangleq\sum_{k}\lvert H_{j}(k)\rvert^{2}\sigma_{S_{k}}^{2},
``` |  | (10) |

where $`I`$ is the total number of frames. Similar definitions hold for
the noise terms $`U`$ and $`N`$. The critical band filters,
$`H_{j}(k)`$, are normalized such that the total energy is preserved in
critical bands, i.e.,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{j}\sigma_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}^{2}=\sum_{k}\sigma_{{S}_{k}}^{2}.
``` |  | (11) |

The critical band SNR at the near-end listener is then,

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\xi_{j}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{j}}^{2}}{\sigma_{\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}_{j}}^{2}+\sigma_{\mathcal{N}_{j}}^{2}}.`$ |  | (12) |

Inserting this into
([5](#S2.E5 "In 2.2 Approximated Mutual Information vs ASII ‣ 2 Existing Work Based on Mutual Information ‣ Joint Far- and Near-End Speech Intelligibility Enhancement based on the Approximated Speech Intelligibility Index")),
we have that

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle f(\xi_{j})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\tfrac{\sum_{k}\lvert H_{j}(k)\rvert^{2}\bm{v}_{k}^{H}\bm{d}_{k}\bm{d}_{k}^{H}\bm{v}_{k}\sigma_{S_{k}}^{2}}{\sum_{k}\lvert H_{j}(k)\rvert^{2}\left(\bm{v}_{k}^{H}\bm{d}_{k}\bm{d}_{k}^{H}\bm{v}_{k}\sigma_{S_{k}}^{2}+\bm{v}_{k}^{H}\Sigma_{U_{k}}\bm{v}_{k}+\sigma_{{N}_{k}}^{2}\right)}`$ |  | (13) |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\triangleq f_{j}\left(\left\{\bm{v}_{k},\Theta_{k}\right\}\right),`$ |  | (14) |

where
$`\Theta_{k}=(\sigma_{S_{k}}^{2},\Sigma_{U_{k}},\sigma_{{N}_{k}}^{2})`$.
In order to limit loudspeaker overload or unpleasant playback levels we
invoke the following equal power constraint,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{k}\bm{v}_{k,i}^{H}\bm{d}_{k,i}\bm{d}_{k,i}^{H}\bm{v}_{k,i}\sigma_{S_{k,i}}^{2}=\sum_{k}\sigma_{S_{k,i}}^{2}.
``` |  | (15) |

That is, for each time frame $`i`$ the total power of the clean speech
is unaltered by processing. The joint far and near-end SI enhancement
problem with equal power constraint is then,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\mathop{\sup}_{\{\bm{v}_{k}\}\in\mathbb{C}^{M}}&\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}f_{j}\left(\left\{\bm{v}_{k},\Theta_{k}\right\}\right)\\
\mbox{subject to}&\sum_{k}\bm{v}_{k}^{H}\bm{d}_{k}\bm{d}_{k}^{H}\bm{v}_{k}\sigma_{S_{k}}^{2}=\sum_{k}\sigma_{S_{k}}^{2}.\end{array}
``` |  | (16) |

We now introduce the real and positive variable, $`\alpha_{k}`$, to
perform a variable transformation
$`\bm{v}_{k}=\alpha_{k}^{1/2}\bm{w}_{k}`$ with the additional constraint
$`\bm{v}_{k}^{H}\bm{d}_{k}\bm{d}_{k}^{H}\bm{v}_{k}=\alpha_{k}`$. This
leads to the equivalent problem,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\mathop{\sup}_{\{\bm{v}_{k}\}\in\mathbb{C}^{M},\{\alpha_{k}\}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}&\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}f_{j}\left(\left\{\alpha_{k},\bm{v}_{k},\Theta_{k}\right\}\right)\\
\mbox{subject to}&\mathcal{C}_{1}~:~\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}\sigma_{S_{k}}^{2}=\sum_{k}\sigma_{S_{k}}^{2},\\
&\mathcal{C}_{2}~:~\bm{v}_{k}^{H}\bm{d}_{k}\bm{d}_{k}^{H}\bm{v}_{k}=\alpha_{k},~\forall k.\end{array}
``` |  | (17) |

The objective function can be rewritten in terms of $`\alpha_{k}`$ and
$`\bm{w}_{k}`$, i.e.,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle f_{j}\left(\left\{\alpha_{k},\bm{w}_{k},,\Theta_{k}\right\}\right)=\tfrac{\sum_{k}\lvert H_{j}(k)\rvert^{2}\alpha_{k}\sigma_{S_{k}}^{2}}{\sum_{k}\lvert H_{j}(k)\rvert^{2}\left(\alpha_{k}\sigma_{S_{k}}^{2}+\alpha_{k}\bm{w}_{k}^{H}\Sigma_{U_{k}}\bm{w}_{k}+\sigma_{{N}_{k}}^{2}\right)}.`$ |  |

We notice that
$`\bm{v}_{k}^{H}\bm{d}_{k}\bm{d}_{k}^{H}\bm{v}_{k}=\alpha_{k}\Leftrightarrow\bm{d}_{k}^{H}\bm{w}_{k}=1.`$
Hence, writing the optimization problem in terms of $`\bm{w}_{k}`$ and
$`\alpha_{k}`$ we have,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\mathop{\sup}_{\{\bm{w}_{k}\}\in\mathbb{C}^{M},\{\alpha_{k}\}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}&\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}f_{j}\left(\left\{\alpha_{k},\bm{w}_{k},\Theta_{k}\right\}\right)\\
\mbox{subject to}&\mathcal{C}_{1}~:~\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}\sigma_{S_{k}}^{2}=\sum_{k}\sigma_{S_{k}}^{2}\\
&\mathcal{C}_{2}~:~\bm{d}_{k}^{H}\bm{w}_{k}=1.\end{array}
``` |  | (18) |

We can separate
([18](#S4.E18 "In 4 Optimal ASII Linear Processor ‣ Joint Far- and Near-End Speech Intelligibility Enhancement based on the Approximated Speech Intelligibility Index"))
across the two variables \[[19](#bib.bib19), p. 133\], i.e.,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\displaystyle\mathop{\sup}_{\{\alpha_{k}\}\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathcal{C}_{1}}\mathop{\sup}_{\{\bm{w}_{k}\}\in\mathbb{C}^{M},\mathcal{C}_{2}}\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}f_{j}\left(\left\{\alpha_{k},\bm{w}_{k},\Theta_{k}\right\}\right).
``` |  |

The inner optimization problem across $`\{\bm{w}_{k}\}`$ corresponds to
the standard Minimum Variance Distortionless Response (MVDR) beamforming
problem with the solution \[[2](#bib.bib2)\],

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\bm{w}_{k}^{*}=\frac{\Sigma_{U_{k,i}}^{-1}\bm{d}_{k,i}}{\bm{d}_{k,i}^{H}\Sigma_{U_{k,i}}^{-1}\bm{d}_{k,i}},\quad\forall k.
``` |  | (19) |

Inserting the MVDR solution into
([18](#S4.E18 "In 4 Optimal ASII Linear Processor ‣ Joint Far- and Near-End Speech Intelligibility Enhancement based on the Approximated Speech Intelligibility Index")),
the remaining problem is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\mathop{\sup}_{\{\alpha_{k}\}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}&\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}\left(\frac{\sum_{k}\lvert H_{j}(k)\rvert^{2}\alpha_{k}\sigma_{S_{k}}^{2}}{\sum_{k}\lvert H_{j}(k)\rvert^{2}\left(\alpha_{k}\sigma_{S_{k}}^{2}+\alpha_{k}\sigma_{B_{k}}^{2}+\sigma_{{N}_{k}}^{2}\right)}\right)\\
\mbox{subject to}&\mathcal{C}_{1}~:~\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}\sigma_{S_{k}}^{2}=\sum_{k}\sigma_{S_{k}}^{2},\end{array}
``` |  | (20) |

where
$`\sigma_{B_{k}}^{2}\triangleq\bm{w}_{k}^{*^{H}}\Sigma_{U_{k}}\bm{w}_{k}^{*}`$
is the residual far-end noise after processing by the MVDR beamformer.

### 4.1 Critical-band near-end optimization

We derive, similarly to existing work on SI enhancement
\[[8](#bib.bib8), [7](#bib.bib7), [13](#bib.bib13), [6](#bib.bib6)\],
the optimal near-end processor based on the assumption that all
frequency gains within a critical band $`j`$ are the same, i.e.,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\alpha_{k}=\alpha_{k^{\prime}},\forall k,k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}_{j},
``` |  | (21) |

where $`\mathcal{K}_{j}`$ is the set of frequency bins belonging to the
$`j`$’th critical band. The gains are then later on converted back to
DFT domain.

Starting from the optimization problem
([20](#S4.E20 "In 4 Optimal ASII Linear Processor ‣ Joint Far- and Near-End Speech Intelligibility Enhancement based on the Approximated Speech Intelligibility Index"))
we have,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\mathop{\sup}_{\{\alpha_{j}\}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}&\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}\frac{\alpha_{j}\sigma_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}^{2}}{\alpha_{j}\sigma_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}^{2}+\alpha_{j}\sigma_{\mathcal{B}_{j}}^{2}+\sigma_{\mathcal{N}_{j}}^{2}}\\
\mbox{subject to}&\mathcal{C}_{1}~:~\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}\sigma_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}^{2}=\sum_{j}\sigma_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}^{2}.\end{array}
``` |  | (22) |

We notice each term in the sum is concave in $`\alpha_{j}`$ for
$`\alpha_{j}\geq 0`$. Therefore, the weighted sum of these concave
functions is also concave. We describe the problem by the Lagrangian
cost-function \[[19](#bib.bib19)\],

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=`$ | $`\displaystyle\sum_{j}\tfrac{\gamma_{j}\alpha_{j}\sigma_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}^{2}}{\alpha_{j}\sigma_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}^{2}+\alpha_{j}\sigma_{\mathcal{B}_{j}}^{2}+\sigma_{\mathcal{N}_{j}}^{2}}-\nu\left(\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}\sigma_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}^{2}-r\right)+\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\alpha_{j},`$ |  |

where $`r=\sum_{j}\sigma_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}^{2}`$, and $`\nu`$ and
$`\lambda_{j}`$ are the Lagrangian multipliers for the energy constraint
and inequality constraint in
([22](#S4.E22 "In 4.1 Critical-band near-end optimization ‣ 4 Optimal ASII Linear Processor ‣ Joint Far- and Near-End Speech Intelligibility Enhancement based on the Approximated Speech Intelligibility Index")).
The KKT conditions \[[19](#bib.bib19)\] for the optimization problem
are,

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  |  |  |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle r`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}\sigma_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}^{2},~0\leq\alpha_{j},~0\leq\lambda_{j},~0=\lambda_{j}\alpha_{j},\forall j,`$ |  | (23a) |
|  | $`\displaystyle 0`$ | $`\displaystyle=\gamma_{j}\frac{\sigma_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}^{2}\sigma_{\mathcal{N}_{j}}^{2}}{\left(\alpha_{j}\left(\sigma_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}^{2}+\sigma_{\mathcal{B}_{j}}^{2}\right)+\sigma_{\mathcal{N}_{j}}^{2}\right)^{2}}-\nu\sigma_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}^{2}+\lambda_{j},\forall j`$ |  | (23b) |

Isolating $`\lambda_{j}`$ in
([23b](#S4.E23.2 "In 23 ‣ 4.1 Critical-band near-end optimization ‣ 4 Optimal ASII Linear Processor ‣ Joint Far- and Near-End Speech Intelligibility Enhancement based on the Approximated Speech Intelligibility Index")),
then using the complimentary slackness condition to set
$`\lambda_{j}=0`$, we solve for the non-zero $`\alpha_{j}`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\alpha_{j}=\max\left\{\frac{\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{N}_{j}}^{2}\gamma_{j}}}{\sqrt{\nu}\left(\sigma_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}^{2}+\sigma_{\mathcal{B}_{j}}^{2}\right)}-\frac{\sigma_{\mathcal{N}_{j}}^{2}}{\sigma_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}^{2}+\sigma_{\mathcal{B}_{j}}^{2}},0\right\},~\forall j,
``` |  | (24) |

where $`\nu`$ is chosen such that the energy constraint in
([23a](#S4.E23.1 "In 23 ‣ 4.1 Critical-band near-end optimization ‣ 4 Optimal ASII Linear Processor ‣ Joint Far- and Near-End Speech Intelligibility Enhancement based on the Approximated Speech Intelligibility Index"))
is satisfied,

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\left(r+\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\tfrac{\sigma_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}^{2}\sigma_{\mathcal{N}_{j}}^{2}}{\sigma_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}^{2}+\sigma_{\mathcal{B}_{j}}^{2}}\right)/\left(\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\left(\tfrac{\sigma_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}^{2}\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{N}_{j}}^{2}\gamma_{j}}}{\left(\sigma_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}^{2}+\sigma_{\mathcal{B}_{j}}^{2}\right)}\right)\right),`$ |  | (25) |

here $`\mathcal{J}=\left\{j\in\mathbb{N}:\alpha_{j}>0\right\}`$ denotes
the set of frequency bins for which the optimal $`\alpha_{j}`$ are
positive. We notice, that the set of indices $`\mathcal{J}`$ depends on
the $`\alpha_{j}`$\[[13](#bib.bib13)\]. Therefore, $`\nu`$ also depends
on $`\alpha_{j}`$. Hence, there is a recursive relationship between
([24](#S4.E24 "In 4.1 Critical-band near-end optimization ‣ 4 Optimal ASII Linear Processor ‣ Joint Far- and Near-End Speech Intelligibility Enhancement based on the Approximated Speech Intelligibility Index"))
and
([25](#S4.E25 "In 4.1 Critical-band near-end optimization ‣ 4 Optimal ASII Linear Processor ‣ Joint Far- and Near-End Speech Intelligibility Enhancement based on the Approximated Speech Intelligibility Index")),
which may be resolved by using, e.g., a bi-section method or evaluating
([24](#S4.E24 "In 4.1 Critical-band near-end optimization ‣ 4 Optimal ASII Linear Processor ‣ Joint Far- and Near-End Speech Intelligibility Enhancement based on the Approximated Speech Intelligibility Index"))
for a range of $`\nu`$ values, such that the energy constraint is
satisfied \[[13](#bib.bib13)\]. Finally, to get the optimal frequency
dependent gains, $`\alpha_{k}^{*}`$, we weight the optimal,
$`\alpha_{j}^{*}`$, using the critical band filters, that is

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\alpha_{k}^{*}=\sum_{j}\lvert H_{j}(k)\rvert^{2}\alpha_{j}^{*},
``` |  | (26) |

where the energy constraint is satisfied in both frequency and
critical-band domain as per the normalization of the critical band
filters in
([11](#S4.E11 "In 4 Optimal ASII Linear Processor ‣ Joint Far- and Near-End Speech Intelligibility Enhancement based on the Approximated Speech Intelligibility Index")),
i.e.,
$`\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}^{*}\sigma_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}^{2}=\sum_{j}\sigma_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}^{2}=\sum_{k}\sigma_{{S}_{k}}^{2}=\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}^{*}\sigma_{{S}_{k}}^{2}.`$

The proposed processor is summarized in
Fig. [2](#S3.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 3 Signal Model ‣ Joint Far- and Near-End Speech Intelligibility Enhancement based on the Approximated Speech Intelligibility Index").
As in \[[8](#bib.bib8)\], the procedure consists of an MVDR beamformer,
$`\bm{w}_{k}`$, followed by a frequency dependent gain, $`\alpha_{k}`$.
In contrast to the results of \[[8](#bib.bib8)\], the frequency bin
gains, $`\alpha_{k}`$, of our procedure are optimized specifically
according to the ASII without approximations and assumptions on signal
distributions and without introduction of additional free parameters to
model natural speech variations.

## 5 Experimental Evaluation

We have seen
(Section [2](#S2 "2 Existing Work Based on Mutual Information ‣ Joint Far- and Near-End Speech Intelligibility Enhancement based on the Approximated Speech Intelligibility Index"))
that by using the production noise model choice in \[[8](#bib.bib8)\]
the MI cost function reduces to the ASII. However, this choice of
production noise is due to a lack of a more accurate model at the time.
Recently, some of the authors have provided an estimated model for the
production noise in \[[14](#bib.bib14), [15](#bib.bib15),
[16](#bib.bib16)\], where $`\rho_{0,j}=0.75~\forall j`$. We compare
performance of our proposed method with \[[8](#bib.bib8)\] using the
newer production noise model of \[[14](#bib.bib14)\]. We consider a
Python simulation of a setup similar to that of \[[8](#bib.bib8),
[20](#bib.bib20)\].

### 5.1 Experimental Setup

We consider a room with dimensions
$`3\times 4\times 3`$ $`\mathrm{m}^{3}`$, a single target speaker
located at $`[1.50,3.00,1]`$ $`\mathrm{m}`$, and an array with two
microphones spaced $`2\text{\,}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{m}`$ apart at
$`[1.50,2.00,1]`$ $`\mathrm{m}`$ and $`[1.50,2.02,1]`$ $`\mathrm{m}`$.
At the far-end there are three speech shaped noise sources located at
$`[0.50,1.00,1]`$ $`\mathrm{m}`$, $`[0.75,3.00,1]`$ $`\mathrm{m}`$ and
$`[3.00,1.60,1]`$ $`\mathrm{m}`$, respectively. The near-end noise is
pink. In addition to the far-end noise each microphone is subject to a
$`60\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}`$ SNR white noise. The source signal
is speech signals from five female and five male speakers from the
TIMIT-database \[[21](#bib.bib21)\] sampled at
$`16\text{\,}\mathrm{k}\mathrm{H}\mathrm{z}`$. Signals were processed
block-wise based on a DFT with $`32\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{s}`$ Hann
windows with $`50\%`$ overlap. Since we assume stationarity, the MVDR
beamformer, $`\bm{w}_{k}`$ and post-filter gains, $`\alpha_{k}`$, are
derived based on the long-term average spectrums, leading to
time-invariant processing. The long-term power of the clean speech,
far-end noise, and the near-end noise are assumed to be known. Hence, we
do not estimate any of these spectrums. Furthermore, the room transfer
functions are assumed to be known, and generated without reverberation
using \[[22](#bib.bib22)\].

### 5.2 Results

Fig. [3](#S5.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 5.2 Results ‣ 5 Experimental Evaluation ‣ Joint Far- and Near-End Speech Intelligibility Enhancement based on the Approximated Speech Intelligibility Index")
shows improvements over ’unprocessed’ in ESTOI \[[17](#bib.bib17)\], of
the proposed method along with the method of \[[8](#bib.bib8)\]. The
results show that generally the two methods achieve a similar
performance, which is expected due to the similarity shown in
Section [2](#S2 "2 Existing Work Based on Mutual Information ‣ Joint Far- and Near-End Speech Intelligibility Enhancement based on the Approximated Speech Intelligibility Index").
However, the proposed method is slightly better when the near-end SNR is
low and the far-end SNR is intermediate or high. Thus, the production
noise model choice of \[[14](#bib.bib14)\] leads to a slightly worse
speech enhancement than using the model based on the SII
weights \[[18](#bib.bib18)\]. ASII plots show identical performance
between the two methods and are thus not reported.

## 6 Conclusion

We have derived a closed-form optimal linear processor for joint far-
and near-end speech intelligibility enhancement based on ASII. The
optimal processor consists of an MVDR beamformer followed by a frequency
dependent post gain. The derived processor is based on a simple model
without relying on assumptions and approximations of the underlying
marginal signal distributions. Furthermore, we do not need to model or
optimize for natural variations in speech. Finally, as a consequence,
the proposed processor has comparable or slightly better ESTOI
performance than existing work.
