###### Abstract

Recent works on deep non-linear spatially selective filters demonstrate
exceptional enhancement performance with computationally lightweight
architectures for stationary speakers of known directions. However, to
maintain this performance in dynamic scenarios, resource-intensive
data-driven tracking algorithms become necessary to provide precise
spatial guidance conditioned on the initial direction of a target
speaker. As this additional computational overhead hinders application
in resource-constrained scenarios such as real-time speech enhancement,
we present a novel strategy utilizing a low-complexity tracking
algorithm in the form of a pf instead. Assuming a causal, sequential
processing style, we introduce temporal feedback to leverage the
enhanced speech signal of the ssf to compensate for the limited modeling
capabilities of the pf. Evaluation on a synthetic dataset illustrates
how the ar interplay between both algorithms drastically improves
tracking accuracy and leads to strong enhancement performance. A
listening test with real-world recordings complements these findings by
indicating a clear trend towards our proposed self-steering pipeline as
preferred choice over comparable methods.

## 1 Introduction

The field of speech enhancement focuses on improving the quality and
intelligibility of a recorded speech signal by suppressing noise and
reverberation artifacts. Additional interfering speakers present a
particularly challenging type of noise due to their similar and
non-stationary statistical characteristics. In such a multi-speaker
environment, the task of tse (tse) lies in identifying and enhancing the
speech signal of a specific target speaker based on supplementary
information, also known as cues. Although a range of cue modalities have
been suggested for this purpose \[zmolikova23tse_overview\], spatial
information becomes especially popular when recordings from a microphone
array are available. Commonly under the assumption that contributing
speakers are distinguishable by their azimuth orientation to the array,
a ssf (ssf) can be employed and steered in the target’s direction to
extract the desired speech signal. Compared to direct separation
approaches disentangling all speech sources at once, ssf have been shown
to be superior in the enhancement task \[tesch24ssf_journal\].
Especially in scenarios with stationary speakers, deep non-linear ssf
demonstrate exceptional performance while retaining lightweight nn (nn)
architectures \[tesch24ssf_journal, briegleb23icospa,
bohlender24sep_journal, pandey12directional_speech_extraction,
gu24rezero\].

A handful of constrained recording setups, such as a seated conference
meeting with a centered microphone array \[chen20libricss\], may
legitimate the assumption of stationary and directionally distinct
speaker locations. However, more general settings, like the dinner party
scenario considered in \[barker18chime5\], leave them unquestionably
invalid. Due to utilizing temporal context in the extraction process
\[tesch24ssf_journal, briegleb23icospa, bohlender24sep_journal,
pandey12directional_speech_extraction, gu24rezero\], the change from a
stationary to a dynamic acoustic scenario results in a significant
mismatch for data-driven ssf. To minimize performance degradation,
speaker movement has to be included during training
\[kienegger25wg_ssf\]. The arising need for time-dependent ground truth
directional cues, which we refer to as strong guidance, bears an
additional challenge, since they are usually not available. To increase
applicability, weakly guided tse relies only on knowledge of the
speakers starting position by including an upstream tracking algorithm
to automate the steering of the ssf. Although a joint training strategy
can increase robustness to cue inaccuracies, previous work has shown
that a resource-intensive tracking algorithm remains necessary to
provide sufficiently precise directional guidance and retain strong
enhancement performance \[kienegger25wg_ssf\].

While the majority of speech enhancement systems conduct offline
processing, an increasing demand in telecommunication, assistive
technologies and consumer electronics boosts the research towards
real-time capable solutions \[tan18cruse, defossez20demucs,
braun21cruse\]. Since they are often implemented as low-complexity and
causal modifications of their offline counterparts, such approaches tend
to suffer from a fundamental disadvantage due to the reduced
computational capacity \[luo19conv_tasnet, chao24SEmamba\]. However, the
sequential processing style also opens new possibilities such as
improving the enhancement performance by utilizing the extracted speech
signal from prior steps. Recent works demonstrate promising results by
introducing a temporal feedback to obtain ar (ar) nn architectures
\[andreev23iterative_autoregression,
pan24paris_autoregressive_separation\]. Nevertheless, the inherent
non-parallelizability during training limits their applicability in
data-driven speech enhancement.

Instead of relying on computationally heavy methods utilizing nn to
guide the ssf, we present a novel strategy to improve the accuracy of a
low-complexity tracking algorithm in form of a pf (pf). Assuming a
sequential processing style common to real-time systems, we propose to
utilize the enhanced speech of the ssf via a temporal feedback to
compensate for the limited modeling capabilities of the pf. To keep the
training effort light, we optimize the algorithms independently and only
use a limited number of fine-tuning steps to train the resulting ar
architecture in an end-to-end fashion. By evaluating our proposed
self-steering pipeline with a synthetic dataset, we can demonstrate a
drastic improvement in tracking accuracy leading to strong enhancement
performance. Complemented by a listening test with real-world
recordings, our method achieves superior speech quality and interference
suppression towards comparable approaches.

## 2 Signal Model

We model the multi-channel observation $`\mathbf{Y}`$ as mixture between
anechoic target speech signal $`\mathbf{S}`$ and noise $`\mathbf{V}`$.
The latter contains interfering speech signals, additive environmental
noise as well as the reverberant components of the target speech. In the
stft (stft) domain, this acoustic setup is resembled by

```math
\mathbf{Y}_{tk}=\mathbf{S}_{tk}+\mathbf{V}_{tk}\,, \tag{1}
```

with $`t`$ and $`k`$ denoting frame and frequency bins respectively and
vectorization conducted over the channel dimension. In this work we set
the goal of reconstructing the anechoic target speech signal
$`\mathbf{S}_{tk}`$ at a predefined reference microphone, which we
indicate as $`S_{tk}`$. Thus, the task of tse lies in recovering the
speech signal $`S_{tk}`$ from the noisy observations $`\mathbf{Y}_{tk}`$
conditioned on a speaker specific characteristic.

## 3 Spatially Guided Speaker Extraction

### 3.1 Strongly guided target speaker extraction

Spatially selective filters (ssf) exploit positional cues to distinguish
the target’s speech signal from interfering sources. In this work, we
abide by the common convention of restraining the cue modality to the
relative azimuth orientation $`\theta_{t}`$, referred to as doa (doa),
of the target speaker to the microphone array \[tesch24ssf*journal,
briegleb23icospa, bohlender24sep_journal\]. If ground truth doa
information is available, a ssf can be directly employed to solve the
extraction task, which we refer to as strongly guided tse. A common
approach to recover the target speech signal is to compute a
time-frequency mask $`\mathcal{M}*{tk}`$ and extract $`S*{tk}`$ by
multiplication with the corresponding reference channel $`Y^{0}*{tk}`$
of observation $`\mathbf{Y}\_{tk}`$ \[tesch24ssf_journal,
bohlender24sep_journal\],

```math
\widehat{S}_{tk}=\mathcal{M}_{tk}Y^{0}_{tk}\,. \tag{2}
```

In terms of sequential processing, the ssf may thus be seen as a
deterministic function $`\mathcal{F}`$ mapping current broadband input
$`\mathbf{Y}_{t}`$ and doa $`\theta_{t}`$ to mask values
$`\mathcal{M}_{tk}`$. Due to utilizing temporal dependencies
\[tesch24ssf*journal, briegleb23icospa, bohlender24sep_journal,
pandey12directional_speech_extraction, gu24rezero\], deep ssf are
parametrized by a hidden state $`\mathbf{z}*{t-1}`$ capturing previous
context in addition to the parameters $`\phi`$ of the nn architecture

```math
\mathcal{M}_{tk}=\mathcal{F}_{k}\!\left(\mathbf{Y}_{t},\theta_{t};\mathbf{z}_{t-1},\phi\right)\,. \tag{3}
```

Regarding the channel dimension, the ssf may thus be seen as a miso
(miso) system by computing a single-channel mask $`\mathcal{M}_{tk}`$
based on multi-channel observations $`\mathbf{Y}_{t}`$.

### 3.2 Weakly guided target speaker extraction

The dependency of strongly guided tse on the continuous provision of
ground truth directional information poses a great limitation in most
practical scenarios. As an alternative, weakly guided tse
\[kienegger25wg*ssf\] restrains the cue only to the target’s initial
orientation $`\theta*{0}`$ wrt the microphone array. In order to
continue using a ssf for the enhancement task, an upstream tracking
algorithm predicting the temporal evolution of the doa $`\theta*{t}`$
becomes necessary. Due to its well-balanced tradeoff between
computational complexity and robustness, we choose a pf for this task,
which has already proven highly effective in the context of acoustic
speaker tracking \[vermaak01pf_nonlinear_speaker_tracking,
ward03basic_particle_filter, dong20pf_doa_coprime\]. Modeling the
tracking problem in a state-space formulation, the pf recovers the doa
$`\theta*{t}`$ encoded in state $`\mathbf{x}_{t}`$ from all available
observations $`\mathbf{Y}_{1:t}`$ and based on the initial condition
$`\mathbf{x}_{0}`$. The state $`\mathbf{x}_{t}`$ contains the doa
$`\theta*{t}`$ either explicitly or implicitly in a different coordinate
representation and is often complemented by velocity or acceleration for
an improved modeling of motion dynamics. A pf progressively updates a
discrete approximation of the posterior
$`p(\mathbf{x}*{t}|\mathbf{Y}_{1:t},\mathbf{x}_{0})`$ using sequential
importance sampling \[hagiwara21state_space_model\]

```math
p(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{Y}_{1:t},\mathbf{x}_{0})\approx\sum_{n=1}^{N}w_{t}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}\delta\!\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\!-\!\mathbf{x}_{t}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}\right)\,, \tag{4}
```

with $`\delta(\cdot)`$ denoting the Dirac delta function. The $`N`$
state samples $`\mathbf{x}_{t}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}`$, referred to as
particles, are drawn according to a proposal distribution
$`q\!\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{Y}_{1:t},\mathbf{x}_{0:t-1}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}\right)`$
and the importance weights $`w_{t}^{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}`$ can be
recursively computed by imposing mild conditions on the underlying
statistical model \[arulampalam02pf*tutorial, chen04pf_sensor_fusion\].
Utilizing the approximated probability density function in (4) enables a
tractable estimation of the posterior mean, which is used as the state
estimate $`\widehat{\mathbf{x}}*{t}`$. The specific pf formulation
referred to as bootstrap filter \[gordon93pf_bootstrap_filter\] allows
for a particularly efficient implementation by only taking the
state-transition model for the proposal distribution into account.
Although the resulting state estimates lack information from current
observations, the algorithmic simplicity makes it a popular choice for
acoustic tracking tasks \[ward03basic_particle_filter,
dong20pf_doa_coprime\]. By leveraging the state estimates of a pf to
guide a downstream ssf based on observations $`\mathbf{Y}_{t}`$ and
starting direction $`\theta_{0}`$, the tse pipeline emerges as
concatenation of both algorithms, see Fig. 1(a).

## 4 Proposed method

The sequential application of tracking and enhancement algorithms may
seem like the natural approach to automate the steering of a ssf. While
this strategy has proven successful in our previous work on tse
\[kienegger25wg*ssf\] as well as for the related task of speech
separation in \[bohlender24sep_journal\], it is overly dependent on the
accuracy of the upstream doa estimates. As a result, computationally
heavy, data-driven tracking algorithms have to be employed to provide
sufficiently precise directional information and maintain a strong
enhancement performance \[bohlender24sep_journal, kienegger25wg_ssf\].
This additional computational overhead naturally limits the
applicability of such methods in resource-constrained scenarios. Instead
of an independent concatenation, we propose a more dynamic interaction
between tracking and enhancement algorithms. Since the nature of
sequential processing grants access to the output of the ssf, we
introduce a temporal feedback to improve the accuracy of a
computationally lightweight pf as opposed to employing a
resource-intensive tracking algorithm. To retain spatial information at
the output of the ssf, we slightly deviate from the original formulation
in (2). Rather than just for the reference channel, we propose to
compute a multi-channel mask $`\mathbfcal{M}*{tk}`$ and extract the
direct path propagated target speech signal $`\mathbf{S}_{tk}`$ by
element-wise multiplication ($`\odot`$) with the observation
$`\mathbf{Y}_{tk}`$

```math
\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{tk}=\mathbfcal{M}_{tk}\odot\mathbf{Y}_{tk}\,. \tag{5}
```

Unlike the original miso formulation in (2), the estimates
$`\widehat{\mathbf{S}}_{tk}`$ of such a mimo (mimo) strategy have
already been proven to result in an improved localization performance
for stationary scenarios \[li23gcc-speaker, chen24locselect,
battula25MIMO*localization\]. Rather than approximating the posterior
based on the broadband observations $`\mathbf{Y}*{t}`$ containing the
directional information of all speakers, the pf algorithm can utilize
the spatial characteristics of the target’s direct path propagation
$`p\big(\mathbf{x}_{t}|\mathbf{S}_{1:t},\mathbf{x}_{0}\big)`$. However,
due to the temporal feedback in the ar structure, only the doa estimates
from the previous frames $`\widehat{\theta}_{t-1}`$ are accessible to
the ssf. This necessitates a directional single-frame lookahead
prediction during mask computation, leading to the adjusted formulation

```math
\mathbfcal{M}_{tk}=\mathbfcal{F}_{k}\!\left(\mathbf{Y}_{t},\theta_{t-1};\mathbf{z}_{t-1},\phi\right)\,. \tag{6}
```

Paired with the need to reconstruct the anechoic propagation between the
microphones of the array, the difficulty in the enhancement task
increases for the ssf compared to its original formulation in (3).
Figure 1(b) displays our proposed weakly guided tse pipeline as an ar
interconnection between pf and our mimo adaption of the ssf.

## 5 Experiments

### 5.1 Dataset

Synthetic dataset We employ a synthetic dataset consisting of noisy and
reverberant two-speaker mixtures to enable nn training, model
optimization and evaluation in a controlled acoustic scenario. In
particular, we use utterances from the LibriSpeech corpus
\[panayotov15librispeech\] and pair them according to the recipe in
Libri2Mix \[cosentino20librimix\]. For spatialization, we utilize
simulated rir in shoe-box shaped rooms based on a GPU accelerated
implementation \[diaz18gpu_rir\] of the image method
\[allen79image_method\]. We closely follow the works from Tesch et al.
\[tesch24ssf_journal, tesch22spatial_spectral_temporal_nonlinear_filter,
tesch23deep_nonliner_filter_multichannel, tesch23ssf\] by using the same
three microphone circular array of $`10\,\mathrm{cm}`$ diameter and
adhering to reverberation times between 0.2 s and 0.5 s. While retaining
the same randomized microphone placement and speaker height, we increase
the distance between array and speakers to a range of 1 m to 3 m whilst
maintaining a minimum distance of 1 m to the surrounding walls and 15 cm
among the speakers themselves. Building on our previous work
\[kienegger25wg_ssf\], we model the speaker trajectories as circular
motion with constant distance and height, allowing for a straightforward
parametrization and intuitive understanding of the spatial movement
patterns. To include directional changes and random acceleration
behavior, we choose the azimuthal motion model

```math
\begin{bmatrix}\theta_{t+1}\\
\dot{\theta}_{t+1}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&\hskip-5.69054pt\Delta T\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\theta_{t}\\
\dot{\theta}_{t}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}\Delta T^{2}/2\\
\Delta T\end{bmatrix}\eta_{t}\,, \tag{7}
```

referred to as cv (cv) or white acceleration model
\[rong03survey*target_tracking\]. The state vector $`\mathbf{x}*{t}`$ in
this linear state-space formulation consists of
$`\begin{bmatrix}\theta*{t},\dot{\theta}*{t}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}`$,
thus, azimuth orientation and instantaneous azimuth velocity,
respectively. The perturbation $`\eta*{t}`$ is a white, zero mean
Gaussian process of variance $`\sigma^{2}`$. Due to the wide range of
distances $`r`$ between speakers and microphone array, we use the
inter-frame velocity $`v*{t}`$ defined as
$`\frac{r}{\Delta T}(\theta*{t}-\theta*{t-1})`$ instead of the angular
displacement in \[kienegger25wg_ssf\] as a means to obtain a physically
meaningful parametrization for the motion model. Since the difference
$`\theta*{t}-\theta*{t-1}`$ is not affected by wrapping effects,
sequentially evaluating the linear state-space model in (7) retains its
Gaussianity. This makes the expected absolute velocity $`|v\_{t}|`$ at
distance $`r`$ tractable and yields the closed-form expression

```math
\mathbb{E}\hskip-1.0pt\big\{|v_{t}|\big|r\big\}=\Delta T\,r\sqrt{\frac{4t-3}{2\pi}}\sigma\,. \tag{8}
```

We will use this correspondence to determine the standard deviation
$`\sigma`$ of the motion perturbation $`\eta_{t}`$ in (7) to reach an
expected absolute velocity $`|v_{t}|`$ of
1.5 $`\frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s}}`$ at trajectory end, which
corresponds to medium walking speed
\[keller96walking*jogging_running_speed,
murtagh21outdoor_walking_speed\]. Due to the steady increase of the
expected value in (8), we will only use trajectories of 5 s length and
truncate the utterances accordingly. Figure 2 illustrates the resulting
distribution of $`|v*{t}|`$ as well as a selection of simulation
scenarios from the test set. The step-size $`\Delta T`$ for the temporal
discretization of the trajectories is aligned with the stft
parametrization, for which we use a square-root Hann window
\[shimauchi14hann_window\] of length $`32\,\mathrm{ms}`$ and
$`16\,\mathrm{ms}`$ hop-size. After spatialization, we add spherical
isotropic white noise according to
\[habets07isotropic_noise_generation\] with a snr (snr) between 20 dB
and 30 dB.

Recorded dataset To test generalizablity and robustness in real-world
scenarios, we also include recordings with human speakers for
evaluation. We use a microphone array matching the training
configuration in a room of
$`9.5\,\mathrm{m}\times 5.1\,\mathrm{m}\times 2.4\,\mathrm{m}`$ extent
and a reverberation time of approximately 0.35 s. Two male speakers are
instructed to simultaneously read out segments from the Rainbow Passage
\[fairbanks60rainbow_passage\] while moving in a circular trajectory.
The speakers start from opposite ends at roughly 1 m and 2 m distance
from the array, traverse to the other end of the room, and back during
the duration of their prompt. We conduct 3 recordings in total, ranging
from 10 s to 20 s. Thus, each scenario contains two speaker crossings
with velocities depending on distance to the array and length of the
prompt. To visualize the setup, we provide a video of two of the
recordings as supplementary material: https://youtu.be/aSKOSh5JZ3o

### 5.2 Algorithm implementation and optimization details

Spatially selective filter For sequential processing, recurrent nn are
particularly convenient as they store the temporal context in a compact
hidden state. Due to its purely recurrent nature combined with a
conceptually simple and lightweight architecture, we choose FT-jnf
\[tesch24ssf_journal\] as ssf, which has seen great popularity in a
variety of applications
\[wechsler24directional_filtering_directivity_control,
briegleb24constrained_vs_unconstained_filtering,
lentz24ftjnf_head_rotation\]. Following our previous work
\[kienegger25wg_ssf\], we slightly deviate from the implementation in
\[tesch24ssf_journal\] and modify the last B-LSTM layer to obtain a
causal ssf while retaining roughly the intended model size. The
modifications to compute a multi-channel mask according to our mimo
formulation in (6) only account for a negligible increase of less than
0.1 % in model parameters and 0.05 % in GMACs. We train FT-jnf in both
miso and mimo configurations for 300 epochs while adhering to the
original scheduler with an initial learning rate of $`10^{-3}`$ and a
joint time and frequency domain loss function \[tesch24ssf_journal\].
The latter is averaged over all channels in the mimo formulation. Based
on our previous findings of improved robustness to tracking inaccuracies
\[kienegger25wg_ssf\], we fine-tune the ssf for weakly guided tse in
both concatenative and self-steering pipelines for 25 additional epochs
with a reduced learning rate of $`10^{-4}`$. Instead of using iterative
approaches to avoid the non-parallelizable training of our ar
architecture \[andreev23iterative_autoregression,
pan24paris_autoregressive_separation\], we found that the small amount
of fine-tuning steps yielded the best performance while remaining
computationally feasible. Furthermore, we observed consistently improved
convergence when including mae (mae) as an auxiliary loss function
\[li23gcc-speaker\].

Particle filter We choose the generic bootstrap filter algorithm in
\[lehmann06pf_resampling_Neff\] as low-complexity tracking framework,
which introduces an additional conditional resampling step to counter
weight degeneracy problems \[doucet01pf_practice\]. Although providing a
zero gradient for frames affected by this resampling step, we found it
yielding better results during fine-tuning of the ssf than a
differentiable resampling algorithm
\[karkus18pf_differentiable_resampling\]. We use the output power of a
das beamformer to construct the likelihood
\[ward03basic_particle_filter\] and employ two different
state-transition densities to investigate the effect of mismatch in
motion dynamics. Thus, we include a simpler, angular rw (rw) model
\[traa13wrapped_kalman_filter\] in addition to the cv formulation in (7)
used in our synthetic dataset. We fix the number of particles $`N`$ to
50 and optimize the remaining model parameters with an exhaustive search
on the validation subset.

To perform a thorough assessment, we use our synthetic dataset for an
objective analysis and our recorded dataset for a subjective evaluation
conducted by human audio experts in a listening experiment. In both
cases, the tse methods are assessed regarding the perceptual speech
quality of the enhanced signal and the sdr.

Synthetic dataset The availability of ground truth directional
information allows the evaluation of the different ssf formulations in a
strongly guided fashion, eliminating the bias from upstream tracking
inaccuracies. Table 1 compares the original miso (1) and our mimo
adaption (2) of FT-jnf using the intrusive metrics pesq (pesq)
\[rix01pesq\] and sisdr (sisdr) \[roux19sisdr_half_baked\]. While we are
on par regarding speech quality, our proposed modifications result in a
lower sisdr score. This can be attributed to the additional challenge
from the delayed directional cue paired with the need of reconstructing
the anechoic inter-microphone propagation. Replacing the oracle doa with
estimated trajectories to evaluate the weakly guided methods, we
complement the analysis by including frame-wise ae (ae) to assess the
accuracy of the pf. In their original formulation, both pf versions
display great inconsistency in their tracking performance (3, 5).
Especially the mismatched rw motion model (3) yields a very poor
accuracy with 25 % of the doa estimations being more then 40° off. By
introducing our proposed ar interconnection, we can drastically improve
the performance of both pf versions (4, 6), as also demonstrated in the
example trajectories in Fig. 2. Table 1 and Fig. 4(a) show how our
method is able to translate the refined tracking into a superior speech
enhancement performance compared with the concatenative approach for
both perceptual quality and sdr criteria (4, 6). To investigate the
dependency of the two tse methods on the precision of the directional
cues, Fig. 3 shows the relative sisdr improvement in relation to
utterance-wise mae. By indicating a stronger tie between tracking and
enhancement metrics, it becomes apparent that our self-steering
framework relies more heavily on spatial information during the
extraction process as opposed to the concatenative method. During
fine-tuning, the latter gains robustness against the imprecise spatial
conditioning and learns to leverage temporal-spectral rather than
spatial information \[kienegger25wg_ssf\].

Recorded dataset Motivated by the unavailability of clean speech signals
in the recorded dataset, a listening test with human participants rating
the enhancement performance is conducted. We design the evaluation
similar to the preference test in \[tesch24ssf_journal\] by asking
questions of the form “Example 1 is preferable over example 2” regarding
speech quality and sdr. A total of 10 audio experts judge the
enhancement performance regarding all 6 speech signals in the 3
recordings for both pf versions, totaling to 12 test cases. The results
of the listening experiment in indicate a clear tendency towards our
proposed ar approach for both versions of the pf. A closer investigation
unveils that the most significant performance gain occurs for the
speaker with greater distance to the array, underlining the benefit of
utilizing spatial information in low snr scenarios.
