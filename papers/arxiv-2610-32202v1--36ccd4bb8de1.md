###### Abstract

This paper presents the ICASSP 2026 Radar Acoustic Speech
Enhancement (RASE) Grand Challenge, the first community challenge
dedicated to benchmarking speech enhancement from
millimeter-wave (mmWave) radar vibrometry. The challenge targets
realistic through-glass sensing scenarios where microphones are
ineffective or impractical. We introduce a curated dataset of paired
radar and clean speech recordings, standardized preprocessing, a
baseline system, and a secure Codabench-based evaluation framework with
unified objective metrics. The RASE Challenge establishes the first
public benchmark for radar-based speech enhancement and aims to foster
reproducible research in this emerging field.

## 1 Introduction

Speech enhancement is an ever more important area of research \[1, 2, 3,
4, 5, 6\]. Recently, through-barrier speech capture has been proposed
for scenarios where microphone systems fail or cannot be deployed
effectively. To this end, millimeter-wave (mmWave) radar vibrometry
offers a promising solution for non-contact signal acquisition, high
spatial resolution, and its ability to penetrate barriers and recover
speech-induced surface vibrations \[7\]. However, recovering
high-quality, wideband speech from radar measurements remains
challenging—radar-derived signals are often band-limited, suffer from
low SNR, and influenced by the mechanical properties of the reflecting
surface and sensing geometry \[8, 9\]. These factors introduce
distortions that differ from those encountered in conventional speech
enhancement research.

Despite growing interest in radar-based speech processing, prior work
has largely relied on bespoke hardware prototypes without standardized
datasets and benchmarks \[10, 11, 12\], limiting reproducibility and the
dissemination of research outcomes within the broader speech enhancement
community. Moreover, there is no widely-adopted benchmark that
incorporates realistic radar acquisitions with paired clean references
and standardized evaluation protocols, making it challenging to evaluate
and compare modeling approaches fairly.

The ICASSP 2026 Radar Acoustic Speech Enhancement (RASE) Grand Challenge
serves as an open platform to bridge these gaps and advance research
into radar-based speech processing by establishing the first community
benchmark for through-glass radar–speech enhancement. The challenge
introduces a dataset of paired radar–speech recordings, baseline
processing pipelines and modeling code, and a standardized evaluation
framework designed to stress-test generalization in the presence of
severe sensing distortions.

Specifically, this challenge provides a streamlined, publicly-available
dataset along with a fully reproducible baseline implementation to
promote transparent and fair benchmarking. The dataset comprises
pre-processed radar-to-audio signals that enable the speech processing
community to focus on developing speech enhancement models. The provided
baseline and codebase establish a well-founded and well-documented
reference system for participants to implement their models. Baseline
architecture and training details, based on WaveVoiceNet \[10\], are
provided in the publicly available implementation and documentation,¹¹ 1
https://github.com/RASE-Challenge/challenge_baseline2026 serving as a
reproducible reference for benchmarking. This challenge establishes a
unified benchmark that supports objective comparison and fosters
collective innovation in this research field.

## 2 Challenge Description and Evaluation

Unlike existing within-the-same-closure speech enhancement
approaches\[13, 14\], the RASE Challenge evaluates speech enhancement
methods for through-glass radar-based sensing. Here, our goal is to
develop models that reconstruct intelligible, full-bandwidth speech from
mmWave FMCW radar measurements that capture only speech-induced surface
vibrations. The task reflects realistic deployment scenarios in which
the radar is placed outside a glass-walled room and acquires vibration
amplitude via radar vibrometry signals.

The dataset comprises speech utterances played via a loudspeaker through
a glass panel under two scenarios: (a) direct diaphragm vibration, where
the radar measures vibrations from the loudspeaker diaphragm; and (b)
secondary-surface vibration, where a thin aluminium foil placed near the
loudspeaker vibrates indirectly, generating a weaker signal. The data
acqusition system was designed to operate across both sensing conditions
to generate enhanced speech suitable for human listening and downstream
tasks.

Both scenarios were recorded using a 77 GHz mmWave FMCW
radar (TI AWR2243BOOST) with a chirp rate of 8 kHz. In particular, for
FMCW radar vibrometry, speech is acquired by sensing vibration-induced
micro-displacements of a target surface encoded in the radar
instantaneous-frequency (IF) signal. Received IF radar signals were then
mapped into vibration-derived speech signals by estimating the IF phase
variation \[15, 16\]. The participants were then provided the
vibration-derived speech signals and the time-aligned clean speech
signals, both in standard audio playback .wav format at 8 kHz sampling
frequency.

The training and validation utterances were sourced from the LibriSpeech
corpus \[17\], ensuring sufficient linguistic and acoustic diversity for
model development. In contrast, the held-out test set is constructed
from speech drawn from an unannounced corpus. This cross-corpus
evaluation was intentionally designed to assess model generalizability
beyond the training distribution, preventing overfitting to
LibriSpeech-specific characteristics to reflect robustness.

The rules of this challenge were designed according to the principles
of: fairness, transparency, and focusing on the development of speech
enhancement models. To ensure fair comparison, all teams were required
to train their models from scratch (i.e., no pre-trained weights) using
only the provided training and development data; the use of external
data or augmentation is prohibited to avoid unfair advantages from
access to large corpora or pre-trained models. To prevent overfitting to
the test data, each team is limited to a maximum of three test-phase
submissions.

The held-out test data were not released and evaluation was conducted
through the Codabench platform \[18\] with a secure server-side
execution ensuring standardized and tamper-resistant benchmarking. To
support model development, participants were granted access to an
unrestricted development-trial portal and a validation phase with up to
thirty submissions for code verification before the test phase.
Templates and a single-line execution command further facilitate
reliable packaging and submission.

Submitted models were evaluated on a held-out test set comprising both
sensing conditions: direct diaphragm vibration and secondary-surface
vibration. For each utterance, we compute four complementary objective
measures capturing intelligibility, perceptual quality, and spectral
consistency. The challenge score is defined as

```math
\text{Avg. Score}=\frac{\text{PESQ}_{n}+\text{ESTOI}+\text{CS-MFCC}+\text{DNSMOS}_{n}}{4},
```

where $`\text{PESQ}_{n}\in[0,1]`$ and $`\text{DNSMOS}_{n}\in[0,1]`$
denote linearly normalized PESQ \[19\] and DNSMOS \[20\], respectively.
The extended short-time objective intelligibility (ESTOI) \[21\]
measures intelligibility and $`\text{CS-MFCC}\in[-1,1]`$ quantifies
spectral and phonetic consistency via cosine similarity between
Mel-frequency cepstral coefficient (MFCC). A single ranking criterion is
obtained by combining metrics using a 40% weighting for the simpler
direct-diaphragm scenario and 60% for the secondary-surface scenario.

## 3 Results and Conclusion

Table 1 summarises the performance of the submitted models evaluated on
the held-out test set. Based on the overall score, Team WHU IASP, ZJU
ShulinZhu, USTC NERCSLIP, Quantz, and Origin Research emerge as the top
five teams, with SSSL and Augm. List. Lab. ranked 6th and 7th,
respectively. These teams outperformed the WavVoiceNet baseline \[10\],
demonstrating their effectiveness. The RASE 2026 Challenge represents,
to our knowledge, the first community challenge for radar-acquired
speech enhancement, with the results establishing the first public
benchmark in this area. The accompanying database, baseline system, and
evaluation framework are released via our website²² 2
https://rase-challenge.github.io/RASE2026-Challenge/ and Codabench
portal³³ 3 https://www.codabench.org/competitions/10857/, enabling
standardized and reproducible benchmarking. We thank all participants
for their efforts and submissions.
