###### Abstract

This paper describes heavy-tailed extensions of a state-of-the-art
versatile blind source separation method called fast multichannel
nonnegative matrix factorization (FastMNMF) from a unified point of
view. The common way of deriving such an extension is to replace the
multivariate complex Gaussian distribution in the likelihood function
with its heavy-tailed generalization, e.g., the multivariate complex
Student’s $`t`$ and leptokurtic generalized Gaussian distributions, and
tailor-make the corresponding parameter optimization algorithm. Using a
wider class of heavy-tailed distributions called a Gaussian scale
mixture (GSM), i.e., a mixture of Gaussian distributions whose variances
are perturbed by positive random scalars called impulse variables, we
propose GSM-FastMNMF and develop an expectation-maximization algorithm
that works even when the probability density function of the impulse
variables have no analytical expressions. We show that existing
heavy-tailed FastMNMF extensions are instances of GSM-FastMNMF and
derive a new instance based on the generalized hyperbolic distribution
that include the normal-inverse Gaussian, Student’s $`t`$, and Gaussian
distributions as the special cases. Our experiments show that the
normal-inverse Gaussian FastMNMF outperforms the state-of-the-art
FastMNMF extensions and ILRMA model in speech enhancement and separation
in terms of the signal-to-distortion ratio.

## I Introduction

($`\alpha`$-FastMNMF), and the leptokurtic generalized Gaussian (GG)

The goal of blind source separation (BSS) is to estimate latent sources
from observed mixtures recorded by multiple microphones
\[[1](#bib.bib1)\]. In general, the audio signal is converted to a
time-frequency (TF) spectrogram obtained with short-time Fourier
transform (STFT). A vast majority of modern statistical BSS methods are
based on the local Gaussian model (LGM) that assumes the STFT
coefficients of each TF bin to follow a zero-mean multivariate complex
Gaussian distribution whose covariance matrix is given by the product of
the nonnegative power spectral density (PSD) and the positive
semidefinite spatial covariance matrix (SCM), where the SCM is a
full-rank matrix under echoic conditions \[[1](#bib.bib1)\].

A typical approach to BSS is to perform maximum-likelihood (ML)
estimation based on a unified probabilistic model of observed mixtures
consisting of source and spatial models representing the PSDs and SCMs
of sources, respectively \[[2](#bib.bib2)\]. Assuming the low-rankness
of source PSDs as is often the case in real sounds (e.g., music), the
source model has often been formulated as a LGM with nonnegative matrix
factorization (NMF), resulting in a versatile BSS method called
multichannel NMF (MNMF) \[[3](#bib.bib3), [4](#bib.bib4)\]. One way of
reducing the computational cost of MNMF stemming from a large number of
SCM inversions is to restrict the SCMs of all sources to rank-1
matrices, resulting in independent low-rank matrix analysis (ILRMA)
\[[5](#bib.bib5)\]. Another promising way is to restrict the source SCMs
to jointly-diagonalizable yet full-rank matrices \[[6](#bib.bib6),
[7](#bib.bib7), [8](#bib.bib8), [9](#bib.bib9)\], i.e., to represent the
SCM of each source as a conical sum of common rank-1 SCMs, resulting in
FastMNMF \[[8](#bib.bib8), [9](#bib.bib9)\]. Although FastMNMF (denoted
as $`\mathcal{N}`$-FastMNMF) outperforms ILRMA under echoic conditions,
the light-tailed LGM inherited from MNMF does not fit impulsive sounds
with a large dynamic range.

To improve the robustness of $`\mathcal{N}`$-FastMNMF against such
perturbations, local heavy-tailed models have often been used instead of
the LGM \[[10](#bib.bib10), [11](#bib.bib11), [12](#bib.bib12),
[13](#bib.bib13), [14](#bib.bib14), [15](#bib.bib15), [16](#bib.bib16),

represented as a Gaussian random vector whose scale is perturbed by a
positive random variable called an impulse
variable \[[20](#bib.bib20)\]. For ML estimation with
$`\alpha`$-FastMNMF, in contrast, the compound GSM representation is
used for addressing the non-closed-form probability density function
(PDF) of the $`\alpha`$-stable distribution \[[17](#bib.bib17)\], but
calls for a stochastic Metropolis-Hastings (MH) step for optimizing the
impulse variables \[[21](#bib.bib21)\]. Note that the GSM model has been
studied for audio source separation \[[22](#bib.bib22)\], speech
enhancement \[[23](#bib.bib23)\], and sparse signal representation
\[[24](#bib.bib24)\], but not within the FastMNMF framework.

In this paper, we propose a general form of heavy-tailed FastMNMF based
on the GSM representation (GSM-FastMNMF) that encompasses the
aforementioned heavy-tailed FastMNMF extensions and a new heavy-tailed
variant based on the generalized hyperbolic (GH)
distribution \[[25](#bib.bib25), [26](#bib.bib26)\] (GH-FastMNMF). A
noticeable instance of GH-FastMNMF is one based on the normal-inverse
Gaussian (NIG) distribution (NIG-FastMNMF), which was experimentally
proven to perform best for speech enhancement and separation. Recent
studies in \[[27](#bib.bib27)\] and \[[28](#bib.bib28)\] for instance
make use of NIG and GH innovations respectively within an autoregressive
model for time series modeling. The ML estimation is done through an
expectation-maximization (EM) framework as in \[[29](#bib.bib29),
[30](#bib.bib30)\] for NIG and \[[31](#bib.bib31), [32](#bib.bib32)\]
for GH model respectively.

For ML estimation with GSM-FastMNMF, we propose a general parameter
optimization algorithm based on the EM principle and called
multiplicative update variational expectation-maximization (MU-VEM).

This readily instantiates a closed-form parameter estimation algorithm
for the above-mentioned variants except for $`\alpha`$-FastMNMF, which
have been tailor-made independently. The key advantage of this technique
is that closed-form update rules might be obtained even when the impulse
variable law is unknown or analytically intractable.

## II Existing Variants of Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization

We review a versatile BSS method called MNMF \[[4](#bib.bib4)\] that
maximizes the multivariate complex Gaussian likelihood (denoted by
$`\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}`$) and its computationally-efficient special
case called FastMNMF \[[8](#bib.bib8)\]. We also introduce heavy-tailed
extensions of FastMNMF that maximize the multivariate complex Student’s
$`t`$, leptokurtic GG, and $`\alpha`$-stable likelihoods (denoted by
$`\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\nu}`$,
$`\mathcal{GG}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\beta}`$, and
$`\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}`$, respectively).

### II-A Problem Specification

Suppose that $`N`$ sources are recorded by $`M`$ microphones. Let
$`\mathbf{X}_{n}\triangleq\{\mathbf{x}_{nft}\}_{f,t=1}^{F,T}\in\mathbb{C}^{F\times T\times M}`$
be the multichannel complex spectrogram of source $`n\in\{1,\dots,N\}`$
(called a source image), where $`\triangleq`$ represents equality by
definition, $`F`$ and $`T`$ represent the number of frequency bins and
that of time frames, respectively. Let
$`\mathbf{X}\triangleq\{\mathbf{x}_{ft}\}_{f,t=1}^{F,T}\in\mathbb{C}^{F\times T\times M}`$
be that of the observed mixture. Assuming the additivity of sources in
the STFT domain, our goal is to estimate the source images
$`\{\mathbf{X}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}`$ from the mixture $`\mathbf{X}`$ such
that

|     |                                                                  |     |     |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{ft}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbf{x}_{nft}.`$ |     | (1) |

### II-B Probabilistic Formulation

The standard approach to BSS is based on the local Gaussian model
(LGM) \[[2](#bib.bib2)\]. Assuming both the independence of sources and
that of time-frequency bins, the source image
$`\mathbf{x}_{nft}\in\mathbb{C}^{M}`$ of source $`n`$ at frequency $`f`$
and time $`t`$ is assumed to independently follow a zero-mean
multivariate circularly-symmetric complex Gaussian distribution as
follows (see
Eq ([61](#Ax1.E61 "In 1st item ‣ Probability Density Functions ofGaussian Scale Mixture Variables ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
for the PDF):

|     |                                                                                                                                       |     |     |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{nft}\sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}\!\left({\lambda_{nft}\mathbf{G}_{nf}\triangleq\mathbf{Y}_{nft}}\right),`$ |     | (2) |

where $`\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}\!\left({\bm{\mu},\bm{\Sigma}}\right)`$
denotes the multivariate complex Gaussian distribution with a mean
vector $`\bm{\mu}`$ and a covariance matrix $`\bm{\Sigma}\succeq 0`$
($`\bm{\mu}`$ is omitted for brevity if $`\bm{\mu}=\bm{0}`$) ,
$`\lambda_{nft}\geq 0`$ is the power spectral density (PSD) of the
source $`n`$ at frequency $`f`$ and time $`t`$ denoted $`s_{nft}`$, and
$`\mathbf{G}_{nf}\succeq\bm{0}`$ is the positive semidefinite spatial
covariance matrix (SCM) of source $`n`$ at frequency $`f`$. Note that
$`\succeq`$ stands for the set of positive semidefinite matrices. Let
$`\bm{\Lambda}\triangleq\{\lambda_{nft}\}_{n,f,t=1}^{N,F,T}`$ and
$`\mathbf{G}\triangleq\{\mathbf{G}_{nf}\}_{n,f=1}^{N,F}`$ be the sets of
the source PSDs and SCMs, respectively.

Using the law stability by linear combination of independent Gaussian
vectors,
Eqs. ([1](#S2.E1 "In II-A Problem Specification ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
and
([2](#S2.E2 "In II-B Probabilistic Formulation ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
give the mixture $`\mathbf{x}_{ft}`$ distributed as follows:

|     |                                                                                                                                                   |     |     |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{ft}\sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}\!\left({\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{nft}\mathbf{G}_{nf}\triangleq\mathbf{Y}_{ft}}\right),`$ |     | (3) |

where $`\lambda_{nft}`$ and $`\mathbf{G}_{nf}`$ are represented by
source and spatial models, respectively, as described in Section
[II-C](#S2.SS3 "II-C Source and Spatial Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation").
Given the mixture $`\mathbf{X}`$ as observed data, we aim to estimate
$`\bm{\Lambda}`$ and $`\mathbf{G}`$ that maximize the likelihood for
$`\mathbf{X}`$ given by
Eq. ([3](#S2.E3 "In II-B Probabilistic Formulation ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation")).

BSS is implemented with a Wiener filter that computes the posterior
distribution of $`\mathbf{x}_{nft}`$ given $`\mathbf{x}_{ft}`$ as
follows:

|     |     |                                                                                                                                                                                          |     |     |
| --- | --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     |     | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{nft}\mid\mathbf{x}_{ft}`$                                                                                                                                     |     |     |
|     |     | $`\displaystyle\sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}\!\left({\mathbf{Y}_{nft}\mathbf{Y}_{ft}^{-1}\mathbf{x}_{ft},\mathbf{Y}_{nft}-\mathbf{Y}_{nft}\mathbf{Y}_{ft}^{-1}\mathbf{Y}_{nft}}\right).`$ |     | (4) |

The maximum-a-posteriori (MAP) estimate of the source image
$`\mathbf{x}_{nft}`$ is thus given by
$`\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{x}_{nft}\mid\mathbf{x}_{ft}]=\mathbf{Y}_{nft}\mathbf{Y}_{ft}^{-1}\mathbf{x}_{ft}`$.

### II-C Source and Spatial Models

MNMF \[[4](#bib.bib4)\] and its constrained versions such as
ILRMA \[[5](#bib.bib5)\] and FastMNMF \[[8](#bib.bib8)\] are based on
the low-rank source model that factorizes the PSDs of each source $`n`$
as

|     |                                                              |     |     |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\lambda_{nft}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}w_{nkf}h_{nkt},`$ |     | (5) |

where $`K`$ is the number of bases, $`w_{nkf}\geq 0`$ is the magnitude
of basis $`k`$ of source $`n`$ at frequency $`f`$, and $`h_{nkt}\geq 0`$
is the activation of basis $`k`$ of source $`n`$ at time $`t`$. Let
$`\mathbf{W}\triangleq\{w_{nkf}\}_{n,k,f=1}^{N,K,F}`$ and
$`\mathbf{H}\triangleq\{h_{nkt}\}_{n,k,t=1}^{N,K,T}`$ be the sets of the
bases and activations, respectively. For ILRMA \[[5](#bib.bib5)\],
MNMF \[[4](#bib.bib4)\], and FastMNMF \[[8](#bib.bib8)\], the rank-1
spatial model, the unconstrained full-rank spatial model, and the
jointly-diagonalizable full-rank spatial model have been proposed,
respectively.

#### II-C1 Rank-1 Spatial Model

Ideally, the sound propagation process in a less-echoic environment is
represented as a time-invariant linear system as follows:

|     |                                   |                                           |     |     |
| --- | --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{nft}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbf{a}_{nf}s_{nft},`$ |     | (6) |

where $`\mathbf{a}_{nf}\in\mathbb{C}^{M}`$ is the steering vector of
source $`n`$ at frequency $`f`$. Eq.
([6](#S2.E6 "In II-C1 Rank-1 Spatial Model ‣ II-C Source and Spatial Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
gives
Eqs. ([2](#S2.E2 "In II-B Probabilistic Formulation ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
and
([3](#S2.E3 "In II-B Probabilistic Formulation ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation")),
where
$`\mathbf{G}_{nf}\triangleq\mathbf{a}_{nf}\mathbf{a}_{nf}^{\mathsf{H}}\succeq\bm{0}`$
is the rank-1 SCM of source $`n`$ at frequency $`f`$ and ^(H) denotes
the conjugate transpose.

ILRMA \[[5](#bib.bib5)\] is based on the low-rank source model given by
Eq. ([5](#S2.E5 "In II-C Source and Spatial Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
and the rank-1 spatial model given by
Eq. ([3](#S2.E3 "In II-B Probabilistic Formulation ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
with $`\mathbf{G}_{nf}=\mathbf{a}_{nf}\mathbf{a}_{nf}^{\mathsf{H}}`$. It
is available only under a determined condition ($`M=N`$) to avoid the
rank deficiency of the SCM $`\mathbf{Y}_{ft}`$ for the observed mixture
$`\mathbf{x}_{ft}`$.

#### II-C2 Full-Rank Spatial Model

Because
Eq. ([6](#S2.E6 "In II-C1 Rank-1 Spatial Model ‣ II-C Source and Spatial Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
does not hold when the reverberation is longer than the window size of
STFT, one may want to allow $`\mathbf{G}_{nf}`$ to be a full-rank
matrix \[[2](#bib.bib2)\]. Note that
Eqs. ([2](#S2.E2 "In II-B Probabilistic Formulation ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
and
([3](#S2.E3 "In II-B Probabilistic Formulation ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
are not changed in form.

MNMF \[[4](#bib.bib4)\] is based on the low-rank source model given by
Eq. ([5](#S2.E5 "In II-C Source and Spatial Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
and the full-rank spatial model given by
Eq. ([3](#S2.E3 "In II-B Probabilistic Formulation ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
with unconstrained $`\mathbf{G}_{nf}`$. Unlike ILRMA, it can be used
even under an underdetermined condition ($`M<N`$) in theory. Because
MNMF has a considerably larger number of spatial parameters than ILRMA
($`NFM(M+1)/2\gg NFM`$), MNMF tends to easily get stuck in a bad local
optimum.

#### II-C3 Jointly-Diagonalizable Spatial Model

An effective way of reducing the complexity of MNMF is to assume
$`\{\mathbf{G}_{nf}\}_{n=1}^{N}`$ to be jointly diagonalizable with a
non-singular matrix $`\mathbf{Q}_{f}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times M}`$ called a
diagonalizer as follows \[[6](#bib.bib6), [7](#bib.bib7),
[9](#bib.bib9), [8](#bib.bib8)\]:

|     |                                                                                                                                                              |     |     |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\forall n,f,\ \mathbf{G}_{nf}=\mathbf{Q}_{f}^{-1}\mathrm{Diag}(\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{nf})\mathbf{Q}_{f}^{-\mathsf{H}}\quad\mbox{(version 1)},`$ |     | (7) |

where
$`\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{nf}\triangleq[\tilde{g}_{nf1},\ldots,\tilde{g}_{nfM}]^{\mathsf{T}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{M}`$
is a nonnegative vector of source $`n`$ at frequency $`f`$,
$`\mathrm{Diag}(\mathbf{v})`$ denotes a diagonal matrix whose diagonal
elements are given by a vector $`\mathbf{v}`$, and ^(T) denotes the
transpose. Because
$`\mathbf{Q}_{f}\triangleq[\mathbf{q}_{f1},\ldots,\mathbf{q}_{fM}]^{\mathsf{H}}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times M}`$
acts as a demixing matrix consisting of $`M`$ demixing filters
$`\{\mathbf{q}_{fm}\}_{m=1}^{M}`$, i.e.,
$`\mathbf{Q}_{f}^{-1}\triangleq[\mathbf{u}_{f1},\cdots,\mathbf{u}_{fM}]`$
acts as a mixing matrix consisting of $`M`$ steering vectors
$`\{\mathbf{u}_{fm}\}_{m=1}^{M}`$ corresponding to different directions,
$`\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{nf}`$ is considered to indicate the weights of the
$`M`$ directions for source $`n`$. This naturally calls for sharing the
direction weights over all frequencies as follows:

|     |                                                                                                                                                             |     |     |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\forall n,f,\ \mathbf{G}_{nf}=\mathbf{Q}_{f}^{-1}\mathrm{Diag}(\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{n})\mathbf{Q}_{f}^{-\mathsf{H}}\quad\mbox{(version 2)},`$ |     | (8) |

where
$`\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{n}\triangleq[\tilde{g}_{n1},\ldots,\tilde{g}_{nM}]^{\mathsf{T}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{M}`$
is a frequency-independent nonnegative vector of source $`n`$
\[[8](#bib.bib8)\]. For better performance, we focus on this
weight-shared version and define its diagonalizer set as
$`\mathbf{Q}\triangleq\{\mathbf{Q}_{f}\}_{f=1}^{F}`$. Note that the
rank-1 spatial model is obtained when $`M=N`$ and
$`\tilde{\mathbf{G}}\triangleq[\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{N}]^{\mathsf{T}}=\mathbf{I}`$,
where $`\mathbf{I}`$ denotes an identity matrix of size $`M`$.

FastMNMF2 \[[8](#bib.bib8)\] (simply called FastMNMF in this paper) is
obtained by integrating the low-rank source model given by
Eq. ([5](#S2.E5 "In II-C Source and Spatial Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
and the jointly-diagonalizable full-rank spatial model given by
Eq. ([3](#S2.E3 "In II-B Probabilistic Formulation ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
with
Eq. ([8](#S2.E8 "In II-C3 Jointly-Diagonalizable Spatial Model ‣ II-C Source and Spatial Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation")).
Since the latent source image $`\mathbf{x}_{nft}`$ and the observed
mixture $`\mathbf{x}_{ft}`$ are Gaussian distributed, the projected
source $`\mathbf{z}_{nft}\triangleq\mathbf{Q}_{f}\mathbf{x}_{nft}`$ and
the projected mixture
$`\mathbf{z}_{ft}\triangleq\mathbf{Q}_{f}\mathbf{x}_{ft}`$ are also
Gaussian distributed as follows:

|     |                                   |                                                                                                                                                                  |     |      |
| --- | --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{z}_{nft}`$ | $`\displaystyle\sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}\!\left({\lambda_{nft}\mathrm{Diag}(\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{n})\triangleq\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{nft}}\right),`$              |     | (9)  |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{z}_{ft}`$  | $`\displaystyle\sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}\!\left({\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{nft}\mathrm{Diag}(\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{n})\triangleq\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{ft}}\right),`$ |     | (10) |

MNMF for $`\mathbf{z}_{ft}`$ is thus a particular case of nonnegative
tensor factorization (NTF) that assumes the elements of
$`\mathbf{z}_{ft}`$ to be independent, whereas those of
$`\mathbf{x}_{ft}`$ are correlated. (see Fig. 2 in \[[8](#bib.bib8)\]).

### II-D Gaussian and Heavy-Tailed Models

We explain the probabilistic model of FastMNMF (called
$`\mathcal{N}`$-FastMNMF \[[8](#bib.bib8)\]) and those of the Student’s
$`t`$ and leptokurtic GG extensions of $`\mathcal{N}`$-FastMNMF that can
handle more impulsive sources. Such an extension is achieved by
replacing the Gaussian distribution with a surrogate distribution in
Eq. ([10](#S2.E10 "In II-C3 Jointly-Diagonalizable Spatial Model ‣ II-C Source and Spatial Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation")).
Let
$`\bm{\Theta}\triangleq\{\mathbf{W},\mathbf{H},\mathbf{Q},\tilde{\mathbf{G}}\}`$
be a set of model parameters.

#### II-D1 Gaussian FastMNMF

Using the change-of-variable principle for
$`\mathbf{z}_{ft}=\mathbf{Q}_{f}\mathbf{x}_{ft}`$, the log-likelihood
(LL) of the parameters $`\bm{\Theta}`$ for the observed mixture
$`\mathbf{X}`$ is given by

|     |                                                       |                                                                                        |                                                 |     |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | --- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\!\!\log p_{\bm{\Theta}}(\mathbf{X})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum*{f,t=1}^{F,T}\log p(\mathbf{z}*{ft})+\sum\_{f,t=1}^{F,T}\log\left | \frac{d\mathbf{z}_{ft}}{d\mathbf{x}_{ft}}\right | `$  |     |      |
|     |                                                       | $`\displaystyle=\sum*{f,t=1}^{F,T}\log p(\mathbf{z}*{ft})+T\sum\_{f=1}^{F}\log\left    | \mathbf{Q}_{f}\mathbf{Q}_{f}^{\mathsf{H}}\right | ,`$ |     | (11) |

where $`\log p(\mathbf{z}_{ft})`$ is given by

|     |                                                                                                                                                 |     |      |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\log p(\mathbf{z}_{ft})\overset{c}{=}-\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{\tilde{z}_{ftm}}{\tilde{y}_{ftm}}-\sum_{m=1}^{M}\log\tilde{y}_{ftm},`$ |     | (12) |

where $`\overset{c}{=}`$ denotes equality up to an additive constant and

|     |                                  |                                                                                                                      |                |            |
| --- | -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------- | ---------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\tilde{z}_{ftm}`$ | $`\displaystyle\triangleq\left                                                                                       | z\_{ftm}\right | ^{2}=\left | \mathbf{q}_{fm}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{x}_{ft}\right | ^{2},`$ |     | (13) |
|     | $`\displaystyle\tilde{y}_{ftm}`$ | $`\displaystyle\triangleq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{nft}\tilde{g}_{nm}=\sum_{n,k=1}^{N,K}w_{nkf}h_{nkt}\tilde{g}_{nm}.`$ |                | (14)       |

#### II-D2 Student’s $`t`$ FastMNMF

$`t`$-FastMNMF \[[11](#bib.bib11)\] with a degree of freedom $`\nu>0`$
controlling the tail lightness reduces to $`\mathcal{N}`$-FastMNMF
\[[8](#bib.bib8)\] when $`\nu\rightarrow\infty`$, and reduces to
$`t`$-R1-FastMNMF when the rank-1 spatial model is used. More
specifically,
Eq. ([10](#S2.E10 "In II-C3 Jointly-Diagonalizable Spatial Model ‣ II-C Source and Spatial Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
is replaced with

|     |                                                                                                             |     |      |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{z}_{ft}\sim\mathcal{T}^{\nu}_{\mathbb{C}}\!\left({\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{ft}}\right),`$ |     | (15) |

where $`\mathcal{T}^{\nu}_{\mathbb{C}}(\bm{\Sigma})`$ denotes a
zero-mean multivariate complex $`t`$ distribution with a degree of
freedom $`\nu>0`$ and a scale matrix $`\bm{\Sigma}\succeq\bm{0}`$ (the
PDF is given by
Eq. ([62](#Ax1.E62 "In 2nd item ‣ Probability Density Functions ofGaussian Scale Mixture Variables ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))).
The $`t`$ distribution approaches the Gaussian distribution as
$`\nu\rightarrow\infty`$. For reference, the real parts of univariate
complex $`t`$ distributions are plotted in
Fig. [2](#S2.F2 "Fig. 2 ‣ II-D4 𝛼-Stable FastMNMF ‣ II-D Gaussian and Heavy-Tailed Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation").
The LL of the parameters $`\bm{\Theta}`$ is the same in form as
Eq. ([11](#S2.E11 "In II-D1 Gaussian FastMNMF ‣ II-D Gaussian and Heavy-Tailed Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation")),
where $`\log p(\mathbf{z}_{ft})`$ is given by

|     |     |                                                                                                                                                                                                  |     |      |
| --- | --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | ---- |
|     |     | $`\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\log p(\mathbf{z}_{ft})`$                                                                                                                                                 |     |      |
|     |     | $`\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\overset{c}{=}-\left(\frac{\nu}{2}+M\right)\log\!\left(1+\frac{2}{\nu}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{\tilde{z}_{ftm}}{\tilde{y}_{ftm}}\right)-\sum_{m=1}^{M}\log\tilde{y}_{ftm}.\!`$ |     | (16) |

#### II-D3 Leptokurtic Generalized Gaussian FastMNMF

Leptokurtic GG-FastMNMF with a shape parameter $`\beta\in(0,2]`$
controlling the tail lightness reduces to $`\mathcal{N}`$-FastMNMF
\[[8](#bib.bib8)\] when $`\beta=2`$, and reduces to leptokurtic
GG-R1-FastMNMF with $`\beta\in(0,2]`$ when the rank-1 spatial model is
used. Note that leptokurtic GG-FastMNMF with $`\beta\in(0,2]`$ has not
been investigated in the literature, whereas platykurtic
GG-FastMNMF \[[13](#bib.bib13)\] and its ILRMA version
 \[[14](#bib.bib14)\] with $`\beta\in[2,4)`$ have already been proposed.
More specifically,
Eq. ([10](#S2.E10 "In II-C3 Jointly-Diagonalizable Spatial Model ‣ II-C Source and Spatial Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
is replaced with

|     |                                                                                                                |     |      |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{z}_{ft}\sim\mathcal{GG}^{\beta}_{\mathbb{C}}\!\left({\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{ft}}\right),`$ |     | (17) |

where $`\mathcal{GG}^{\beta}_{\mathbb{C}}\!\left({\bm{\Sigma}}\right)`$
denotes a zero-mean leptokurtic multivariate complex GG distribution
\[[33](#bib.bib33)\] with a shape parameter $`\beta\in(0,2]`$ and a
scale matrix $`\bm{\Sigma}\succeq\bm{0}`$ (the PDF is given by
Eq. ([63](#Ax1.E63 "In 3rd item ‣ Probability Density Functions ofGaussian Scale Mixture Variables ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))).
The GG distribution with $`\beta=2`$ reduces to the Gaussian
distribution. For reference, the real parts of leptokurtic univariate
complex GG distributions are plotted in
Fig. [2](#S2.F2 "Fig. 2 ‣ II-D4 𝛼-Stable FastMNMF ‣ II-D Gaussian and Heavy-Tailed Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation").
The LL of the parameters $`\bm{\Theta}`$ is the same in form as
Eq. ([11](#S2.E11 "In II-D1 Gaussian FastMNMF ‣ II-D Gaussian and Heavy-Tailed Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation")),
where $`\log p(\mathbf{z}_{ft})`$ is given by

|     |     |                                                                                                                                                                                |     |      |
| --- | --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | ---- |
|     |     | $`\displaystyle\log p(\mathbf{z}_{ft})\overset{c}{=}-\left(\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{\tilde{z}_{ftm}}{\tilde{y}_{ftm}}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}-\sum_{m=1}^{M}\log\tilde{y}_{ftm}.`$ |     | (18) |

#### II-D4 $`\alpha`$-Stable FastMNMF

$`\alpha`$-FastMNMF \[[17](#bib.bib17)\] with a characteristic exponent
$`\alpha\in[0,2)`$ controlling the tail lightness reduces to
$`\mathcal{N}`$-FastMNMF \[[8](#bib.bib8)\] when $`\alpha=2`$, and
reduces to $`\alpha`$-R1-FastMNMF \[[18](#bib.bib18)\] when the rank-1
spatial model is used. More specifically,
Eq. ([10](#S2.E10 "In II-C3 Jointly-Diagonalizable Spatial Model ‣ II-C Source and Spatial Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
is replaced with

|     |                                                                                                                |     |      |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{z}_{ft}\sim\mathcal{S}^{\alpha}_{\mathbb{C}}\!\left({\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{ft}}\right),`$ |     | (19) |

where $`\mathcal{S}^{\alpha}_{\mathbb{C}}(\bm{\Sigma})`$ denotes a
zero-mean non-skewed multivariate elliptically complex $`\alpha`$-stable
distribution with a characteristic exponent $`\alpha>0`$ and a scale
matrix $`\bm{\Sigma}\succeq\bm{0}`$ \[[34](#bib.bib34)\]. For reference,
the real parts of univariate complex $`\alpha`$-stable distributions are
plotted in
Fig. [2](#S2.F2 "Fig. 2 ‣ II-D4 𝛼-Stable FastMNMF ‣ II-D Gaussian and Heavy-Tailed Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation").
The LL of the parameters $`\bm{\Theta}`$ is the same in form as
Eq. ([11](#S2.E11 "In II-D1 Gaussian FastMNMF ‣ II-D Gaussian and Heavy-Tailed Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation")),
where in general $`\log p(\mathbf{z}_{ft})`$ cannot be expressed in a
closed form except for $`\alpha\in\{\frac{1}{2},1,2\}`$, making ML
estimation of $`\bm{\Theta}`$ challenging. To circumvent this problem,
one can rewrite
Eq. ([19](#S2.E19 "In II-D4 𝛼-Stable FastMNMF ‣ II-D Gaussian and Heavy-Tailed Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
as an analytically-tractable GSM representation (cf.
Section [III](#S3 "III Gaussian Scale Mixture Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation")),
where the auxiliary impulse variable needs to be marginalized out with a
computationally-expensive MH algorithm \[[17](#bib.bib17)\]. Note that
$`\alpha`$-FastMNMF is not dealt with in this paper because
deterministic parameter update rules cannot be obtained.

## III Gaussian Scale Mixture Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization

We propose GSM-FastMNMF, a general form of heavy-tailed FastMNMF,
including $`\mathcal{N}`$-FastMNMF \[[8](#bib.bib8)\]
(Section [II-D1](#S2.SS4.SSS1 "II-D1 Gaussian FastMNMF ‣ II-D Gaussian and Heavy-Tailed Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation")),
its heavy-tailed extensions such as $`t`$-FastMNMF \[[11](#bib.bib11)\]
(Section [II-D2](#S2.SS4.SSS2 "II-D2 Student’s 𝑡 FastMNMF ‣ II-D Gaussian and Heavy-Tailed Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation")),
leptokurtic GG-FastMNMF
(Section [II-D3](#S2.SS4.SSS3 "II-D3 Leptokurtic Generalized Gaussian FastMNMF ‣ II-D Gaussian and Heavy-Tailed Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation")),
and $`\alpha`$-FastMNMF \[[17](#bib.bib17)\]
(Section [II-D4](#S2.SS4.SSS4 "II-D4 𝛼-Stable FastMNMF ‣ II-D Gaussian and Heavy-Tailed Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
and the rank-1 counterparts such as $`t`$-R1-FastMNMF, leptokurtic
GG-R1-FastMNMF, and $`\alpha`$-R1-FastMNMF \[[18](#bib.bib18)\]. The
closed-form deterministic parameter update rules have been tailor-made
independently for the existing variants except for $`\alpha`$-FastMNMF
based on the stochastic parameter update rules with the MH sampler.

We explain a probabilistic model of GSM-FastMNMF and derive its
parameter estimation algorithm. As a concrete example of GSM-FastMNMF,
we then instantiate GH-FastMNMF based on the generalized hyperbolic (GH)
distribution as a wide family of heavy-tailed FastMNMF including
$`\mathcal{N}`$-FastMNMF \[[8](#bib.bib8)\] and
$`t`$-FastMNMF \[[11](#bib.bib11)\]. As a well-performing special case
of GH-FastMNMF, we focus on NIG-FastMNMF based on the normalized inverse
Gaussian (NIG) distribution.

### III-A Probabilistic Formulation

GSM-FastMNMF is obtained by extending the multivariate complex Gaussian
distributions used in
Eqs. ([9](#S2.E9 "In II-C3 Jointly-Diagonalizable Spatial Model ‣ II-C Source and Spatial Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
and
([10](#S2.E10 "In II-C3 Jointly-Diagonalizable Spatial Model ‣ II-C Source and Spatial Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
to multivariate complex GSMs represented as compound probability
distributions as follows:

|     |                                                            |                                                                                                  |     |      |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\phi_{ft}`$                                 | $`\displaystyle\sim p(\phi_{ft}),`$                                                              |     | (20) |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{z}_{nft}\mid\bm{\Theta},\phi_{ft}`$ | $`\displaystyle\sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}\!\left({\phi_{ft}\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{nft}}\right),`$ |     | (21) |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{z}_{ft}\mid\bm{\Theta},\phi_{ft}`$  | $`\displaystyle\sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}\!\left({\phi_{ft}\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{ft}}\right),`$  |     | (22) |

with $`\phi_{ft}>0`$ is an auxiliary nonnegative random variable called
an impulse variable that stochastically perturbs the covariance matrices
$`\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{nft}`$ and $`\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{ft}`$ according
to some prior distribution $`p(\phi_{ft})`$. The LL of the parameters
$`\bm{\Theta}`$ is given by

|     |                                                                                                                            |     |      |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\log p_{\bm{\Theta}}(\mathbf{X})=\log\int p_{\bm{\Theta}}(\mathbf{X}\mid\bm{\Phi})p(\bm{\Phi})d\bm{\Phi},`$ |     | (23) |

where $`\bm{\Phi}\triangleq\{\phi_{ft}\}_{f,t=1}^{F,T}`$ and
$`p_{\bm{\Theta}}(\mathbf{X}\mid\bm{\Phi})`$ is the same in form as
Eq. ([11](#S2.E11 "In II-D1 Gaussian FastMNMF ‣ II-D Gaussian and Heavy-Tailed Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
except that the Gaussian density $`p(\mathbf{z}_{ft})`$ is replaced with
the conditional Gaussian density $`p(\mathbf{z}_{ft}\mid\phi_{ft})`$
given by

|     |                                                                                                                                                                                |     |      |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\log p(\mathbf{z}_{ft}\mid\phi_{ft})\overset{c}{=}-\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{\tilde{z}_{ftm}}{\phi_{ft}\tilde{y}_{ftm}}-\sum_{m=1}^{M}\log\phi_{ft}\tilde{y}_{ftm}.`$ |     | (24) |

Note that several existing heavy-tailed extensions of FastMNMF are
obtained by marginalizing $`\bm{\Phi}`$ out with the mixing distribution
$`p(\bm{\Phi})`$ according to
Eq. ([23](#S3.E23 "In III-A Probabilistic Formulation ‣ III Gaussian Scale Mixture Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation")).

### III-B Multiplicative Update Variational Expectation-Maximization Algorithm

We describe in that Section how parameters $`\bm{\Theta}`$ are
estimated. Since the LL of $`\bm{\Theta}`$,
$`\log p_{\bm{\Theta}}(\mathbf{X})`$, given by
Eq. ([23](#S3.E23 "In III-A Probabilistic Formulation ‣ III Gaussian Scale Mixture Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
is hard to directly maximize with respect to $`\bm{\Theta}`$, we use a
multiplicative update variational expectation-maximization (MU-VEM)
principle, i.e., derive a variational lower bound
$`\mathcal{L}(\bm{\Theta},q(\bm{\Phi}),\bm{\Psi})`$ of
$`\log p_{\bm{\Theta}}(\mathbf{X})`$ using an arbitrary distribution
$`q(\bm{\Phi})`$ of the latent impulse variables $`\bm{\Phi}`$ and a set
of auxiliary variables $`\bm{\Psi}`$ (Section
[III-B1](#S3.SS2.SSS1 "III-B1 Lower Bound ‣ III-B Multiplicative Update Variational Expectation-Maximization Algorithm ‣ III Gaussian Scale Mixture Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
and iteratively update $`q(\bm{\Phi})`$ and $`\bm{\Psi}`$ in the E-step
(Section
[III-B2](#S3.SS2.SSS2 "III-B2 E-Step ‣ III-B Multiplicative Update Variational Expectation-Maximization Algorithm ‣ III Gaussian Scale Mixture Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
and $`\bm{\Theta}`$ in the M-step (Section
[III-B3](#S3.SS2.SSS3 "III-B3 M-Step ‣ III-B Multiplicative Update Variational Expectation-Maximization Algorithm ‣ III Gaussian Scale Mixture Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
such that $`\mathcal{L}(\bm{\Theta},q(\bm{\Phi}),\bm{\Psi})`$
monotonically non-decreases.

#### III-B1 Lower Bound

Let $`q(\bm{\Phi})\triangleq\prod_{f,t=1}^{F,T}q(\phi_{ft})`$ be an
arbitrary distribution on the latent impulse variables $`\bm{\Phi}`$.
Using Jensen’s inequality,
Eq. ([23](#S3.E23 "In III-A Probabilistic Formulation ‣ III Gaussian Scale Mixture Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
can be lower bounded as follows:

|     |                                                   |                                                                                                                                           |                                                 |      |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ---- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\log p_{\bm{\Theta}}(\mathbf{X})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{f,t=1}^{F,T}\log\int p_{\bm{\Theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{ft}\mid\phi_{ft})p(\phi_{ft})d\phi_{ft}`$                          |                                                 |      |
|     |                                                   | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{f,t}\log\int q(\phi_{ft})\frac{p_{\bm{\Theta}}(\mathbf{x}_{ft}\mid\phi_{ft})p(\phi_{ft})}{q(\phi_{ft})}d\phi_{ft}`$ |                                                 |      |
|     |                                                   | $`\displaystyle\geq\sum*{f,t}\Big(\mathbb{E}*{q(\phi*{ft})}\left[\log p*{\bm{\Theta}}(\mathbf{z}_{ft}\mid\phi_{ft})\right]+\left          | \mathbf{Q}_{f}\mathbf{Q}_{f}^{\mathsf{H}}\right | `$   |     |     |
|     |                                                   | $`\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad-\mathrm{KL}\left[q(\phi_{ft})\parallel p(\phi_{ft})\right]\Big)`$                                     |                                                 |      |
|     |                                                   | $`\displaystyle\triangleq\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(\bm{\Theta},q(\bm{\Phi})),`$                                                                |                                                 | (25) |

where $`\mathrm{KL}(q\parallel p)`$ denotes the Kullback-Leibler (KL)
divergence from $`q`$ to $`p`$ \[[35](#bib.bib35)\], and
$`p_{\bm{\Theta}}(\mathbf{z}_{ft}\mid\phi_{ft})`$ is given by
Eq. ([24](#S3.E24 "In III-A Probabilistic Formulation ‣ III Gaussian Scale Mixture Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation")).
The equality condition that maximizes
$`\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(\bm{\Theta},q(\bm{\Phi}))`$ is given by

|     |                                                                                                 |     |      |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle q(\phi_{ft})=p(\phi_{ft}\mid\mathbf{x}_{ft})=p(\phi_{ft}\mid\mathbf{z}_{ft}).`$ |     | (26) |

Let $`\bm{\Psi}\triangleq\{\bm{\Pi},\bm{\Omega}\}`$ be a set of
arbitrary nonnegative variables, where
$`\bm{\Pi}\triangleq\{\pi_{ftmnk}\}_{f,t,m,n,k=1}^{F,T,M,N,K}`$
satisfying $`\sum_{n,k=1}^{N,K}\pi_{ftmnk}=1`$ and
$`\bm{\Omega}\triangleq\{\omega_{ftm}\}_{f,t,m=1}^{F,T,M}`$. Since
$`\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(\bm{\Theta},q(\bm{\Phi}))`$ is still hard to
maximize with respect to $`\bm{\Theta}`$, it is further lower bounded as
in NMF based on the Itakura-Saito (IS) divergence\[[36](#bib.bib36)\] as
follows:

with respect to $`\bm{\Psi}`$ equal to zero, the equality condition that

#### III-B2 E-Step

Given the current estimate of $`\bm{\Theta}`$, we update
$`q(\bm{\Phi})`$ using

such that the lower bound
$`\mathcal{L}(\bm{\Theta},q(\bm{\Phi}),\bm{\Psi})`$ given by

that the optimal estimate of $`q(\phi_{ft})`$ given by
Eq. ([26](#S3.E26 "In III-B1 Lower Bound ‣ III-B Multiplicative Update Variational Expectation-Maximization Algorithm ‣ III Gaussian Scale Mixture Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))

$`\tilde{\phi}_{ft}^{-1}\triangleq\mathbb{E}_{p(\phi_{ft}\mid\mathbf{z}_{ft})}\big[\phi_{ft}^{-1}\big]`$
is thus a key for deriving closed-form update rules. Let
$`\tilde{\bm{\Phi}}\triangleq\{\tilde{\phi}_{ft}\}_{f,t=1}^{F,T}`$ be a
set of the posterior expectations. As derived in the Appendix, we have

|     |                                                                                                                                                     |     |      |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\mathbf{z}_{ft}^{\mathsf{H}}}\log p(\mathbf{z}_{ft})=-2\tilde{\phi}_{ft}^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{ft}^{-1}\mathbf{z}_{ft}`$ |     | (30) |

where $`\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{ft}`$ is defined in

Note that even if the posterior density
$`p(\phi_{ft}\mid\mathbf{z}_{ft})`$ is intractable,
$`\tilde{\phi}_{ft}^{-1}`$ is tractable if the log-marginal density
$`\log p(\mathbf{z}_{ft})`$ is differentiable with respect to
$`\mathbf{z}_{ft}`$ (e.g., GG-FastMNMF).

Given the current estimates of $`q(\bm{\Phi})`$ and $`\bm{\Psi}`$, we

with respect to $`\mathbf{W}`$, $`\mathbf{H}`$, and
$`\tilde{\mathbf{G}}`$ equal to zero and using
Eq. ([26](#S3.E26 "In III-B1 Lower Bound ‣ III-B Multiplicative Update Variational Expectation-Maximization Algorithm ‣ III Gaussian Scale Mixture Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation")),
([28](#S3.E28 "In III-B1 Lower Bound ‣ III-B Multiplicative Update Variational Expectation-Maximization Algorithm ‣ III Gaussian Scale Mixture Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation")),
and
([29](#S3.E29 "In III-B1 Lower Bound ‣ III-B Multiplicative Update Variational Expectation-Maximization Algorithm ‣ III Gaussian Scale Mixture Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation")),

$`\tilde{\mathbf{G}}`$ are obtained in a closed form as follows:

|     |                                 |                                                                                                                                                                                         |     |      |
| --- | ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle w_{nkf}`$       | $`\displaystyle\leftarrow w_{nkf}\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{t,m=1}^{T,M}h_{nkt}\tilde{g}_{nm}\tilde{y}_{ftm}^{-2}\hat{z}_{ftm}}{\sum_{t,m=1}^{T,M}h_{nkt}\tilde{g}_{nm}\tilde{y}_{ftm}^{-1}}},`$ |     | (31) |

|     | $`\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{nm}`$ | $`\displaystyle\leftarrow\tilde{g}_{nm}\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{f,t=1}^{F,T}\lambda_{nft}\tilde{y}_{ftm}^{-2}\hat{z}_{ftm}}{\sum_{f,t=1}^{F,T}\lambda_{nft}\tilde{y}_{ftm}^{-1}}},`$           |     | (33) |

|     |                                                                       |     |      |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\hat{z}_{ftm}=\tilde{\phi}_{ft}^{-1}\tilde{z}_{ftm}.`$ |     | (34) |

|                                                    |                          |                                                                                       |                                                                                             |     |     |

| w*{nkf}\leftarrow r*{f}^{-1}w\_{nkf},\end{cases}`$ |                          | (38)                                                                                  |
|                                                    | $`\displaystyle u_{n}`$  | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\tilde{g}_{nm},`$                                       | $`\displaystyle\begin{cases}\tilde{g}_{nm}\leftarrow u_{n}^{-1}\tilde{g}\_{nm},\\           |
| w*{nkf}\leftarrow u*{n}w\_{nkf}.\end{cases}`$      |                          | (39)                                                                                  |
|                                                    | $`\displaystyle v_{nk}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{f=1}^{F}w_{nkf},`$                                              | $`\displaystyle\begin{cases}w*{nkf}\leftarrow v*{nk}^{-1}w\_{nkf},\\                        |
| h*{nkt}\leftarrow v*{nk}h\_{nkt}.\end{cases}`$     |                          | (40)                                                                                  |

### III-D Generalized Hyperbolic FastMNMF

We propose a new instance of GSM-FastMNMF based on the multivariate
complex generalized hyperbolic (GH) likelihood (denoted by
$`\mathcal{GH}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\gamma,\rho,\eta}`$), called GH-FastMNMF.
Its constrained version called GH-R1-FastMNMF is obtained when the
rank-1 spatial model is used. The multivariate GH
distribution \[[25](#bib.bib25)\] has infinite divisibility
property \[[38](#bib.bib38)\], i.e., a GH random vector can be
decomposed into the sum of i.i.d. random vectors \[[39](#bib.bib39)\].
Since the GH distribution is closed under affine transformation, it has
high affinity to the joint diagonalizability of FastMNMF given by
Eq. ([8](#S2.E8 "In II-C3 Jointly-Diagonalizable Spatial Model ‣ II-C Source and Spatial Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
because the observed mixture $`\mathbf{x}_{ft}`$ following a GH
distribution with a full scale matrix can be transformed to the
projected mixture $`\mathbf{z}_{ft}=\mathbf{Q}_{f}\mathbf{x}_{ft}`$
following a GH distribution with a diagonal scale matrix.

#### III-D1 Probabilistic Formulation

GH-FastMNMF is obtained by replacing
Eq. ([10](#S2.E10 "In II-C3 Jointly-Diagonalizable Spatial Model ‣ II-C Source and Spatial Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))

| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mbox{GH-FastMNNF:}\ \mathbf{z}_{ft}\sim\mathcal{GH}^{\gamma,\rho,\eta}_{\mathbb{C}}\!\left({\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}_{ft}}\right),`$ |     | (47) |

where
$`\mathcal{GH}^{\gamma,\rho,\eta}_{\mathbb{C}}\!\left({\bm{\Sigma}}\right)`$
denotes a zero-mean non-skewed multivariate complex GH distribution with
a shape parameter $`\gamma\in\mathbb{R}`$, a concentration parameter
$`\rho>0`$, a scaling parameter $`\eta>0`$, and a scale matrix
$`\bm{\Sigma}\succeq\bm{0}`$. Note that the $`M`$ elements of
$`\mathbf{z}_{ft}`$ are mutually dependent except for
$`\mathcal{N}`$-FastMNMF, a special case of GH-FastMNMF. In
GH-R1-FastMNMF (GH-FastMNMF with $`M=N`$ and
$`\tilde{\mathbf{G}}=\mathbf{I}`$),
Eq. ([47](#S3.E47 "In III-D1 Probabilistic Formulation ‣ III-D Generalized Hyperbolic FastMNMF ‣ III Gaussian Scale Mixture Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
reduces to

|     |                                                                                                                                                            |     |      |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mbox{GH-R1-FastMNNF:}\ \mathbf{z}_{ft}\sim\mathcal{GH}^{\gamma,\rho,\eta}_{\mathbb{C}}\!\left({\mathrm{Diag}(\bm{\lambda}_{ft})}\right),`$ |     | (48) |

where
$`\bm{\lambda}_{ft}\triangleq[\lambda_{1ft},\cdots,\lambda_{Mft}]^{\mathsf{T}}`$
and the $`M`$ elements of $`\mathbf{z}_{ft}`$ are assumed to have a
one-to-one correspondence to $`N`$ sources ($`M=N`$). A reason why the
rank-1 version of GH-FastMNMF is called GH-R1-FastMNMF is that the
estimated sources are not made independent. To formulate a generalized
hyperbolic extension of ILRMA, one can assume a univariate complex GH
distribution for each element of
$`\mathbf{z}_{ft}\triangleq[z_{ft1},\dots,z_{ftM}]^{\top}`$ in exchange
for loosing the analytical expression of $`\mathbf{x}_{ft}`$ (beyond the
scope of this paper) as follows:

|     |                                                                                                          |     |      |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle z_{ftm}\sim\mathcal{GH}^{\gamma,\rho,\eta}_{\mathbb{C}}\!\left({\lambda_{mft}}\right).`$ |     | (49) |

Note that
Eq. ([49](#S3.E49 "In III-D1 Probabilistic Formulation ‣ III-D Generalized Hyperbolic FastMNMF ‣ III Gaussian Scale Mixture Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))

Eq. ([48](#S3.E48 "In III-D1 Probabilistic Formulation ‣ III-D Generalized Hyperbolic FastMNMF ‣ III Gaussian Scale Mixture Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation"))
only for the case of $`\mathcal{N}`$-FastMNMF, because even when an
elliptically-contoured multivariate distribution has a diagonal scale
matrix, it cannot generally be factorized into the product of
independent dimension-wise univariate distributions.

The LL of the parameters
$`\bm{\Theta}=\{\mathbf{W},\mathbf{H},\mathbf{Q},\tilde{\mathbf{G}}\}`$
is the same in form as
Eq. ([11](#S2.E11 "In II-D1 Gaussian FastMNMF ‣ II-D Gaussian and Heavy-Tailed Models ‣ II Existing Variants of Fast MultichannelNonnegative Matrix Factorization ‣ Generalized Fast Multichannel Nonnegative Matrix Factorization Based on Gaussian Scale Mixturesfor Blind Source Separation")),

| --- | ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\log p(\mathbf{z}_{ft})`$ | $`\displaystyle\overset{c}{=}\frac{\gamma-M}{2}\log\!\left(1+\frac{2}{\rho\eta}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{\tilde{z}_{ftm}}{\tilde{y}_{ftm}}\right)`$        |     |      |

We estimated the parameters
$`\bm{\Theta}=\{\mathbf{W},\mathbf{H},\mathbf{Q},\tilde{\mathbf{G}}\}`$
of each method for an observed mixture spectrogram $`\mathbf{X}`$
obtained by applying STFT with a Hann window of 1024 points ($`F=513`$)
and a 75% overlap to a multichannel mixture signal sampled at 16 kHz.
All elements of the parameters $`\mathbf{W}`$ and $`\mathbf{H}`$ of the
NMF-based source model were initialized to the absolute values of random
samples drawn from a standard Gaussian distribution. As proposed in
\[[8](#bib.bib8)\], the parameters $`\mathbf{Q}`$ and
$`\tilde{\mathbf{G}}`$ of the jointly-diagonalizable spatial model were
initialized as $`\mathbf{Q}_{f}\leftarrow\mathbf{I}`$ and
$`\tilde{\mathbf{G}}\leftarrow\mathbf{J}`$, respectively, where
$`\mathbf{I}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{M\times M}`$ is an identity matrix and
$`\mathbf{J}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N\times M}`$ is a circulant matrix given
by

|     |                                                                                                        |     |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{J}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}1&\epsilon&\ldots&\epsilon&1&\epsilon&\ldots\\ |

\epsilon&1&\ldots&\epsilon&\epsilon&1&\ldots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\\
\epsilon&\epsilon&\ldots&1&\epsilon&\epsilon&\ldots\end{array}\right),`$ | |

where $`\epsilon`$ is set to a small value ($`\epsilon=10^{-2}`$ in this
paper) for the FastMNMF variants or zero for the R1-FastMNMF variants.

For fair comparison, we made two disjoint datasets (validation and test
sets) in speech enhancement and separation tasks. In each task, the

### IV-B Speech Enhancement with Determined Configurations

We report a comparative experiment on speech enhancement that aims to
extract a single speech source from a noisy mixture. The audio data were
taken from the REVERB Challenge dataset \[[47](#bib.bib47)\], where the
length of each sample is between 3 \[s\] and 10 \[s\].; Multichannel
mixtures ($`M\in\{2,5,8\}`$) were simulated with a signal-to-noise ratio
(SNR) of $`0`$, $`5`$, or $`10`$ dB and a reverberation time
($`\text{RT}_{60}`$) of $`250`$, $`500`$, or $`700`$ ms under a near or
far condition that the distance between a microphone array and a speaker
was $`0.5`$ or $`2.0`$ m. The validation set consists of 100 randomly
selected mixtures with an SNR of $`5`$ dB under the near condition. The
test set consists of 200 randomly selected mixtures with all conditions.
For fair comparison and the determined nature of the rank-1 spatial
model, all methods were used with a determined configuration ($`N=M`$)
and the predominant source with the highest average energy was then
selected as a target speaker.

## V Conclusion

This paper has described GSM-FastMNMF, a robust generalization of
Gaussian FastMNMF ($`\mathcal{N}`$-FastMNMF), that incorporates a
general expression of heavy-tailed probability distributions called a
Gaussian scale mixture (GSM) into the jointly-diagonalizable spatial
model FastMNMF. We have developed a multiplicative update variational
expectation-maximization (MU-VEM) algorithm for GSM-FastMNMF. As an
instance of GSM-FastMNMF, we have derived generalized hyperbolic
FastMNMF (GH-FastMNMF), which encompasses not only
$`\mathcal{N}`$-FastMNMF and Student’s $`t`$ FastMNMF ($`t`$-FastMNMF)
but also a new variant called normal-inverse Gaussian FastMNMF
(NIG-FastMNMF). We showed that leptokurtic generalized Gaussian FastMNMF
(GG-FastMNMF), which does not belong to GH-FastMNMF, can also be
instantiated from GSM-FastMNMF. The speech enhancement and separation
results revealed the experimental advantages of NIG-FastMNMF in most
conditions.

Considering the recent advance of deep learning techniques, one
important future direction is to use a normalizing
flow \[[52](#bib.bib52)\] for formulating an adaptive time-varying
spatial model as proposed in \[[53](#bib.bib53)\]. Another complementary
direction is to use a deep generative model of speech for improving the
expression capability of the source model as proposed in
\[[54](#bib.bib54), [55](#bib.bib55)\]. From the laborious grid study of
this paper, a next step will be also to estimate the tail-index
parameters of a given mixture $`\mathbf{X}`$ as in \[[56](#bib.bib56)\].

## Probability Density Functions of Gaussian Scale Mixture Variables

Let $`\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{C}^{M}`$ be a $`M`$-dimensional complex
random vector following a zero-mean elliptically-contoured multivariate
complex Gaussian scale mixture (GSM) with a positive semidefinite scale
matrix $`\bm{\Sigma}\succeq\bm{0}`$. Concrete examples are described
below:

- •
  A centralized Gaussian distribution is denoted by
  $`\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}\!\left({\bm{\Sigma}}\right)`$
  and the PDF of $`\mathbf{x}`$ is given by

  |     |                                                |             |                                                                           |
  | --- | ---------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
  |     | $`\displaystyle p(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{\pi^{M} | \bm{\Sigma} | }\exp\!\left(-\mathbf{x}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1}\mathbf{x}\right).`$ |     | (61) |

- •
  A Student’s $`t`$ distribution with a degree of freedom $`\nu>0`$ is
  denoted by
  $`\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{T}^{\nu}_{\mathbb{C}}\!\left({\bm{\Sigma}}\right)`$
  and the PDF of $`\mathbf{x}`$ is given by

  |     |                                                                                                                                           |             |                                                                                                       |
  | --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
  |     | $`\displaystyle p(\mathbf{x})=\frac{2^{M}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{2M+\nu}{2}\right)}{\left(\pi\nu\right)^{M}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\nu}{2}\right) | \bm{\Sigma} | }\left(1+\frac{2}{\nu}\mathbf{x}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1}\mathbf{x}\right)^{-\frac{2M+\nu}{2}}.`$ |     | (62) |

- •
  A generalized Gaussian (GG) distribution with a shape parameter
  $`\beta>0`$ is denoted by
  $`\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{GG}^{\beta}_{\mathbb{C}}\!\left({\bm{\Sigma}}\right)`$
  and the PDF of $`\mathbf{x}`$ is given by

  |     |                                                                                                                                             |             |                                                                                                          |
  | --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
  |     | $`\displaystyle p(\mathbf{x})=\frac{\frac{\beta}{2}\Gamma\!\left(M\right)}{2^{\frac{2M}{\beta}}\pi^{M}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{2M}{\beta}\right) | \bm{\Sigma} | }\exp\!\left(-\left(\mathbf{x}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1}\mathbf{x}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\right).`$ |     | (63) |

- •
  A generalized hyperbolic (GH) distribution with a shape parameter
  $`\gamma\in\mathbb{R}`$, a concentration parameter $`\rho>0`$, and a
  scaling parameter $`\eta>0`$ is denoted by
  $`\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{GH}^{\gamma,\rho,\eta}_{\mathbb{C}}\!\left({\bm{\Sigma}}\right)`$
  and the PDF of $`\mathbf{x}`$ is given by

  |     |                                  |                                                                                                                                        |             |                                                                                                            |
  | --- | -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
  |     | $`\displaystyle p(\mathbf{x})=`$ | $`\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\pi\eta)^{M}\mathcal{K}\_{\gamma}(\rho)                                                                       | \bm{\Sigma} | }\left(1+\frac{2}{\rho\eta}\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{H}}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1}\mathbf{x}\right)^{\frac{\gamma-M}{2}}`$ |     |     |
  |     |                                  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\gamma-M}\!\left(\rho\eta^{-1}\sqrt{\rho\eta+2\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{H}}\bm{\Sigma}^{-1}\mathbf{x}}\right).`$ |             | (64)                                                                                                       |

## Probability Density Functions of Impulse Variables

Let $`x`$ be a nonnegative random variable. Concrete examples are
described below:

- •
  An inverse gamma (IG) distribution with a shape parameter $`\alpha>0`$
  and a scale parameter $`\sigma>0`$ is denoted by
  $`x\sim\mathcal{IG}\!\left({\alpha},{\sigma}\right)`$ and the PDF of
  $`x`$ is given by

  |     |                                                                                               |     |      |
  | --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
  |     | $`\displaystyle p(x)=\frac{\sigma^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}x^{-\alpha-1}e^{-\sigma x^{-1}}.`$ |     | (65) |

- •
  A generalized inverse Gaussian (GIG) distribution with a shape
  parameter $`\gamma\in\mathbb{R}`$, a concentration parameter
  $`\rho>0`$, and a scaling parameter $`\eta>0`$ is denoted by
  $`x\sim\mathcal{GIG}\!\left({\gamma},{\rho},{\eta}\right)`$ and the
  PDF of $`x`$ is given by

  |     |                                                                                                                                                |     |      |
  | --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
  |     | $`\displaystyle p(x)=\frac{1}{{2\eta^{\gamma}\mathcal{K}_{\gamma}(\rho)}}x^{\gamma-1}e^{-\frac{\rho}{2}\left(\eta^{-1}x+\eta x^{-1}\right)}.`$ |     | (66) |
