###### Abstract

In this article we introduce a broad family of adaptive, linear
time-frequency representations termed superposition frames, and show
that they admit desirable fast overlap-add reconstruction properties
akin to standard short-time Fourier techniques. This approach stands in
contrast to many adaptive time-frequency representations in the existing
literature, which, while more flexible than standard fixed-resolution
approaches, typically fail to provide for efficient reconstruction and
often lack the regular structure necessary for precise frame-theoretic
analysis. Our main technical contributions come through the development
of properties which ensure that our superposition construction provides
for a numerically stable, invertible signal representation. Our primary
algorithmic contributions come via the introduction and discussion of
specific signal adaptation criteria in deterministic and stochastic
settings, based respectively on time-frequency concentration and
nonstationarity detection. We conclude with a short speech enhancement
example that serves to highlight potential applications of our approach.

###### Index Terms: 

Adaptive short-time Fourier analysis, frame theory, Gabor frames,
overlap-add synthesis, speech enhancement.

## I Introduction

Overcomplete short-time Fourier methods are frequently used to analyze
the time-varying spectral content of discrete-time waveforms $`x[t]`$
arising in a variety of signal processing applications. Since the choice
of localizing window function effectively controls the balance between
time and frequency resolution _a priori_, standard representations
cannot modulate this trade-off to adapt to the local spectral content of
$`x[t]`$. Over the past two decades, this shortcoming has motivated the
development of various linear and nonlinear adaptive time-frequency
analysis methods \[[2](#bib.bib2), [3](#bib.bib3), [4](#bib.bib4),
[5](#bib.bib5), [6](#bib.bib6), [7](#bib.bib7), [8](#bib.bib8),
[9](#bib.bib9), [10](#bib.bib10), [11](#bib.bib11), [12](#bib.bib12),
[13](#bib.bib13), [14](#bib.bib14)\], in applications ranging from
biomedical engineering \[[15](#bib.bib15)\] to radar signal
analysis \[[16](#bib.bib16)\] and speech
processing \[[17](#bib.bib17)\].

Despite the recognized importance of overcomplete signal-adaptive
time-frequency analysis, the above methods generally fail to admit _fast
reconstruction_ of the signal $`x[t]`$ from its time-frequency
representation, by which we mean any non-iterative method that avoids
direct (pseudo-)inversion of the corresponding analysis operator. While
approaches such as modulated lapped transforms for audio
coding \[[18](#bib.bib18)\], wavelet packet decompositions via best
basis \[[19](#bib.bib19), [20](#bib.bib20)\] and adaptive segmentation
via dynamic programming \[[21](#bib.bib21), [22](#bib.bib22),
[23](#bib.bib23), [24](#bib.bib24), [25](#bib.bib25)\] can lead to
flexible tilings of the time-frequency plane, the general goal of
efficient reconstruction from signal-adaptive, _overcomplete
time-frequency_ representations remains an open problem. This issue is
particularly important, given the recent interest in oversampled,
modulated filter banks \[[26](#bib.bib26), [27](#bib.bib27),
[28](#bib.bib28)\].

In this article we introduce a broad family of adaptive, linear
time-frequency representations that admit a fast overlap-add
reconstruction property akin to standard short-time Fourier techniques.
We do so by adapting a given discrete Gabor frame to an observed signal
$`x[t]`$ via superpositions of neighboring translates of a single window
function, to yield the superposition frames of the article title.
Related procedures include the multi-window constructions
of \[[29](#bib.bib29)\], in which multiple systems are defined on the
same time-frequency lattice; and the multi-Gabor expansions
of \[[30](#bib.bib30)\], in which multiple time lattices and windows are
employed. However, neither of these schemes treats the use of subset
selection to achieve a signal-adaptive system in the manner of the
present article. More recent approaches \[[9](#bib.bib9),
[10](#bib.bib10), [11](#bib.bib11), [12](#bib.bib12)\] address subset
selection from a Gabor frame or union of Gabor frames, but do not
consider the structure of the corresponding canonical dual.

A very recent approach in this direction is the study of general
nonstationary Gabor frames \[[31](#bib.bib31)\], and indeed our
contribution can be viewed as one possible instantiation of this
framework. However, as we show below, the additional structure induced
by our superposition construction yields several important properties,
including, among other results, a _preservation_ of the lower frame
bound of the original Gabor frame, a generalized constant overlap-add
property that avoids the explicit computation of dual windows, and a
means of generating new families of adaptive lapped frames.

The article is organized as follows. We begin by reviewing the

Next, we introduce superposition windows in

and use them to construct superposition systems in

In

## II Preliminaries

We first review some well-known properties of Gabor
frames \[[32](#bib.bib32), [33](#bib.bib33), [28](#bib.bib28)\] and
discuss their relationship to short-time Fourier analysis. We take as
our setting the space $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$, and interpret its members as
discrete-time $`L`$-periodic signals $`x\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{L})`$,
with $`\mathbb{Z}_{L}`$ denoting the integers $`\mathbb{Z}`$ modulo
$`L`$. The _short-time Fourier transform_ (STFT) on $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$
uses a well-concentrated window function in order to localize $`x`$ in
time prior to the analysis of its frequency content.

###### Definition 1 (Short-Time Fourier Transform)

Fix a window $`w\in\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ and time-frequency lattice constants
$`a,b>0`$ that divide $`L`$, with $`a`$ an integer, and define
$`M,N:Na=Mb=L`$. Then for the $`m`$th frequency bin index and $`n`$th
window shift, with $`m\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}`$ and $`n\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}`$,
the Gabor or subsampled short-time Fourier transform $`X[m,n]`$ of
$`x\in\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ is given by

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
X[m,n]\triangleq\sum_{t=0}^{L-1}x[t]\overline{w[t-na]e^{2\pi imbt/L}}\text{,}
``` |  | (1) |

where $`i=\sqrt{-1}`$ and $`\overline{\,\cdot\,}`$ denotes complex
conjugation. The expression
of ([1](#S2.E1 "In Definition 1 (Short-Time Fourier Transform) ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
can be viewed as a set of inner products of $`x`$ with $`NM`$
time-frequency shifts of the chosen window $`w`$. To realize this
correspondence, and to set notation, we introduce explicit translation
and modulation operators as follows.

###### Definition 2 (Translation and Modulation Operators)

Let the translation and modulation operators $`\mathcal{T}`$ and
$`\mathcal{M}`$ be defined as maps from $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ to itself
acting according to:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{T}_{na}w[t]\triangleq w[t-na],\hskip 8.19447pt\mathcal{M}_{mb}w[t]\triangleq w[t]\,e^{2\pi imbt/L}\text{.}
``` |  |

Through the action of these operators, time-frequency shifts of the
chosen window $`w\in\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ may be indexed as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\phi_{m,n}[t]\triangleq\mathcal{M}_{mb}\mathcal{T}_{na}w[t],\hskip 8.19447ptm\in\mathbb{Z}_{M},n\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}\text{,}
``` |  | (2) |

and one speaks of a Gabor system $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)=\{\phi_{m,n}\}`$.
In order to ensure a reconstruction property for any $`x`$ from its
subsampled short-time Fourier transform $`X[m,n]`$, the Gabor system
$`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ must form a *frame* for $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ as
follows.

###### Definition 3 (Gabor Systems and Frames)

A denumerable set $`\{\phi_{m,n}\}`$ of vectors comprising
time-frequency shifts of a single window function $`w\in\mathbb{C}^{L}`$
is called a *Gabor system*, and is said to be a *Gabor frame* for
$`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ if there exist constants $`0<A\leq B<\infty`$ termed
*frame bounds* such that:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\textstyle\forall x\in\mathbb{C}^{L},\,A\|x\|^{2}\leq\sum_{m,n}|\langle x,\phi_{m,n}\rangle|^{2}\leq B\|x\|^{2}\text{,}
``` |  | (3) |

with inner product
$`\langle x,\phi_{m,n}\rangle\triangleq\sum_{t=0}^{L-1}x[t]\overline{\phi_{m,n}[t]}=X[m,n]`$.

An upper frame bound $`B`$
for ([3](#S2.E3 "In Definition 3 (Gabor Systems and Frames) ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
is guaranteed whenever the set $`\{\phi_{m,n}\}`$ is finite, and so the
existence of a lower frame bound $`A>0`$, for a finite Gabor system
$`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$, is equivalent to the requirement that its
elements span $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$. This occurs *if and only if* the
*frame operator* is of full rank.

###### Definition 4 (Gabor Frame Operator)

Let $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)=\{\phi_{m,n}\}`$ be a Gabor system on
$`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$, and define the *frame operator*
$`S:\mathbb{C}^{L}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ through its action on
$`x`$ as $`Sx=\sum_{m,n}\langle x,\phi_{m,n}\rangle\phi_{m,n}`$. Then
$`S`$ is represented by the $`L\times L`$ symmetric and positive
semi-definite matrix with entries

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
S[t,t^{\prime}]\triangleq\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{M}_{mb}\mathcal{T}_{na}w[t]\overline{\mathcal{M}_{mb}\mathcal{T}_{na}w[t^{\prime}]}\text{.}
``` |  | (4) |

###### Remark 1 (Strict Positive-Definiteness of Frame Operator)

By
Definition [4](#Thmdefn4 "Definition 4 (Gabor Frame Operator) ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"),
the frame condition
of ([3](#S2.E3 "In Definition 3 (Gabor Systems and Frames) ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
is equivalent to *strict* positive definiteness of $`S`$ and hence a
necessary condition is that $`MN\geq L`$ (i.e., $`ab\leq L`$). Moreover,
the minimal and maximal eigenvalues of $`S`$ yield optimal frame bounds,
since ([3](#S2.E3 "In Definition 3 (Gabor Systems and Frames) ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
may be expressed as the requirement that
$`A\langle x,x\rangle\leq\langle Sx,x\rangle\leq B\langle x,x\rangle,\,\forall x\in\mathbb{C}^{L}`$.

The frame condition
of ([3](#S2.E3 "In Definition 3 (Gabor Systems and Frames) ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
in turn implies the following reconstruction property:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\forall x\in\mathbb{C}^{L},t\in\mathbb{Z}_{L},\,\,x[t]=\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\langle x,\phi_{m,n}\rangle\widetilde{\phi}_{m,n}[t]\text{,}
``` |  |

where the elements $`\{\widetilde{\phi}_{m,n}\}`$ comprise a (not
necessarily unique) *dual frame*. However, to each frame may be
associated a unique canonical dual, whose elements are given by the
action of the frame operator inverse $`S^{-1}`$ on each $`\phi_{m,n}`$.
Moreover, in the Gabor setting, this canonical dual takes the form of
another Gabor system $`\mathscr{G}(\widetilde{w},a,b)`$, with
$`\widetilde{w}\triangleq S^{-1}w`$.

Any $`S`$ can be written as a sum of outer products of each frame vector
with itself;
from ([4](#S2.E4 "In Definition 4 (Gabor Frame Operator) ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
via the orthogonality relation

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}e^{2\pi imb(t-t^{\prime})/L}=\begin{cases}M&\text{when $M$ divides $t-t^{\prime}$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\text{,}\end{cases}
``` |  |

we obtain the so-called Walnut representation \[[34](#bib.bib34)\] of a
Gabor frame operator $`S`$, which will be used repeatedly throughout.

###### Definition 5 (Discrete Walnut Representation)

Denote by $`M\backslash(t-t^{\prime})`$ the condition that $`M`$ divides
$`t-t^{\prime}`$, and by
$`\mathbb{I}_{M\backslash(t-t^{\prime})}[t-t^{\prime}]`$ the
corresponding indicator function on $`\mathbb{Z}_{L}`$. Then the frame
operator $`S`$ of a finite Gabor system $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ has
banded structure, and satisfies the entrywise relation

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
S[t,t^{\prime}]=\mathbb{I}_{M\backslash(t-t^{\prime})}[t-t^{\prime}]\cdot M\!\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{T}_{na}w[t]\overline{\mathcal{T}_{na}w[t^{\prime}]}\text{.}
``` |  | (5) |

###### Remark 2 (Covering Condition)

Note that if $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ is a frame for $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$,
then ([5](#S2.E5 "In Definition 5 (Discrete Walnut Representation) ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
implies that the covering condition

|     |                                                             |     |     |
|-----|-------------------------------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|w[t-na]|^{2}>0,\forall\,t\in\mathbb{Z}_{L}
       ```                                                          |     | (6) |

must be satisfied, since a necessary condition for positive definiteness
of $`S`$ is that its diagonal entries are positive.

###### Remark 3 (Window Length as Distinct from Support)

The *support* of $`w\in\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ refers to the set of indices
$`t`$ for which $`w[t]\neq 0`$, with $`|\operatorname{supp}(w)|`$ its
cardinality. Bearing in mind the summands
of ([5](#S2.E5 "In Definition 5 (Discrete Walnut Representation) ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
and ([6](#S2.E6 "In Remark 2 (Covering Condition) ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")),
define the *length* of $`w`$ by

|     |                                                         |     |     |
|-----|---------------------------------------------------------|-----|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \operatorname{len}(w)\triangleq|\operatorname{supp}(w)|
       ```                                                      |     | (7) |

if $`\operatorname{supp}(w)`$ is contiguous, as is often the case in
practice, and
$`\min_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{L}}(\max_{t,t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{L}}|t-t^{\prime}+1|\!:\!\mathcal{T}_{n}(w[t]\overline{w[t^{\prime}]})\neq 0)`$
otherwise.

###### Remark 4 (Diagonal Frame Operator)

It follows
from ([5](#S2.E5 "In Definition 5 (Discrete Walnut Representation) ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
and ([7](#S2.E7 "In Remark 3 (Window Length as Distinct from Support) ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
that $`S`$ is diagonal if $`M\geq\operatorname{len}(w)`$, since
$`\mathcal{T}_{na}w[t]\overline{\mathcal{T}_{na}w[t^{\prime}]}=0`$ for
all $`|t-t^{\prime}|\geq\operatorname{len}(w)`$, including those for
which $`M`$ divides $`t-t^{\prime}`$. In turn, this implies efficient
computation of the dual frame
$`\{\mathcal{M}_{mb}\mathcal{T}_{na}\widetilde{w}\}`$, with
$`\widetilde{w}=S^{-1}w`$ obtained via element-wise division of $`w[t]`$
by $`S[t,t]=M\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|w[t-na]|^{2}`$. In this case the condition
of ([6](#S2.E6 "In Remark 2 (Covering Condition) ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
is sufficient to guarantee the frame condition
of ([3](#S2.E3 "In Definition 3 (Gabor Systems and Frames) ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")).

We conclude by using the arguments of
Remarks [3](#Thmrem3 "Remark 3 (Window Length as Distinct from Support) ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
and
[4](#Thmrem4 "Remark 4 (Diagonal Frame Operator) ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
to establish a result required for our subsequent development.

###### Lemma 1

Fix any $`w\in\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ and $`a,b`$ such that
$`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ is a frame for $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$, with
$`M=L/b`$. Then for any integral
$`M^{\prime}\geq\operatorname{len}(w)`$, the Gabor system
$`\mathscr{G}(w,a,L/M^{\prime})`$ is also a frame for
$`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$, with diagonal frame operator and maximal lower frame
bound given by $`(M^{\prime}/M)\min_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{L}}S[t,t]`$.

###### Proof:

We must show that if the frame operator $`S`$ of a Gabor system
$`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ on $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ is full rank, then so is
the frame operator $`S^{\prime}`$ of any system
$`\mathscr{G}(w,a,L/M^{\prime})`$. For any
$`M^{\prime}\geq\operatorname{len}(w)`$, the argument of
Remark [4](#Thmrem4 "Remark 4 (Diagonal Frame Operator) ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
implies that $`S^{\prime}`$ is diagonal, with eigenvalues
$`S^{\prime}[t,t]=M^{\prime}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|w[t-na]|^{2}`$; the Walnut
representation
of ([5](#S2.E5 "In Definition 5 (Discrete Walnut Representation) ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
further implies that $`S^{\prime}[t,t]=(M^{\prime}/M)S[t,t]`$, for
$`M=L/b`$. As $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ is a frame for $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$,
it follows that $`S[t,t]>0`$. Hence $`S^{\prime}[t,t]>0`$ for all
$`t\in\mathbb{Z}_{L}`$, and thus $`S^{\prime}`$ is of full rank. ∎

## III Superposition Windows

Having outlined the connections between Gabor systems and the short-time
Fourier transform, we now introduce the central ingredient of our
signal-adaptive time-frequency analysis framework—the *superposition
window* construction, illustrated in
Figure [1(a)](#S2.F1.sf1 "In Fig. 1 ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction").

###### Definition 6 (Superposition Window)

Fix a real, nonnegative window $`w`$ on $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ and an
integer $`a=L/N`$, along with some $`r\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}`$. We then
define the *superposition window* $`w_{r}`$ to be a linear sum of
$`r+1`$ adjacent translates of $`w[t]`$ as follows:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
w_{r}[t]\triangleq\sum_{n=0}^{r}\mathcal{T}_{na}w[t],\hskip 8.19447ptr\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}\text{.}
``` |  | (8) |

###### Remark 5 (Fourier Transform Support)

Let $`\widehat{w}`$ denote the (discrete) Fourier transform of
$`w\in\mathbb{C}^{L}`$. Linearity
of ([8](#S3.E8 "In Definition 6 (Superposition Window) ‣ III Superposition Windows ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
implies that the support of $`\widehat{w_{r}}`$ is contained within that
of $`\widehat{w}`$, as
$`\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{w_{r}})=\operatorname{supp}(\sum_{n=0}^{r}e^{-2\pi ina(\cdot)/L}\widehat{w})\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{w})`$.

###### Remark 6 (Fourier Transform Decay)

As $`r`$ increases, it is clear that $`w_{r}`$ can become more like a
rectangular window (see, e.g.,
Fig. [1(a)](#S2.F1.sf1 "In Fig. 1 ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"));
this effect is illustrated in
Fig. [2](#S2.F2 "Fig. 2 ‣ Proof: ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
for the case of Hamming superposition windows. Consequently, the main
lobe width of $`\widehat{w_{r}}`$ shrinks, leading to improved frequency
resolution relative to $`\widehat{w_{0}}`$; this main lobe resolution,
however, comes at the expense of decreasing sidelobe attenuation.
Spectral leakage—a function of the window smoothness—remains superior,
as does overall spectral decay for small $`r`$.

Our subsequent construction of superposition frames employs sets of
modulated superposition windows, and to this end we establish the
following two energy “conservation” properties, proved in the appendix.

###### Lemma 2 (Localized Parseval Property)

Fix any $`w\in\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ and an integer
$`M=L/b\geq\operatorname{len}(w)`$. Then

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\forall x\in\mathbb{C}^{L},\,\,\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\left|\langle x,\mathcal{M}_{mb}w\rangle\right|^{2}=M\sum_{t=0}^{L-1}|x[t]|^{2}|w[t]|^{2}\text{.}
``` |  | (9) |

###### Lemma 3 (Superadditivity of Superposition Energy)

Let real, nonnegative superposition windows $`w_{p}`$ and $`w_{q}`$ be
derived from a Gabor system $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ on
$`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$, and merge them to obtain a new superposition window
$`w_{p}+{w_{q}}^{\prime}=w_{p}+\mathcal{T}_{(p+1)a}w_{q}`$. Then, *if*
and *only if* $`M=L/b\geq\operatorname{len}(w_{p}+{w_{q}}^{\prime})`$,
the following holds for every
$`M_{0}=L/b_{0}\in\{\max(\operatorname{len}(w_{p}),\operatorname{len}(w_{q})),\ldots,M\}`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{m=0}^{M_{0}-1}\left|\langle x,\mathcal{M}_{mb_{0}}w_{p}\rangle\right|^{2}+\left|\langle x,\mathcal{M}_{mb_{0}}{w_{q}}^{\prime}\rangle\right|^{2}\\
\leq\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\left|\langle x,\mathcal{M}_{mb}(w_{p}+{w_{q}}^{\prime})\rangle\right|^{2},\hskip 8.19447pt\forall x\in\mathbb{C}^{L}\text{.}
``` |  | (10) |

This superadditivity property, which is invariant to translation of
$`w_{p}+{w_{q}}^{\prime}`$, will be used in
Section [V](#S5 "V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
to show that adapting a Gabor frame through the superposition
construction *preserves* the original Gabor lower frame bound—an
important consideration for numerical stability.

## IV Construction of Superposition Systems

We now describe how to employ the superposition windows of
Section [III](#S3 "III Superposition Windows ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
above to create a signal-adaptive analysis framework. Let
$`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ represent a Gabor system, which induces a
short-time Fourier transform on $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ according to
Definition [1](#Thmdefn1 "Definition 1 (Short-Time Fourier Transform) ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction").
Beginning with $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$, we then form a signal-dependent,
variable-resolution STFT by using the superposition sum
of ([8](#S3.E8 "In Definition 6 (Superposition Window) ‣ III Superposition Windows ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
to adaptively merge neighboring translates from the set
$`\{\mathcal{T}_{n{a}}w,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}\}`$. Later we will
demonstrate how this signal-adaptive analysis can be coupled with a
variety of different algorithms; we begin, however, by studying the
general set of *superposition systems* independently of any algorithmic
construction. To this end, we introduce the notion of *ordered partition
functions* as a means of indexing arbitrary sets of superposition
windows, and then extend these to yield a full time-frequency analysis.

### IV-A Ordered Partition Functions

Observe that exactly $`2^{N-1}`$ distinct sets of variable-length
superposition windows may be derived by merging window translates from a
given Gabor system $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,\cdot)`$. As a means of indexing
these sets, the following “stick-breaking” analogy is helpful. Consider
a “stick” composed of $`N`$ ordered, unit-length pieces, representing
elements of the set $`\{\mathcal{T}_{n{a}}w,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}\}`$.
Merging adjacent windows in this set can be thought of as fusing
neighboring pieces of the stick. Each stick partition thus induces an
ordered partition of the set $`\{1,2,\ldots,N\}`$, with each piece
uniquely identified by an initial index and length, and we may formalize
this analogy as follows.

###### Definition 7 (Ordered Partition Functions)

We call any
$`\widetilde{I}:\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}\to\{0,1\}`$ an
*ordered partition function* if it is not identically zero, and
satisfies the following three properties:

1.  1.
    Each piece of the stick is distinct:

    |  |  |  |
    |----|----|----|
    |  |
    ``` math
    \widetilde{I}[n,r]=1\,\Rightarrow\,\widetilde{I}[n,r^{\prime}]=0\,\,\,\forall\,r^{\prime}\neq r,r^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}\text{.}
    ``` |  |
2.  2.
    The length of each piece is denoted by $`r+1`$:

    |  |  |  |
    |----|----|----|
    |  |
    ``` math
    \widetilde{I}[n,r]=1\,\Rightarrow\,\widetilde{I}[n^{\prime},r^{\prime}]=0\,\,\text{on}\,\,\{n+1,\ldots,n+r\}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}\text{.}
    ``` |  |
3.  3.
    All pieces of the stick are accounted for:

    |  |  |  |
    |----|----|----|
    |  |
    ``` math
    \widetilde{I}[n,r]=1\,\Rightarrow\,\widetilde{I}[n+r,r^{\prime}]=1\,\,\text{for exactly one}\,\,r^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}\text{.}
    ``` |  |

Definition [7](#Thmdefn7 "Definition 7 (Ordered Partition Functions) ‣ IV-A Ordered Partition Functions ‣ IV Construction of Superposition Systems ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
clearly implies that the “length” of the stick remains unchanged:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}\widetilde{I}[n,r]\,(r+1)=N\text{;}
``` |  | (11) |

moreover, each ordered partition function $`\widetilde{I}`$ can be
associated with a set of translated superposition windows, which
includes $`T_{na}w_{r}`$ whenever $`\widetilde{I}[n,r]=1`$. The
following examples of ordered partition functions are illustrated in
Fig. [1(b)](#S2.F1.sf2 "In Fig. 1 ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction").

###### Example 1 ($`N`$-Part Partition)

The ordered partition function associated to the top panel of
Fig. [1(b)](#S2.F1.sf2 "In Fig. 1 ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
is

|     |                                                                   |     |
|-----|-------------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \widetilde{I}[\cdot,r]\triangleq\begin{cases}1&\text{if $r=0$,}\\
       0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
       ```                                                                |     |

This clearly recovers the window translates of any Gabor system
$`\mathscr{G}(w,a,\cdot)`$. Note that in accordance
with ([11](#S4.E11 "In IV-A Ordered Partition Functions ‣ IV Construction of Superposition Systems ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")),
we have that
$`\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}\widetilde{I}[n,r]\,(r+1)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\widetilde{I}[n,0]=N=8`$.

###### Example 2 ($`(N-3)`$-Part Partition)

The ordered partition function associated to the bottom panel of
Fig. [1(b)](#S2.F1.sf2 "In Fig. 1 ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
is

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\widetilde{I}[n,r]=\begin{cases}\widetilde{I}[2,0]=\widetilde{I}[6,0]=\widetilde{I}[7,0]=1&\text{not merged,}\\
\widetilde{I}[0,1]=\widetilde{I}[3,2]=1&\text{merged.}\end{cases}
``` |  |

Note again that in accordance
with ([11](#S4.E11 "In IV-A Ordered Partition Functions ‣ IV Construction of Superposition Systems ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")),
we have that
$`\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}\widetilde{I}[n,r](r+1)=3+2+3=8`$.

### IV-B Superposition Systems

We now employ the above construction to arrive at a variable-resolution
time-frequency analysis via superposition windows. To this end, let the
set $`\mathscr{F}`$ be defined as a function of any Gabor system
$`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ on $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ as follows:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathscr{F}\triangleq\bigcup_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}}\mathscr{G}(w_{r},a,b_{L})\text{,}
``` |  |

where the frequency lattice $`b_{L}\mathbb{Z}`$ encompasses all possible
Gabor systems on $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ for a fixed choice of integral
$`M`$:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
b_{L}\mathbb{Z};\,M_{L}\triangleq\operatorname{lcm}(\{1,2\ldots,\max(L,M)\}),\,b_{L}\triangleq L/M_{L}\text{.}\!
``` |  |

Elements of $`\mathscr{F}`$ may then be defined in analogy
to ([2](#S2.E2 "In II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
as

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\phi_{m,n,r}\triangleq\mathcal{M}_{mb_{L}}\mathcal{T}_{na}w_{r}=\textstyle\mathcal{M}_{mb_{L}}\!\!\left(\sum_{n^{\prime}=0}^{r}\mathcal{T}_{(n^{\prime}+n)a}w\right)\!\text{,}
``` |  |

and in turn give rise to *superposition systems*, defined as
appropriately chosen subsets of $`\mathscr{F}`$.

###### Definition 8 (Superposition Systems and Admissibility)

Fix an ordered partition function $`\widetilde{I}[n,r]`$ and a function
$`M[n,r]:\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{M_{L}}`$.
We call any
$`I[m,n,r]:\mathbb{Z}_{M_{L}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}\to\{0,1\}`$
an *admissible selection function* on
$`\mathscr{F}=\cup_{r}\mathscr{G}(w_{r},a,b_{L})`$ if it satisfies the
following two properties:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle I[0,n,r]`$ | $`\displaystyle\!=\!\widetilde{I}[n,r]\hskip 8.19447pt\forall\,n,r\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}\times\mathbb{Z}_{N}\text{,}`$ |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle I[0,n,r]`$ | $`\displaystyle\!=\!1\,\Rightarrow I[{\textstyle\frac{M_{L}}{M[n,r]}}m,n,r]=1,\,\,\forall\,m\in Z_{M[n,r]}\text{.}`$ |  | (12) |

Furthermore, we call the induced set of elements a *superposition
system* $`\mathscr{F}(I)`$:

|     |                                                                 |     |
|-----|-----------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \phi_{m,n,r}\in\mathscr{F}(I)\Leftrightarrow I[m,n,r]=1\text{.}
       ```                                                              |     |

It follows
from ([12](#S4.E12 "In Definition 8 (Superposition Systems and Admissibility) ‣ IV-B Superposition Systems ‣ IV Construction of Superposition Systems ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
that the first $`M[n,r]`$ modulates of each selected superposition
window are included in $`\mathscr{F}(I)`$, and thus we later suppress
the dependence of $`I`$ on frequency bin index $`m`$ when possible, by
abbreviating $`I[\cdot,n,r]`$ as $`I[n,r]`$.

## V Superposition Frames: Main Results

Starting from a Gabor system $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$, we see that any
superposition system
$`\mathscr{F}(I)\subset\cup_{r}\mathscr{G}(w_{r},a,b_{L})`$ effectively
yields a “variable-resolution” subsampled short-time Fourier transform,
defined for all $`m\in\mathbb{Z}_{M_{L}}`$ and $`n,r\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}`$
as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
X[m,n,r]\triangleq\begin{cases}\langle x,\phi_{m,n,r}\rangle&\text{if $\phi_{m,n,r}\in\mathscr{F}(I)$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
``` |  | (13) |

Consequently, we now establish conditions under which superposition
systems $`\mathscr{F}(I)`$ form frames for $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$, in
analogy to the relation between a Gabor frame and its corresponding
fixed-resolution short-time Fourier transform.

### V-A General Case: Sufficiency

To begin our analysis, consider the case of an admissible selection
function $`I[m,n,r]`$ for which $`M[n,r]=M_{\textrm{g}}`$ for all
$`n,r\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}`$, corresponding to the notion of a *global*
frequency lattice of arbitrary resolution: $`b_{\textrm{g}}\mathbb{Z}`$
with $`b_{\textrm{g}}=L/M_{\textrm{g}}`$. Our first result, proved in
the appendix, ensures that the induced superposition system
$`\mathscr{F}(I)`$ is a frame for $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ if the following
test condition holds.

###### Theorem 1 (Sufficiency Condition, Superposition Frames)

Fix a Gabor system $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,\cdot)`$ on $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$,
with $`N\!=\!L/a`$, $`w`$ real and nonnegative, and define for
$`s,t\!\in\!\mathbb{Z}_{L}`$, $`n,r\!\in\!\mathbb{Z}_{N}`$, the term

|     |                                                            |     |
|-----|------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \beta_{nr}(s,t)\triangleq w_{r}[t-na]w_{r}[t-na-s]\text{.}
       ```                                                         |     |

Let $`I[n,r]`$ be *any* admissible selection function for which
$`M[n,r]=M_{\textrm{g}}`$ for some
$`M_{\textrm{g}}\in\{1,2,\ldots,L\}`$. Then, for index term
$`k\in\{\lceil(t-(L-1))/M_{\textrm{g}}\rceil,\ldots,\lfloor t/M_{\textrm{g}}\rfloor\}`$,
the condition

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\forall t\in\mathbb{Z}_{L},\,\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}I[n,r]\Big(\beta_{nr}(0,t)-\sum_{k\neq 0}\beta_{nr}(kM_{\textrm{g}},t)\Big)>0
``` |  | (14) |

is sufficient to guarantee that the superposition system
$`\mathscr{F}(I)\subset\cup_{r}\mathscr{G}(w_{r},a,b_{L})`$ is a frame
for $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$.

Satisfying the criterion
of ([14](#S5.E14 "In Theorem 1 (Sufficiency Condition, Superposition Frames) ‣ V-A General Case: Sufficiency ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
implies that the underlying frame operator is *strictly diagonally
dominant*—a sufficient condition for strict positive definiteness. This
is a popular criterion in the literature (see, e.g., \[[32](#bib.bib32),
Corollary 6\], \[[33](#bib.bib33), Theorem 8.4.4\]) and, as can be seen
from ([14](#S5.E14 "In Theorem 1 (Sufficiency Condition, Superposition Frames) ‣ V-A General Case: Sufficiency ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")),
takes a particularly simple form in the superposition setting.

### V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds

In
Theorem [1](#Thmthm1 "Theorem 1 (Sufficiency Condition, Superposition Frames) ‣ V-A General Case: Sufficiency ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
above, we considered a general class of superposition frames associated
with an arbitrary frequency lattice $`b_{\textrm{g}}\mathbb{Z}`$. In
Theorems [2](#Thmthm2 "Theorem 2 (Local and Global Superposition Frames) ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
and [3](#Thmthm3 "Theorem 3 (Superposition Frame Bound Properties) ‣ Proof: ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
below, we study two distinct classes of superposition systems using
non-uniform (local) and uniform (global) modulation structures defined
as follows.

###### Definition 9 (Admissible Selection Functions $`{I^{l}}`$ and $`{I^{\textrm{g}}}`$)

Fix a Gabor system $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,L/M)`$, associate to it any ordered
partition function $`\widetilde{I}[n,r]`$, and define

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle M_{r}`$ | $`\displaystyle\triangleq\,\,\max\,\left(\operatorname{len}(w_{r}),M\right);\hskip 8.19447ptb_{r}\triangleq L/M_{r}\text{,}`$ |  | (15) |
|  | $`\displaystyle M_{\textrm{g}}`$ | $`\displaystyle\triangleq\!\!\max_{r\,:\,\widetilde{I}[\cdot,r]\,=\,1}M_{r};\hskip 16.38895pt\hskip 16.38895ptb_{\textrm{g}}\triangleq L/M_{\textrm{g}}\text{.}`$ |  | (16) |

These quantities induce, via $`M[n,r]=M[\cdot,r]=M_{r}`$ or
$`M[n,r]=M_{\textrm{g}}`$ constant, respective classes of *local* and
*global* admissible selection functions $`I^{l}[m,n,r]`$ and
$`I^{\textrm{g}}[m,n,r]`$. Note that when the admissible selection
functions $`I_{0}`$ or $`I_{N-1}`$ occur, the constants
of ([15](#S5.E15 "In Definition 9 (Admissible Selection Functions 𝐼^𝑙 and 𝐼^"g") ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
and ([16](#S5.E16 "In Definition 9 (Admissible Selection Functions 𝐼^𝑙 and 𝐼^"g") ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
are equal.

We now show that superposition systems $`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})`$
and $`\mathscr{F}(I^{l})`$ are frames for $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$. Later, we
will verify that such frames admit *diagonal* frame operators. This
special structure leads not only to fast reconstruction algorithms, but
also to the preservation of lower frame bounds.

###### Theorem 2 (Local and Global Superposition Frames)

Let $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ be a Gabor frame for $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$, with
$`w`$ real and nonnegative. Then for any choice of admissible selection
functions $`I^{l}`$ and $`I^{\textrm{g}}`$, the local and global
superposition systems $`\mathscr{F}(I^{l})`$ and
$`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})`$ are also frames for $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$.

###### Proof:

As our finite-dimensional setting implies the existence of an upper
frame bound for any admissible $`I`$, only the existence of a lower
frame bound need be established. The proof proceeds via
Lemma [3](#Thmlem3 "Lemma 3 (Superadditivity of Superposition Energy) ‣ III Superposition Windows ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
and an iterative argument.

To begin, consider the admissible selection function
$`I^{\textrm{g}}_{0}[m,n,r]=I^{l}_{0}[m,n,r]`$ induced by an $`N`$-part
ordered partition, which is associated to the event that no merging of
windows in $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ occurs, and hence
$`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}}_{0})=\mathscr{G}(w,a,b_{\textrm{g}})`$,
with $`b_{\textrm{g}}=L/M_{\textrm{g}}`$ and
$`M_{\textrm{g}}=\max(\operatorname{len}(w),M)`$ according
to ([16](#S5.E16 "In Definition 9 (Admissible Selection Functions 𝐼^𝑙 and 𝐼^"g") ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")).
Lemma [1](#Thmlem1 "Lemma 1 ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
then ensures that
$`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}}_{0})=\mathscr{F}(I^{l}_{0})`$ is a frame
for $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$, with maximal lower frame bound
$`M_{\textrm{g}}=\min_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|\mathcal{T}_{na}w[t]|^{2}>0`$.

Next consider any admissible selection function
$`I^{\textrm{g}}_{1}[m,n,r]`$ induced by an $`(N-1)`$-part ordered
partition, corresponding to the case that *exactly one* pair of windows
$`w\equiv w_{0}`$ from the initial Gabor frame $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ is
merged via the superposition sum
of ([8](#S3.E8 "In Definition 6 (Superposition Window) ‣ III Superposition Windows ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")).
In this case, there exists one $`n^{*}\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}`$ such that
$`I^{\textrm{g}}_{1}[0,n^{*},1]=1`$, and
so ([12](#S4.E12 "In Definition 8 (Superposition Systems and Admissibility) ‣ IV-B Superposition Systems ‣ IV Construction of Superposition Systems ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
implies that $`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}}_{1})`$ contains the elements
$`\{\mathcal{M}_{mb_{\textrm{g}}}\mathcal{T}_{n^{*}a}w_{1}:m\in\mathbb{Z}_{M_{\textrm{g}}}\}`$.
Each of these elements can in turn be decomposed into the following sum:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{M}_{mb_{\textrm{g}}}\mathcal{T}_{n^{*}a}w_{1}=\mathcal{M}_{mb_{\textrm{g}}}\mathcal{T}_{n^{*}a}w_{0}+\mathcal{M}_{mb_{\textrm{g}}}\mathcal{T}_{(n^{*}+1)a}w_{0}\text{.}
``` |  | (17) |

Since ([16](#S5.E16 "In Definition 9 (Admissible Selection Functions 𝐼^𝑙 and 𝐼^"g") ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
implies
$`M_{\textrm{g}}\geq\operatorname{len}(w_{1})=\operatorname{len}(w_{0}+\mathcal{T}_{a}w_{0})`$,
we obtain
by ([17](#S5.E17 "In Proof: ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
and the superadditivity property of
Lemma [3](#Thmlem3 "Lemma 3 (Superadditivity of Superposition Energy) ‣ III Superposition Windows ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"):

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{m=0}^{M_{\textrm{g}}-1}|\langle x,\mathcal{M}_{mb_{\textrm{g}}}\mathcal{T}_{n^{*}a}w_{1}\rangle|^{2}\geq\sum_{m=0}^{M_{\textrm{g}}-1}|\langle x,\mathcal{M}_{mb_{\textrm{g}}}\mathcal{T}_{n^{*}a}w_{0}\rangle|^{2}\\
+\sum_{m=0}^{M_{\textrm{g}}-1}|\langle x,\mathcal{M}_{mb_{\textrm{g}}}\mathcal{T}_{(n^{*}+1)a}w_{0}\rangle|^{2},\hskip 8.19447pt\forall x\in\mathbb{C}^{L}\text{.}
``` |  | (18) |

Next, noting that by the decomposition
of ([17](#S5.E17 "In Proof: ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")),
we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}}_{1})=\mathscr{G}(w,a,b_{\textrm{g}})\cup\{\mathcal{M}_{mb_{\textrm{g}}}\mathcal{T}_{n^{*}a}w_{1}\}\\
\setminus\left(\{\mathcal{M}_{mb_{\textrm{g}}}\mathcal{T}_{n^{*}a}w_{0}\}\cup\{\mathcal{M}_{mb_{\textrm{g}}}\mathcal{T}_{(n^{*}+1)a}w_{0}\}\right)\text{,}
``` |  | (19) |

we see
that ([18](#S5.E18 "In Proof: ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
and ([19](#S5.E19 "In Proof: ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
together imply that for all $`x\in\mathbb{C}^{L}`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{\phi\in\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}}_{1})}|\langle x,\phi_{m,n,r}\rangle|^{2}\geq\!\!\!\sum_{\phi\in\mathscr{G}(w,a,b_{\textrm{g}})}|\langle x,\phi_{m,n}\rangle|^{2}\text{.}
``` |  | (20) |

Since
$`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b_{\textrm{g}})=\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}}_{0})`$ is a
frame, the existence of a lower frame bound for
$`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}}_{1})`$ is guaranteed
by ([20](#S5.E20 "In Proof: ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")).

Now consider the general case in which $`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})`$
contains multiple merges. Since our construction ensures that all
admissible selection functions can be obtained by iterative partitioning
in the manner above, we can always recover the inequality
of ([20](#S5.E20 "In Proof: ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
for $`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})`$ and any $`b_{\textrm{g}}`$ according
to ([16](#S5.E16 "In Definition 9 (Admissible Selection Functions 𝐼^𝑙 and 𝐼^"g") ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")),
by linearity of superposition and repeated application of
Lemma [3](#Thmlem3 "Lemma 3 (Superadditivity of Superposition Energy) ‣ III Superposition Windows ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction").

A similar iterative argument holds for $`\mathscr{F}(I^{l}_{1})`$, with
$`b_{\textrm{g}}`$ replaced by $`b_{0}`$
from ([15](#S5.E15 "In Definition 9 (Admissible Selection Functions 𝐼^𝑙 and 𝐼^"g") ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")).
In place
of ([19](#S5.E19 "In Proof: ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
we obtain

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathscr{F}(I^{l}_{1})=\left(\mathscr{G}(w,a,b_{0})\cup\{\mathcal{M}_{mb_{1}}\mathcal{T}_{n^{*}a}w_{1}\}\right)\\
\setminus\left(\{\mathcal{M}_{mb_{0}}\mathcal{T}_{n^{*}a}w_{0}\}\cup\{\mathcal{M}_{mb_{0}}\mathcal{T}_{(n^{*}+1)a}w_{0}\}\right)\text{,}
``` |  | (21) |

with $`b_{1}=L/M_{1}`$ according
to ([15](#S5.E15 "In Definition 9 (Admissible Selection Functions 𝐼^𝑙 and 𝐼^"g") ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")).
Note that

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle M_{1}`$ | $`\displaystyle\geq\operatorname{len}(w_{1})=\operatorname{len}(w_{0}+\mathcal{T}_{a}w_{0})\text{,}`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle M_{0}`$ | $`\displaystyle\geq\operatorname{len}(w_{0})\text{,~~and }M_{1}\geq M_{0}\text{;}`$ |  |

thus, we may apply
Lemma [3](#Thmlem3 "Lemma 3 (Superadditivity of Superposition Energy) ‣ III Superposition Windows ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
to the latter three terms
of ([21](#S5.E21 "In Proof: ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")),
yielding the required result for $`\mathscr{F}(I^{l}_{1})`$: for all
$`x\in\mathbb{C}^{L}`$,

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{\phi\in\mathscr{F}(I^{l}_{1})}|\langle x,\phi_{m,n,r}\rangle|^{2}\geq\!\!\!\sum_{\phi\in\mathscr{G}(w,a,b_{0})}|\langle x,\phi_{m,n,r}\rangle|^{2}\text{.}
``` |  |

As above, the proof for general $`\mathscr{F}(I^{l})`$ then follows. ∎

Thus we see that for every Gabor system on $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ and any
associated ordered partition function, setting $`M[n,r]`$ in accordance
with $`M_{r}`$ or $`M_{\textrm{g}}`$ will yield local or global
superposition frames. Moreover, as we detail later, the iterative
arguments employed above suggest precise algorithmic constructions.

We now proceed to establish the important property that, for all local
and global $`{I[m,n,r]}`$ of
Definition [9](#Thmdefn9 "Definition 9 (Admissible Selection Functions 𝐼^𝑙 and 𝐼^"g") ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"),
*superposition frames preserve lower frame bounds*, thus ensuring
numerical stability of the resultant representation. The following
result also formulates the corresponding minimax-optimal superposition
frame bounds.

###### Theorem 3 (Superposition Frame Bound Properties)

Let $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ be a frame for $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$, with
associated maximal lower frame bound $`A>0`$. Then for any admissible
$`I^{l}`$ and $`I^{\textrm{g}}`$:

1.  1.
    The quantity $`A`$ remains a valid lower frame bound for both
    $`\mathscr{F}(I^{l})`$ and $`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})`$.
2.  2.
    The *minimum* maximal lower superposition frame bound over all
    admissible $`I^{l}`$ and $`I^{\textrm{g}}`$ is

    |  |  |  |
    |----|----|----|
    |  |
    ``` math
    A_{\mathrm{opt}}=\frac{L}{b^{\mathrm{max}}}\cdot\min_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{L}}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|\mathcal{T}_{na}w[t]|^{2}\right)\text{,}
    ``` |  |

    with $`b^{\text{max}}=\min(b,L/\operatorname{len}(w))`$. It is
    attained in the absence of merging:
    $`\mathscr{F}(I^{l})=\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})=\mathscr{G}(w,a,b^{\mathrm{max}})`$.
3.  3.
    The *maximum* minimal upper superposition frame bound over all
    admissible $`I^{l}`$ and $`I^{\textrm{g}}`$ is

    |  |  |  |
    |----|----|----|
    |  |
    ``` math
    B_{\mathrm{opt}}=\frac{L}{b^{\mathrm{min}}}\cdot\max_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{L}}\left(\Big|\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{T}_{na}w[t]\Big|^{2}\right)\text{,}
    ``` |  |

    with $`b^{\mathrm{min}}=\min(b,1)`$. It is attained when all
    translates of $`w`$ have been merged:
    $`\mathscr{F}(I^{l})=\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})=\mathscr{G}(w_{N-1},L,b^{\mathrm{min}})`$,
    with $`w_{N-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{T}_{na}w[t]`$.

###### Proof:

Let $`S`$ be the frame operator associated to $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$,
with smallest eigenvalue $`A`$, and observe that
$`\min_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{L}}S[t,t]\geq A`$ by the Schur-Horn convexity
theorem. Now, for any admissible $`I^{\textrm{g}}`$ or $`I^{l}`$, the
proof of
Theorem [2](#Thmthm2 "Theorem 2 (Local and Global Superposition Frames) ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
shows that the maximal lower frame bound of
$`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})`$ or $`\mathscr{F}(I^{l})`$ is bounded
from below by that of some $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b^{\prime})`$, with
$`b^{\prime}`$ denoting respectively $`b_{\textrm{g}}`$ or $`b_{0}`$.
Therefore, [1](#S5.I1.i1 "item 1 ‣ Theorem 3 (Superposition Frame Bound Properties) ‣ Proof: ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
will follow if we can show the maximal lower frame bound of
$`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b^{\prime})`$ to be no less than
$`\min_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{L}}S[t,t]`$. To do so, note that
Lemma [1](#Thmlem1 "Lemma 1 ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
implies that the frame operator $`S^{\prime}`$ of
$`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b^{\prime})`$ is diagonal, with smallest eigenvalue
$`\min_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{L}}S^{\prime}[t,t]=(M^{\prime}/M)\min_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{L}}S[t,t]`$; ([15](#S5.E15 "In Definition 9 (Admissible Selection Functions 𝐼^𝑙 and 𝐼^"g") ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
and ([16](#S5.E16 "In Definition 9 (Admissible Selection Functions 𝐼^𝑙 and 𝐼^"g") ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
then yield $`M^{\prime}\geq M`$.

Next recall that superposition frames are induced from a Gabor frame
$`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ by merging $`n\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}`$ neighboring
window translates $`\mathcal{T}_{na}w`$. By the above argument,
$`\min_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{L}}S^{\prime}[t,t]`$ is itself a maximal lower
frame bound for the case $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b^{\prime})`$ attained
whenever *no* window translates are merged; likewise, the merging of
*all* translates yields $`\mathscr{G}(w_{N-1},L,b^{\prime\prime})`$ for
some unique $`b^{\prime\prime}`$, with minimal upper frame bound
$`\max_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{L}}S^{\prime\prime}[t,t]`$.

To show that these cases are in fact extremal as claimed, we appeal to
the same iterative argument used to prove
Theorem [2](#Thmthm2 "Theorem 2 (Local and Global Superposition Frames) ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction").
There, the superadditivity property of
Lemma [3](#Thmlem3 "Lemma 3 (Superadditivity of Superposition Energy) ‣ III Superposition Windows ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
was invoked to show that for any admissible $`I^{\textrm{g}}`$ or
$`I^{l}`$, merging superposition windows *cannot decrease* the overall
energy of the resultant frame coefficients. Thus, the case of
$`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b^{\prime})`$ considered above represents attainment
of the *minimum* maximal lower superposition frame bound, and moreover
$`M^{\prime}=\max(M,\operatorname{len}(w))`$
via ([15](#S5.E15 "In Definition 9 (Admissible Selection Functions 𝐼^𝑙 and 𝐼^"g") ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
and ([16](#S5.E16 "In Definition 9 (Admissible Selection Functions 𝐼^𝑙 and 𝐼^"g") ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")).
Likewise, $`\mathscr{G}(w_{N-1},L,b^{\prime\prime})`$ yields the
*maximum* minimal upper superposition frame bound, with
$`M^{\prime\prime}=\max(M,\operatorname{len}(w_{N-1}))`$.
Lemma [2](#Thmlem2 "Lemma 2 (Localized Parseval Property) ‣ III Superposition Windows ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
then establishes the bound directly:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\sum_{\phi\in\mathscr{G}(w_{N-1},L,b^{\prime\prime})}\!\!\!\!|\langle x,\phi_{m,n,r}\rangle|^{2}=\textstyle\sum_{m=0}^{M^{\prime\prime}-1}|\langle x,\mathcal{M}_{mb^{\prime\prime}}w_{N-1}\rangle|^{2}`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=M^{\prime\prime}\textstyle\sum_{t=0}^{L-1}|x[t]|^{2}|w_{N-1}[t]|^{2}\leq\|x\|^{2}M^{\prime\prime}\displaystyle\max_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{L}}|w_{N-1}[t]|^{2}\text{,}`$ |  |

and the proof is completed by noting that as $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ is
assumed a Gabor frame for $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$, the covering condition of
Remark [2](#Thmrem2 "Remark 2 (Covering Condition) ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
implies that
$`|\operatorname{supp}(w_{N-1})|=\operatorname{len}(w_{N-1})=L`$, and
hence $`M^{\prime\prime}=\max(M,L)`$ as claimed. ∎

## VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames

We now show how the special structure of our superposition construction
gives rise to a number of efficient reconstruction procedures. For any
signal of interest $`x\in\mathbb{C}^{L}`$, the superposition frame
analysis coefficients $`X[m,n,r]=I[m,n,r]\langle x,\phi_{m,n,r}\rangle`$
can be computed via fast Fourier transform (FFT) once an admissible
selection function has been specified. Superposition frames also enable
*fast* (FFT-based) *reconstruction* from the corresponding analysis
coefficients, in contrast to the general case of $`\mathcal{O}(L^{3})`$
complexity for frame-based reconstruction via inversion of the frame
operator.

We first provide a fast constant-overlap-add reconstruction method,
which obviates the need for canonical dual frames. We next show that
reconstruction via the canonical dual can also proceed by way of a
pointwise modification of each superposition window $`\phi_{0,n,r}`$,
followed by the application of FFTs, as in the case of general
nonstationary Gabor frames \[[31](#bib.bib31)\]. Third, we show that in
settings reminiscent of lapped orthogonal transforms, calculation of
canonical dual windows is possible *independently* of any
$`I^{\textrm{g}}`$—in contrast to the typical signal-adaptive setting,
where the structure of the frame operator is a function of the
instantiated signal adaptation. Last, we compare the computational
complexity of these procedures.

### VI-A Reconstruction via the Constant Overlap-Add Method

The classical “overlap-add” approach to signal reconstruction from
short-time Fourier coefficients proceeds as
follows \[[35](#bib.bib35)\]. Recall the covering condition
of ([6](#S2.E6 "In Remark 2 (Covering Condition) ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
which is necessary for a Gabor system $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ to form a
frame for $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$, and also sufficient if
$`M=L/b\geq\operatorname{len}(w)`$. Clearly, this covering condition
holds if translates $`\{\mathcal{T}_{na}w:n\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}\}`$ form a
partition of unity on $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ (see, e.g., the top panel of
Fig. [1(b)](#S2.F1.sf2 "In Fig. 1 ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")),
and to this end we obtain the following definition, long popular in the
signal processing literature.

###### Definition 10 (Constant Overlap-Add Window Constraint)

Fix a Gabor system $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,\cdot)`$ on $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$.
Then, noting the discrete Fourier transform evaluation
$`\widehat{w}[0]=\sum_{t=0}^{L-1}w[t]`$, the window $`w`$ is said to
satisfy the *constant overlap-add* constraint if

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\forall\,t\in\mathbb{Z}_{L},\,\,\,\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}w[t-na]=\frac{\widehat{w}[0]}{a}\text{.}
``` |  | (22) |

To clarify the role of this overlap-add constraint in fast
reconstruction, consider a Gabor frame $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ on
$`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ for which $`w`$
satisfies ([22](#S6.E22 "In Definition 10 (Constant Overlap-Add Window Constraint) ‣ VI-A Reconstruction via the Constant Overlap-Add Method ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")),
with $`M=L/b`$ chosen such that $`M\geq\operatorname{len}(w)`$. The
associated short-time analysis coefficients
$`\{X[m,n]\,:\,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{M},n\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}\}`$ are obtained as
inner products of any $`x\in\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ according
to ([1](#S2.E1 "In Definition 1 (Short-Time Fourier Transform) ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")),
and it is easy to show that

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
x[t]=\frac{a}{\widehat{w}[0]}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\left(\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}X[m,n]e^{2\pi imbt/L}\right)\text{.}
``` |  | (23) |

The constraint thus admits a reconstruction procedure based on the
overlapping additions of a sequence of discrete Fourier transforms on
$`\mathbb{C}^{M}`$.

###### Remark 7 (Superposition Windows Preserve Overlap-Add)

By their *linear* construction, superposition windows $`w_{r}`$ preserve
the constant overlap-add constraint of
Definition [10](#Thmdefn10 "Definition 10 (Constant Overlap-Add Window Constraint) ‣ VI-A Reconstruction via the Constant Overlap-Add Method ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
for any Gabor system $`\mathscr{G}(w_{r},a,\cdot)`$. To see this, note
that the discrete Poisson summation formula on $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$, for
$`N=L/a`$, is given by
$`\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{I}_{0}[t-na]=a^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{a-1}e^{2\pi itkN/L}`$.
Applying this expression to $`\{\mathcal{T}_{na}w:n\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}\}`$
yields the relation

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}w[t-na]=\frac{1}{a}\sum_{k=0}^{a-1}e^{2\pi itkN/L}\widehat{w}[kN]\text{,}
``` |  |

and it follows that the constraint
of ([22](#S6.E22 "In Definition 10 (Constant Overlap-Add Window Constraint) ‣ VI-A Reconstruction via the Constant Overlap-Add Method ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
holds (for a given time lattice constant $`a`$) if the Fourier transform
$`\widehat{w}`$ satisfies

|     |                                                             |     |
|-----|-------------------------------------------------------------|-----|
|     |
       ``` math
       \widehat{w}[kN]=0,\,\,\forall\,k\in\{1,\ldots,a-1\}\text{.}
       ```                                                          |     |

Since
$`\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{w_{r}})\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{w})`$,
in accordance with the argument of
Remark [5](#Thmrem5 "Remark 5 (Fourier Transform Support) ‣ III Superposition Windows ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"),
it follows that
if ([22](#S6.E22 "In Definition 10 (Constant Overlap-Add Window Constraint) ‣ VI-A Reconstruction via the Constant Overlap-Add Method ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
holds for a given $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,\cdot)`$, then it will also hold for
any $`\mathscr{G}(w_{r},a,\cdot)`$, for $`r\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}`$.

The popularity of the overlap-add constraint of
Definition [10](#Thmdefn10 "Definition 10 (Constant Overlap-Add Window Constraint) ‣ VI-A Reconstruction via the Constant Overlap-Add Method ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
is due in large part to its simplicity, coupled with the efficiency of
evaluating ([23](#S6.E23 "In VI-A Reconstruction via the Constant Overlap-Add Method ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")).
An important property of our superposition construction is that it
preserves this constraint not only for Gabor frames
$`\mathscr{G}(w_{r},a,b)`$, but also for all induced superposition
frames $`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})`$ and $`\mathscr{F}(I^{l})`$.

###### Theorem 4 (Superposition Frames Preserve Overlap-Add)

Consider a Gabor frame $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ on $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$
satisfying the constant overlap-add constraint
of ([22](#S6.E22 "In Definition 10 (Constant Overlap-Add Window Constraint) ‣ VI-A Reconstruction via the Constant Overlap-Add Method ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")).
The following statements hold for any $`I^{\textrm{g}}`$, and also for
any $`I^{l}`$, with $`M_{\textrm{g}},b_{\textrm{g}}`$ replaced by
$`M_{r},b_{r}`$.

1.  1.
    The superposition frame $`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})`$ satisfies
    the following *generalized* overlap-add constraint:

    |  |  |  |  |
    |----|----|----|----|
    |  |
    ``` math
    \sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}I^{\textrm{g}}[n,r]\mathcal{T}_{na}w_{r}[t]=\frac{\widehat{w}[0]}{a}\text{.}
    ``` |  | (24) |
2.  2.
    Each $`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})`$ satisfies the overlap-add
    reconstruction property that, for any $`x\in\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ with
    corresponding frame coefficients $`\{X[m,n,r]\}`$ defined
    by ([13](#S5.E13 "In V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")),

    |  |  |  |  |
    |----|----|----|----|
    |  |
    ``` math
    x[t]\!=\!\frac{a}{\widehat{w}[0]}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\!\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}\!\left(\!\frac{1}{M_{\textrm{g}}}\!\!\sum_{m=0}^{M_{\textrm{g}}-1}\!\!\!X[\textstyle{\frac{M_{L}}{M_{\textrm{g}}}}m,n,r]e^{2\pi imb_{\textrm{g}}t/L}\!\right)\!\!\text{.}
    ``` |  | (25) |

###### Proof:

First consider $`I^{\textrm{g}}_{0}[n,r]`$, the global selection
function associated to the event that no windows are merged. In this
case, the generalized overlap-add constraint
of ([24](#S6.E24 "In item 1 ‣ Theorem 4 (Superposition Frames Preserve Overlap-Add) ‣ VI-A Reconstruction via the Constant Overlap-Add Method ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
is satisfied by hypothesis, since it follows directly
from ([22](#S6.E22 "In Definition 10 (Constant Overlap-Add Window Constraint) ‣ VI-A Reconstruction via the Constant Overlap-Add Method ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
that

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}I^{\textrm{g}}_{0}[n,r]\mathcal{T}_{na}w_{r}[t]=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{T}_{na}w[t]=\frac{\widehat{w}[0]}{a}\text{.}
``` |  |

Now consider the global selection function $`I^{\textrm{g}}_{1}[n,r]`$,
corresponding to the case that exactly one pair of windows
$`w\equiv w_{0}`$ is merged. In this case, there exists some
$`n^{*}\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}`$ such that $`I^{\textrm{g}}_{1}[n^{*},1]=1`$.
Thus, the frame $`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}}_{1})`$ contains the element
$`\{\mathcal{T}_{n^{*}a}w_{1}\}`$, which can be decomposed according
to ([8](#S3.E8 "In Definition 6 (Superposition Window) ‣ III Superposition Windows ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{T}_{n^{*}a}w_{1}=\mathcal{T}_{n^{*}a}w_{0}+\mathcal{T}_{(n^{*}+1)a}w_{0}\text{.}
``` |  | (26) |

Admissibility of $`I^{\textrm{g}}_{1}`$ implies that
$`I^{\textrm{g}}_{1}[n^{*},1]=1`$, but that
$`I^{\textrm{g}}_{1}[n^{*},0]=I^{\textrm{g}}_{1}[n^{*}\!+\!1,0]=0`$.
Therefore,
by ([26](#S6.E26 "In Proof: ‣ VI-A Reconstruction via the Constant Overlap-Add Method ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
we have

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}I^{\textrm{g}}_{1}[n,r]\mathcal{T}_{na}w_{r}[t]`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{n^{*}a}w_{1}[t]+\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\hskip 9.80002ptn\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}\setminus\{n^{*},n^{*}\!+\!1\}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!I^{\textrm{g}}_{1}[n,0]\mathcal{T}_{na}w_{0}[t]`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\!\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{T}_{na}w_{0}[t]=\frac{\widehat{w}[0]}{a}\text{,}`$ |  |

and we see
that ([24](#S6.E24 "In item 1 ‣ Theorem 4 (Superposition Frames Preserve Overlap-Add) ‣ VI-A Reconstruction via the Constant Overlap-Add Method ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
holds for $`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}}_{1})`$. Naturally, the selection
function $`I^{\textrm{g}}`$ may index many merged windows—not just one,
as in the case of $`I^{\textrm{g}}_{1}`$. However, repeated application
of the above argument shows that $`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})`$
satisfies ([24](#S6.E24 "In item 1 ‣ Theorem 4 (Superposition Frames Preserve Overlap-Add) ‣ VI-A Reconstruction via the Constant Overlap-Add Method ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
for any $`I^{\textrm{g}}`$.

To prove
Statement [2](#S6.I1.i2 "item 2 ‣ Theorem 4 (Superposition Frames Preserve Overlap-Add) ‣ VI-A Reconstruction via the Constant Overlap-Add Method ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"),
note first that $`M_{\textrm{g}}\geq\operatorname{len}(\phi_{0,n,r})`$
for each $`\phi_{0,n,r}\in\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})`$, in accordance
with ([16](#S5.E16 "In Definition 9 (Admissible Selection Functions 𝐼^𝑙 and 𝐼^"g") ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")).
Recalling that
$`X[m,n,r]=I^{\textrm{g}}[m,n,r]\langle x,\phi_{m,n,r}\rangle`$
by ([13](#S5.E13 "In V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")),
we observe that the innermost summation
of ([25](#S6.E25 "In item 2 ‣ Theorem 4 (Superposition Frames Preserve Overlap-Add) ‣ VI-A Reconstruction via the Constant Overlap-Add Method ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
is recognizable as an inverse discrete Fourier transform on
$`\mathbb{C}^{M_{\textrm{g}}}`$, with
$`M_{\textrm{g}}=L/b_{\textrm{g}}`$. For fixed $`n`$ and
$`t\!-\!na\in\mathbb{Z}_{M_{\textrm{g}}}`$, this term evaluates to
$`I^{\textrm{g}}[n,r]\mathcal{T}_{na}w_{r}[t]x[t]`$:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{1}{M_{\textrm{g}}}\!\!\sum_{m=0}^{M_{\textrm{g}}-1}\!X[\textstyle{\frac{M_{L}}{M_{\textrm{g}}}}m,n,r]e^{2\pi imb_{\textrm{g}}t/L}\!\!\!\ \!\!\!\ \!\!\!\ \!\!\!\ \!\!\!\ \!\!\!\ \!\!\!\ \!\!\underset{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle~}}{{{\hskip 4.90001pt\footnotesize t-na\in\mathbb{Z}_{M_{\textrm{g}}}}}}}{=}\!\!\!\ \!\!\!\ \!\!\!\ \!\!\!\ \!\!\!\ \!\!\!\ \!\!I^{\textrm{g}}[n,r]\mathcal{T}_{na}w_{r}[t]x[t]\text{.}
``` |  |

Perfect reconstruction then follows from the generalized overlap-add
constraint
of ([24](#S6.E24 "In item 1 ‣ Theorem 4 (Superposition Frames Preserve Overlap-Add) ‣ VI-A Reconstruction via the Constant Overlap-Add Method ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")),
as

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\frac{a}{\widehat{w}[0]}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}I^{\textrm{g}}[n,r]\mathcal{T}_{na}w_{r}[t]x[t]=\frac{a}{\widehat{w}[0]}\frac{\widehat{w}[0]}{a}x[t]=x[t]\text{.}
``` |  |

For the case of a local selection function $`I^{l}`$, note that the
generalized overlap-add constraint
of ([24](#S6.E24 "In item 1 ‣ Theorem 4 (Superposition Frames Preserve Overlap-Add) ‣ VI-A Reconstruction via the Constant Overlap-Add Method ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
is still implied
by ([22](#S6.E22 "In Definition 10 (Constant Overlap-Add Window Constraint) ‣ VI-A Reconstruction via the Constant Overlap-Add Method ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")),
since the argument for the case of admissible $`I^{\textrm{g}}`$ holds
independently of the modulation structure employed. Consequently, by
substituting $`M_{r},b_{r}`$ for $`M_{\textrm{g}},b_{\textrm{g}}`$ and
noting that $`M_{r}\geq\operatorname{len}(\phi_{0,n,r})`$
by ([15](#S5.E15 "In Definition 9 (Admissible Selection Functions 𝐼^𝑙 and 𝐼^"g") ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
for each $`\phi_{0,n,r}\in\mathscr{F}(I^{l})`$, we see that the result
of ([25](#S6.E25 "In item 2 ‣ Theorem 4 (Superposition Frames Preserve Overlap-Add) ‣ VI-A Reconstruction via the Constant Overlap-Add Method ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
also holds for all $`\mathscr{F}(I^{l})`$. ∎

### VI-B Reconstruction via Canonical Dual Superposition Frames

We next develop the reconstruction properties of our superposition
families in a frame-theoretic context, noting that they qualify as
“painless nonorthogonal expansions” \[[36](#bib.bib36)\], and that the
development below also holds for more general nonstationary Gabor
frames \[[31](#bib.bib31)\]. In analogy to
Definition [4](#Thmdefn4 "Definition 4 (Gabor Frame Operator) ‣ II Preliminaries ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"),
we associate a superposition frame operator
$`S_{I}:\mathbb{C}^{L}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ through its action on
any $`x\in\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ as
$`S_{I}x=\sum_{\phi\in\mathscr{F}(I)}\langle x,\phi_{m,n,r}\rangle\phi_{m,n,r}`$.

###### Definition 11 (Superposition Frame Operator, Walnut Form)

The discrete Walnut representations of superposition frame operators
$`S_{I^{\textrm{g}}}`$ and $`S_{I^{l}}`$ are respectively given by the
$`L\times L`$ positive semi-definite matrices with entries

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle S_{I^{\textrm{g}}}[t,t^{\prime}]`$ | $`\displaystyle\triangleq\!M_{\textrm{g}}\,\mathbb{I}_{M_{\textrm{g}}\backslash(t-t^{\prime})}[t\!-\!t^{\prime}]\!\!\sum_{n,r=0}^{N-1}I^{\textrm{g}}[n,r]\mathcal{T}_{na}w_{r}[t]\overline{\mathcal{T}_{na}w_{r}[t^{\prime}]}\text{,}`$ |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle S_{I^{l}}[t,t^{\prime}]`$ | $`\displaystyle\triangleq\!\sum_{n,r=0}^{N-1}I^{l}[n,r]M_{r}\mathbb{I}_{M_{r}\backslash(t-t^{\prime})}[t\!-\!t^{\prime}]\mathcal{T}_{na}w_{r}[t]\overline{\mathcal{T}_{na}w_{r}[t^{\prime}]}\text{.}`$ |  |

Theorem [2](#Thmthm2 "Theorem 2 (Local and Global Superposition Frames) ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
implies that any Gabor frame $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ and admissible
$`I^{\textrm{g}}`$ or $`I^{l}`$ together give rise to a superposition
frame, and hence the corresponding superposition frame operators are of
full rank. Thus, to each global superposition frame
$`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})=\{\phi_{m,n,r}\}`$ corresponds a unique
canonical dual frame $`\{\widetilde{\phi}_{m,n,r}\}`$, whose elements
are obtained in turn as
$`\widetilde{\phi}_{m,n,r}\triangleq S_{I^{\textrm{g}}}^{-1}\phi_{m,n,r}`$.
Accordingly, when $`S_{I^{\textrm{g}}}`$ is diagonal we may index
elements of $`\{\widetilde{\phi}_{m,n,r}\}`$ by the same admissible
$`I^{\textrm{g}}`$, and we obtain the following reconstruction property:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\forall x\in\mathbb{C}^{L},t\in\mathbb{Z}_{L},\,\,\,x[t]=\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\hskip 9.80002ptm,n,r:I^{\textrm{g}}[m,n,r]=1}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\langle x,\phi_{m,n,r}\rangle\widetilde{\phi}_{m,n,r}[t]\text{,}
``` |  |

with the above also holding for local $`I^{l}`$ by
Theorem [2](#Thmthm2 "Theorem 2 (Local and Global Superposition Frames) ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction").
We thus denote by $`\widetilde{\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})}`$ or
$`\widetilde{\mathscr{F}(I^{l})}`$ the corresponding dual frames, and
observe the following consequence of the Walnut representation of
Definition [11](#Thmdefn11 "Definition 11 (Superposition Frame Operator, Walnut Form) ‣ VI-B Reconstruction via Canonical Dual Superposition Frames ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
above.

###### Theorem 5 (Fast Inversion via Canonical Dual)

For any $`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})`$, $`\mathscr{F}(I^{l})`$ derived
from a Gabor frame $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ on $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$, the
corresponding operators $`S_{I^{\textrm{g}}}`$ and $`S_{I^{l}}`$ are
*diagonal*, and each canonical dual frame element takes the form

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\widetilde{\phi}_{m,n,r}[t]=\frac{\mathcal{M}_{mb_{L}}\mathcal{T}_{na}w_{r}[t]}{M_{\textrm{g}}\textstyle{\sum_{n^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}\sum_{r^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}}I^{\textrm{g}}[n^{\prime},r^{\prime}]\left|\mathcal{T}_{n^{\prime}a}w_{r^{\prime}}[t]\right|^{2}}
``` |  | (27) |

for $`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})`$, and similarly for
$`\mathscr{F}(I^{l})`$ with respect to each $`M_{r}`$.

Note that the corresponding formula for the nonstationary Gabor frames
of \[[31](#bib.bib31)\] in the diagonal case is similar
to ([27](#S6.E27 "In Theorem 5 (Fast Inversion via Canonical Dual) ‣ VI-B Reconstruction via Canonical Dual Superposition Frames ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")).
However, in the superposition frame setting, the constraints on the
window structure not only preserve lower frame bounds and yield fast
inversion via the constant overlap-add method, but also enable
signal-independent evaluation of the canonical dual in certain cases, as
we now show.

### VI-C Adaptive Lapped Superposition Frames

Reconstruction via the canonical dual
$`\widetilde{\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})}`$ according
to ([27](#S6.E27 "In Theorem 5 (Fast Inversion via Canonical Dual) ‣ VI-B Reconstruction via Canonical Dual Superposition Frames ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
requires knowledge of the admissible selection function
$`I^{\textrm{g}}[n,r]`$ corresponding to a given signal adaptation. This
stands in contrast not only to the usual Gabor setting, wherein the form
of the canonical dual frame can be obtained immediately, but also to the
constant overlap-add approach described in
Section [VI-A](#S6.SS1 "VI-A Reconstruction via the Constant Overlap-Add Method ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"),
which avoids computation of the canonical dual entirely. However, by
coupling our superposition construction with the following *neighbor
overlap* condition, we are able to compute
$`\widetilde{\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})}`$ *prior* to adaptation—that
is, without knowledge of which ordered partition function will be used
in subsequent signal analysis.

###### Definition 12 (Neighbor Overlap Condition)

Let $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,\cdot)`$ be a Gabor system on $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$,
with $`N=L/a`$. It is said to satisfy the *neighbor overlap* condition
if, for all $`n,n^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\operatorname{supp}\left(\mathcal{T}_{na}w\right)\,\cap\,\operatorname{supp}\left(\mathcal{T}_{n^{\prime}a}w\right)=\emptyset\,\,\text{if}\,\,|n-n^{\prime}|>1\text{.}
``` |  | (28) |

Any admissible selection function preserves the neighbor overlap
property, leading to the following notion of *lapped superposition
frames*, whose properties we develop below.

###### Definition 13 (Adaptive Lapped Superposition Frames)

Let $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,\cdot)`$ be a Gabor frame on $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$
that *simultaneously* satisfies the overlap-add constraint
of ([22](#S6.E22 "In Definition 10 (Constant Overlap-Add Window Constraint) ‣ VI-A Reconstruction via the Constant Overlap-Add Method ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
and the neighbor-overlap condition
of ([28](#S6.E28 "In Definition 12 (Neighbor Overlap Condition) ‣ VI-C Adaptive Lapped Superposition Frames ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")).
Then for any admissible $`I^{\textrm{g}}`$, we call
$`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})`$ an adaptive *lapped superposition
frame*.

Note that the overlap-add constraint
of ([22](#S6.E22 "In Definition 10 (Constant Overlap-Add Window Constraint) ‣ VI-A Reconstruction via the Constant Overlap-Add Method ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
ensures a partition of unity by window translates, while the neighbor
overlap condition
of ([28](#S6.E28 "In Definition 12 (Neighbor Overlap Condition) ‣ VI-C Adaptive Lapped Superposition Frames ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
is also required in the case of lapped *orthogonal* transforms (see,
e.g., \[[7](#bib.bib7)\]). While our construction retains the flavor of
time-varying lapped transforms \[[37](#bib.bib37), [38](#bib.bib38)\],
we emphasize that the resultant frames can avoid the lack of translation
invariance inherent in the orthogonal setting, while still ensuring fast
reconstruction.

We show below that if $`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})`$ is a lapped
superposition frame derived from a Gabor frame $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$,
then its canonical dual frame elements may be pre-computed. This
situation is illustrated in
Fig. [3](#S6.F3 "Fig. 3 ‣ VI-C Adaptive Lapped Superposition Frames ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"),

|  |  |  |  |

and note that, for any global selection function $`I^{\textrm{g}}`$,
$`\phi_{\cdot,n,r}\in\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})`$ and its dual

canonical dual frame of any $`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})`$ can be
computed independently of any ordered partition function, and,
therefore, can be computed *prior* to observing any data.

###### Theorem 6 (Canonical Duals of Adaptive Lapped Frames)

Let $`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})`$ arise from a Gabor frame
$`\mathscr{G}(w,a,\cdot)`$ for $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$
satisfying ([22](#S6.E22 "In Definition 10 (Constant Overlap-Add Window Constraint) ‣ VI-A Reconstruction via the Constant Overlap-Add Method ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
and ([28](#S6.E28 "In Definition 12 (Neighbor Overlap Condition) ‣ VI-C Adaptive Lapped Superposition Frames ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")).
Then every
$`\widetilde{\phi}_{m,n,r}\in\widetilde{\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})}`$
can be constructed by modulations $`\mathcal{M}_{mb_{L}}`$ and
translations $`\mathcal{T}_{na}`$ of lapped windows corresponding to
each $`r`$ as follows:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\widetilde{\phi}_{0,0,r}[t]\triangleq\,\frac{1}{M_{\textrm{g}}}\textstyle\!\cdot\!\begin{cases}\frac{w[t]}{\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|w[t-na]|^{2}}&\text{if $t\in L_{r}$,}\\
\frac{a}{\widehat{w}[0]}&\text{if $t\in C_{r}$,}\\
\frac{w[t-(r+1)a]}{\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|w[t-na]|^{2}}&\text{if $t\in R_{r}$.}\end{cases}
``` |  |

Here the sets $`L_{r},C_{r},R_{r}`$ are defined
in ([29](#S6.E29 "In VI-C Adaptive Lapped Superposition Frames ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")),
and we note that only the expression for $`C_{r}`$ is to be employed
when $`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})`$ is comprised entirely of
modulations of $`w_{N-1}`$.

We note that many popular Gabor systems $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,\cdot)`$
satisfy the requirements of this theorem—including triangular, Hamming,
and raised-cosine windows $`w`$ at $`50\%`$ overlap—thus enabling a
variety of new adaptive, lapped superposition frame families that all
admit fast reconstruction.

### VI-D Adaptive Dyadic Superposition Frames

Here we construct a class of so-called *dyadic* superposition frames
that admit offline canonical dual construction even when the overlap-add
constraint
of ([22](#S6.E22 "In Definition 10 (Constant Overlap-Add Window Constraint) ‣ VI-A Reconstruction via the Constant Overlap-Add Method ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
is *not* satisfied. We base this construction on the notion of *dyadic*
ordered partition functions, which may be thought of as indexing binary
trees.

###### Definition 14 (Dyadic Admissible Selection Functions)

Let the number of translates $`N`$ of $`w`$ in $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ be
a power of two, and define the set
$`\mathscr{H}\triangleq\{0,1,\ldots,\log_{2}N\}`$. An ordered partition
function $`\widetilde{I}^{d}[n,r]`$ is *dyadic* if it satisfies the
conditions of
Definition [7](#Thmdefn7 "Definition 7 (Ordered Partition Functions) ‣ IV-A Ordered Partition Functions ‣ IV Construction of Superposition Systems ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
and

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\widetilde{I}^{d}[n,r]=1\,\,\text{\emph{only} if}\,\,r=2^{h}-1\,\,\text{for some}\,\,h\in\mathscr{H}\text{.}
``` |  |

We denote by $`I^{d}[m,n,r]`$ a *dyadic* admissible selection function
induced by $`\widetilde{I}^{d}[n,r]`$ and a global frequency lattice
constant $`b_{\textrm{g}}=L/M_{\textrm{g}}`$, with $`M_{\textrm{g}}`$
defined according
to ([16](#S5.E16 "In Definition 9 (Admissible Selection Functions 𝐼^𝑙 and 𝐼^"g") ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")).

Viewing the $`N`$ translates of $`w`$ in $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ as
leaves of a binary tree of height $`\log_{2}N`$, a dyadic ordered
partition function selects windows corresponding to some tree level
$`h`$.

###### Definition 15 (Dyadic Gabor and Superposition Frames)

Fix an initial Gabor frame $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$, such that $`N=L/a`$
is a power of two, and let $`h\in\mathscr{H}`$ index *height* in a
binary tree. Now restrict $`r\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}`$ to the index set
$`\mathscr{R}\triangleq\{2^{h}-1\}`$, and fix for each
$`r\in\mathscr{R}`$ a time lattice constant $`a_{r}\triangleq a(r+1)`$,
and a dyadic admissible selection function $`I^{d}`$ with associated
global frequency lattice constant $`b_{\textrm{g}}`$. Then:

1.  1.
    We define *dyadic superposition Gabor frame*
    $`G^{d}_{r}(b_{\textrm{g}})`$ for all $`r\in\mathscr{R}`$, and their
    union $`G^{d}_{\cup r}(b_{\textrm{g}})`$, as follows:

    |  |  |  |  |
    |----|----|----|----|
    |  | $`\displaystyle G^{d}_{r}(b_{\textrm{g}})`$ | $`\displaystyle\triangleq\mathscr{G}(w_{r},a_{r},b_{\textrm{g}})\subseteq\mathscr{G}(w_{r},a,b_{L})\text{,}`$ |  |
    |  | $`\displaystyle G^{d}_{\cup r}(b_{\textrm{g}})`$ | $`\displaystyle\triangleq\displaystyle{\cup_{r\in\mathscr{R}}}G_{r}^{d}(b_{\textrm{g}})\subseteq\cup_{r=1}^{N-1}\mathscr{G}(w_{r},a,b_{L})\text{.}`$ |  |
2.  2.
    We call
    $`\mathscr{F}(I^{d})\!\subset\!G^{d}_{\cup r}(b_{\textrm{g}})`$ a
    *dyadic superposition frame*.

The fact that $`\mathscr{F}(I^{d})`$ and every dyadic
$`G^{d}_{r}(b_{\textrm{g}})=\mathscr{G}(w_{r},a_{r},b_{\textrm{g}})`$
are frames for $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ follows from the assumption that
$`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ is a frame, coupled with the result of
Theorem [2](#Thmthm2 "Theorem 2 (Local and Global Superposition Frames) ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction").

An example of this construction is illustrated in
Fig. [4](#S6.F4 "Fig. 4 ‣ VI-D Adaptive Dyadic Superposition Frames ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"):

Fig. 4: Repeated superposition merges of Hamming windows, following the
structure of a binary tree, are shown in panels (a-c), along with the
canonical dual windows associated to each corresponding Gabor system
(e-g). A dyadic superposition frame $`\mathscr{F}(I^{d})`$ can be formed
from the selected unmodulated elements of (d), which in turn will admit
the corresponding canonical dual windows shown in (h), computed
according
to ([27](#S6.E27 "In Theorem 5 (Fast Inversion via Canonical Dual) ‣ VI-B Reconstruction via Canonical Dual Superposition Frames ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")).

The left panel shows Hamming windows $`w\equiv w_{0}`$ at 50% overlap,
along with the corresponding dyadically-indexed superposition windows
$`w_{1}`$ and $`w_{3}`$, and an example dyadic superposition frame
$`\mathscr{F}(I^{d})`$ at bottom. The right panel shows canonical duals
associated to each dyadic superposition Gabor frame
$`G^{d}_{r}(\cdot)`$, for $`r\in\{0,1,3\}`$, along with the

bottom right. The fact that $`\widetilde{\mathscr{F}(I^{d})}`$ contains
elements from each of these individual dual Gabor frames
$`\widetilde{G^{d}_{r}(\cdot)}`$ is verified by the following theorem.

###### Theorem 7 (Canonical Duals of Adaptive Dyadic Frames)

Let a dyadic superposition frame
$`\mathscr{F}(I^{d})\subset G^{d}_{\cup r}(b_{\textrm{g}})`$ arise from
a Gabor frame $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ for $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ satisfying
the neighbor-overlap condition
of ([28](#S6.E28 "In Definition 12 (Neighbor Overlap Condition) ‣ VI-C Adaptive Lapped Superposition Frames ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")).
Then its canonical dual $`\widetilde{\mathscr{F}(I^{d})}`$ can be
computed by either path of the following commutative diagram, where
$`S_{I^{d}}`$ is the frame operator associated to
$`\mathscr{F}(I^{d})`$, and $`S^{d}_{r}`$ is that associated to each
dyadic Gabor frame $`G^{d}_{r}`$:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\begin{CD}G^{d}_{\cup r}(b_{\textrm{g}})@>{(S^{d}_{r})^{-1},r\in\mathscr{R}}>{}>\cup_{r\in\mathscr{R}}\widetilde{G^{d}_{r}(b_{\textrm{g}})}\\
@V{}V{I^{d}}V@V{}V{I^{d}}V\\
\mathscr{F}(I^{d})@>{(S_{I^{d}})^{-1}}>{}>\widetilde{\mathscr{F}(I^{d})}\end{CD}\text{.}
``` |  | (30) |

Observe that computing the $`\widetilde{\mathscr{F}(I^{d})}`$ via direct
inversion of its (diagonal) frame operator $`S_{I^{d}}`$ corresponds to
the down-and-right path
in ([30](#S6.E30 "In Theorem 7 (Canonical Duals of Adaptive Dyadic Frames) ‣ VI-D Adaptive Dyadic Superposition Frames ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")),
and hence requires knowledge of $`I^{d}`$. However,

implies that all elements in $`\widetilde{\mathscr{F}(I^{d})}`$ can be

plays a key role.

### VI-E Computational Complexity

We now address the relative computational complexity of the various
reconstruction methods presented above. Recall that these algorithms all
yield a diagonal frame operator, implying that only $`\mathcal{O}(L)`$
operations are required for its inversion, compared to
$`\mathcal{O}(L^{3})`$ in the worst case of a non-diagonal frame
operator lacking any special structure. While in some circumstances this
complexity can be reduced (see, e.g., \[[32](#bib.bib32)\]), such
schemes remain super-linear in $`L`$, rendering them impractical for use
in applications with $`L\gg 1`$.

Recall that when no windows are merged, we recover a Gabor frame
$`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ containing $`NM`$ elements, with $`Na=Mb=L`$. If
the associated frame operator is diagonal, an FFT-based approach
requires $`NM(1+\log_{2}M)`$ complex multiplications to compute the
short-time analysis coefficients $`X[m,n]`$ from $`x\in\mathbb{C}^{L}`$,
with $`NM`$ of these needed to obtain the $`N`$ individual short-time
segments
$`\{\mathcal{T}_{na}w[t]x[t]\mathbb{I}_{\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{T}_{na}w)}[t]\}`$.
When reconstruction proceeds via inversion of the frame operator, then
$`NM\log_{2}M`$ operations are required to compute the necessary inverse
FFTs, plus $`NM`$ operations to multiply each resultant segment by the
appropriate dual window. In the overlap-add setting, this window is the
identity, and hence these latter $`MN`$ operations are avoided. An
in-depth discussion of the general (non-diagonal) case, including
methods based on the Zak transform, is given in \[[32](#bib.bib32)\]
and \[[39](#bib.bib39)\].

In the case that windows are merged, note that a global superposition
frame $`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})`$ derived from
$`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ comprises
$`M_{\textrm{g}}=L/b_{\textrm{g}}\geq M`$ modulations of
$`N_{\textrm{g}}\leq N`$ windows:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
N_{\textrm{g}}=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}I^{\textrm{g}}[n,r]\text{.}
``` |  | (31) |

It follows that
$`N_{\textrm{g}}M_{\textrm{g}}(\log_{2}M_{\textrm{g}}+1)`$ complex
multiplications are required to compute the analysis coefficients
$`X[m,n,r]`$ via $`N_{\textrm{g}}`$ FFTs, followed by
$`N_{\textrm{g}}M_{\textrm{g}}\log_{2}M_{\textrm{g}}`$ operations
required for the $`N_{\textrm{g}}`$ inverse FFTs required for
reconstruction.

If $`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})`$ arises from the special case
described in
Theorem [6](#Thmthm6 "Theorem 6 (Canonical Duals of Adaptive Lapped Frames) ‣ VI-C Adaptive Lapped Superposition Frames ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"),
then the elements of $`\widetilde{\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})}`$ can be
pre-computed, and only $`N_{\textrm{g}}M_{\textrm{g}}`$ extra operations
are necessary for multiplication by the requisite canonical dual
windows. In the general case, elements of
$`\widetilde{\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})}`$ must be computed directly
via ([27](#S6.E27 "In Theorem 5 (Fast Inversion via Canonical Dual) ‣ VI-B Reconstruction via Canonical Dual Superposition Frames ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")),
as a function of the chosen selection function $`I^{\textrm{g}}`$.
Appealing
to ([31](#S6.E31 "In VI-E Computational Complexity ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")),
we see that this computation can be accomplished using another
$`2N_{\textrm{g}}M_{\textrm{g}}`$ calculations, leading to a total of
$`N_{\textrm{g}}M_{\textrm{g}}(3+\log_{2}M_{\textrm{g}})`$ complex
multiplications. This analysis is also an upper bound for the worst-case
complexity for any $`\mathscr{F}(I^{l})`$ having the same ordered
partition function as $`\mathscr{F}(I^{\textrm{g}})`$.

The various analysis and synthesis complexities discussed above are
summarized in
Table [I](#S6.T1 "TABLE I ‣ VI-C Adaptive Lapped Superposition Frames ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction").
In practice, the complexity of the signal adaptation procedure must also
be taken into account. This complexity depends both on the method for
searching among ordered partition functions $`\widetilde{I}[n,r]`$, and
the cost function used to compare them. Since it is clearly infeasible
to compare all $`2^{N-1}`$ ordered partition functions by exhaustive
search, we next consider greedy and dynamic-programming-based approaches
below. In both cases, the complexity of evaluating the associated cost
functions increases with window length, and therefore it is advisable in
practice to set an upper bound on the maximal number of window merges.

## VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples

Signal-adaptive modification of an initial Gabor frame
$`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ on $`\mathbb{C}^{L}`$ via superposition can
produce any one of the possible $`2^{N-1}`$ superposition frames whose
properties we characterized in
Sections [V](#S5 "V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
and [VI](#S6 "VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction").
We now detail two instances of a broad class of signal adaptation
algorithms, any of which can be used to select a superposition frame for
subsequent signal analysis. We propose both greedy and dynamic
programming approaches in
Section [VII-A](#S7.SS1 "VII-A Signal-Adaptive Superposition Frame Selection ‣ VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"),
and illustrate their performance with two brief examples in
Section [VII-B](#S7.SS2 "VII-B Illustrative Examples ‣ VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction").

### VII-A Signal-Adaptive Superposition Frame Selection

The first of the two Gabor frame adaptation algorithms we describe is a
simple greedy approach that can be implemented by “growing” a given
window forward in time through successive attempts to merge it with its
subsequent neighboring translates \[[1](#bib.bib1)\]. Whenever a
proposed merge fails, the procedure resets and repeats, halting when the
end of the data stream is reached (or, equivalently in our cyclic
setting, when the initial window is once again encountered).

A decision whether or not to merge adjacent windows can be made based on
any suitable cost function. As one example, we employ the time-frequency
concentration measure appearing in the popular work of \[[3](#bib.bib3),
[4](#bib.bib4)\] on adaptive optimal-kernel time-frequency
representations. Specifically, consider a short-time segment
$`x_{n,r}[t]\triangleq\{\mathcal{T}_{na}w[t]x[t]\mathbb{I}_{\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{T}_{na}w)}[t]\}`$,
and define its time-frequency concentration in the manner
of \[[2](#bib.bib2), [3](#bib.bib3), [4](#bib.bib4), [1](#bib.bib1)\]

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
C(x_{n,r})\triangleq\frac{\sum_{m=0}^{M_{r}-1}\left|\langle x,\mathcal{M}_{mb_{r}}\mathcal{T}_{na}w_{r}\rangle\right|^{4}}{\left(\sum_{m=0}^{M_{r}-1}\left|\langle x,\mathcal{M}_{mb_{r}}\mathcal{T}_{na}w_{r}\rangle\right|^{2}\right)^{2}},
``` |  | (32) |

with $`M_{r},b_{r}`$ defined
via ([15](#S5.E15 "In Definition 9 (Admissible Selection Functions 𝐼^𝑙 and 𝐼^"g") ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")).
This ratio of powers of norms of short-time Fourier coefficients is
suggestive of an “empirical spectral kurtosis,” and has also been used
in minimum entropy deconvolution \[[40](#bib.bib40)\]; other choices are
also possible \[[14](#bib.bib14)\].

As shown in \[[3](#bib.bib3)\],
maximizing ([32](#S7.E32 "In VII-A Signal-Adaptive Superposition Frame Selection ‣ VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
favors short-time segments that *concentrate* local signal energy within
the smallest regions of the time-frequency plane. Indeed, below we
obtain similar results on an example akin to the one employed
in \[[3](#bib.bib3)\]: the resultant superposition frames comprise
shorter windows near time-localized transients, and longer windows near
oscillatory signal portions. The resulting procedure requires
$`\mathcal{O}(N)`$ iterations and is summarized in
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ VII-A Signal-Adaptive Superposition Frame Selection ‣ VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction").

  Example [1](#Thmexmp1 "Example 1 (𝑁-Part Partition) ‣ IV-A Ordered Partition Functions ‣ IV Construction of Superposition Systems ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")

- •
  Compute a merged window

  ``` |  |

  and $`C(x_{n_{p},p+1})`$, $`C(x_{n_{p},p})`$ and $`C(x_{n,0})`$
  via ([32](#S7.E32 "In VII-A Signal-Adaptive Superposition Frame Selection ‣ VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))

The second algorithm we present is based on the dynamic programming
approach to adaptive segmentation popular in the audio coding
literature \[[21](#bib.bib21), [22](#bib.bib22), [23](#bib.bib23),
[24](#bib.bib24), [25](#bib.bib25)\]. The basic idea is to fix an
*additive* cost function $`J(\cdot)`$, and find an optimal ordered
partition function $`\widetilde{I}^{*}[n,r]`$ in the sense that it
minimizes the sum of individual segment costs $`J(x_{n,r}[t])`$:

|  |  |  |
|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\widetilde{I}^{*}[n,r]\triangleq\underset{\widetilde{I}[n,r]}{\operatorname{argmin}}\!\!\!\sum_{n,r:\widetilde{I}[n,r]=1}\!\!\!\!\!\widetilde{I}[n,r]J(x_{n,r}[t])\text{.}
``` |  |

Many choices for $`J(\cdot)`$ are possible, including rate-distortion
cost functions \[[22](#bib.bib22)\], sparsity-inducing
measures \[[14](#bib.bib14)\], and the well-known entropy cost
of \[[19](#bib.bib19)\], which we employ below.

To formalize our approach, define $`J^{*}_{n}`$ as the minimum cost
among ordered partition functions on $`\{0,1,\ldots,na-1\}`$, and let
$`J_{n,r}\triangleq J(x_{n,r}[t])`$ represent the cost associated to
covering the region $`\{na,na+1,\ldots,(n+r)a\}`$. The resulting dynamic
program requires $`\mathcal{O}(N^{2})`$ iterations and is summarized in
Algorithm [2](#alg2 "Algorithm 2 ‣ VII-A Signal-Adaptive Superposition Frame Selection ‣ VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction").

Initialization

- •
  Fix input data $`x\in\mathbb{C}^{L}`$, a Gabor frame
  $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,b)`$ and initialize the cost function
  $`J^{*}_{0}=0`$
- •
  For each $`\mathcal{T}_{na}w\in\{T_{na}w:n\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}\}`$
  calculate the support set

  |  |  |  |
  |----|----|----|
  |  |
  ``` math
  \mathscr{D}_{n}\triangleq\{t:\mathcal{T}_{na}w[t]>\mathcal{T}_{n^{\prime}a}w[t],n\neq n^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}\}
  ``` |  |

Dynamic Program

- •
  For $`n=0,1,\ldots,N-1`$, compute sequentially the nth segmental cost
  and associated boundary by

  |  |  |  |
  |----|----|----|
  |  | $`\displaystyle J_{n}^{*}=\min_{0\leq r<n}\left(J_{r}^{*}+J_{n,r}\right)\hskip 18.49988ptb_{n}^{*}=\operatorname{arg}\min_{0\leq r<n}\left(J_{r}^{*}+J_{n,r}\right)\text{,}`$ |  |

  with $`J_{n,r}`$ calculated using signal data supported on
  $`\cup_{k=n}^{n+r}\mathscr{D}_{k}`$
- •
  Compute the optimal selection function $`\widetilde{I}^{*}[n,r]`$
  using $`\{b_{n}^{*}:n\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}\}`$ via the standard
  “backtracking” procedure \[[21](#bib.bib21)\]

non-orthogonal windows,
Algorithm [2](#alg2 "Algorithm 2 ‣ VII-A Signal-Adaptive Superposition Frame Selection ‣ VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
evaluates $`J(x_{n,r}[t])`$ on regions *smaller* than those covered by

Once a set of variable-length windows is obtained via any selection
procedure returning an ordered partition function, a local or global
modulation structure can be chosen
via ([15](#S5.E15 "In Definition 9 (Admissible Selection Functions 𝐼^𝑙 and 𝐼^"g") ‣ V-B Superposition Frames and Frame Bounds ‣ V Superposition Frames: Main Results ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))

respectively, in order to obtain a signal-adaptive superposition frame.
In practice, application-specific considerations are likely to play a
role in superposition frame selection, and to this end we note that a
variety of other algorithms and approaches are possible (see,
e.g., \[[17](#bib.bib17), [41](#bib.bib41), [42](#bib.bib42)\]).

### VII-B Illustrative Examples

To conclude our investigation of superposition frames, we now consider
two illustrative examples that combine
Algorithms [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ VII-A Signal-Adaptive Superposition Frame Selection ‣ VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
and [2](#alg2 "Algorithm 2 ‣ VII-A Signal-Adaptive Superposition Frame Selection ‣ VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
with the analysis and reconstruction procedures presented earlier. These
examples—a stylized synthetic waveform akin to the example employed
in \[[3](#bib.bib3)\] and a phonetically balanced speech utterance from
the TIMIT corpus \[[43](#bib.bib43)\]—both exhibit varying
time-frequency structure, which in turn motivates signal-adaptive
analysis and reconstruction.

Our first example signal $`x`$
(Fig. [5](#S7.F5 "Fig. 5 ‣ VII-A Signal-Adaptive Superposition Frame Selection ‣ VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"),
top left) comprises a local and global sinusoidal term, two impulses,
and a bump function. We conducted a variety of experiments in which
varying levels of white Gaussian noise $`n`$ were added to $`x`$, and
Algorithms [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ VII-A Signal-Adaptive Superposition Frame Selection ‣ VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
and [2](#alg2 "Algorithm 2 ‣ VII-A Signal-Adaptive Superposition Frame Selection ‣ VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
were then applied to $`y\triangleq x+n`$ to obtain signal-adaptive frame
analysis coefficients $`Y[m,n,r]`$ on a global frequency lattice
($`M_{\textrm{g}}=6000`$). Using these as well as fixed-resolution
analyses for a range of window lengths, we then applied to $`Y[m,n,r]`$
both an “oracle” Wiener suppression rule (local signal spectrum
estimated by $`|X[m,n,r]|^{2}`$) and a two-stage Wiener suppression rule
(by appropriately soft-thresholding $`|Y[m,n,r]|^{2}`$), and obtained a
time-domain reconstruction $`\widehat{x}`$ via the corresponding
canonical dual superposition frame.

The remainder of
Fig. [5](#S7.F5 "Fig. 5 ‣ VII-A Signal-Adaptive Superposition Frame Selection ‣ VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
reports the results of a typical run at $`10`$ dB signal-to-noise ratio
(SNR), with the superposition system of
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ VII-A Signal-Adaptive Superposition Frame Selection ‣ VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
derived from an initial Gabor system $`\mathscr{G}(w,a,\cdot)`$
comprising a $`100`$-sample Hamming window $`w`$ and time lattice
constant $`a=50`$, and that of
Algorithm [2](#alg2 "Algorithm 2 ‣ VII-A Signal-Adaptive Superposition Frame Selection ‣ VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
based on a $`65`$-sample Hamming window with $`a=32`$. Although we have
observed
Algorithm [2](#alg2 "Algorithm 2 ‣ VII-A Signal-Adaptive Superposition Frame Selection ‣ VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
to be more noise-robust in practice, and to yield better performance
with somewhat shorter initial windows, it may be seen that both
algorithms yield broadly similar analyses with respect to dominant
signal features at $`10`$ dB SNR. Moreover, over a range of noise levels
and fixed-resolution analyses, we have observed improved SNR gains
$`20\log_{10}(\|y-x\|/\|\widehat{x}-x\|)`$ in both the oracle and
two-stage cases, as shown in the bottom-left panels of
Fig. [5](#S7.F5 "Fig. 5 ‣ VII-A Signal-Adaptive Superposition Frame Selection ‣ VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction").

Reconstruction spectrograms $`20\log_{10}|\widehat{X}[m,n,r]|`$—based on
the *oracle* denoising for visual clarity—are shown in the right-hand
panel of
Fig. [5](#S7.F5 "Fig. 5 ‣ VII-A Signal-Adaptive Superposition Frame Selection ‣ VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction").
They indicate that, in comparison to an *a priori* well chosen
fixed-resolution analysis using $`800`$-sample Hamming windows with
$`a=400`$, the onsets and offsets of localized time-frequency features
are better preserved by superposition frames. Since the best
fixed-resolution window length is not known *a priori* in practice, the
adaptive approach remains attractive, despite the lessening SNR gains
obtained in the simple two-stage denoising approach. These results
suggest the investigation of more sophisticated denoising schemes, also
bearing in mind that in the case of nonstationary noise, the best
adaptive analysis may well be SNR-dependent.

We repeated the same battery of tests with our second example signal
$`x`$
(Fig. [6](#S7.F6 "Fig. 6 ‣ VII-B Illustrative Examples ‣ VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"),
bottom), a portion of the phonetically-balanced TIMIT speech waveform
/train/dr1/fsah0/si1244.wav corresponding to the phrase “…\[eye\]d and
amazed.” This utterance, chosen to illustrate time-varying spectral
content typical of speech, contains two plosives (\[eye\]*d*, amaze*d*),
two steady vowels (*a*nd, *a*mazed), and a time-varying diphthong
(am*a*zed). The exact phonetic TIMIT segmentation (si1244.phn) is shown
between the two spectrogram panels of
Fig. [6](#S7.F6 "Fig. 6 ‣ VII-B Illustrative Examples ‣ VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"),
which correspond respectively to reconstructions based on an *a priori*
well chosen fixed-resolution (top, $`30`$ ms) and adaptive-resolution
(middle,
Algorithm [2](#alg2 "Algorithm 2 ‣ VII-A Signal-Adaptive Superposition Frame Selection ‣ VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"),
starting from $`3`$ ms Hamming windows with $`a=1.5`$ ms) oracle-Wiener
denoising, respectively, at $`10`$ dB SNR. The bottom panel of
Fig. [6](#S7.F6 "Fig. 6 ‣ VII-B Illustrative Examples ‣ VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
illustrates the corresponding adaptive analysis, which is seen to agree
well with major features of the given TIMIT segmentation;
Algorithm [1](#alg1 "Algorithm 1 ‣ VII-A Signal-Adaptive Superposition Frame Selection ‣ VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")
also yielded a similar analysis.

Following the same experimental procedure as in the case of
Fig. [5](#S7.F5 "Fig. 5 ‣ VII-A Signal-Adaptive Superposition Frame Selection ‣ VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"),
we observed broadly similar results—though with lower overall SNR gains
obtained for this less stylized example. Importantly, however,
superposition windows are seen to better preserve vowel onsets and
plosives; see in particular the boxed regions of the fixed-resolution

Fig. [6](#S7.F6 "Fig. 6 ‣ VII-B Illustrative Examples ‣ VII Signal Adaptation Algorithms and Examples ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"),
corresponding to the two plosives and initial vowel-diphthong onsets in
the word “amazed.”

In this manner we see that superposition frames, when coupled with
appropriate waveform adaptation criteria, show strong potential for use
in a variety of signal-adaptive analysis-synthesis settings. For signal
enhancement applications, a natural next step would be to extend the
approach of \[[17](#bib.bib17)\], in which adaptive segmentation is used
to estimate the local signal spectrum for enhancement purposes, but
reconstruction is done using a fixed-resolution time-frequency lattice.
A variety of other multi-stage or iterative approaches suggest
themselves, given the additional flexibility engendered by the
overcomplete, signal-adaptive superposition frames presented in this
article.

## VIII Discussion

In this article we have introduced a broad family of adaptive, linear
time-frequency representations termed superposition frames, and showed
that they admit a host of desirable properties, including fast
overlap-add reconstruction akin to standard short-time Fourier
techniques. Through a discussion of signal adaptation criteria and
multiple examples, the resultant analysis-synthesis systems were seen to
provide an effective and practical method for realizing signal-adaptive
time-frequency analysis coupled with fast reconstruction.

Relative to other adaptive time-frequency methods, a number of open
questions remain. First, while many aspects of our construction admit
straightforward extension to other Hilbert spaces of interest such as
$`\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})`$ or $`L^{2}(\mathbb{R})`$, the present article
has not addressed window requirements to ensure the existence of upper
superposition frame bounds in infinite-dimensional settings, or
attempted to characterize the structure of canonical superposition duals
in such cases. Second, establishing additional connections to lapped
transform constructions, in particular the tight lapped frames recently
proposed in \[[44](#bib.bib44)\], seem a promising avenue for further
investigation.

$`\mathscr{F}_{I^{d}}(\cup\widetilde{G^{d}_{r}})`$, the set of elements
selected by $`I^{d}`$ from
$`\cup_{r\in\mathscr{R}}\widetilde{G^{d}_{r}}`$. To establish the
result, we must verify the claimed equality

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\widetilde{\mathscr{F}_{I^{d}}}(G_{\cup r}^{d})=\mathscr{F}_{I^{d}}(\cup\widetilde{G^{d}_{r}})\text{.}
``` |  | (38) |

We proceed to establish the equality
of ([38](#A0.E38 "In Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ VIII Discussion ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
elementwise, noting first that the number of modulates of some
$`T_{na}w_{r}`$ in $`\widetilde{\mathscr{F}_{I^{d}}}(G_{\cup r}^{d})`$
is given by $`M_{\textrm{g}}=L/b_{\textrm{g}}`$ and, by construction, is
equal to the number of modulates of the same shifted window in each
$`G^{d}_{r}(b_{\textrm{g}})=\mathscr{G}(w_{r},a,b_{\textrm{g}})`$. We
therefore fix $`m\in\mathbb{Z}_{M_{\textrm{g}}}`$ and
$`r\in\mathscr{R}`$ for the remainder of the proof.

Since the dyadic superposition frame operator $`S_{I^{d}}`$ is diagonal,
the superposition Walnut formula
of ([27](#S6.E27 "In Theorem 5 (Fast Inversion via Canonical Dual) ‣ VI-B Reconstruction via Canonical Dual Superposition Frames ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
implies that we may write each
$`\widetilde{\phi}_{m,n,r}\in\widetilde{\mathscr{F}_{I^{d}}}(G_{\cup r}^{d})`$
as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\widetilde{\phi}_{m,n,r}[t]=\frac{\mathcal{M}_{mb_{L}}\mathcal{T}_{na}w_{r}[t]}{M_{\textrm{g}}\textstyle{\sum_{n^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}\sum_{r^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}}I^{d}[n^{\prime},r^{\prime}]|\mathcal{T}_{n^{\prime}a}w_{r^{\prime}}[t]|^{2}}\text{.}
``` |  | (39) |

Each element
$`\widehat{\phi}_{m,n^{\prime\prime},r}\in\widetilde{G^{d}_{r}}`$ can be
likewise written as:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\widehat{\phi}_{m,n^{\prime\prime},r}[t]=\frac{\mathcal{M}_{mb_{L}}\mathcal{T}_{n^{\prime\prime}a_{r}}w_{r}[t]}{\textstyle{M_{\textrm{g}}\sum_{n^{*}=0}^{N_{r}-1}}|\mathcal{T}_{n^{*}a_{r}}w_{r}[t]|^{2}}\text{,}
``` |  | (40) |

with $`a_{r}=a(r+1)`$, $`N_{r}=L/a_{r}`$, and
$`0\leq n^{\prime\prime}<N_{r}`$, in accordance with
Definition [15](#Thmdefn15 "Definition 15 (Dyadic Gabor and Superposition Frames) ‣ VI-D Adaptive Dyadic Superposition Frames ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction").
Note that we ordinarily index modulations of Gabor frame elements by
$`mb_{\textrm{g}},m\in\mathbb{Z}_{M_{\textrm{g}}}`$, but
in ([40](#A0.E40 "In Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ VIII Discussion ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
we adopt the indexing scheme $`mb_{L}`$ for appropriate
$`m\in\mathbb{Z}_{M_{L}}`$, in order to facilitate its direct comparison
to ([39](#A0.E39 "In Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ VIII Discussion ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")).

In order to establish the equality of sets
in ([38](#A0.E38 "In Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ VIII Discussion ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")),
we need to show that if
$`\widetilde{\phi}_{m,n,r}\in\widetilde{\mathscr{F}_{I^{d}}}(G_{\cup r}^{d})`$,
then the expressions
in ([39](#A0.E39 "In Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ VIII Discussion ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
and ([40](#A0.E40 "In Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ VIII Discussion ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
are equivalent; i.e.,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
I^{d}[m,n,r]=1\,\Rightarrow\,\widetilde{\phi}_{m,n,r}=\widehat{\phi}_{m,n,r}\text{.}
``` |  | (41) |

To establish the implication
of ([41](#A0.E41 "In Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ VIII Discussion ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")),
we first show that the condition $`I^{d}[m,n,r]=1`$ implies that the
numerators
of ([39](#A0.E39 "In Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ VIII Discussion ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
and ([40](#A0.E40 "In Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ VIII Discussion ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
agree. Since $`m`$ and $`r`$ are fixed, this means that for all $`n`$
such that $`I^{d}[m,n,r]=1`$, there must exist an
$`0\leq n^{\prime\prime}<N_{r}`$ satisfying

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{M}_{mb_{L}}\mathcal{T}_{na}w_{r}=\mathcal{M}_{mb_{L}}\mathcal{T}_{n^{\prime\prime}a_{r}}w_{r}\text{.}
``` |  | (42) |

Equality
in ([42](#A0.E42 "In Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ VIII Discussion ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
is achieved when $`na=n^{\prime\prime}a_{r}=n^{\prime\prime}(r+1)a`$,
which clearly holds if $`r+1`$ divides $`n`$. But since $`I^{d}[m,n,r]`$
selects elements from
$`G^{d}_{r}=\mathscr{G}(w_{r},a(r+1),b_{\textrm{g}})`$, then $`r+1`$
divides $`n`$ by construction,
and ([42](#A0.E42 "In Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ VIII Discussion ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
follows.

The argument for agreement of the denominators is more delicate, because
it is *not* true that for all $`t\in\mathbb{Z}_{L}`$,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\textstyle\sum_{n^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}\sum_{r^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}I^{d}[n^{\prime},r^{\prime}]|\mathcal{T}_{n^{\prime}a}w_{r^{\prime}}[t]|^{2}=\sum_{n^{*}=0}^{N_{r}-1}|\mathcal{T}_{n^{*}a_{r}}w_{r}[t]|^{2}\text{.}
``` |  | (43) |

Instead, we show
that ([43](#A0.E43 "In Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ VIII Discussion ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
holds for all
$`t\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\mathcal{T}_{na}w_{r}\right)`$—which,
together
with ([42](#A0.E42 "In Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ VIII Discussion ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")),
is sufficient to
establish ([41](#A0.E41 "In Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ VIII Discussion ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")),
and consequently our claimed result.

Let $`S_{n,r}\triangleq\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{T}_{na}w_{r})`$,
with $`\mathbb{I}_{S_{n,r}}[t]`$ the corresponding indicator function.
Using the same arguments as in the penultimate portion of the proof of
Theorem [6](#Thmthm6 "Theorem 6 (Canonical Duals of Adaptive Lapped Frames) ‣ VI-C Adaptive Lapped Superposition Frames ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"),
observe that the left-hand side
of ([43](#A0.E43 "In Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ VIII Discussion ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
can be decomposed as follows:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\textstyle\left(\sum_{r^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}\sum_{n^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}I^{d}[n^{\prime},r^{\prime}]|\mathcal{T}_{n^{\prime}a}w_{r^{\prime}}[t]|^{2}\right)\mathbb{I}_{S_{n,r}}[t]`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\textstyle\left(\sum_{r^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}\sum_{n^{\prime}=0}^{n-1}I^{d}[n^{\prime},r^{\prime}]|\mathcal{T}_{n^{\prime}a}w_{r^{\prime}}[t]|^{2}+\mathcal{T}_{na}w_{r}^{2}[t]\right.`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\textstyle\hskip 16.38895pt\hskip 16.38895pt\,\left.+\sum_{r^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}\sum_{n^{\prime}=n+1}^{N-1}I^{d}[n^{\prime},r^{\prime}]|\mathcal{T}_{n^{\prime}a}w_{r^{\prime}}[t]|^{2}\right)\mathbb{I}_{S_{n,r}}[t]`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\left(|\mathcal{T}_{(n-1)a}w[t]|^{2}+|\mathcal{T}_{na}w_{r}[t]|^{2}+|\mathcal{T}_{(n+r+1)a}w[t]|^{2}\right)\mathbb{I}_{S_{n,r}}[t]\text{.}`$ |  |

Applying the neighbor-overlap requirement
of ([28](#S6.E28 "In Definition 12 (Neighbor Overlap Condition) ‣ VI-C Adaptive Lapped Superposition Frames ‣ VI Fast Reconstruction via Superposition Frames ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
to the right-hand side
of ([43](#A0.E43 "In Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ VIII Discussion ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction"))
then yields

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\textstyle\left(\sum_{n^{*}=0}^{N_{r}-1}|\mathcal{T}_{n^{*}a_{r}}w_{r}[t]|^{2}\right)\mathbb{I}_{S_{n,r}}[t]`$ |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle=\left(|\mathcal{T}_{(n-1)a}w[t]|^{2}+|\mathcal{T}_{na}w_{r}[t]|^{2}+|\mathcal{T}_{(n+r+1)a}w[t]|^{2}\right)\mathbb{I}_{S_{n,r}}[t]\text{,}`$ |  |

thus establishing the equality
of ([43](#A0.E43 "In Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ Proof: ‣ VIII Discussion ‣ Superposition Frames for Adaptive Time-Frequency Analysis and Fast Reconstruction")),
and hence the result. ∎
````
