# Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation

Rintaro Ikeshita    Tomohiro Nakatani ^(†)^(†)thanks: Manuscript
received Feb xx, 2021. (Corresponding author: Rintaro Ikeshita)

###### Abstract

We address a blind source separation (BSS) problem in a noisy
reverberant environment in which the number of microphones $`M`$ is
greater than the number of sources of interest, and the other noise
components can be approximated as stationary and Gaussian distributed.
Conventional BSS algorithms for the optimization of a multi-input
multi-output convolutional beamformer have suffered from a huge
computational cost when $`M`$ is large. We here propose a
computationally efficient method that integrates a weighted prediction
error (WPE) dereverberation method and a fast BSS method called
independent vector extraction (IVE), which has been developed for less
reverberant environments. We show that, given the power spectrum for
each source, the optimization problem of the new method can be reduced
to that of IVE by exploiting the stationary condition, which makes the
optimization easy to handle and computationally efficient. An experiment
of speech signal separation shows that, compared to a conventional
method that integrates WPE and independent vector analysis, our proposed
method achieves much faster convergence while maintaining its separation
performance.

###### Index Terms: 

Blind source separation, dereverberation, independent vector analysis,
block coordinate descent method

## I Introduction

When multiple speech signals are observed by distant microphones (e.g.,
in a conference room), they are contaminated with reverberation and
background noise. The problem of extracting each speech signal and
removing the reverberation and background noise from only the observed
signal is called (convolutive) blind source separation or extraction
(BSE) \[[1](#bib.bib1), [2](#bib.bib2), [3](#bib.bib3)\]. Here, we
consider BSE in the short-term Fourier transform (STFT) domain under the
following two conditions:

- •
  The reverberation time ($`\mathrm{RT}_{60}`$) is larger than the frame
  length of the STFT, and the mixture should be treated as a convolutive
  mixture in the STFT domain as well.
- •
  The number of microphones $`M`$ is greater than that of speech signals
  $`K`$ and there can be background noise.

To cope with reverberation, one can apply a dereverberation
method \[[4](#bib.bib4)\] such as weighted prediction error
(WPE) \[[5](#bib.bib5), [6](#bib.bib6), [7](#bib.bib7)\] as
preprocessing of BSE for instantaneous mixtures in the STFT domain
(called BSE-inst in this paper). We then apply some BSE-inst method such
as independent vector analysis (IVA) \[[8](#bib.bib8), [9](#bib.bib9),
[10](#bib.bib10)\] and independent vector extraction
(IVE) \[[11](#bib.bib11), [12](#bib.bib12), [13](#bib.bib13),
[14](#bib.bib14), [15](#bib.bib15), [16](#bib.bib16), [17](#bib.bib17)\]
developed for less reverberant environments, to extract $`K`$ speech
signals. Such a cascade configuration of WPE and IVA/IVE has a low
computational cost, but the WPE dereverberation filter is estimated
without considering the separation attained by IVA/IVE following WPE.

To jointly optimize the WPE dereverberation and separation filters
through a unified optimization, methods that integrate WPE and several
BSE-inst methods have been proposed \[[6](#bib.bib6), [7](#bib.bib7),
[18](#bib.bib18), [19](#bib.bib19), [20](#bib.bib20)\], and it has been
reported that these methods can give higher separation performance than
the cascade configuration of WPE and BSE-inst (see,
e.g., \[[18](#bib.bib18)\]). However, the computational cost of
optimizing both WPE and BSE-inst models becomes huge when $`M`$ is
large.

To reduce the computational cost of the conventional joint optimization
methods while maintaining their separation performance, we propose a new
BSE method called IVE for convolutive mixtures (IVE-conv), which
integrates WPE and IVE
(Section [III](#S3 "III Probabilistic model ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation")).
We show that, given source power spectra, the IVE-conv optimization
problem can be reduced to the IVE optimization problem by exploiting the
stationary condition, and this reduction is not computationally
intensive
(Section [IV-A](#S4.SS1 "IV-A Reduction from IVE-conv to IVE when v is kept fixed ‣ IV Optimization algorithm ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation")).
The IVE optimization problem can be solved fast \[[13](#bib.bib13),
[14](#bib.bib14), [15](#bib.bib15), [16](#bib.bib16),
[17](#bib.bib17)\], and so can the IVE-conv optimization problem
(Section [IV-B](#S4.SS2 "IV-B Algorithm 1: Update each convolutional filter one by one ‣ IV Optimization algorithm ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation")).
We also propose another new algorithm for IVE-conv that alternately
optimizes WPE and IVE
(Section [IV-C](#S4.SS3 "IV-C Algorithm 2: Alternate update of WPE and ICA ‣ IV Optimization algorithm ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation")).
Similar algorithms have already been developed in \[[6](#bib.bib6),
[7](#bib.bib7)\], but our proposed one significantly reduces the
computational time complexity of the conventional ones. In a numerical
experiment in which two speech signals are extracted from mixtures, we
show the effectiveness of our new approach.

## II Blind source extraction problem

Let $`M`$ be the number of microphones. Suppose that an observed mixture
$`\bm{x}\coloneqq\{\bm{x}(f,t)\}_{f,t}\subset\mathbb{C}^{M}`$ in the
STFT domain is a convolutive mixture of $`K`$ nonstationary source
signals and $`N_{\z}\coloneqq M-K`$ background noise signals:¹¹ 1 The
assumption that the dimension of the noise signal is $`M-K`$ concerns
the rigorous development of efficient algorithms and can be violated to
some extent when applied in practice (see numerical experiments in
Section [V](#S5 "V Experiment ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation")).

|     |                                                                                                                                                   |     |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\bm{x}(f,t)=\sum_{\tau=0}^{N_{\tau}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{K}\bm{a}_{i}(f,\tau)s_{i}(f,t-\tau)+A_{\z}(f,\tau)\bm{z}(f,t-\tau)\right],`$ |     |

|     |     |                                     |     |                                                       |                              |                                                          |     |     |
| --- | --- | ----------------------------------- | --- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     |     | $`\displaystyle\bm{a}_{i}(f,\tau)`$ |     | $`\displaystyle\in\mathbb{C}^{M},\quad`$              | $`\displaystyle s_{i}(f,t)`$ | $`\displaystyle\in\mathbb{C},\quad i\in\{1,\ldots,K\},`$ |     | (1) |
|     |     | $`\displaystyle A_{\z}(f,\tau)`$    |     | $`\displaystyle\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times N_{\z}},\quad`$ | $`\displaystyle\bm{z}(f,t)`$ | $`\displaystyle\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\z}}.`$                 |     | (2) |

Here, $`f=1,\ldots,F`$ and $`t=1,\ldots,T`$ denote the frequency bin and
time frame indexes, respectively. Also, $`s_{i}(f,t)\in\mathbb{C}`$ and
$`\bm{z}(f,t)\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\z}}`$ are the signals of the target
source $`i=1,\ldots,K`$ and the background noises, respectively.
$`\{\bm{a}_{i}(f,\tau)\}_{\tau=0}^{N_{\tau}}`$ and
$`\{A_{\z}(f,\tau)\}_{\tau=0}^{N_{\tau}}`$ are the acoustic transfer
functions (ATFs) for the corresponding sources, where $`N_{\tau}+1`$ is
the length of the ATFs. The BSE problem addressed in this paper is
defined as the problem of estimating the sources of interest, i.e.,
$`\{s_{i}(f,t)\}_{i,f,t}`$. We assume that $`K`$ is given and the
background noises are more stationary than the sources of interest.

## III Probabilistic model

We present the proposed IVE-conv model that integrates
WPE \[[6](#bib.bib6), [7](#bib.bib7), [5](#bib.bib5)\] and
IVE \[[13](#bib.bib13), [14](#bib.bib14), [15](#bib.bib15),
[17](#bib.bib17), [16](#bib.bib16), [11](#bib.bib11),
[12](#bib.bib12)\]. Let $`\hat{\bm{x}}(f,t)\in\mathbb{C}^{M+L}`$ with
$`L=M(D_{2}-D_{1}+1)`$ and $`0<D_{1}\leq D_{2}`$ be given by

|     |                                                                                                                              |     |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\hat{\bm{x}}(f,t)=[\,\bm{x}(f,t)^{\top},\bm{x}(f,t-D_{1})^{\top},\ldots,\bm{x}(f,t-D_{2})^{\top}\,]^{\top},`$ |     |

where ^(⊤) is the transpose of a vector. Suppose that there exists a
convolutional filter $`\hat{W}(f)\in\mathbb{C}^{(M+L)\times M}`$
satisfying

|     |                              |                                                                                                                  |     |     |
| --- | ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle s_{i}(f,t)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\hat{\bm{w}}_{i}(f)^{h}\hat{\bm{x}}(f,t)\in\mathbb{C},\quad i\in\{1,\ldots,K\},`$                |     | (3) |
|     | $`\displaystyle\bm{z}(f,t)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\hat{W}_{\z}(f)^{h}\hat{\bm{x}}(f,t)\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\z}},`$                                    |     | (4) |
|     | $`\displaystyle\hat{W}(f)`$  | $`\displaystyle=[\hat{\bm{w}}_{1}(f),\ldots,\hat{\bm{w}}_{K}(f),\hat{W}_{\z}(f)]\in\mathbb{C}^{(M+L)\times M},`$ |     | (5) |

where ^(h) denotes the conjugate transpose. As pointed out
in \[[21](#bib.bib21), [22](#bib.bib22)\], convolutional filter
$`\hat{W}(f)`$ can be decomposed into the WPE prediction matrix
$`G(f)\in\mathbb{C}^{L\times M}`$ and the ICA separation matrix
$`W(f)\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times M}`$:

|     |                             |                                       |     |     |
| --- | --------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\hat{W}(f)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}W(f)\\ |

-G(f)W(f)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}I\_{M}\\
-G(f)\end{bmatrix}W(f).`$ | | (6) |

Here, $`I_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times d}`$ is the identity matrix.

We also assume that the original source signals are mutually independent
and that the target source (resp. noise) signals obey time-dependent
(resp. time-independent) complex Gaussian distributions in the same way
as in IVE \[[16](#bib.bib16), [17](#bib.bib17), [14](#bib.bib14),
[15](#bib.bib15), [13](#bib.bib13), [12](#bib.bib12),
[11](#bib.bib11)\]:

|     |                                |                                                                                                                              |     |      |
| --- | ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\bm{s}_{i}(t)`$ | $`\displaystyle\coloneqq[s_{i}(1,t),\ldots,s_{i}(F,t)]^{\top}\in\mathbb{C}^{F},`$                                            |     | (7)  |
|     | $`\displaystyle\bm{s}_{i}(t)`$ | $`\displaystyle\sim\mathbb{C}\mathcal{N}\left(\bm{0}_{F},v_{i}(t)I_{F}\right),\quad v_{i}(t)\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},`$            |     | (8)  |
|     | $`\displaystyle\bm{z}(f,t)`$   | $`\displaystyle\sim\mathbb{C}\mathcal{N}\left(\bm{0}_{N_{\z}},\Omega(f)\right),\quad\Omega(f)\in\mathcal{S}_{++}^{N_{\z}},`$ |     | (9)  |
|     |                                | $`\{\bm{s}_{i}(t),\bm{z}(f,t)\}_{i,f,t}`$ are mutually independent.                                                          |     | (10) |

Here, $`\bm{0}_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}`$ is the zero vector,
$`\mathcal{S}_{++}^{d}`$ denotes the set of all Hermitian positive
definite matrices of size $`d\times d`$, and
$`\mathbb{R}_{>0}=\mathcal{S}_{++}^{1}`$. Assumption
([9](#S3.E9 "In III Probabilistic model ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation"))
that the background noise signal is stationary and Gaussian distributed
is essential for developing computationally efficient algorithms. In
Section [V](#S5 "V Experiment ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation"),
we will experimentally show that this assumption can be violated to some
extent when applied in practice.

The IVE-conv model is defined by
([3](#S3.E3 "In III Probabilistic model ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation"))–([10](#S3.E10 "In III Probabilistic model ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation")).
The parameters $`\hat{W}\coloneqq\{\hat{W}(f)\}_{f}`$,
$`v\coloneqq\{v_{i}(t)\}_{i,t}`$, and
$`\Omega\coloneqq\{\Omega(f)\}_{f}`$ can be estimated based on maximum
likelihood, which is equivalent to minimizing
$`\hat{g}(\hat{W},\Omega,v)\coloneqq-\frac{1}{T}\log p(\bm{x})`$:

|     |                                            |                                                                                                                                                         |     |      |
| --- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\hat{g}(\hat{W},\Omega,v)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{f=1}^{F}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\Big[\hat{\bm{w}}_{i}(f)^{h}\hat{R}_{i}(f)\hat{\bm{w}}_{i}(f)+\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log v_{i}(t)\Big]`$ |     |      |
|     |                                            | $`\displaystyle+\sum_{f=1}^{F}\trace\big(\hat{W}_{\z}(f)^{h}\hat{R}_{\z}(f)\hat{W}_{\z}(f)\Omega(f)^{-1}\big)`$                                         |     |      |
|     |                                            | $`\displaystyle-\sum_{f=1}^{F}\log\det\big(W(f)^{h}W(f)\Omega(f)^{-1}\big),`$                                                                           |     | (11) |
|     | $`\displaystyle\hat{R}_{i}(f)`$            | $`\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\hat{\bm{x}}(f,t)\hat{\bm{x}}(f,t)^{h}}{v_{i}(t)},\quad i\in\{1,\ldots,K,\z\},`$                         |     |      |

where we define $`v_{\z}(t)=1`$ for all $`t=1,\ldots,T`$ (see,
e.g., \[[19](#bib.bib19)\] for the derivation of $`\hat{g}`$). If
$`L=0`$ and $`\hat{W}(f)=W(f)`$, then objective function $`\hat{g}`$ has
the same form as the counterparts of ICA, IVA, and IVE, which has been
discussed extensively in the literature \[[23](#bib.bib23),
[24](#bib.bib24), [25](#bib.bib25), [26](#bib.bib26), [27](#bib.bib27),
[16](#bib.bib16), [17](#bib.bib17), [13](#bib.bib13), [14](#bib.bib14),
[15](#bib.bib15)\]. For $`L\geq 1`$, $`K=M`$, and $`N_{\z}=0`$, the
optimization problem has been discussed explicitly
in \[[19](#bib.bib19), [18](#bib.bib18)\] and implicitly
in \[[6](#bib.bib6), [7](#bib.bib7), [20](#bib.bib20)\].

###### Remark 1.

The proposed IVE-conv is an integration of WPE and IVE. If we replace
IVE with ICA, IVA, or independent low-rank matrix analysis
(ILRMA) \[[28](#bib.bib28)\], then the IVE-conv turns out to be the
method that integrates WPE with ICA \[[6](#bib.bib6)\], WPE with IVA
(IVA-conv) \[[18](#bib.bib18)\], or WPE with ILRMA \[[20](#bib.bib20),
[19](#bib.bib19)\], respectively. In this sense, the novelty of the
IVE-conv model might seem limited. However, if $`M`$ gets large,
computationally efficient algorithms can be developed only for IVE-conv,
which is our main contribution.²² 2 This letter is based on our
work \[[29](#bib.bib29)\] reported in a domestic workshop in which an
algorithm similar to but less efficient than Algorithm 1 (proposed in
Section [IV-B](#S4.SS2 "IV-B Algorithm 1: Update each convolutional filter one by one ‣ IV Optimization algorithm ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation"))
was first presented. Recently, as follow-up research of our previous
work \[[29](#bib.bib29)\], a method has been
developed \[[30](#bib.bib30)\] that replaces the IVE-conv spectrum
model ([7](#S3.E7 "In III Probabilistic model ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation"))–([8](#S3.E8 "In III Probabilistic model ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation"))
with a model using nonnegative matrix factorization
(NMF) \[[31](#bib.bib31), [32](#bib.bib32), [33](#bib.bib33)\]. In
contrast, here, we develop a more efficient Algorithm 1 in a rigorous
way by providing new insight into the IVE-conv optimization problem in
Section [IV-A](#S4.SS1 "IV-A Reduction from IVE-conv to IVE when v is kept fixed ‣ IV Optimization algorithm ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation").
In addition, Algorithm 2 proposed in
Section [IV-C](#S4.SS3 "IV-C Algorithm 2: Alternate update of WPE and ICA ‣ IV Optimization algorithm ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation")
is completely new.

update $`v`$ and $`(\hat{W},\Omega)`$ alternately. The flowchart of

|     | $`\displaystyle v_{i}(t)=\frac{1}{F}\|\bm{s}_{i}(t)\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{F}\bm{s}_{i}(t)^{h}\bm{s}_{i}(t).`$ |     | (12) |

$`f`$ is dropped off to ease the notation. Also, we will denote the

$`i\in\{1,\ldots,K,\z\}`$ as

### IV-A Reduction from IVE-conv to IVE when $`v`$ is kept fixed

Before developing the algorithms, we show that the problem of minimizing
$`\hat{g}`$ with respect to $`\hat{W}`$ and $`\Omega`$ (when source
power spectra $`v=\{v_{i}(t)\}_{i,t}`$ are kept fixed), i.e.,

|     |                                                                                            |     |      |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle(\hat{W},\Omega)\in\argmin_{(\hat{W},\,\Omega)}\hat{g}(\hat{W},\Omega,v),`$ |     | (15) |

can be reduced to problem
([18](#S4.E18 "In IV-A Reduction from IVE-conv to IVE when v is kept fixed ‣ IV Optimization algorithm ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation"))
below that has been addressed in the study of IVE \[[16](#bib.bib16),
[17](#bib.bib17), [13](#bib.bib13), [14](#bib.bib14),
[15](#bib.bib15)\].

Every optimal $`\bar{W}`$ (the lower part of $`\hat{W}`$) in
problem ([15](#S4.E15 "In IV-A Reduction from IVE-conv to IVE when v is kept fixed ‣ IV Optimization algorithm ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation"))
satisfies the stationary condition \[[35](#bib.bib35)\], which is
computed as

|     |                                                                          |                                      |     |                                                                                                   |     |                                                                                                 |     |      |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ | --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\frac{\partial\hat{g}}{\partial\bar{\bm{w}}_{i}^{\ast}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\bm{0}_{L}`$         |     | $`\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow`$                                                              |     | $`\displaystyle\bar{P}_{i}\bm{w}_{i}+\bar{R}_{i}\bar{\bm{w}}_{i}=\bm{0}_{L}\in\mathbb{C}^{L},`$ |     |      |
|     |                                                                          | $`\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow`$ |     | $`\displaystyle\bar{\bm{w}}_{i}=-\bar{R}_{i}^{-1}\bar{P}_{i}\bm{w}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{L},`$        |     |                                                                                                 |     | (16) |
|     | $`\displaystyle\frac{\partial\hat{g}}{\partial\bar{W}_{\z}^{\ast}}`$     | $`\displaystyle=O`$                  |     | $`\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow`$                                                              |     | $`\displaystyle\bar{P}_{\z}W_{\z}+\bar{R}_{\z}\bar{W}_{\z}=O\in\mathbb{C}^{L\times N_{\z}},`$   |     |      |
|     |                                                                          | $`\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow`$ |     | $`\displaystyle\bar{W}_{\z}=-\bar{R}_{\z}^{-1}\bar{P}_{\z}W_{\z}\in\mathbb{C}^{L\times N_{\z}},`$ |     |                                                                                                 |     | (17) |

where ^(∗) denotes the element-wise conjugate. Eqs.
([16](#S4.E16 "In IV-A Reduction from IVE-conv to IVE when v is kept fixed ‣ IV Optimization algorithm ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation"))
and
([17](#S4.E17 "In IV-A Reduction from IVE-conv to IVE when v is kept fixed ‣ IV Optimization algorithm ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation"))
imply that the optimal $`\bar{W}`$ is a function of $`W`$ and that the
variable $`\bar{W}`$ can be removed from $`\hat{g}`$ by substituting
([16](#S4.E16 "In IV-A Reduction from IVE-conv to IVE when v is kept fixed ‣ IV Optimization algorithm ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation"))
and
([17](#S4.E17 "In IV-A Reduction from IVE-conv to IVE when v is kept fixed ‣ IV Optimization algorithm ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation")).
In other words, problem
([15](#S4.E15 "In IV-A Reduction from IVE-conv to IVE when v is kept fixed ‣ IV Optimization algorithm ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation"))
is equivalent to the following problem through
([16](#S4.E16 "In IV-A Reduction from IVE-conv to IVE when v is kept fixed ‣ IV Optimization algorithm ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation"))
and
([17](#S4.E17 "In IV-A Reduction from IVE-conv to IVE when v is kept fixed ‣ IV Optimization algorithm ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation")):

|     |                         |                                                                                                                                                      |     |      |
| --- | ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     |                         | $`\displaystyle(W,\Omega)\in\argmin_{(W,\,\Omega)}g(W,\Omega,v),`$                                                                                   |     | (18) |
|     | $`\displaystyle g`$     | $`\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\bm{w}_{i}^{h}V_{i}\bm{w}_{i}+\trace\big(W_{\z}^{h}V_{\z}W_{\z}\Omega^{-1}\big)-\log\det\big(W^{h}W\Omega^{-1}\big),`$ |     |      |
|     | $`\displaystyle V_{i}`$ | $`\displaystyle\coloneqq R_{i}-\bar{P}_{i}^{h}\bar{R}_{i}^{-1}\bar{P}_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{++}^{M},\quad i\in\{1,\ldots,K,\z\}.`$                      |     | (19) |

Since problem
([18](#S4.E18 "In IV-A Reduction from IVE-conv to IVE when v is kept fixed ‣ IV Optimization algorithm ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation"))
is nothing but the problem addressed in the study of IVE, we can
directly apply efficient algorithms that have been developed for

### IV-B Algorithm 1: Update each convolutional filter one by one

To solve problem

Dataset: We generated synthesized convolutive noisy mixtures of two
speech signals. We obtained speech signals from the test set of the
TIMIT corpus \[[36](#bib.bib36)\] and concatenated them so that the
length of each signal exceeded 10 seconds. We obtained point-source
noise signals recorded in a cafe (CAF) and a pedestrian area (PED) from
the third ‘CHiME’ Speech Separation and Recognition Challenge
(CHiME-3) \[[37](#bib.bib37)\]. Note that the noise signals are
nonstationary, but are considered to be more stationary than speech
signals. We obtained RIR data recorded in room OFC from the RWCP Sound
Scene Database in Real Acoustical Environments \[[38](#bib.bib38)\]. The
reverberation time ($`\mathrm{RT}_{60}`$) of room OFC is 780 ms.

The generated mixtures consisted of $`K=2`$ speech signals and six noise
signals randomly chosen from the above dataset. The SNR of each mixture
was adjusted to
$`\mathrm{SNR}=10\log_{10}\frac{(\lambda_{1}^{(\mathrm{s})}+\lambda_{2}^{(\mathrm{s})})/2}{\lambda_{1}^{(\mathrm{n})}+\cdots+\lambda_{6}^{(\mathrm{n})}}=5`$
or 10 \[dB\], where $`\lambda_{i}^{(\mathrm{s})}`$ and
$`\lambda_{j}^{(\mathrm{n})}`$ denote the sample variances of the
$`i`$-th speech signal ($`i=1,2`$) and the $`j`$-th noise singal
($`j=1,\ldots,6`$).

Criteria: Using museval \[[39](#bib.bib39)\], we measured the
signal-to-distortion ratio (SDR) \[[40](#bib.bib40)\] between the
separated and oracle spatial images of the speech signals at the first
microphone. The oracle spatial images were obtained by truncating the
RIRs at 32 ms (i.e., the points after 32 ms were replaced by 0) and
convolving them with the speech signals.

Conditions: The sampling rate was 16 kHz, the frame length was 2048 (128
ms), and the frame shift was 512 (32 ms).

Initialization: For all methods, we initialized the convolutional filter
as $`W(f)=-I_{M}`$ and $`\bar{W}(f)=G(f)=O`$, and then updated
$`W_{\z}(f)`$ once
using ([26](#S4.E26 "In IV-B Algorithm 1: Update each convolutional filter one by one ‣ IV Optimization algorithm ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation"))
before the optimization.

### V-A Experimental results

Figure [2](#S5.F2 "Fig. 2 ‣ V Experiment ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation")
shows the convergence of the SDR when each method was applied. Compared
to IVE, which does not handle reverberation, both IVA-conv and IVE-conv
showed the higher SDRs. Although the SDR performance at the convergence
points is comparable, the convergence of the proposed IVE-conv was much
faster than that of IVA-conv since the computational cost to update
$`\hat{W}_{\z}`$ is much lower. This fast convergence behavior is
important in practice, since using more microphones can improve the SDR
at the expense of increased runtime as observed in
Fig. [2](#S5.F2 "Fig. 2 ‣ V Experiment ‣ Independent Vector Extraction for Fast Joint Blind Source Separation and Dereverberation").
IVE-conv-(Alg2) converged faster than IVE-conv-(Alg1), but gave a
slightly lower SDR.

## VI Conclusion

To achieve joint source separation and dereverberation with a small
computational cost, we proposed IVE-conv, which is an integration of IVE
and WPE. We also developed two efficient BCD algorithms for optimizing
IVE-conv. The experimental results showed that IVE-conv yields
significantly faster convergence than the integration of IVA and WPE
while maintaining its separation performance.
