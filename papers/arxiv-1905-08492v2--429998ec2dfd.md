# DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimation for Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement

Marvin Tammen    Dörte Fischer    Bernd T. Meyer    Simon Doclo
^(†)^(†)thanks: This work was funded by the Deutsche
Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG, German Research Foundation) – Project ID
390895286 – EXC 2177/1.

###### Abstract

Multi-frame approaches for single-microphone speech enhancement, e.g.,
the multi-frame minimum-power-distortionless-response (MFMPDR) filter,
are able to exploit speech correlations across neighboring time frames.
In contrast to single-frame approaches such as the Wiener gain, it has
been shown that multi-frame approaches achieve a substantial noise
reduction with hardly any speech distortion, provided that an accurate
estimate of the correlation matrices and especially the speech
interframe correlation (IFC) vector is available. Typical estimation
procedures of the IFC vector require an estimate of the speech presence
probability (SPP) in each time-frequency (TF) bin. In this paper, we
propose to use a bi-directional long short-term memory deep neural
network (DNN) to estimate the SPP for each TF bin. Aiming at achieving a
robust performance, the DNN is trained for various noise types and
within a large signal-to-noise-ratio range. Experimental results show
that the MFMPDR in combination with the proposed data-driven SPP
estimator yields an increased speech quality compared to a
state-of-the-art model-based SPP estimator. Furthermore, it is confirmed
that exploiting interframe correlations in the MFMPDR is beneficial when
compared to the Wiener gain especially in adverse scenarios.

Speech Presence Probability, Deep Neural Network, Single-Microphone
Speech Enhancement, Multi-Frame Filtering

## 1 Introduction

In many hands-free speech communication systems such as hearing aids,
mobile phones and smart speakers, ambient noise may degrade the speech
quality and intelligibility of the recorded microphone signals. Hence,
several single- and multi-microphone speech enhancement approaches have
been proposed \[[1](#bib.bib1), [2](#bib.bib2), [3](#bib.bib3),
[4](#bib.bib4), [5](#bib.bib5)\]. Typical single-microphone speech
enhancement approaches apply a real-valued spectro-temporal gain, e.g.,
the Wiener gain (WG) \[[1](#bib.bib1)\], to the noisy short-time Fourier
transform (STFT) coefficients to obtain an estimate of the clean speech
signal. A disadvantage of these methods is that stronger noise reduction
typically goes hand-in-hand with increased speech distortion.

In contrast to these single-frame approaches, multi-frame
approaches \[[6](#bib.bib6), [7](#bib.bib7), [8](#bib.bib8),
[9](#bib.bib9), [10](#bib.bib10)\] apply a complex-valued filter to the
noisy STFT coefficients and are able to take into account the speech
correlation across consecutive time frames. Similarly to the
minimum-variance-distortionless-response (MVDR) beamformer and the
minimum-power-distortionless-response beamformer (MPDR) for
multi-microphone speech enhancement \[[4](#bib.bib4),
[11](#bib.bib11)\], a multi-frame MPDR (MFMPDR) filter has been proposed
for single-microphone speech enhancement \[[6](#bib.bib6),
[7](#bib.bib7), [10](#bib.bib10)\]. This multi-frame filter requires an
estimate of the noisy correlation matrix and the speech interframe
correlation (IFC) vector in each time-frequency (TF) bin. When oracle
estimates of these quantities are available, it has been shown
in \[[6](#bib.bib6), [12](#bib.bib12)\] that the MFMPDR filter achieves
a good noise reduction and hardly any speech distortion in contrast to
the WG. However, it has also been shown that the speech enhancement
performance is very sensitive to estimation errors of the highly
time-varying speech IFC vector \[[12](#bib.bib12)\].

In \[[7](#bib.bib7)\] a maximum likelihood (ML)-based approach has been
proposed to estimate the speech IFC vector from the noisy microphone
signals. The ML estimator typically requires an estimate of the speech
presence probability (SPP) in each TF bin. Several model-based SPP
estimators have been proposed \[[13](#bib.bib13), [14](#bib.bib14),
[15](#bib.bib15), [16](#bib.bib16)\] based on the assumption that the
speech and noise STFT coefficients are uncorrelated, complex Gaussian
distributed random variables. These estimators, however, have
difficulties with accurately estimating the SPP in the short STFT frames
that are required to capture the highly time-varying speech IFC vector.

In recent years, data-driven supervised learning-based approaches have
gained a lot of attention in a multitude of applications, including
single-microphone speech enhancement \[[17](#bib.bib17),
[18](#bib.bib18), [19](#bib.bib19), [20](#bib.bib20), [21](#bib.bib21),
[22](#bib.bib22)\]. A common approach is to estimate real-valued TF
masks, which are applied to the noisy STFT coefficients. Furthermore,
mask-based approaches have been recently proposed to estimate the speech
and noise correlation matrices that are required by multi-microphone
speech enhancement approaches such as the MVDR beamformer or the
generalized eigenvalue beamformer \[[23](#bib.bib23),
[24](#bib.bib24)\].

Inspired by the approach in \[[23](#bib.bib23)\], in this paper we
propose to use a data-driven SPP to estimate the required speech IFC
vector for the MFMPDR filter. More in particular, we use a bidirectional
long short-term memory (BLSTM) \[[25](#bib.bib25)\] deep neural network
(DNN) to estimate the SPP in each TF bin given the noisy STFT
coefficients. Aiming at achieving a robust performance, the DNN is
trained on the WSJ0 \[[26](#bib.bib26)\] and
NOISEX92 \[[27](#bib.bib27)\] datasets using a signal-to-noise ratio
(SNR) range from 0 to 20 dB. Experimental results for non-matched noise
types and partially non-matched SNRs show that using the proposed
DNN-based SPP estimate yields a larger speech quality improvement
compared to the model-based SPP estimate \[[16](#bib.bib16)\].
Furthermore, when utilizing either of the SPP estimates to implement an
MFMPDR or a WG, the benefit of exploiting speech IFCs is
confirmed \[[7](#bib.bib7), [9](#bib.bib9)\].

## 2 Signal Model

We consider an acoustic scenario with one speech source and ambient
noise, recorded using a single microphone. In the STFT domain, the noisy
microphone signal is given by

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

       ``` math
       Y(k,\ l)=X(k,\ l)+N(k,\ l),
       ```                          |     | (1) |

where $`X(k,\ l)`$ denotes the speech component and $`N(k,\ l)`$ denotes
the noise component at the $`k`$-th frequency bin and the $`l`$-th time
frame. Multi-frame speech enhancement approaches \[[6](#bib.bib6),
[7](#bib.bib7), [8](#bib.bib8), [10](#bib.bib10)\] estimate the speech
component by applying a finite impulse response filter with $`N`$ taps
to the noisy STFT coefficients, i.e.,

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

       ``` math
       \widehat{X}(k,\ l)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}H^{*}_{n}(k,\ l)Y(k,\ l-n),
       ```                                                             |     | (2) |

where $`\widehat{\circ}`$ denotes an estimate of $`\circ`$,
$`H_{n}(k,\ l)`$ denotes the $`n`$-th filter coefficient, and ^(∗)
denotes the complex-conjugate operator. Using vector notation,
([1](#S2.E1 "In 2 Signal Model ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement"))
and
([2](#S2.E2 "In 2 Signal Model ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement"))
can be written as

|     |                                     |                                                           |     |     |
| --- | ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{y}(k,\ l)`$  | $`\displaystyle=\mathbf{x}(k,\ l)+\mathbf{n}(k,\ l)`$     |     | (3) |
|     | $`\displaystyle\widehat{X}(k,\ l)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbf{h}^{H}(k,\ l)\mathbf{y}(k,\ l),`$ |     | (4) |

where ^(H) denotes the Hermitian operator and the $`N`$-dimensional
vectors $`\mathbf{h}(k,l)`$ and $`\mathbf{y}(k,l)`$ contain the filter
coefficients and $`N`$ consecutive STFT coefficients, i.e.,

|     |                                    |                                                                               |     |     |
| --- | ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{h}(k,\ l)`$ | $`\displaystyle=[H_{0}(k,\ l),\ H_{1}(k,\ l),\ \dots,\ H_{N-1}(k,\ l)]^{T},`$ |     | (5) |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{y}(k,l)`$   | $`\displaystyle=[Y(k,\ l),\ Y(k,\ l-1),\ \dots,\ Y(k,\ l-N+1)]^{T}.`$         |     | (6) |

This is analogous to multi-microphone beamforming
approaches \[[4](#bib.bib4), [5](#bib.bib5), [11](#bib.bib11)\] by
considering the FIR filter as a spatial filter and frames as microphone
inputs. Since all frequency bins are treated individually, in the
remainder of this paper we omit the frequency index $`k`$.

Assuming that the speech and noise components are uncorrelated, the
noisy correlation matrix
$`\boldsymbol{\Phi_{y}}(l)=\mathcal{E}\left\{\mathbf{y}(l)\mathbf{y}^{H}(l)\right\}`$,
with $`\mathcal{E}\{\circ\}`$ the expectation operator, can be written
as

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
\boldsymbol{\Phi_{y}}(l)=\boldsymbol{\Phi_{x}}(l)+\boldsymbol{\Phi_{n}}(l),
``` |  | (7) |

with the speech and noise correlation matrices
$`\boldsymbol{\Phi_{x}}(l)=\mathcal{E}\left\{\mathbf{x}(l)\mathbf{x}^{H}(l)\right\}`$
and
$`\boldsymbol{\Phi_{n}}(l)=\mathcal{E}\left\{\mathbf{n}(l)\mathbf{n}^{H}(l)\right\}`$.
In \[[6](#bib.bib6)\], it has been proposed to exploit the speech
correlation across consecutive time frames by separating the speech
component into a correlated and an uncorrelated part, i.e.,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathbf{x}(l)=\underbrace{\boldsymbol{\gamma_{x}}(l)X(l)}_{\mathclap{\mathrm{correlated}}}\quad+\quad\underbrace{\mathbf{x^{\prime}}(l)}_{\mathclap{\mathrm{uncorrelated}}},
``` |  | (8) |

where the (highly time-varying) normalized speech IFC vector
$`\boldsymbol{\gamma_{x}}(l)`$ describes the correlation between the
current and previous time frames w.r.t. the speech STFT coefficient
$`X(l)`$, i.e.,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\boldsymbol{\gamma_{x}}(l)=\frac{\mathcal{E}\left\{\mathbf{x}(l)X^{*}(l)\right\}}{\mathcal{E}\left\{\left|X(l)\right|^{2}\right\}}=\frac{\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbf{x}}(l)\mathbf{e}}{\mathbf{e}^{T}\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbf{x}}(l)\mathbf{e}},
``` |  | (9) |

with the vector $`\mathbf{e}`$ selecting the first column of
$`\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbf{x}}(l)`$ and
$`\mathbf{e}^{T}\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbf{x}}(l)\mathbf{e}=\phi_{X}(l)=\mathcal{E}\left\{\left|X(l)\right|^{2}\right\}`$
the speech power spectral density (PSD). Note that since $`X(l)`$ is
fully correlated with itself, the first element of the speech IFC vector
$`\boldsymbol{\gamma_{x}}(l)`$ in
([9](#S2.E9 "In 2 Signal Model ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement"))
is equal to 1, such that the first element of the uncorrelated speech
vector $`\mathbf{x^{\prime}}(l)`$ is equal to 0. Substituting
([8](#S2.E8 "In 2 Signal Model ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement"))
in
([3](#S2.E3 "In 2 Signal Model ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement")),
we obtain the multi-frame signal model

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{y}(l)=\boldsymbol{\gamma_{x}}(l)X(l)+\mathbf{x^{\prime}}(l)+\mathbf{n}(l),`$ |  | (10) |

where the uncorrelated speech component $`\mathbf{x^{\prime}}`$ is
treated as an interference.

Similarly to the speech IFC vector in
([9](#S2.E9 "In 2 Signal Model ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement")),
the noisy IFC vector and the noise IFC vector can be defined as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\boldsymbol{\gamma_{y}}(l)=\frac{\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbf{y}}(l)\mathbf{e}}{\mathbf{e}^{T}\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbf{y}}(l)\mathbf{e}},\quad\boldsymbol{\gamma_{n}}(l)=\frac{\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbf{n}}(l)\mathbf{e}}{\mathbf{e}^{T}\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbf{n}}(l)\mathbf{e}},
``` |  | (11) |

with
$`\mathbf{e}^{T}\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbf{y}}(l)\mathbf{e}=\mathcal{E}\left\{\left|Y(l)\right|^{2}\right\}`$
and
$`\mathbf{e}^{T}\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbf{n}}(l)\mathbf{e}=\phi_{N}(l)=\mathcal{E}\left\{\left|N(l)\right|^{2}\right\}`$
denoting the noisy and noise PSDs, respectively. Using
([11](#S2.E11 "In 2 Signal Model ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement"))
in
([7](#S2.E7 "In 2 Signal Model ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement")),
the speech IFC vector $`\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{\mathbf{x}}(l)`$ can be
obtained as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{\mathbf{x}}(l)=\frac{1+\xi(l)}{\xi(l)}\boldsymbol{\gamma_{y}}(l)-\frac{1}{\xi(l)}\boldsymbol{\gamma_{n}}(l),`$ |  | (12) |

with the a-priori SNR $`\xi(l)=\frac{\phi_{X}(l)}{\phi_{N}(l)}`$.

## 3 Multi-Frame MPDR Filter

In \[[6](#bib.bib6)\], the MFMPDR filter for single-microphone speech
enhancement was proposed, which aims at minimizing the output PSD while
preserving the correlated speech component. The corresponding
constrained optimization problem is given by

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{h}(l)\,\in\,\mathbb{C}^{N}}\quad\mathbf{h}^{H}(l)\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbf{y}}(l)\mathbf{h}(l),\quad\text{s.t. }\ \mathbf{h}^{H}(l)\boldsymbol{\gamma_{x}}(l)=1.`$ |  | (13) |

Solving this problem, the filter vector is equal to \[[6](#bib.bib6)\]

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{h}_{\text{MFMPDR}}=\frac{\boldsymbol{\Phi}^{-1}_{\mathbf{y}}(l)\boldsymbol{\gamma_{x}}(l)}{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{H}_{\mathbf{x}}(l)\boldsymbol{\Phi}^{-1}_{\mathbf{y}}(l)\boldsymbol{\gamma_{x}}(l)}`$ |  | (14) |

## 4 Parameter Estimation

In practice, the performance of the MFMPDR filter depends on how well
the time-varying correlation matrix $`\boldsymbol{\Phi_{y}}(l)`$ as well
as the highly time-varying speech IFC vector
$`\boldsymbol{\gamma_{x}}(l)`$ can be estimated from the noisy
microphone signals. In \[[12](#bib.bib12)\] it has been shown that the
performance of the MFMPDR filter is very sensitive to estimation errors
of the speech IFC vector. Whereas estimating the noisy correlation
matrix $`\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbf{y}}(l)`$ is rather straightforward,
accurately estimating the speech IFC vector
$`\boldsymbol{\gamma_{x}}(l)`$ is not so trivial \[[7](#bib.bib7),
[8](#bib.bib8), [10](#bib.bib10), [12](#bib.bib12)\]. Typically, this
vector requires an estimate of the a-priori SNR $`\xi(l)`$ and the noise
correlation matrix $`\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbf{n}}(l)`$, which in turn
require an estimate of the SPP in each TF bin \[[7](#bib.bib7)\]. The
following subsections discuss the estimation of the noisy and noise
correlation matrices, the speech IFC vector, as well as the a-priori SNR
using either a state-of-the-art model-based SPP estimator or the
proposed DNN-based SPP estimator.
Fig. [1](#S4.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 4.1 Correlation Matrices Estimation ‣ 4 Parameter Estimation ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement")
depicts the parameter estimation and multi-frame filtering process.

### 4.1 Correlation Matrices Estimation

Figure 1: Diagram of parameter estimation and multi-frame filtering.

The noisy correlation matrix $`\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{\mathbf{y}}(l)`$ is
estimated using recursive smoothing with smoothing constant
$`\lambda_{y}`$, i.e.,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{y}}(l)=\lambda_{y}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{y}}(l-1)+(1-\lambda_{y})\mathbf{y}(l)\mathbf{y}^{H}(l).
``` |  | (15) |

To estimate the noise correlation matrix $`\boldsymbol{\Phi_{n}}(l)`$,
similarly to \[[28](#bib.bib28)\] we apply a recursive smoothing
procedure to the noisy microphone signals, where the smoothing factor
for each TF bin depends on a time-varying SPP estimate
$`\widehat{\mathrm{SPP}}(l)`$ and a smoothing constant $`\alpha_{n}`$,
i.e.,

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{n}}(l)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\lambda_{n}(l)\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{n}}(l-1)+(1-\lambda_{n}(l))\mathbf{y}(l)\mathbf{y}^{H}(l)`$ |  | (16) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\lambda_{n}(l)`$ | $`\displaystyle=\alpha_{n}+(1-\alpha_{n})\widehat{\mathrm{SPP}}(l).`$ |  | (17) |

In the limiting cases, we have

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{SPP}}(l)`$ | $`\displaystyle=0\Rightarrow\lambda_{n}(l)=\alpha_{n}`$ |  | (18) |
|  | $`\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{SPP}}(l)`$ | $`\displaystyle=1\Rightarrow\lambda_{n}(l)=1\Rightarrow\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{n}}(l)=\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{n}}(l-1).`$ |  | (19) |

We consider two approaches to estimate the SPP for each TF bin required
in
([17](#S4.E17 "In 4.1 Correlation Matrices Estimation ‣ 4 Parameter Estimation ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement")).
As the reference, denoted with subscript $`\circ_{\mathrm{R}}`$, we use
the model-based approach from \[[16](#bib.bib16)\], which assumes that
the speech and noise STFT coefficients are complex Gaussian distributed.
Using this assumption, likelihood functions for speech presence and
speech absence can be derived, yielding the SPP estimate

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{SPP}}_{\mathrm{R}}(l)=\left(1+\frac{P(\mathcal{H}_{0})}{P(\mathcal{H}_{1})}(1+\xi_{\mathcal{H}_{1}})e^{-\frac{|Y(l)|^{2}}{\widehat{\phi}_{N}(l-1)}\frac{\xi_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}}{1+\xi_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}}}\right)^{-1}`$ |  | (20) |

where $`P(\mathcal{H}_{1})`$ and $`P(\mathcal{H}_{0})`$ denote the prior
probability of speech presence and absence, respectively, and the
parameter $`\xi_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}`$ denotes a typical a-priori SNR
encountered during speech presence. Note that this method relies on the
noise PSD estimate of the *previous* frame
$`\widehat{\phi}_{N}(l-1)=\mathbf{e}^{T}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{n}}(l-1)\mathbf{e}`$.

Alternatively, in this paper we propose to exploit the capabilities of a
BLSTM DNN to capture temporal and spectral structures in order to
estimate the SPP. The DNN is trained to perform a mapping between the
noisy STFT coefficient magnitudes and the SPP, i.e.,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{SPP}}_{\mathrm{DNN}}(l)=f_{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}\left\{\mathbf{\left\lvert Y\right\rvert}\right\}(l)`$ |  | (21) |

with $`\mathbf{\left\lvert Y\right\rvert}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times L}`$
containing all $`K`$ frequency bins and $`L`$ time frames of the noisy
STFT coefficient magnitudes of the considered signal,
$`f_{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}`$ the trained DNN with parameters
$`\boldsymbol{\Theta}`$, and
$`\widehat{\mathrm{SPP}}_{\mathrm{DNN}}(l)`$ the DNN-based SPP estimate.
The training process is detailed in
Sec. [5](#S5 "5 DNN Training Process ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement").

### 4.2 Speech IFC Vector Estimation

Similarly to
([12](#S2.E12 "In 2 Signal Model ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement")),
the ML-based approach in \[[7](#bib.bib7)\] estimates the speech IFC
vector as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\mathbf{x}}(l)=\frac{1+\widehat{\xi}(l)}{\widehat{\xi}(l)}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\mathbf{y}}(l)-\frac{1}{\widehat{\xi}(l)}\boldsymbol{\mu_{\gamma_{n}}}`$ |  | (22) |

where $`\widehat{\xi}(l)`$ is an estimate of the a-priori SNR and
$`\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\mathbf{y}}(l)`$ is an estimate of the
noisy IFC vector obtained similarly as in
([11](#S2.E11 "In 2 Signal Model ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement"))
using $`\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{y}}(l)`$ from
([15](#S4.E15 "In 4.1 Correlation Matrices Estimation ‣ 4 Parameter Estimation ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement")).
The fixed mean noise IFC vector $`\boldsymbol{\mu_{\gamma_{n}}}`$ can be
computed based on the analysis window and overlap
settings \[[7](#bib.bib7)\].

Alternatively, by replacing the fixed mean noise IFC vector by a
TF-varying noise IFC vector estimate
$`\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\mathbf{n}}(l)`$, the speech IFC vector
can be computed as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\mathbf{x}}(l)=\frac{1+\widehat{\xi}(l)}{\widehat{\xi}(l)}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\mathbf{y}}(l)-\frac{1}{\widehat{\xi}(l)}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\mathbf{n}}(l)`$ |  | (23) |

where $`\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\mathbf{n}}(l)`$ is obtained
similarly to
([11](#S2.E11 "In 2 Signal Model ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement"))
using $`\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}_{\mathbf{n}}(l)`$ from
([16](#S4.E16 "In 4.1 Correlation Matrices Estimation ‣ 4 Parameter Estimation ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement")).

To estimate the a-priori SNR $`\xi(l)`$, we apply the well-known
decision-directed approach (DDA) \[[29](#bib.bib29)\], i.e.,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\widehat{\xi}(l)=\lambda_{\mathrm{DDA}}\frac{\widehat{X}(l-1)}{\widehat{\phi}_{N}(l-1)}+(1-\lambda_{\mathrm{DDA}})\frac{|Y(l)|^{2}}{\widehat{\phi}_{N}(l-1)},
``` |  | (24) |

with weighting constant $`\lambda_{\mathrm{DDA}}`$ and
$`\widehat{X}(l-1)`$ denoting the speech estimate of the previous frame.

## 5 DNN Training Process

As described
in ([21](#S4.E21 "In 4.1 Correlation Matrices Estimation ‣ 4 Parameter Estimation ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement")),
the DNN is trained to map the input features, i.e., the noisy STFT
coefficient magnitudes, to the SPP. More specifically, we train the DNN
with the target defined as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathrm{SPP}_{\mathrm{DNN}}(l)=\left(1+\frac{P(\mathcal{H}_{0})}{P(\mathcal{H}_{1})}(1+\xi_{\mathcal{H}_{1}})e^{-\frac{|Y(l)|^{2}}{\phi_{N}(l)}\frac{\xi_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}}{1+\xi_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}}}\right)^{-1}.`$ |  | (25) |

For this target, we compute the noise PSD $`\phi_{N}(l)`$ via recursive
averaging of the noise component, which is available during training,
i.e.,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\phi_{N}(l)=\alpha_{n}\phi_{N}(l-1)+(1-\alpha_{n})\left\lvert N(l)\right\rvert^{2}.
``` |  | (26) |

As loss function, we use the mean-squared difference between the target
SPP defined
in ([25](#S5.E25 "In 5 DNN Training Process ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement"))
and the estimated SPP
$`\widetilde{\mathrm{SPP}}_{\mathrm{DNN}}(k,\ l)`$, i.e.,

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\hskip-5.0pt\frac{1}{LK}\sum_{l=0}^{L-1}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\left(\widetilde{\mathrm{SPP}}_{\mathrm{DNN}}(k,\ l)-\mathrm{SPP}_{\mathrm{DNN}}(k,\ l)\right)^{2},
``` |  | (27) |

where
$`\widetilde{\mathrm{SPP}}_{\mathrm{DNN}}(k,\ l)=f_{\boldsymbol{\tilde{\Theta}}}\left\{\mathbf{\left\lvert Y\right\rvert}\right\}(k,\ l)`$
uses the current set of parameters $`\boldsymbol{\tilde{\Theta}}`$. The
DNN is composed of an input layer with 33 input nodes, a hidden BLSTM
layer with 256 nodes for each direction, two hidden fully-connected
layers with 513 nodes each, and an output layer with 33 nodes. The
corresponding activation functions of the hidden and output layers are
$`\tanh`$, rectifying linear unit ($`\mathrm{ReLU}`$),
$`\mathrm{ReLU}`$, and $`\mathrm{sigmoid}`$, respectively, inherently
restricting the SPP estimates to $`]0,\ 1[`$. This network architecture
is inspired by the DNN used in \[[23](#bib.bib23)\] and has been tested
for various sets of hyperparameters.

The network weights are initialized using a uniform distribution
$`U(-a,\ a)`$, with $`a=\sqrt{6/(n_{\text{in}}+n_{\text{out}})}`$, and
$`n_{\text{in}}`$ and $`n_{\text{out}}`$ the number of input and output
neurons of the layer, respectively \[[30](#bib.bib30)\]. All bias values
are initialized with 0. To decrease the dynamic range of the input data
and to stabilize the training process, we apply batch normalization to
the input and before the activations of the hidden
layers \[[31](#bib.bib31)\]. To optimize the network parameters, the
Adam optimizer is utilized with parameters as proposed
in \[[32](#bib.bib32)\], with the learning rate set to $`10^{-3}`$ and
the smoothing parameters for the gradient and the squared gradient set
to 0.9 and 0.999, respectively. If the $`l^{2}`$-norm of a gradient is
larger than 1, the gradient is divided by this norm.

To evaluate the model performance, we make use of a separate validation
set as described in
Sec. [6.1](#S6.SS1 "6.1 Dataset ‣ 6 Experimental Results ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement").
The training is stopped either after 100 epochs or after the validation
loss as measured
by ([27](#S5.E27 "In 5 DNN Training Process ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement"))
has not decreased for 5 epochs. The DNN is implemented in PyTorch
1.2.0 \[[33](#bib.bib33)\], and training and evaluation are performed on
a multi-GPU system utilizing 3 NVIDIA GeForce^(®) GTX 1080 Ti graphics
cards.

## 6 Experimental Results

In this section, we compare the speech enhancement performance of the
MFMPDR filter in
([14](#S3.E14 "In 3 Multi-Frame MPDR Filter ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement"))
using

1.  1.
    to estimate the SPP required
    in ([17](#S4.E17 "In 4.1 Correlation Matrices Estimation ‣ 4 Parameter Estimation ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement")):
    either the model-based SPP estimator
    $`\widehat{\mathrm{SPP}}_{\mathrm{R}}`$ in
    ([20](#S4.E20 "In 4.1 Correlation Matrices Estimation ‣ 4 Parameter Estimation ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement"))
    or the proposed DNN-based estimator
    $`\widehat{\mathrm{SPP}}_{\mathrm{DNN}}`$ in
    ([21](#S4.E21 "In 4.1 Correlation Matrices Estimation ‣ 4 Parameter Estimation ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement")).
2.  2.
    to estimate the speech IFC vector: either the fixed mean noise IFC
    vector $`\boldsymbol{\mu_{\gamma_{n}}}`$ in
    ([22](#S4.E22 "In 4.2 Speech IFC Vector Estimation ‣ 4 Parameter Estimation ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement"))
    or the estimated time-varying noise IFC vector
    $`\widehat{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{\mathbf{n}}(l)`$
    in ([23](#S4.E23 "In 4.2 Speech IFC Vector Estimation ‣ 4 Parameter Estimation ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement")).

In addition, to investigate the impact of exploiting speech IFCs, we
also use the SPP estimators
in ([20](#S4.E20 "In 4.1 Correlation Matrices Estimation ‣ 4 Parameter Estimation ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement"))
and ([21](#S4.E21 "In 4.1 Correlation Matrices Estimation ‣ 4 Parameter Estimation ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement"))
in a (single-frame) Wiener gain (WG), resulting in a total of 6 compared
methods.

### 6.1 Dataset

As clean speech material, we have used the training, development, and
test sets of the WSJ0 corpus \[[26](#bib.bib26)\] for training, model
validation, and evaluation, respectively. The noisy microphone signals
have been generated by adding scaled (randomly chosen) noise segments to
the clean speech signals at a sampling frequency of 16 kHz. Regarding
noise, we have used the NOISEX92 database \[[27](#bib.bib27)\] for
training and the Aurora database \[[34](#bib.bib34)\] for evaluation,
resulting in a strong mismatch between training and evaluation
conditions in order to evaluate the generalization capability of the
proposed method. For each training utterance, the corresponding
broadband SNR has been uniformly sampled from $`[0,20]\,\mathrm{dB}`$.
For evaluation, 4 random utterances from the WSJ0 test set have been
used at broadband SNRs $`\in\{-5,\ 0,\ 5,\ 10,\ 15,\ 20\}\,\mathrm{dB}`$
for each of the 8 noise types in the Aurora
database \[[34](#bib.bib34)\]. In total, this results in 12776, 2348,
and 192 utterances for training, validation, and evaluation,
respectively.

### 6.2 Simulation Settings

Since the speech IFC vector is highly time-varying, we employ an STFT
with a high temporal resolution, i.e., a frame length of 4 ms and a
frame shift of 1 ms, similarly as in \[[6](#bib.bib6), [7](#bib.bib7),
[9](#bib.bib9), [10](#bib.bib10)\]. A Hann window is used for both STFT
analysis and synthesis. The parameters of both the model-based SPP
estimator $`\widehat{\mathrm{SPP}}_{\mathrm{R}}`$ in
([20](#S4.E20 "In 4.1 Correlation Matrices Estimation ‣ 4 Parameter Estimation ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement"))
and the DNN-based SPP estimator
$`\widehat{\mathrm{SPP}}_{\mathrm{DNN}}`$ in
([21](#S4.E21 "In 4.1 Correlation Matrices Estimation ‣ 4 Parameter Estimation ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement"))
are set as proposed in \[[16](#bib.bib16)\], i.e.,
$`P(\mathcal{H}_{1})=P(\mathcal{H}_{0})=0.5`$ and
$`\xi_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}=15\,\mathrm{dB}`$. As recursive smoothing
constants, we use $`\alpha_{n}=0.98`$, $`\lambda_{y}=0.92`$, and
$`\lambda_{\mathrm{DDA}}=0.97`$. The MFMPDR filters use a filter length
of $`N=18`$, such that correlations within a window of 21 ms can be
exploited. To be more comparable to the MFMPDR filters, the WG methods
are used with the same settings, except for $`N=1`$. To improve
numerical stability when inverting a matrix, we perform regularization
using diagonal loading as in \[[6](#bib.bib6), [7](#bib.bib7)\] with
regularization parameter $`\delta=10^{-3}`$. Finally, all compared
methods use a minimum gain of -17 dB.

For the 6 considered methods,
Tab. [1](#S6.T1 "Table 1 ‣ 6.3 Results ‣ 6 Experimental Results ‣ DNN-Based Speech Presence Probability Estimationfor Multi-Frame Single-Microphone Speech Enhancement")
depicts the improvements in terms of the perceptual evaluation of speech
quality (PESQ) \[[35](#bib.bib35)\] measure w.r.t. the noisy microphone
signals as a function of the input SNR. The clean speech signal has been
used as the reference signal. Subscripts denote which SPP estimator was
used and, in the case of the MFMPDR filters, whether the mean IFC noise
vector $`\boldsymbol{\mu_{\gamma_{n}}}`$ or the time-varying noise IFC
vector $`\boldsymbol{\gamma_{n}}(l)`$ was utilized. The presented values
are averaged over all utterances and noise types included in the
evaluation set.

First, it can be observed that the MFMPDR filter utilizing the proposed
DNN-based SPP estimate $`\widehat{\mathrm{SPP}}_{\mathrm{DNN}}(l)`$ and
the fixed mean noise IFC vector $`\boldsymbol{\mu_{\gamma_{n}}}`$
($`\textrm{MFMPDR}_{\mathrm{DNN},\mu}`$), yields the highest PESQ
improvements for all input SNRs except 20 dB. Second, comparing the
methods utilizing either the model-based SPP estimate
$`\widehat{\mathrm{SPP}}_{\mathrm{R}}(l)`$ or the DNN-based SPP estimate
$`\widehat{\mathrm{SPP}}_{\mathrm{DNN}}(l)`$, the advantages of using
the DNN-based estimator are evident. This may be explained by the fact
that, in contrast to the model-based estimator, the DNN can exploit
spectral structures of speech and noise. Third, contrasting the MFMPDR
filters and the WG, it can be confirmed that exploiting speech IFCs may
yield higher speech quality improvements than directly using the SPP
estimate in a WG approach \[[7](#bib.bib7), [9](#bib.bib9)\] (except for
an input SNR of 20 dB). The difference between the MFMPDR-based methods
and the WG-based methods increases for lower input SNRs, suggesting that
exploiting the speech IFCs is especially helpful in adverse scenarios.
Fourth, using the fixed mean noise IFC vector in the MFMPDR filters
consistently leads to larger PESQ improvements than using the estimated
time-varying noise IFC vector. Considering the results in
\[[7](#bib.bib7), [36](#bib.bib36)\], this suggests that a filter bank
with higher frequency resolution is required to effectively incorporate
an estimate of the time-varying noise IFC vector into the MFMPDR filter.

## 7 Conclusion

In this paper we considered a DNN-based SPP estimator for multi-frame
approaches in single-microphone speech enhancement. Since the MFMPDR
filter requires accurate estimates of the time-varying noisy correlation
matrix and especially the speech IFC vector, in this paper we propose to
use a DNN to improve the estimation of the speech IFC vector. The DNN is
trained to map noisy STFT coefficient magnitudes to an SPP on a database
comprising multiple noise types within a large SNR range to improve the
generalization capability of the DNN. We demonstrate a higher objective
speech quality improvement when using the proposed DNN-based SPP
estimator instead of a state-of-the-art model-based estimator.
Furthermore, by comparing the MFMPDR filters with Wiener gains based on
equal SPP estimates, we confirm that utilizing interframe correlations
can be beneficial especially in adverse scenarios.
