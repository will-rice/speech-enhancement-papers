# An Explicit Consistency-Preserving Loss Function for Phase Reconstruction and Speech Enhancement

Pin-Jui Ku¹, Chun-Wei Ho¹, Hao Yen¹, Sabato Marco Siniscalchi¹² and
Chin-Hui Lee¹ Affiliation: ¹Georgia Institute of Technology, USA
Affiliation: ²Università degli Studi di Palermo, Italy

###### Abstract

In this work, we propose a novel consistency-preserving loss function
for recovering the phase information in the context of phase
reconstruction (PR) and speech enhancement (SE). Different from
conventional techniques that directly estimate the phase using a deep
model, our idea is to exploit ad-hoc constraints to directly generate a
consistent pair of magnitude and phase. Specifically, the proposed loss
forces a set of complex numbers to be a consistent short-time Fourier
transform (STFT) representation, i.e., to be the spectrogram of a real
signal. Our approach thus avoids the difficulty of estimating the
original phase, which is highly unstructured and sensitive to time
shift. The influence of our proposed loss is first assessed on a PR
task, experimentally demonstrating that our approach is viable. Next, we
show its effectiveness on an SE task, using both the VB-DMD and
WSJ0-CHiME3 data sets. On VB-DMD, our approach is competitive with
conventional solutions. On the challenging WSJ0-CHiME3 set, the proposed
framework compares favourably over those techniques that explicitly
estimate the phase.

###### Index Terms: 

STFT, Phase Reconstruction, Griffin-Lim, Magnitude-phase Consistency,
Speech Enhancement

## I Introduction

Phase reconstruction (PR) of short-term Fourier transform coefficients
(STFT) is an important challenge in speech enhancement (SE)
\[[1](#bib.bib1), [2](#bib.bib2), [3](#bib.bib3)\] and speech separation
tasks \[[4](#bib.bib4), [5](#bib.bib5), [6](#bib.bib6),
[7](#bib.bib7)\]. Traditional approaches have mainly focused on
manipulating the noisy magnitude, leaving the noisy phase spectrogram
unchanged \[[8](#bib.bib8), [9](#bib.bib9), [10](#bib.bib10),
[11](#bib.bib11), [12](#bib.bib12), [13](#bib.bib13), [14](#bib.bib14),
[15](#bib.bib15)\]. However, pairing estimated magnitidue spectrograms
with noisy phase information can lead to magnitude distortion and
introduce artifacts into the speech signal\[[1](#bib.bib1),
[16](#bib.bib16), [17](#bib.bib17), [18](#bib.bib18)\], which mainly
arises from inconsistencies between magnitude and phase spectrograms.

The idea of the magnitude-phase leveraging consistency constraints
during phase reconstructio dates back to the Griffin-Lim algorithm
(GLA) \[[19](#bib.bib19)\]. GLA iteratively transforms the
complex-valued spectrogram into a time-domain signal and converts it
back to the short-time frequency domain. During each iteration, the
magnitude information is preserved while the phase estimate is refined.
Building upon GLA concepts, \[[20](#bib.bib20)\] derives an explicit
consistency constraints for complex-valued spectrograms. These
constraints were used to develop a flexible phase reconstruction
algorithm that was used to perform a real-time processing based on
sliding-block analysis, avoiding the GLA iterative refinements.

The growing research on deep SE models that aim to estimate both the
magnitude and phase of the clean spectrogram simultaneously highlights
the significance of accurate phase estimation \[[21](#bib.bib21),
[22](#bib.bib22), [23](#bib.bib23), [24](#bib.bib24), [25](#bib.bib25),
[26](#bib.bib26), [27](#bib.bib27), [28](#bib.bib28)\]. To achieve phase
estimation, different phase losses have been proposed, for instance,
complex-domain loss \[[22](#bib.bib22), [26](#bib.bib26)\], cosine
distance loss \[[29](#bib.bib29)\], time-domain loss \[[30](#bib.bib30),
[23](#bib.bib23)\], and anti-wrapping loss \[[24](#bib.bib24),
[25](#bib.bib25)\]. These losses assume the deep SE model directly
estimates the phase. However, the original phase spectrogram is highly
unstructured due to phase wrapping \[[31](#bib.bib31)\] and is extremely
sensitive to time shifts \[[32](#bib.bib32)\], making training with such
losses particularly challenging. Another major drawback is that the SE
model attempts to generate the original phase while ignoring other
plausible phase solutions that could still produce perceptually good
speech. For instance, two signals $`x(n)`$ and $`-x(n)`$ have the same
magnitude spectrogram, but their phases differ by a constant $`\pi`$.
This is known as the sign indeterminacy problem, which highlights the
existence of at least two viable phase solutions for a given magnitude.
Zhang \[[28](#bib.bib28)\] reached similar conclusions, demonstrating
that phase shifts by an arbitrary constant do not degrade speech
quality.

In this work, we show indeed that the quality of the generated speech
signal can be preserved as long as magnitude and phase spectrogram are
consistent. In order to generate consistent magnitude and phase pairs,
we propose a novel loss function for phase spectrogram reconstruction,
which is to be used during the SE deep model training phase. Our SE deep
model is no longer forced to explicitly estimate a single phase
solution, overcoming the above-mentioned problems, such as sign
indeterminacy, by focusing on the generation of phase spectrogram
consistent with its magnitude. While a loss function based on the
consistency constraint is used in recent SE models\[[25](#bib.bib25),
[27](#bib.bib27)\] through consistency projection \[[19](#bib.bib19),
[33](#bib.bib33)\], a key difference is that these works still primarily
rely on conventional phase loss functions, such as anti-wrapping or
complex-domain loss, with the consistency-based loss treated merely as
an auxiliary loss. In other words, the main objective remains the direct
estimation of the original phase, leaving the challenges of phase
estimation unresolved. To the best of our knowledge, our paper is the
first to introduce the consistency constraint as the only phase loss
function to train SE models. The feasibility of our approach is
initially evaluated on the PR task by comparing it with existing methods
that directly perform phase estimation. Experimental results validate
the effectiveness of our technique. Next we consider the SE task, using
the VB-DMD and WSJ0-CHiME3 data sets. We found that leveraging the
proposed loss, the SE deep models attain consistent improvement in
speech quality evaluation metrics.

## II Related Techniques

Let $`\mathbf{H}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times N}`$ represent the complex-valued
STFT spectrogram of the target signal with $`M`$ time frames and $`N`$
frequency bins. The magnitude spectrogram is denoted by
$`\mathbf{A}=|\mathbf{H}|`$, and the phase spectrogram by
$`\mathbf{P}=\angle\mathbf{H}`$. In the PR task, the deep model
generates a phase estimate $`\mathbf{P}^{\prime}`$ given the magnitude
$`\mathbf{A}`$ as input. Different loss functions can be employed to
achieve this task.

Cosine Distance Loss: It is based on the von Mises
distribution \[[29](#bib.bib29)\] and measures the distance between
$`\mathbf{P}`$ and $`\mathbf{P}^{\prime}`$ using the cosine
distance \[[29](#bib.bib29)\]:

|     |     |     |     |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|     |

````math
\mathcal{L}_{cos}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{P}^{\prime})=-\sum_{m,n}\cos(\mathbf{P}_{m,n}-\mathbf{P}^{\prime}_{m,n})
``` |  | (1) |

Since the cosine function is periodic in $`2\pi`$, this loss function
naturally addresses the phase wrapping issue.

Complex-Domain Loss: $`L1`$, or $`L2`$ distances have also been proposed
to compute the distance between $`\mathbf{P}`$ and
$`\mathbf{P}^{\prime}`$ in the complex domain. For the $`L2`$ distance,
the loss would be:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{comp\_L2}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{P}^{\prime})=\sum_{m,n}\|\mathbf{H}_{m,n}-\mathbf{H}^{\prime}_{m,n}\|_{2}^{2}
``` |  | (2) |

where $`\mathbf{H}^{\prime}=\mathbf{A}e^{j\mathbf{P}^{\prime}}`$. This
loss can be interpreted as a cosine distance loss weighted by the
magnitude $`\mathbf{A}`$:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\sum_{m,n}\mathbf{A}_{m,n}\|e^{j\mathbf{P}_{m,n}}`$ | $`\displaystyle-e^{j\mathbf{P}^{\prime}_{m,n}}\|_{2}^{2}=`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle\sum_{m,n}2\mathbf{A}_{m,n}(1-\cos(\mathbf{P}_{m,n}-\mathbf{P}^{\prime}_{m,n}))`$ |  | (3) |

Time-Domain Loss: $`L1`$, or $`L2`$ distances can be computed in the
time domain as well. The $`L2`$ loss in the time domain is:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\mathcal{L}_{time\_L2}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{P}^{\prime})=\sum_{n}\|\text{iSTFT}(\mathbf{H})-\text{iSTFT}(\mathbf{H}^{\prime})\|_{2}^{2}
``` |  | (4) |

where the summation is over all time indices $`n`$ after applying the
inverse STFT.

Anti-wrapping Loss: The anti-wrapping loss \[[24](#bib.bib24)\] avoids
phase wrapping when computing the L2 distance between $`\mathbf{P}`$ and
$`\mathbf{P}^{\prime}`$ as follows:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{AW}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{P}^{\prime})=`$ |  |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\sum_{m,n}\|\mathbf{P}_{m,n}-`$ | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{\prime}_{m,n}-2\pi\cdot\text{round}\left(\frac{\mathbf{P}_{m,n}-\mathbf{P}^{\prime}_{m,n}}{2\pi}\right)\|_{2}^{2}`$ |  | (5) |

Loss Functions Based on Phase Derivatives: It has been shown beneficial
to define losses based on the distances between the target and predicted
phase derivatives along the time and frequency axes, i.e., the group
delay (GD), and the instantaneous frequency (IF) \[[24](#bib.bib24),
[29](#bib.bib29), [28](#bib.bib28)\]. Those quantities are more
structured and less sensitive to time shifts compared to the phase
itself. For example, the cosine distance loss can be modified as
discussed in \[[29](#bib.bib29), [32](#bib.bib32)\]:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{cos+derv}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{P}^{\prime})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{cos}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{P}^{\prime})`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle+\mathcal{L}_{cos}(\text{GD}(\mathbf{P}),\text{GD}(\mathbf{P}^{\prime}))`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle+\mathcal{L}_{cos}(\text{IF}(\mathbf{P}),\text{IF}(\mathbf{P}^{\prime}))`$ |  | (6) |

where $`\text{GD}(\cdot)`$ and $`\text{IF}(\cdot)`$ are functions that
compute GD and IF from the phase, respectively. Similarly, the
anti-wrapping loss can be modified as shown in  \[[24](#bib.bib24)\]:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{AW+derv}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{P}^{\prime})`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{AW}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{P}^{\prime})`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle+\mathcal{L}_{AW}(\text{GD}(\mathbf{P}),\text{GD}(\mathbf{P}^{\prime}))`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle+\mathcal{L}_{AW}(\text{IF}(\mathbf{P}),\text{IF}(\mathbf{P}^{\prime}))`$ |  | (7) |

## III Explicit Consistency-Preserving Loss

### III-A Magnitude-Phase Consistency Constraints

We define the following STFT quantities: window length $`N`$, hop length
$`R`$, analysis window function $`W[n]`$, and synthesis window function
$`S[n]`$. $`W[n]`$ and $`S[n]`$ are zero outside the interval
$`0\leq n\leq N-1`$, and the window length $`N`$ is an integer multiple
of the hop length $`R`$, with $`Q=\frac{N}{R}`$.

For $`\mathbf{H}`$ to be a consistent spectrogram, it must correspond to
the STFT of a time-domain signal, which should also be, by definition,
the result of applying the inverse STFT to $`\mathbf{H}`$. Thus, a
necessary and sufficient condition for $`\mathbf{H}`$ to be a consistent
spectrogram is that it equals the STFT of its inverse STFT. Therefore,
any consistent spectrogram $`\mathbf{H}`$ must obey the following
equation $`\forall n^{\prime}\in[0,N-1]`$ \[[20](#bib.bib20)\]:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{m,n^{\prime}}=`$ | $`\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k}W[k]e^{-j2\pi k\frac{n^{\prime}}{N}}`$ |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle(`$ | $`\displaystyle S[k]\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbf{H}_{m,n}e^{j2\pi n\frac{k}{N}}`$ |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle+`$ | $`\displaystyle\sum_{q=1}^{Q-1}S[k+qR]\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbf{H}_{m-q,n}e^{j2\pi n\frac{k+qR}{N}}`$ |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle+`$ | $`\displaystyle\sum_{q=1}^{Q-1}S[k-qR]\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbf{H}_{m+q,n}e^{j2\pi n\frac{k-qR}{N}})`$ |  | (8) |

By introducing the coefficients

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\alpha_{q}^{(R)}(p)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k}W[k]S[k+qR]e^{-j2\pi p\frac{k+qR}{N}}-\delta_{p}\delta_{q}
``` |  | (9) |

where $`-(N-1)\leq p\leq N-1`$, $`-(Q-1)\leq q\leq Q-1`$, and
$`\delta_{i}`$ is the Kronecker delta, \[[20](#bib.bib20)\] simplifies
Eq. [8](#S3.Ex7 "In III-A Magnitude-Phase Consistency Constraints ‣ III Explicit Consistency-Preserving Loss ‣ An Explicit Consistency-Preserving Loss Function for Phase Reconstruction and Speech Enhancement")
and shows that $`\mathbf{H}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times N}`$ is a consistent
spectrogram if and only if

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\sum_{q=-(Q-1)}^{Q-1}e^{j2\pi\frac{qR}{N}n}(\alpha_{q}^{(R)}*\mathbf{H})_{m-q,n}=0
``` |  | (10) |

is satisfied for all $`m\in[0,M-1]`$ and for all $`n\in[0,N-1]`$, where
the convolution acts on the frequency axis of $`\mathbf{H}`$.

### III-B Proposed Explicit Consistency Loss

Eq. ([10](#S3.E10 "In III-A Magnitude-Phase Consistency Constraints ‣ III Explicit Consistency-Preserving Loss ‣ An Explicit Consistency-Preserving Loss Function for Phase Reconstruction and Speech Enhancement"))
can be used to derive a loss function $`\mathcal{L}_{EC}`$ that measures
the consistency of $`\mathbf{H}`$ as follows:

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{EC}(\mathbf{H})=\sum_{m,n}\left|\sum_{q=-(Q-1)}^{Q-1}e^{j2\pi\frac{qR}{N}n}(\alpha_{q}^{(R)}*\mathbf{H})_{m-q,n}\right|^{2}`$ |  | (11) |

which is the reconstruction criterion employed in \[[20](#bib.bib20)\].
Thus, $`\mathcal{L}_{EC}(\mathbf{H})`$ can directly serve as the
optimization criterion to train deep models generating complex-valued
spectrograms. For instance, in the PR task, $`\mathcal{L}_{EC}`$ can be
used to force the phase prediction $`\mathbf{P}^{\prime}`$ such that
$`\mathbf{H}^{\prime}=\mathbf{A}e^{j\mathbf{P}^{\prime}}`$ is
consistent:

|  |  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|----|
|  | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{EC}(\mathbf{P}^{\prime})`$ | $`\displaystyle=`$ |  |  |
|  | $`\displaystyle\sum_{m,n}`$ | $`\displaystyle\left|\sum_{q=-(Q-1)}^{Q-1}e^{j2\pi\frac{qR}{N}n}(\alpha_{q}^{(R)}*\mathbf{A}e^{j\mathbf{P}^{\prime}})_{m-q,n}\right|^{2}`$ |  | (12) |

A key difference between $`\mathcal{L}_{EC}(\mathbf{P}^{\prime})`$ and
the losses discussed in
Sec. [II](#S2 "II Related Techniques ‣ An Explicit Consistency-Preserving Loss Function for Phase Reconstruction and Speech Enhancement")
is that it does not involve any direct distance measurement between
$`\mathbf{P}`$ and $`\mathbf{P}^{\prime}`$. Therefore, the estimated
phase spectrogram can deviate significantly from the original phase, as
long as it results in a consistent spectrogram when combined with the
magnitude spectrogram. $`\mathcal{L}_{EC}(\mathbf{P}^{\prime})`$ thereby
implicitly handles the time shift issue and allows to explore a broader
solution space that satisfies the consistency conditions. For instance,
our loss function explains the observation by \[[28](#bib.bib28)\] that
a global phase shift does not degrade speech quality. If $`\mathbf{H}`$
is a consistent spectrogram, then any spectrogram
$`\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{H}e^{j\theta}`$, with a global phase shift
$`\theta`$, remains consistent, as

|  |  |  |  |
|----|----|----|----|
|  |
``` math
\displaystyle\sum_{q=-(Q-1)}^{Q-1}e^{j2\pi\frac{qR}{N}n}(\alpha_{q}^{(R)}*\mathbf{K})_{m-q,n}=
``` |  |  |
|  |
``` math
\displaystyle e^{j\theta}\left(\sum_{q=-(Q-1)}^{Q-1}e^{j2\pi\frac{qR}{N}n}(\alpha_{q}^{(R)}*\mathbf{H})_{m-q,n}\right)=0
``` |  | (13) |

for all $`m,n`$. Since the consistency of the spectrogram is unaffected
by phase shifts, the speech quality remains intact.

## IV Experimental Setup & Results

### IV-A Experimental Data Sets

We use the VoiceBank-DEMAND (VB-DMD) \[[34](#bib.bib34)\] and
WSJ0-CHIME3 \[[35](#bib.bib35)\] data sets in our experiments. For
VB-DMD, we use the conventional train, validation, and test splits from
\[[36](#bib.bib36)\], resulting in 10,802 training, 770 validation, and
824 evaluation utterances. The WSJ0-CHiME3 data set was prepared as
described in \[[35](#bib.bib35)\], with 12,776 training, 1,206
validation, and 651 test utterances. The Remix and BandMask data
augmentation methods proposed in \[[37](#bib.bib37)\] are adopted. Our
evaluation metrics include wide-band perceptual evaluation of speech
quality (PESQ) \[[38](#bib.bib38)\], extended short-term objective
intelligibility (ESTOI) \[[39](#bib.bib39)\], prediction of signal
distortion (CSIG), and prediction of overall speech quality (COVL).

### IV-B Inputs and Outputs in the PR and SE Experiments

TF-GridNet \[[40](#bib.bib40)\] is the backbone model used across all
experiments. It was implemented with hyperparameters $`D=64`$,
$`H=128`$, $`I=J=1`$, and $`B=3`$ as in \[[40](#bib.bib40)\], excluding
the global attention layer. This results in a model with approximately
1.3 million trainable parameters.

Fig. [1](#S3.F1 "Fig. 1 ‣ III-B Proposed Explicit Consistency Loss ‣ III Explicit Consistency-Preserving Loss ‣ An Explicit Consistency-Preserving Loss Function for Phase Reconstruction and Speech Enhancement")
illustrates the inputs and outputs adopted in TF-GridNet for the PR and
SE experimental tasks. In the “cheating” PR task assuming the magnitude
of clean speech $`\mathbf{A}_{clean}`$ is known, the model has two
outputs, $`\mathbf{C}_{1}`$ and
$`\mathbf{C}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}`$, to compute the phase
prediction $`\mathbf{P}^{\prime}`$ as
$`\mathbf{P}^{\prime}=\arctan\left(\frac{\mathbf{C}_{1}}{\mathbf{C}_{2}}\right)`$.
The predicted phase $`\mathbf{P}^{\prime}`$ is either compared with the
original phase of clean speech $`\mathbf{P}_{clean}`$ using one of the
losses in
Sec. [II](#S2 "II Related Techniques ‣ An Explicit Consistency-Preserving Loss Function for Phase Reconstruction and Speech Enhancement"),
or combined with the clean magnitude $`\mathbf{A}_{clean}`$ as indicated
in the proposed loss function $`\mathcal{L}_{EC}`$. As in the “real” SE
task in
Fig. [1](#S3.F1 "Fig. 1 ‣ III-B Proposed Explicit Consistency Loss ‣ III Explicit Consistency-Preserving Loss ‣ An Explicit Consistency-Preserving Loss Function for Phase Reconstruction and Speech Enhancement")(b)
using noisy magnitude and phase spectrograms
$`(\mathbf{A}_{noisy},\mathbf{P}_{noisy})`$ as inputs, the model
produces three outputs, with the third $`\mathbf{C}_{3}`$ used as a
real-valued mask to generate the estimated magnitude spectrogram
$`\mathbf{A}^{\prime}`$. The $`L1`$ loss is used to compute the error
between $`\mathbf{A}^{\prime}`$ and clean magnitude
$`\mathbf{A}_{clean}`$. Different from what done for PR,
$`\mathbf{P}^{\prime}`$ is paired with $`\mathbf{A}^{\prime}`$ instead
of $`\mathbf{A}_{clean}`$ when computing $`\mathcal{L}_{EC}`$ in
Eq. ([10](#S3.E10 "In III-A Magnitude-Phase Consistency Constraints ‣ III Explicit Consistency-Preserving Loss ‣ An Explicit Consistency-Preserving Loss Function for Phase Reconstruction and Speech Enhancement")).

The training uses 32-second batches for 50 and 160 epochs in the PR and
SE tasks, respectively. The Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate
of $`1\times 10^{-3}`$, which decays to $`1\times 10^{-5}`$ via cosine
annealing, is used. STFT is computed with a 512-sample window with a
128-sample hop size of a Hann window function. Magnitude compression is
then applied per \[[35](#bib.bib35)\] with the compression parameters
$`a=0.5`$ and $`b=1`$.

Table [I](#S4.T1 "TABLE I ‣ IV-B Inputs and Outputs in the PR and SE Experiments ‣ IV Experimental Setup & Results ‣ An Explicit Consistency-Preserving Loss Function for Phase Reconstruction and Speech Enhancement"),
when the loss is calculated directly on phase spectrograms (i.e.,
without taking phase derivatives into account), deep models trained
using any of the losses discussed in
Sec. [II](#S2 "II Related Techniques ‣ An Explicit Consistency-Preserving Loss Function for Phase Reconstruction and Speech Enhancement")
fail to produce phase spectrograms that result in high-quality speech.
On the average, the speech quality is worse than that attained using the
noisy phase as indicated in the top row of
Table [I](#S4.T1 "TABLE I ‣ IV-B Inputs and Outputs in the PR and SE Experiments ‣ IV Experimental Setup & Results ‣ An Explicit Consistency-Preserving Loss Function for Phase Reconstruction and Speech Enhancement").
This highlights the difficulty of direct phase estimation due to the
randomness and time-shift sensitivity of the target phase. In contrast,
when the deep model is trained with $`\mathcal{L}_{EC}`$, the generated
phase to reconstruct a speech signal indeed outperforms the quality
attained with the noisy phase, achieving an average PESQ of 4.15. This
result supports our hypothesis that the quality of the generated speech
signal can be maintained as long as the magnitude and phase are
consistent in order to effectively guide a deep model in the “cheating”
PR task.

Those results might call into question the need for the proposed loss
function. However, we found that the benefits of incorporating phase
derivative loss are only evident when the ground-truth magnitude
information is available, which is in line with \[[32](#bib.bib32),

the “real” SE task, and that makes an accurate phase derivative
estimation much more challenging due to the coupling between phase and
magnitude \[[42](#bib.bib42), [43](#bib.bib43)\]. Furthermore, in the SE
task the model is provided with noisy phase, which serves as a good
intial estimate of the clean phase as shown in the first row in
Table [I](#S4.T1 "TABLE I ‣ IV-B Inputs and Outputs in the PR and SE Experiments ‣ IV Experimental Setup & Results ‣ An Explicit Consistency-Preserving Loss Function for Phase Reconstruction and Speech Enhancement")
and alleviates the difficulty of minimizing the direct phase loss
without considering phase derivatives.

### IV-D Speech Enhancement Results

We now assess the use of the proposed loss in the SE task. Both the
cosine distance and the anti-wrapping losses are used for comparison,
with or without phase derivatives. Furthermore, we analyze the effect of
including the MetricGAN loss \[[21](#bib.bib21)\], which has proven
useful in the SE task.

Table [II](#S4.T2 "TABLE II ‣ IV-C Phase Reconstruction Results ‣ IV Experimental Setup & Results ‣ An Explicit Consistency-Preserving Loss Function for Phase Reconstruction and Speech Enhancement")
presents the evaluation results on the VB-DMD data set. It can be
observed that the cosine distance and anti-wrapping losses lead to
similar SE results. As expected, no improvement is observed when
including a loss based on phase derivatives. In contrast, leveraging
$`\mathcal{L}_{EC}`$, a marginal improvement in PESQ is obtained
independently of the MetricGAN loss. Remarkably, our results using
$`\mathcal{L}_{EC}`$ as the only phase loss are comparable to
state-of-the-art solutions, such as MP-SENet \[[25](#bib.bib25)\] and
SEMamba \[[27](#bib.bib27)\], which are trained using multiple phase
loss functions with carefully fine-tuned weights for each individual
loss.

To further test the effectiveness of the proposed loss function in a
more challenging scenario, we conduct the same experiment on the
WSJ0-Chime3 data set, where SNR values can be as low as -6 dB. As shown

proposed consistency-preserving loss function provides an even more
remarkable improvement on this more challenging data set, with a boost

This result confirms our assumption that the proposed function serves as
an ideal phase loss in the SE task by considering the consistency
between the estimated magnitude and phase and facilitates the deep model
to explore a broader solution space instead of trying to directly
estimate the original phase.

## V Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a novel loss function for phase estimation
based on the magnitude-phase consistency constraint. Unlike existing
phase losses, the proposed approach does not enforce a single-phase
solution path. Instead, it requires only that the model generates a
phase spectrogram consistent with its corresponding magnitude without
specifying any details of the original phase, thus allowing for a
broader solution space. Experimental results confirm the effectiveness
of the proposed loss function, demonstrating superior performance
compared to existing cosine distance loss and anti-wrapping loss,
particularly in low-SNR scenarios.
````
