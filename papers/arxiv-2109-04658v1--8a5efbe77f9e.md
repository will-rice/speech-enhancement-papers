###### Abstract

Rank-constrained spatial covariance matrix estimation (RCSCME) is a
method for the situation that the directional target speech and the
diffuse noise are mixed. In conventional RCSCME, independent low-rank
matrix analysis (ILRMA) is used as the preprocessing method. We propose
RCSCME using independent deeply learned matrix analysis (IDLMA), which
is a supervised extension of ILRMA. In this method, IDLMA requires deep
neural networks (DNNs) to separate the target speech and the noise. We
use Denoiser, which is a single-channel speech enhancement DNN, in IDLMA
to estimate not only the target speech but also the noise. We also
propose noise self-supervised RCSCME, in which we estimate the
noise-only time intervals using the output of Denoiser and design the
prior distribution of the noise spatial covariance matrix for RCSCME. We
confirm that the proposed methods outperform the conventional methods
under several noise conditions.

## 1 Introduction

In this study, we deal with speech enhancement. In particular, we deal
with the problem of extracting directional target speech from diffuse
noise. This situation arises, for example, when the speaker is close to
the microphones in a noisy environment such as a crowded place or a
train station.

From a practical perspective, it is effective to use blind source
separation (BSS) \[[1](#bib.bib1)\], which does not require any spatial
information or characteristics of each source. In a determined or
overdetermined situation (number of microphones $`M`$ $`\geq`$ number of
sources $`N`$), independent vector analysis (IVA) \[[2](#bib.bib2),
[3](#bib.bib3), [4](#bib.bib4)\] and independent low-rank matrix
analysis (ILRMA) \[[5](#bib.bib5)\] have been proposed for BSS. ILRMA
assumes that the power spectrogram for each source is modeled by
nonnegative matrix factorization (NMF) \[[6](#bib.bib6)\]. Since these
methods assume that the rank of the spatial covariance matrix (SCM) of
each source is unity, we call these methods rank-1 methods. In a rank-1
method, the noise remains in the separated target signal when the noise
has a full-rank SCM, such as diffuse noise \[[7](#bib.bib7),
[8](#bib.bib8), [9](#bib.bib9)\].

Multichannel NMF (MNMF) \[[10](#bib.bib10), [11](#bib.bib11)\] and
FastMNMF \[[12](#bib.bib12), [13](#bib.bib13)\] have been proposed as
methods to model full-rank SCMs for each source. MNMF adopts the
full-rank nature for the source model \[[14](#bib.bib14)\] and can
handle diffuse sources, but it is computationally expensive and highly
dependent on initial values \[[5](#bib.bib5)\]. In FastMNMF, the
computational complexity is reduced by assuming that the SCMs are
jointly diagonalizable. However, the problem that the performance is not
robust to changes in the initial value still remains.

Rank-constrained spatial covariance matrix estimation (RCSCME)
\[[15](#bib.bib15), [16](#bib.bib16)\] has been proposed as a method of
blind speech enhancement for mixed signals of directional target speech
and diffuse noise. RCSCME uses a rank-1 method such as ILRMA in advance.
Since a rank-1 method accurately obtains $`M-1`$ noise signals, we can
obtain the rank-($`M-1`$) component of the noise SCM from these signals.
RCSCME estimates the time-varying variance of the target speech and the
diffuse noise, and complements the deficient rank-1 component of the
noise SCM at the same time. As shown in \[[15](#bib.bib15)\], this
method is robust and converges rapidly because there are fewer estimated
parameters than in MNMF and FastMNMF.

For the case that training data are available, many supervised source
separation methods using a deep neural network (DNN) have been proposed.
For a determined or overdetermined situation, independent deeply learned
matrix analysis (IDLMA) \[[17](#bib.bib17)\] has been proposed to
improve source separation performance by changing the source model of
ILRMA from NMF to DNNs. Owing to the appropriately trained DNNs, IDLMA
achieves higher performance than ILRMA. Since IDLMA is a rank-1 method
similarly to ILRMA, the noise remains in the separated target signal in
a situation with diffuse noise.

In this paper, we propose the use of IDLMA, which is a rank-1 method, as
the preprocessing method of RCSCME. Since IDLMA was originally proposed
for musical source separation, we should prepare DNNs that estimate the
power spectrogram of speech or noise. One of the DNN-based
single-channel speech enhancement methods is
Denoiser \[[18](#bib.bib18)\], which estimates the target speech from a
mixed-signal input in the waveform domain. Whereas IDLMA requires DNNs
for each source, Denoiser extracts only the target speech. Thus, we also
propose a scheme to estimate the power spectrogram of the noise using
Denoiser. Furthermore, considering the high capability of Denoiser to
reduce noise, we can find the noise-only time intervals of the observed
signal using the output of Denoiser. The SCM of this noise-only signal
is a good approximation of the noise SCM. Thus, we design the prior
distribution of the noise using this approximated noise SCM for RCSCME,
and we call this method noise self-supervised RCSCME. We derive the new
update rule on the basis of the EM algorithm for noise self-supervised
RCSCME. We conduct a simulated experiment to verify the effectiveness of
the proposed methods.

## 2 Conventional Methods

### 2.1 ILRMA and IDLMA

Let $`\bm{s}_{ij}=(s_{ij,1},\ldots,s_{ij,N})^{\mathsf{T}}`$,
$`\bm{x}_{ij}=(x_{ij,1},\ldots,x_{ij,M})^{\mathsf{T}}`$, and
$`\bm{y}_{ij}=(y_{ij,1},\ldots,y_{ij,N})^{\mathsf{T}}`$ denote the
short-time Fourier transform (STFT) of the source, observed, and
separated signals, respectively, where $`i=1,\ldots,I`$ and
$`j=1,\ldots,J`$ are the indices of the frequency bin and time frame,
respectively. Here, ^(T) denotes transpose. We also represent the
spectrograms for each signal as
$`S_{n},X_{m},Y_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{I\times J}`$ whose $`(i,j)`$th
elements are $`s_{ij,n},x_{ij,m}`$, and $`y_{ij,n}`$, where
$`n=1,\ldots,N`$ and $`m=1,\ldots,M`$ are the source and channel
indices, respectively.

When the reverberation time is sufficiently shorter than the window
length of the STFT and each source is a point source, we can denote the
observed signal as

|     |                                                |     |     |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\bm{x}_{ij}=A_{i}\bm{s}_{ij},`$ |     | (1) |

where
$`A_{i}=\quantity(\bm{a}_{i, 1}, \ldots,\bm{a}_{i, N})\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times N}`$
is a mixing matrix. Assuming that $`N=M`$ and $`A_{i}`$ is invertible,
we can obtain the separated signal by estimating its inverse matrix
$`W_{i}=\quantity(\bm{w}_{i, 1}, \ldots,\bm{w}_{i, N})^{\mathsf{H}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times M}`$,
which is called the demixing matrix, such that

|     |                                                |     |     |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\bm{y}_{ij}=W_{i}\bm{x}_{ij},`$ |     | (2) |

where ^(H) denotes the Hermitian transpose.

In ILRMA\[[5](#bib.bib5)\] and IDLMA\[[17](#bib.bib17)\], it is assumed
that the separated signals are mutually independent and that the $`n`$th
separated signal in each time-frequency frame is generated by the
univariate complex Gaussian distribution as

|     |                                                                                         |     |     |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle y_{ij,n}\sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{c}}\left(0,\sigma_{ij,n}^{2}\right),`$ |     | (3) |

where $`0`$ and $`\sigma_{ij,n}^{2}`$ are the mean and variance,
respectively. We use the negative log-likelihood of the observed signal
as the cost function:

|     |                                            |                                                              |                         |                                              |
| --- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{I}}=`$ | $`\displaystyle-\log\prod_{i,j,n}p(x_{ij,n})`$               |                         |                                              |
|     | $`\displaystyle=`$                         | $`\displaystyle-\log\prod*{i,j,n}p(y*{ij,n})-2J\sum\_{i}\log | \det W\_{i}             | `$                                           |     |     |
|     | $`\displaystyle=`$                         | $`\displaystyle\sum\_{i,j,n}\quantity(\frac{                 | w*{in}^\mathsf{H}x*{ij} | ^2}{\sigma*{ij,n}^2} + 2\log\sigma*{ij,n})`$ |     |     |
|     |                                            | $`\displaystyle-2J\sum\_{i}\log                              | \det W\_{i}             | +\mathrm{const.},`$                          |     | (4) |

where $`\mathrm{const.}`$ includes the terms independent of the target
variables. In ILRMA, $`\sigma_{ij,n}^{2}`$ is modeled by NMF
\[[6](#bib.bib6)\] as

|     |                                                              |     |     |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\sigma_{ij,n}^{2}=\sum_{k}t_{ik,n}v_{kj,n},`$ |     | (5) |

where $`t_{ik,n}\geq 0`$ and $`v_{kj,n}\geq 0`$ are the NMF variables.
Instead, in IDLMA, we use a source separation DNN that estimates the
power spectrogram:

|     |                                    |                                                                                            |     |     |
| --- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\sigma_{ij,n}^{2}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\max\quantity{\abs{\qty(\mathrm{DNN}_n \qty(Y_n))_{ij}}^2, \varepsilon},`$ |     | (6) |

where $`\varepsilon>0`$ is a small number used to avoid numerical
instability. Here, $`\mathrm{DNN}_{n}`$ is the trained DNN corresponding
to the $`n`$th source and its input is the current separated signal.

Since diffuse noise is not a point source, ILRMA and IDLMA cannot
separate noise in the same direction as the directional target speech in
principle \[[7](#bib.bib7), [8](#bib.bib8), [9](#bib.bib9)\]. Therefore,
we cannot directly apply ILRMA and IDLMA to diffuse noise reduction,
which often arises in practical speech enhancement application.

### 2.2 RCSCME

RCSCME \[[15](#bib.bib15), [16](#bib.bib16)\] deals with the problem of
speech enhancement when directional target speech and diffuse noise are
mixed. In \[[15](#bib.bib15)\], it is assumed that the generative model
of the observed signal is the multivariate complex Gaussian distribution

|     |                                                                                                                                         |     |     |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle p(x_{ij};0,R_{ij}^{(\mathrm{o})})\propto\frac{\exp(-x_{ij}^\mathsf{H}\scm{ij}{o}x_{ij})}{\det R_{ij}^{(\mathrm{o})}},`$ |     | (7) |

where $`R_{ij}^{(\mathrm{o})}`$ denotes the covariance matrix of the
observed signal. $`R_{ij}^{(\mathrm{o})}`$ is modeled as

|     |                                                                                                                              |     |     |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle R_{ij}^{(\mathrm{o})}=r_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})}R_{i}^{(\mathrm{t})}+r_{ij}^{(\mathrm{n})}R_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})},`$ |     | (8) |

where $`r_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})}>0`$ and $`r_{ij}^{(\mathrm{n})}>0`$ are
the variances of the target speech and the noise, and
$`R_{i}^{(\mathrm{t})}`$ and $`R_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}`$ are the SCMs of
the target speech and the noise, respectively. To induce the sparsity of
the target speech, we assume the inverse gamma distribution for the
prior distribution on $`r_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})}`$,

|     |                                                                                                                                       |     |     |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle p(r_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})};\alpha,\beta)\propto\quantity(\covar{ij}{t})^{-\alpha-1}\exp(-\frac{\beta}{\covar{ij}{t}}),`$ |     | (9) |

where $`\alpha>0`$ and $`\beta>0`$ are the shape parameter and scale
parameter, respectively. Using the estimated mixing matrix
$`W_{i}^{-1}=A_{i}`$, we model the SCMs of the target source and noise
as

|     |                                                    |                                                                                                                      |     |      |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle R_{i}^{(\mathrm{t})}`$             | $`\displaystyle=\bm{a}_{i,n_{t}}\bm{a}_{i,n_{t}}^{\mathsf{H}},`$                                                     |     | (10) |
|     | $`\displaystyle R_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}`$             | $`\displaystyle={{R^{\prime}}_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}}+\lambda_{i}\bm{v}_{i}\bm{v}_{i}^{\mathsf{H}},`$                    |     | (11) |
|     | $`\displaystyle{{R^{\prime}}_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j}\hat{y}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{n})}\quantity(\hat{y}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{n})})^{\mathsf{H}},`$ |     | (12) |
|     | $`\displaystyle\hat{y}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{n})}`$       | $`\displaystyle=A_{i}\left(w_{i,1}^{\mathsf{H}}x_{ij},\ldots,w_{i,n_{t}-1}^{\mathsf{H}}x_{ij},0,\right.`$            |     |      |
|     |                                                    | $`\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\left.w_{i,n_{t}+1}^{\mathsf{H}}x_{ij},\ldots,w_{i,N}x_{ij}\right)^{\mathsf{T}},`$   |     | (13) |

where $`n_{t}`$ denotes the target index and
$`{{R^{\prime}}_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times M}`$ is the
rank-($`M-1`$) SCM of the diffuse noise estimated by a rank-1 method.
Since the rank of $`{{R^{\prime}}_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}}`$ is $`M-1`$,
$`\bm{v}_{i}`$ must be linearly independent of any column vectors of
$`{{R^{\prime}}_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}}`$ in order that
$`R_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}`$ has full rank. For example, the unit
eigenvector corresponding to the zero eigenvalue of
$`{{R^{\prime}}_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}}`$ satisfies this condition for
$`\bm{v}_{i}`$. RCSCME simultaneously estimates $`\lambda_{i}`$,
$`r_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})}`$, and $`r_{ij}^{(\mathrm{n})}`$ by maximum a
posteriori estimation. Using
([7](#S2.E7 "In 2.2 RCSCME ‣ 2 Conventional Methods ‣ Speech Enhancement by Noise Self-Supervised Rank-Constrained Spatial Covariance Matrix Estimation via Independent Deeply Learned Matrix Analysis"))
and
([9](#S2.E9 "In 2.2 RCSCME ‣ 2 Conventional Methods ‣ Speech Enhancement by Noise Self-Supervised Rank-Constrained Spatial Covariance Matrix Estimation via Independent Deeply Learned Matrix Analysis")),
we calculate the log-posterior of the observed signal as

|     |                                                                  |                                                                                                                                   |                                                             |      |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ---- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{RC}}(\Theta_{\mathrm{c}})=`$ | $`\displaystyle\log\prod*{i,j}p(\bm{x}*{ij}                                                                                       | \Theta*{\mathrm{c}})p(r*{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})};\alpha,\beta)`$ |      |     |
|     | $`\displaystyle=`$                                               | $`\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j}\Biggl(\bm{x}_{ij}^{\mathsf{H}}R_{ij}^{(\mathrm{o})}\bm{x}_{ij}+\log\det R_{ij}^{(\mathrm{o})}\Biggr.`$ |                                                             |      |
|     |                                                                  | $`\displaystyle+\left.(\alpha+1)\log r_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})}+\frac{\beta}{r_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})}}\right)+\mathrm{const.},`$          |                                                             | (14) |

where
$`\Theta_{\mathrm{c}}=\{\lambda_{i},r_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})},r_{ij}^{(\mathrm{n})}\}`$.
We maximize the log-posterior function using the EM algorithm with the
following $`Q`$ function (see \[[15](#bib.bib15)\] for details):

|     |                                                                                               |                                                                                                                                        |     |      |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle Q_{\mathrm{RC}}\quantity(\Theta_{\mathrm{c}}; \tilde{\Theta}_{\mathrm{c}})=`$ | $`\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j}\Bigg((\alpha+2)\log r_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})}+\frac{\hat{r}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})}+\beta}{r_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})}}`$ |     |      |
|     |                                                                                               | $`\displaystyle+M\log r_{ij}^{(\mathrm{n})}+\log\det R_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}`$                                                            |     |      |
|     |                                                                                               | $`\displaystyle+\frac{\tr(\hat{R}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{n})}\qty(R_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})})^{-1})}{r_{ij}^{(\mathrm{n})}}\Bigg)+\mathrm{const.},`$ |     | (15) |

where
$`\tilde{\Theta}_{\mathrm{c}}=\{\tilde{\lambda}_{i},\tilde{r}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})},\tilde{r}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{n})}\}`$
is the set of the up-to-date parameters, and
$`\hat{r}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})}`$ and $`\hat{R}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{n})}`$ are
calculated in the E-step as follows:

|     |                                                |                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |                                                                                          |         |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}=`$ | $`\displaystyle{{R^{\prime}}_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}}+\tilde{\lambda}_{i}\bm{v}_{i}\bm{v}_{i}^{\mathsf{H}},`$                                                                                                         |                                                                                          | (16)    |
|     | $`\displaystyle R_{ij}^{(\mathrm{o})}=`$       | $`\displaystyle\tilde{r}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})}R_{i}^{(\mathrm{t})}+\tilde{r}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{n})}\tilde{R}_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})},`$                                                                                    |                                                                                          | (17)    |
|     | $`\displaystyle\hat{r}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})}=`$  | $`\displaystyle\tilde{r}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})}-\quantity(\tilde{r}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})})^{2}\bm{a}_{i,n_{t}}^{\mathsf{H}}\quantity(\scm{ij}{o})^{-1}\bm{a}_{i,n_{t}}`$                                              |                                                                                          |         |
|     |                                                | $`\displaystyle+\quantity                                                                                                                                                                                        | \tilde{r}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})}\bm{x}_{ij}^\mathsf{H}\qty(\scm{ij}{o})^{-1}\bm{a}\_{i,n_t} | ^{2},`$ |     | (18) |
|     | $`\displaystyle\hat{R}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{n})}=`$  | $`\displaystyle\tilde{r}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{n})}\tilde{R}_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}-\quantity(\tilde{r}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})})^{2}\tilde{R}_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}\quantity(\scm{ij}{o})^{-1}\tilde{R}_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}`$       |                                                                                          |         |
|     |                                                | $`\displaystyle+\quantity(\tilde{r}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})})^{2}\tilde{R}_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}\quantity(\scm{ij}{o})^{-1}\bm{x}_{ij}\bm{x}_{ij}^{\mathsf{H}}\quantity(\scm{ij}{o})^{-1}\tilde{R}_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}.`$ |                                                                                          | (19)    |

In the M-step, by finding the stationary point of $`Q_{\mathrm{RC}}`$,
we obtain the update rules as

|     |                                         |                                                                                                                                                              |     |      |
| --- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle r_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})}`$ | $`\displaystyle\leftarrow\frac{\hat{r}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})}+\beta}{\alpha+2},`$                                                                               |     | (20) |
|     | $`\displaystyle\lambda_{i}`$            | $`\displaystyle\leftarrow\bm{u}_{i}^{\mathsf{H}}\quantity(\frac{1}{J}\sum_j \frac{1}{\tilde{r}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{n})}}\hat{R}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{n})})\bm{u}_{i},`$ |     | (21) |
|     | $`\displaystyle R_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}`$  | $`\displaystyle\leftarrow{{R^{\prime}}_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}}+{\lambda}_{i}\bm{v}_{i}\bm{v}_{i}^{\mathsf{H}},`$                                                 |     | (22) |
|     | $`\displaystyle r_{ij}^{(\mathrm{n})}`$ | $`\displaystyle\leftarrow\frac{1}{M}\tr(\hat{R}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{n})}\qty(R_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})})^{-1}),`$                                                       |     | (23) |

where $`\bm{u}_{i}`$ is the eigenvector corresponding to the zero
eigenvalues of $`{{R^{\prime}}_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}}`$ satisfying
$`\bm{u}_{i}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{v}_{i}=1`$. Here, we use the following
property for
$`R_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}={{R^{\prime}}_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}}+\lambda_{i}\bm{v}_{i}\bm{v}_{i}^{\mathsf{H}}`$,
as proved in \[[16](#bib.bib16)\], in obtaining the update rule of
$`\lambda_{i}`$:

|     |                                                |                                                                                                                                                                                              |     |      |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\log\det R_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\log\lambda_{i}+\mathrm{const.},`$                                                                                                                                           |     | (24) |
|     | $`\displaystyle\quantity(\scm{i}{n})^{-1}`$    | $`\displaystyle=\quantity(E - \bm{u}_i\bm{v}_i^{\mathsf{H}})\quantity(\scmd{i}{n})^{+}\quantity(E - \bm{v}_i\bm{u}_i^{\mathsf{H}})+\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}\bm{u}_{i}\bm{u}_{i}^{\mathsf{H}},`$ |     | (25) |

where $`E\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times M}`$ is the identity matrix and ⁺
denotes the Moore–Penrose inverse.

Finally, we obtain the extracted target speech using the multichannel
Wiener filter as

|     |                                                                                                                               |     |      |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\hat{s}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})}=r_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})}R_{i}^{(\mathrm{t})}\quantity(\scm{ij}{o})^{-1}\bm{x}_{ij}.`$ |     | (26) |

## 3 Proposed Method

### 3.1 Motivation and Framework

In recent years, research on DNNs has become more popular and DNN-based
source separation methods have been proposed \[[19](#bib.bib19),
[18](#bib.bib18), [20](#bib.bib20), [21](#bib.bib21),
[17](#bib.bib17)\]. IDLMA \[[17](#bib.bib17)\] has been proposed for
determined audio source separation. IDLMA has higher separation
performance than ILRMA by introducing the supervised DNNs into the
source models. Since IDLMA is also a rank-1 method similarly to ILRMA,
we propose the use of IDLMA instead of ILRMA as the preprocessing method
for RCSCME. We expect that RCSCME will achieve high separation
performance using highly accurate parameters pre-estimated by IDLMA.
However, since the original IDLMA dealt with musical source separation,
we should prepare new DNNs for the speech and the noise. For the
single-channel speech enhancement DNN, Denoiser \[[18](#bib.bib18)\] has
been proposed. Here, we propose a scheme to estimate the power
spectrogram not only of the target speech, but also of the noise using
Denoiser.

As shown in Table
[1](#S3.T1 "Table 1 ‣ 3.1 Motivation and Framework ‣ 3 Proposed Method ‣ Speech Enhancement by Noise Self-Supervised Rank-Constrained Spatial Covariance Matrix Estimation via Independent Deeply Learned Matrix Analysis"),
Denoiser tends to have very high accuracy in terms of the
source-to-inference ratio (SIR) \[[22](#bib.bib22)\] improvement, which
is a metric of denoising, but poor results in terms of the
sources-to-artifact ratio (SAR) \[[22](#bib.bib22)\], which is a metric
of artifacts. In other words, Denoiser is very good at removing noise,
but it also tends to partially remove the target speech. Considering the
high SIR of Denoiser, we can extract the noise-only time intervals from
the observed signal corresponding to the quiet time intervals of the
output of Denoiser and approximate the noise SCM from these intervals.
Thus, we propose noise self-supervised RCSCME, in which the prior
distribution of the noise SCM is introduced using the approximated noise
SCM.

|                        | Noise  |       |         |         |
| ---------------------- | ------ | ----- | ------- | ------- |
|                        | Babble | Cafe  | Station | Traffic |
| SIR improvement \[dB\] | 17.45  | 19.23 | 18.33   | 20.64   |
| SAR \[dB\]             | 8.89   | 9.94  | 7.54    | 10.83   |

Table 1: SIR improvement and SAR of Denoiser

Finally, an overview of the proposed method is as follows:

1.  1.  Prepare the trained Denoiser.
2.  2.  Estimate the demixing matrix $`W_{i}`$ by IDLMA using Denoiser.
3.  3.  Calculate the quiet time intervals of the output using Denoiser, and
        set the corresponding time intervals of the observed signal as the
        noise-only signal.
4.  4.  Determine the prior distribution of the noise from the noise-only
        signal calculated in 3) and perform RCSCME with the result of 2).
5.  5.  Construct the multichannel Wiener filter with the result of 4).

The process flow of the proposed method is shown in Fig.

### 3.2 IDLMA for Speech Enhancement

In the separated signals $`Y_{n}`$ obtained by IDLMA, the $`n_{t}`$th
signal must be the target speech, and the other channels must be noise
signals. Therefore, for the variance of the $`n_{t}`$th separated
signal, we use the output of the DNN, and for the other channels, we
subtract the outputs of the DNN from the separated signals to simulate
the estimation of the noise signal:

|                                                                               |                                    |                                                                                          |               |                     |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------- | ------------------- | --- | ---- |
|                                                                               | $`\displaystyle\zeta_{ij,n}`$      | $`\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\quantity(\mathrm{DNN}\qty(Y*n))*{ij}&\text{if }n=n\_{t},\\ |
| y*{ij,n}-\quantity(\mathrm{DNN}\qty(Y_n))*{ij}&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}`$ |                                    | (27)                                                                                     |
|                                                                               | $`\displaystyle\sigma_{ij,n}^{2}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\max\quantity{\qty                                                       | \zeta\_{ij,n} | ^2, \varepsilon}.`$ |     | (28) |

The reason for using the DNN for each channel is that each separated
signal and each output of the DNN need to be in phase when we obtain the
noise signal by subtraction.

### 3.3 Self-Estimation of Prior Distribution of Noise

We consider the silent parts of the output of Denoiser to be the
noise-only parts in the observed signal. By thresholding the denoised
signal by Denoiser, we extract the noise-only signal
$`\quantity{{\bm{x}}_{ij'}}_{j^{\prime}\in\mathcal{J}^{\prime}}`$, where

|     |                                       |                                                             |                                                                                           |     |
| --- | ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\left\{j\in\left\{1,\cdots,J\right\}\middle | \sqrt{\sum*{i}\absolutevalue{\qty(\mathrm{DNN}\qty(X*{n*t}))*{ij}}^{2}}<\theta\right\}.`$ |     | (29) |

Here, $`\theta>0`$ is a threshold parameter. The SCM of the noise-only
time intervals is calculated as

|     |                                               |                                                                                                                                     |     |      |
| --- | --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\breve{R}_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}`$ | $`\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\,\quantity[{\bm{x}}_{ij'}{\bm{x}}_{ij'}^\mathsf{H}]`$                                                    |     |      |
|     |                                               | $`\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{J^{\prime}}}\sum_{j^{\prime}}{\bm{x}}_{ij^{\prime}}{\bm{x}}_{ij^{\prime}}^{\mathsf{H}},`$ |     | (30) |

where $`\#`$ denotes the cardinality of a finite set.

One of the conjugate prior distributions for the covariance matrix in
the multivariate complex Gaussian distribution is the complex inverse
matrix gamma distribution (CIMGD) \[[23](#bib.bib23)\], which is an
extension of the inverse gamma distribution to a positive definite
Hermitian matrix. We assume that $`R_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}`$ is generated
by the $`M`$-dimensional CIMGD:

|     |     |                                                                                    |                                                                                               |     |      |
| --- | --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     |     | $`\displaystyle p\quantity(\scm{i}{n};\alpha',\beta',\breve{R}_i^{(\mathrm{n})})`$ |                                                                                               |     |      |
|     |     | $`\displaystyle\propto\quantity(\det\scm{i}{n})^{-(\alpha^{\prime}+M)}`$           | $`\displaystyle\exp(-\frac{1}{\beta'}\tr(\breve{R}_i^{(\mathrm{n})}\qty(\scm{i}{n})^{-1})),`$ |     | (31) |

where $`\alpha^{\prime}>M-1`$, $`\beta^{\prime}>0`$, and
$`\breve{R}_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}`$ are the shape parameter, the scale
parameter, and the scale matrix, respectively. If
$`\alpha^{\prime}=\tau`$ and $`\beta^{\prime}=1`$ in
([31](#S3.E31 "In 3.3 Self-Estimation of Prior Distribution of Noise ‣ 3 Proposed Method ‣ Speech Enhancement by Noise Self-Supervised Rank-Constrained Spatial Covariance Matrix Estimation via Independent Deeply Learned Matrix Analysis")),
this distribution coincides with the complex inverse Wishart
distribution with degree of freedom $`\tau`$.

### 3.4 Log-Posterior with Prior Distribution of Noise

The log-posterior likelihood given the prior distributions for the
variance of target speech and the noise SCM is

|     |                                       |                                                                                                                           |                                                                                                                  |      |
| --- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\Theta)=`$ | $`\displaystyle\log\prod*{i,j}p(\bm{x}*{ij}                                                                               | \Theta)p(r\_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})};\alpha,\beta)p\quantity(\scm{i}{n};\alpha',\beta',\breve{R}\_i^{(\mathrm{n})})`$ |      |     |
|     | $`\displaystyle=`$                    | $`\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j}\Bigg(\bm{x}_{ij}^{\mathsf{H}}R_{ij}^{(\mathrm{o})}\bm{x}_{ij}+\log\det R_{ij}^{(\mathrm{o})}`$ |                                                                                                                  |      |
|     |                                       | $`\displaystyle+(\alpha+1)\log r_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})}+\frac{\beta}{r_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})}}\Bigg)`$                          |                                                                                                                  |      |
|     |                                       | $`\displaystyle-\sum_{i}\Bigg((\alpha^{\prime}+M)\log\det R_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}`$                                          |                                                                                                                  |      |
|     |                                       | $`\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}\tr(\breve{R}_i^{(\mathrm{n})}\qty(\scm{i}{n})^{-1})\Bigg)+\mathrm{const.}`$      |                                                                                                                  | (32) |

We calculate the $`Q`$ function in the same manner as
in \[[15](#bib.bib15)\].

|     |                                                       |                                                                                                                                        |     |     |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
|     | $`\displaystyle Q\quantity(\Theta; \tilde{\Theta})=`$ | $`\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j}\Bigg((\alpha+2)\log r_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})}+\frac{\hat{r}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})}+\beta}{r_{ij}^{(\mathrm{t})}}`$ |     |     |
|     |                                                       | $`\displaystyle+M\log r_{ij}^{(\mathrm{n})}+\log\det R_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}`$                                                            |     |     |
|     |                                                       | $`\displaystyle+\frac{\tr(\hat{R}_{ij}^{(\mathrm{n})}\qty(R_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})})^{-1})}{r_{ij}^{(\mathrm{n})}}\Bigg)`$                  |     |     |
|     |                                                       | $`\displaystyle-\sum\_{i}\Bigg(\quantity(                                                                                              |

\alpha' + M
)\log\det R\_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}`$ |  |  |
|  |  | $`\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}\tr(
\breve{R}\_i^{(\mathrm{n})}\qty(\scm{i}{n})^{-1}
)\Bigg)+\mathrm{const.}`$ | | (33) |

It is easily seen that we only need to change the update rule of
$`\lambda_{i}`$ in
([21](#S2.E21 "In 2.2 RCSCME ‣ 2 Conventional Methods ‣ Speech Enhancement by Noise Self-Supervised Rank-Constrained Spatial Covariance Matrix Estimation via Independent Deeply Learned Matrix Analysis")).
Using
([24](#S2.E24 "In 2.2 RCSCME ‣ 2 Conventional Methods ‣ Speech Enhancement by Noise Self-Supervised Rank-Constrained Spatial Covariance Matrix Estimation via Independent Deeply Learned Matrix Analysis"))
and
([25](#S2.E25 "In 2.2 RCSCME ‣ 2 Conventional Methods ‣ Speech Enhancement by Noise Self-Supervised Rank-Constrained Spatial Covariance Matrix Estimation via Independent Deeply Learned Matrix Analysis")),
we focus on the term depending on $`\lambda_{i}`$ and modify
([33](#S3.E33 "In 3.4 Log-Posterior with Prior Distribution of Noise ‣ 3 Proposed Method ‣ Speech Enhancement by Noise Self-Supervised Rank-Constrained Spatial Covariance Matrix Estimation via Independent Deeply Learned Matrix Analysis"))

| --- | ----------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |

## 4 Experiment

### 4.1 Experimental Conditions

We conducted a simulated experiment to verify the effectiveness of the
proposed method. The diffuse noise was simulated by convolution of the
impulse response from 19 loudspeakers to four microphones. We used the
four noise signals: babble, cafe, station, and traffic noise. For the
babble noise, we used 19 speech signals from the JNAS speech corpus
\[[24](#bib.bib24)\]. For the cafe, station, and traffic noise, we used
19 signals, which were obtained by dividing each noise signal from
DEMAND \[[25](#bib.bib25)\]. The target speech signal was simulated by
convolution of the impulse response from a closer location. For the
target speech signal, we used one speech signal from the JNAS speech
corpus \[[24](#bib.bib24)\]. We used the conditions in
Fig. [2](#S3.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 3.4 Log-Posterior with Prior Distribution of Noise ‣ 3 Proposed Method ‣ Speech Enhancement by Noise Self-Supervised Rank-Constrained Spatial Covariance Matrix Estimation via Independent Deeply Learned Matrix Analysis")
to record the impulse response \[[16](#bib.bib16)\]. The sampling rate
was 16 kHz. The input signal-to-noise ratio was set to 0 dB. The STFT
was performed by using a 64-ms-long Hamming window and 32-ms-long shift.

We compared ILRMA, IDLMA, RCSCME using ILRMA (ILRMA+RCSCME), RCSCME
using IDLMA with $`\sigma_{ij,n}`$ updated by
([27](#S3.E27 "In 3.2 IDLMA for Speech Enhancement ‣ 3 Proposed Method ‣ Speech Enhancement by Noise Self-Supervised Rank-Constrained Spatial Covariance Matrix Estimation via Independent Deeply Learned Matrix Analysis"))
and
([28](#S3.E28 "In 3.2 IDLMA for Speech Enhancement ‣ 3 Proposed Method ‣ Speech Enhancement by Noise Self-Supervised Rank-Constrained Spatial Covariance Matrix Estimation via Independent Deeply Learned Matrix Analysis"))
(proposed method 1), and noise self-supervised RCSCME using IDLMA with
$`\lambda_{i}`$ updated by
([35](#S3.E35 "In 3.4 Log-Posterior with Prior Distribution of Noise ‣ 3 Proposed Method ‣ Speech Enhancement by Noise Self-Supervised Rank-Constrained Spatial Covariance Matrix Estimation via Independent Deeply Learned Matrix Analysis"))
(proposed method 2). We used source-to-distortion ratio (SDR)
\[[22](#bib.bib22)\] improvement as a measure. We used
$`(\alpha,\beta,\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})=(1.3,10^{-16},8\times 10^{2},10^{4})`$
for each method, which were selected experimentally. The flooring
parameter in
([6](#S2.E6 "In 2.1 ILRMA and IDLMA ‣ 2 Conventional Methods ‣ Speech Enhancement by Noise Self-Supervised Rank-Constrained Spatial Covariance Matrix Estimation via Independent Deeply Learned Matrix Analysis"))
and
([28](#S3.E28 "In 3.2 IDLMA for Speech Enhancement ‣ 3 Proposed Method ‣ Speech Enhancement by Noise Self-Supervised Rank-Constrained Spatial Covariance Matrix Estimation via Independent Deeply Learned Matrix Analysis"))
was set to $`\varepsilon=0.1\times\sum_{i,j}\sigma_{ij,n}^{2}/(IJ)`$ and
the threshold parameter in
([29](#S3.E29 "In 3.3 Self-Estimation of Prior Distribution of Noise ‣ 3 Proposed Method ‣ Speech Enhancement by Noise Self-Supervised Rank-Constrained Spatial Covariance Matrix Estimation via Independent Deeply Learned Matrix Analysis"))
was set to $`\theta=10^{-3}`$. The demixing matrix $`W_{i}`$ in ILRMA
and IDLMA was initialized by the identity matrix. The number of NMF
bases of ILRMA was set to $`10`$ and the NMF variables were initialized
by random values taken from the uniform distribution on $`[0,1]`$. We
used the unit eigenvector corresponding to the zero eigenvalue of
$`{{R^{\prime}}_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}}`$ as $`\bm{v}_{i}`$ in RCSCME. For
Denoiser, we used the trained model provided by \[[18](#bib.bib18)\]
with $`H=48`$ hidden channels. Since Denoiser is the waveform-domain
model, we used Denoiser as

|     |                                                                                                                  |     |      |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | ---- |
|     | $`\displaystyle\mathrm{DNN}(Y_{n})=\mathrm{STFT}\quantity[\mathrm{Denoiser}\qty(\mathrm{STFT}^{-1}\qty[Y_n])].`$ |     | (36) |

We set the number of iterations in ILRMA to 50, that in IDLMA to 90, and
that in RCSCME for ILRMA+RCSCME, proposed method 1, and proposed method
2 to 10. In IDLMA, $`\sigma_{ij,n}`$ was updated by DNN for every 30
times $`W_{i}`$ was updated. These numbers of iterations were
experimentally determined.

### 4.2 Results

We took the average of the SDR improvement results for 10 different sets
of randomly initialized values of the NMF variables in ILRMA and
ILRMA+RCSCME. The SDR improvements for each noise are shown in Fig.
[3](#S3.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 3.4 Log-Posterior with Prior Distribution of Noise ‣ 3 Proposed Method ‣ Speech Enhancement by Noise Self-Supervised Rank-Constrained Spatial Covariance Matrix Estimation via Independent Deeply Learned Matrix Analysis").
For each RCSCME method, we show the largest SDR improvement among the
iterations. As shown in Fig
[3](#S3.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 3.4 Log-Posterior with Prior Distribution of Noise ‣ 3 Proposed Method ‣ Speech Enhancement by Noise Self-Supervised Rank-Constrained Spatial Covariance Matrix Estimation via Independent Deeply Learned Matrix Analysis"),
Denoiser outperformed IDLMA for babble, cafe, and traffic noise. The
reason is that Denoiser includes a nonlinear transformation that can
provide stronger noise reduction than linear systems, whereas IDLMA
estimates the output signals via a linear time-invariant spatial filter,
i.e., demixing matrix $`W_{i}`$. However, proposed method 1 shows better
performance than Denoiser and IDLMA for all the noises, implying the
advantage of the time-variant properties in RCSCME. It is also shown
that proposed method 2 achieves the best separation performance among
all methods; this would be due to introduction of the self-estimated
prior distribution of the noise SCM. The SDR improvement for each
iteration is shown in Fig.
[4](#S3.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 3.4 Log-Posterior with Prior Distribution of Noise ‣ 3 Proposed Method ‣ Speech Enhancement by Noise Self-Supervised Rank-Constrained Spatial Covariance Matrix Estimation via Independent Deeply Learned Matrix Analysis").
Fig.
[4](#S3.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 3.4 Log-Posterior with Prior Distribution of Noise ‣ 3 Proposed Method ‣ Speech Enhancement by Noise Self-Supervised Rank-Constrained Spatial Covariance Matrix Estimation via Independent Deeply Learned Matrix Analysis")
shows that ILRMA+RCSCME requires more than five iterations to converge,
while proposed method 1 and proposed method 2 take the maximum value in
less than five iterations.

## 5 Conclusions

We proposed RCSCME using IDLMA, which is applied to speech enhancement
by preparing a single-channel speech enhancement DNN for estimating the
power spectrogram of the target speech and the noise. We used Denoiser
as the single-channel speech enhancement DNN model for estimating the
power spectrogram of the target speech. To estimate the power
spectrogram of diffuse noise, we subtracted the speech extracted by
Denoiser from the separated signal. We also proposed a method to
estimate the noise-only time intervals using the output of Denoiser and
design the prior distribution of the noise SCM for RCSCME. We confirmed
that noise self-supervised RCSCME using IDLMA outperformed the other
methods and took the maximum value in fewer iterations than the
conventional methods.
